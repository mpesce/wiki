# WebGL

## TL;DR

The in-browser graphics API that, in the corpus, is VRML's successor — "which enabled high-performance 3D graphics in web browsers" and made the VR resurgence possible.

## Overview

WebGL enters the corpus as the technology that inherited VRML's role. The stable account: in 1994 Pesce and Tony Parisi built **VRML**, "the first 3D interface for the World Wide Web, which was later superseded by WebGL" (Internet Transformations, 2021) ([[sources/internet-transformations-5oct21-mp4]]); the early 1994 interface **Labyrinth** "evolved into modern WebGL standards" (ARIN 2610, 2021) ([[sources/arin-2610-internet-transformations-the-ar-dilemm-2]]). In the 2016 VR Creative Cluster talk, WebGL is one of the two reasons VR returns: "the advancement of WebGL, which enabled high-performance 3D graphics in web browsers" — alongside the ubiquity of smartphones that drove down display and sensor costs ([[sources/vr-creative-cluster-mark-pesce-9-aug-video1-mp4]]). In the 2016 Monash data-visualization lecture, WebGL is part of Pesce's own career résumé: "contributing to the development of VRML and WebGL standards" ([[sources/monash-wav]]). The corpus holds WebGL as the browser-native successor to the 1994 VRML line — the technology that made real-time 3D available in ordinary web browsers without plug-ins — but gives no specification detail, no Khronos/W3C process, and no timeline of the transition.

## Debates / Open Questions


- **VRML → WebGL: how did the transition happen?** The corpus states the succession (VRML superseded by WebGL; Labyrinth evolved into WebGL) but gives no mechanism, date, or specification detail. The two entity pages (vrml, webgl) hold the succession claim without a bridge.

# The Next Billion Seconds

## TL;DR

Pesce's book, then lecture title, then podcast: the "gigasecond" frame (1995–2026 and beyond) for the hyper-connectivity era, and the brand for his futurist work from 2011 to the present.

## Overview

The corpus meets The Next Billion Seconds first as a book in the making. The 2011 "hyper-economics" lectures (given on the day of Steve Jobs's death) are the book's public preview: the 1995–2026 window framed as as significant to the species as the invention of language, market friction eliminated by connectivity — fishermen in Kerala pricing their catch, farmers in Kenya reading SMS market data, barbers in Karachi — and the prediction that platform companies (Kogan, Uber, Airbnb) are interim arbitrageurs to be disintermediated by API-based, gig-work economies ([[sources/hypereconomics-flv]], [[sources/hypereconomics-2-flv]]). The September 2011 education keynote describes him "currently writing" the sixth book, arguing first-phone ownership has fallen from age 11.5 (2003 survey) to eight, and that schools must teach the "hygiene of connectivity" ([[sources/thenextbillionsecondsineducation-1sep11-wav]]). The 2012 Melbourne launch ("Embiggen") adds the book's closing register: the woman seeking a neural implant, the argument that the phone *is* the implant, "Homo nexus" replacing Dunbar's number, and connectivity's double edge — the Arab Spring and WikiLeaks against state control ([[sources/embiggen-17aug12-mp4]]).

Then the title becomes a lecture series. "Listen" (2011) distills the marketing corollary — markets are conversations, so businesses must listen in real time ([[sources/listen-25oct11-mp3]]); the 2012 Heritage Film Festival talk treats the phone as phantom limb and social networks as filters ([[sources/thenextbillionseconds-atp-25feb12-wav]]); the 2013 university lecture runs the frame back 318,000 years, arguing hyper-connectivity reverses Heidegger's "enframing" toward a re-enchantment of objects ([[sources/powersofthen-9apr13-m4v]]); the Adelaide lecture introduces the "gigasecond" metaphor, the Safecast example of crowdsourced hyper-intelligence, and a live post-it-note truth experiment ([[sources/mind-share-hd-mov]]); the 2019 Canterbury INSPIRE talk reformulates the era as three partnerships — machine-to-machine, human-to-machine, human-to-human ([[sources/canterbury-inspire19-mp3]]); and the 2019 Citi keynote locates the human edge ("alpha") in forward intuition while machines remain backward-looking ([[sources/citi-wav]]). The undated AIJA talk fixes the arithmetic — a billion seconds from the moment of speaking lands in late January 2050 — and extends the projections to Australia's population, autonomous trucking on the Hume and Sturt, and peer-evaluated education ([[sources/aija-wav]]).

From 2017 the title becomes a podcast, and Pesce is repeatedly introduced as its host — by 2017's OzWater keynote and in every CFO-series talk that follows. The GIO-partnership promos (2020) advertise "a user's guide to the future" in six episodes ([[sources/gio-pc1-mark-pesce-video-01-final-4x5-mp4]], [[sources/gio-pc1-mark-pesce-video-02-final-4x5-mp4]]); he hosted the 2020 current-affairs podcast awards (Gold to Background Briefing, Silver to Full Story, Bronze to 7am) ([[sources/pesce-best-current-affairs-podcast-mp4]]). The pandemic-era CFO series is where the brand does its heaviest 2020–2021 work: the "sterile economy" and the contactless culture (Melbourne, June 2020), the "dome" and biosecurity-as-national-security (New Zealand, June 2020), the three states' distinct lessons and the airline-to-cargo pivot (Queensland, September 2020), post-classical finance — Bitcoin, CBDCs, Diem, NFTs, financialization (Sydney, May 2021) ([[sources/the-knew-normal-cfo-series-melbourne-10jun20-mp4]], [[sources/cfo-series-nz-18jun20-the-bubble-mp4]], [[sources/beyond-the-horizon-cfo-series-qld-10sep20-mp4]], [[sources/post-classical-finance-cfo-series-sydney-11may21]]). Supporting interviews from the same period — the December 2020 digital-currency conversation (AR headsets as an "80 percent substitute" by the decade's end; non-human wallets for Teslas and dairy cows) and the Alchemist interview (2021) ([[sources/mark-pesce-interview-9dec20-mp3]], [[sources/mark-pesce-alchemist-23jun21-wav]]) — keep the brand in circulation.

The frame's own consistency is the corpus's quiet finding: "a billion seconds" is ~32 years, and the speaker never predicts events, only climates — "a futurist is much more like a climatologist" — with William Gibson's "the future is already here, it's just not evenly distributed" as the house epigraph ([[sources/walking-with-the-ancestors-mp3]], [[sources/recording-7-wav]]). Even the industry talks wear it: a memorials-and-celebrants audience gets a 5/10/15/30-year roadmap ending in "walking with the ancestors" through AR spectacles, and property investors get "Property Over the Next Billion Seconds" ([[sources/walking-with-the-ancestors-mp3]], [[sources/recording-7-wav]]). The 2023 metaverse history series announced in the corpus's earliest interview is released on the same podcast, closing the loop ([[sources/mark-pesce-session-1-wav]]).

## Debates / Open Questions


- **The moving horizon.** The book's window is 1995–2026; the 2012 launch describes "out to approximately 2045"; the AIJA talk fixes a billion seconds at January 2050. The frame is a brand, not a constant — it stretches as the speaker ages. Worth noting, not worth "correcting."
- **Book, lecture, podcast, brand.** The corpus uses the title four ways; the ingest keys them to one entity. The brand's own consistency (same thesis, same epigraphs, same "climatologist not psychic" framing) justifies the merge.
- **The podcast's archive.** Only a handful of podcast episodes are in the corpus (promos, the awards show, the Genevieve Bell episode); the podcast's full output is not represented, so this page describes the brand's *use* in talks, not its archive.

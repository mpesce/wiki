# RMIT

## TL;DR

The Royal Melbourne Institute of Technology — in the corpus, the venue of the 2000 designers' keynote where everything is becoming "a single discipline."

## Overview

RMIT (the Royal Melbourne Institute of Technology) appears in the corpus as the venue of a single 2000 keynote, delivered to "a room full of designers", where Pesce opens with "you all will be [designers], because this is the future" ([[sources/rmit-mp3]]). The talk's argument: we are "hurtling into a future of design", riffing on **Buckminster Fuller's** call for a "comprehensive design science revolution" (design every object for maximum utility and efficiency) and the artifacts that have appeared since Fuller died in 1983. The talk is in three parts — **matter, intelligence, and design** — which "are not separate disciplines but co-evolving, dynamically changing each other, 'becoming a single discipline.'" The lineage of thinking machines runs from Babbage's difference engine and the punched-card looms, through Hollerith's 1880s census cards ("still in use in Florida in 2000"), to Turing and von Neumann as "the twin pillars of computer science", and McCarthy, Papert and Minsky founding the MIT AI lab. The talk closes with a design-ethics warning on head-mounted displays: the brain uses **SIX cues for depth** (parallax is only one); if a helmet gives only one, the brain trains on that single cue — "which is why pilots aren't allowed to fly a real plane for 24 hours after a simulator" — and "Every object you create is a designer in the age of active materials. Think ethically; design for openness, interaction, and play" ([[sources/rmit-mp3]]). The corpus's RMIT is the designers' venue; the page holds the keynote as the corpus's RMIT content.

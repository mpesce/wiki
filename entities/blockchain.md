# blockchain

## TL;DR

The technology that, in the corpus, appears almost entirely as Mark Pesce's financial-future toolkit — ledger first, coins second, with a stubbornly Australian cast of examples.

## Overview

Blockchain (ASR variants: blockchain, distributed ledger technology) enters the corpus in May 2016 with BlockHack 16, a two-day internal Westpac hackathon that Pesce hosted, with judges Nitin Gaur (IBM) and Nick Wales (UNSW) — an event born of an internal survey showing employee interest, and framed by Pesce as "the first World Wide Web conference in 1994" moment for blockchain ([[sources/zoom0013-tr2-wav]], [[sources/blockhack16-talk-wav]]). The Westpac panel (CTO Rob Wilson, Mike Baldwin, Ian Hill) is the corpus's most corporate treatment: blockchain as a disruption wave removing friction, a successful Ripple proof-of-concept for payments to Fiji and New Zealand, and the "build to think" method ([[sources/blockhack16-talk-wav]]).

In his futurist tours (CFO talks, Blue Sky Brisbane, Grant Thornton, Fintech Futures, a CFO series in Sydney) the same argument recurs in stable form: (1) the mechanism — blockchain as a chain of cryptographic hashes, explained with taxi receipts and travel-expense analogies; (2) the ledger-vs-coin distinction — speculative cryptocurrency trading is not the technology, and the "killer app" is settlement, not speculation; (3) the Australian cast — Power Ledger (Perth, peer-to-peer solar trading), Full Profile (immediate payment for grain farmers), Agridigital (agricultural settlements), and the ASX's move to blockchain-based settlement; (4) the cautionary tale — the DAO incident, a $150M code-governed fund hacked for $50M, forcing a controversial rollback; (5) the advice — banks should acquire fintech startups, and future professionals should take dual degrees in law/finance and computer science ([[sources/cfo-wav]], [[sources/smarter-money-19oct16-mp3]], [[sources/iag-m4a]], [[sources/gt-mel-wav]], [[sources/fintech-futures-wav]], [[sources/post-classical-finance-cfo-series-sydney-11may21]]).

The two outliers are the personal and the platform-scale: at the Blue Sky talks Pesce announces the **Mark Pesce Token**, a personal coin offering that tokenizes his own professional time, framed as an experiment in professional currencies ([[sources/iag-m4a]], [[sources/recording-1-m4a]]); and in June 2019 he reads Facebook's Libra as the moment the technology met scale — a stablecoin for the four billion people who have a phone but fewer than one billion who have a bank account, with Maxine Waters's congressional warning and Facebook's data advantage as the regulatory flashpoint ([[sources/rnd-20190619-1906-fb-libra-mp3]]). By 2021 the frame has widened to "post-classical finance": CBDCs (Digital Yuan, digital euro, RBA pilots), Diem, DeFi, and NFTs as the completion of the financialization of data ([[sources/post-classical-finance-cfo-series-sydney-11may21]], [[sources/uptown-radio-recording-1-2021-04-26-t12-31-18am-2]]). The one voice of skepticism in the corpus is guest Jason Calacanis, who distrusts most ICOs while allowing that compliant tools may eventually integrate with venture capital ([[sources/s01e01-mark-wav]]).

## Debates / Open Questions


- **Ledger or coin?** The corpus's own argument is that the two were repeatedly conflated (Calacanis's ICO skepticism, the "Bitcoin 1.0" adoption hurdle vs enterprise iterations) — but the speakers never resolve whether the conflation was market failure or a feature.
- **The Mark Pesce Token** is the only self-referential blockchain experiment in the corpus — an entity that exists to test whether a professional can price their own time as a currency. No source records the outcome.

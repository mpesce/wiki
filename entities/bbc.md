# BBC

## TL;DR

The British broadcaster that, in the corpus, is the traditional media the human network outpaces, and the source of two referenced properties (Doctor Who, Antiques Roadshow).

## Overview

BBC (the British Broadcasting Corporation) appears in the corpus in three roles. First, **the traditional news outlet that the human network outpaces**: in the Next Wave talk (2007), Twitter users in China "provided immediate reports of the Sichuan earthquake, identifying the epicenter and damage within minutes, long before traditional news outlets like the BBC or New York Times could dispatch reporters" — the distributed network (Shanghai user Dedrick Lam as central clearinghouse) outpaces the broadcast institution ([[sources/nextwave-mp4]]). Second, **the broadcaster of Doctor Who**: in the "Human Use of Human Networks" lecture, the **Doctor Who Effect** — "a leaked episode of the revived BBC series was widely distributed via BitTorrent, potentially to generate interest after a US channel declined to purchase it" — is the case that distribution control has moved from broadcasters to users ([[sources/the-human-use-of-human-networks-1-mp3]]). Third, **the broadcaster of Antiques Roadshow**: a 2015 source is a segment of the BBC program Antiques Roadshow concluding an event at Cheltenham Town Hall (two appraisals — a Winchcombe pottery earthenware pot, and a tiara from the Governor-General of New Zealand) ([[sources/000000001ab02d7f-mpg]]). The corpus's BBC is the British public broadcaster referenced in the Australian futurist circuit — the institution of the old media economy that Pesce's arguments (hyperdistribution, the swarm, the human network) are measured against.

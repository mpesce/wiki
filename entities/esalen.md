# Esalen Institute

## TL;DR

The Big Sur institute where, in 2001, Pesce, Rushkoff and Davis ran the participatory "Trilogues for a New Millennium" weekend — the corpus's single richest set of unedited conversations.

## Overview

In the corpus, Esalen (the Esalen Institute) is a venue for one major event: the 2001 "Trilogues for a New Millennium" weekend, a multi-day participatory dialogue among Mark Pesce, Douglas Rushkoff and Erik Davis, recorded as eleven sessions ("Techno-Pagans at the End of History" episodes 1–11, plus the Friday and Sunday session recordings). The event was conceived in the spirit of Terence McKenna's conversations with Rupert Sheldrake and Ralph Abraham; after McKenna's 2000 death, his memorial at Esalen that year inspired Nancy Lunney, who runs Esalen's programs, to ask the three of them to carry the work forward ([[sources/friday-rm]], [[sources/friday-mp3]]). The format was deliberately not lecture: long-form three-way conversation alternating with open audience discussion, an evening ritual invoking the "Holy Guardian Angel" (the higher self), a guided chakra/sound exercise, and Pesce's assertion that "reality is plastic" — the mic is passed around the room to collect hopes, fears and dreams for the 21st century ([[sources/friday-rm]], [[sources/techno-pagans-at-the-end-of-history-9-esalen-mp3]]).

Thematically the weekend spans virtual reality (Pesce's recollections of *Ephemer*, TVision, his 1992 basement HMD lab, the "rising sense of ontological uncertainty"), psychedelics (LSD at nineteen, ayahuasca, DMT sources), nanotechnology (the 1993 UNC nano-manipulator, the gray-goo problem as "the thing that keeps me worried at night"), McKenna's time-wave theory (the 2011/2012 convergence), overpopulation, and the economics of the web ([[sources/techno-pagans-at-the-end-of-history-6-esalen-mp3]], [[sources/techno-pagans-at-the-end-of-history-7-esalen-mp3]], [[sources/techno-pagans-at-the-end-of-history-3-esalen-mp3]], [[sources/techno-pagans-at-the-end-of-history-8-esalen-mp3]], [[sources/techno-pagans-at-the-end-of-history-11-esalen-mp]]). The Sunday session is the most philosophically settled: a collective-resonance experience during the night ritual leads Pesce to conclude "God exists but is only conscious through group interaction", with a Jewish-informed emphasis on community, text-based inquiry and deconstruction over individual faith ([[sources/sunday-mp3]]). The corpus knows nothing of Esalen beyond this weekend — its history, its physical plant, its staff beyond Lunney — so the entity is defined by what happened there, not the other way around.

## In the Corpus


- The 14 sources are all the single 2001 Trilogues weekend; there is no other Esalen content in the corpus ([[sources/friday-rm]], [[sources/sunday-mp3]]).
- The weekend's recorded output (the eleven numbered "Techno-Pagans" sessions plus Friday/Sunday) is the source of some of the corpus's most quoted Pesce formulations — the gray-goo argument, the ontological-uncertainty account, the group-consciousness-of-God conclusion ([[sources/techno-pagans-at-the-end-of-history-6-esalen-mp3]], [[sources/techno-pagans-at-the-end-of-history-3-esalen-mp3]], [[sources/sunday-mp3]]).

## Debates / Open Questions


- **Series title vs event name.** The event is "Trilogues for a New Millennium"; the recordings are titled "Techno-Pagans at the End of History". Which title the participants used for the published version is not stated in the corpus.
- **ASR reliability on co-hosts.** One digest renders the third participant as "Eric Reiss, journalist and author of Technosis" — almost certainly the same person as Erik Davis (whose 1999 book *TechGnosis* fits). The corpus treats all three hosts as the same trio.
- **Where does "Techno-Pagans" come from?** Erik Davis's "techno-pagan" framing is well-known, but the corpus does not state who named the series.

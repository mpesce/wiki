# Terence McKenna

## TL;DR

The dead author whose time-wave eschaton Mark Pesce adopted as a "foundational fact" in 2001, then explicitly walked back in 2012 — the corpus's most clearly argued-about thinker.

## Overview

Terence McKenna (ASR variants: Terence McKenna, Terrence McKenna) appears in the corpus as a presence, not a speaker — a thinker Pesce and the corpus argue with, over, and past. The through-line: **McKenna is the origin of the corpus's eschaton framing, and the corpus records Pesce's gradual move from adoption to qualified rejection.**

The adoption (2001, Esalen): the Trilogues weekend was conceived in the spirit of McKenna's conversations with Rupert Sheldrake and Ralph Abraham, launched by the 2000 Esalen memorial; in the weekend's second session Pesce introduces McKenna's **time wave** theory — history charted into a 67-year section, a 13-month section in 2011, and a six-day section before the 2012 winter solstice, during which "there will be more novelty than has existed on the planet since ever" — and states that working up from Alvin Toffler to Teilhard de Chardin to McKenna, the premise that "the universe is seeking a type of closure" became "a foundational fact" all his subsequent work is based on ([[sources/friday-rm]], [[sources/techno-pagans-at-the-end-of-history-2-esalen-mp3]]).

The synthesis (2013, Alchemical Arts): McKenna's eschaton is merged with quantum evolution (John McFadden), Wolfram's iterative complexity, and Dawkins's meme — three nested waves of emergence (life ~4 billion years, linguistic consciousness ~150,000, techno-civilization ~5,500) converging on a point of maximum novelty; the psychedelic community is positioned as the authentic middle path between fundamentalist retreat and blind techno-optimism; and Pesce references **McKenna's final inscription on a copy of *The Invisible Landscape*** as a guide to transmuting consciousness ([[sources/biosandlogos-mp3]]).

The rejection (2012, Speaker's Corner): Pesce **distinguishes his views from McKenna's** — he does not endorse a sudden catastrophic end; he aligns the eschaton with the **technological singularity** (a term he credits to Vernor Vinge) but rejects the discontinuous leap in intelligence (the "separate, alien entity" model, which he attributes to a misreading of Kurzweil), arguing instead for **hyperintelligence** grown through subsumption and emergence ([[sources/3-05-the-future-of-all-mp3]]). McKenna also survives as a definitional resource: the shaman as one who "stepped outside ordinary space-time to gain knowledge and return to the community" ([[sources/bt-3-mp4]]), a figure cited alongside Daniel Pinchbeck as an example of the messianic turn some take after psychedelic experience ([[sources/3-03-the-future-of-entheogens-mp3]]), and — in Shira Chess's 2026 book launch — one of the 1960s–70s counterculture figures whose influence on early computing, technopaganism and chaos magic is the book's subject ([[sources/the-unseen-internet-the-alembic-9jan26-mp4]]).

## Debates / Open Questions


- **Adopted then rejected — which Pesce is authoritative?** The corpus records both the 2001 "foundational fact" and the 2012 explicit distinction. The later talk is the more self-conscious statement, but the corpus does not say which framing Pesce settled on.
- **The time wave's dates.** 2011/2012 winter solstice is the corpus's date; the actual McKenna wave is commonly tied to 2012. The corpus does not reconcile the two.
- **Invisible Landscape inscription.** The corpus references the final inscription but does not quote it; the entity page therefore holds the reference, not the content.

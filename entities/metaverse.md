# the Metaverse

## TL;DR

The concept Mark Pesce keeps redefining — "not a single place but a set of qualities" — and claims a co-inventor's title in, anchored to Tony Parisi's "only one metaverse".

## Overview

The metaverse in the corpus is a concept in constant negotiation, and Pesce is its consistent (and only) re-definer. The stable definitions: the **set of qualities** — "interactivity, sensory richness, and planetary-scale connectivity" (LEAP, October 2022), "not a single place" — and **a set of human agreements rather than a purely technological achievement** (A House Built on Sand), against which Decentraland, The Sandbox, Cryptovoxels and Meta's Horizon Worlds are found to be fragmented walled gardens: "according to Tony Parisi's definition, there should be only one metaverse" ([[sources/leap-interview-13oct22-mp4]], [[sources/a-house-built-on-sand-interoperability-in-the-me]]).

The intellectual lineage is fixed in the 2021 Swarm interview: **Vinge's True Names (1981), William Gibson's cyberspace, Neal Stephenson's Snow Crash (1993)** — works that "reflected the technological realities of their times"; Snow Crash is the term's origin, and Pesce warns that "artistic depictions can sometimes hinder technological progress by fixing public perception" ([[sources/swarm-2021-mp4]], [[sources/uptown-radio-recording-1-2021-04-26-t12-31-18am]]). Pesce distinguishes the broad possibility-set from **Facebook's "economically driven instrumentality"** — headsets that are "uncomfortable and biologically limiting" for women and older adults — and argues virtual spaces should be tools for planetary management and community building, not paid-labor environments ([[sources/swarm-2021-mp4]]). The **waves** run VRML worlds (mid-1990s) → Second Life (early 2000s) → Decentraland (third wave), with Decentraland's MANA token a "Tinkerbell operation" (value by collective belief, "Landrush" as casino dynamics) ([[sources/uptown-radio-recording-1-2021-04-26-t12-31-18am]]).

Two personal claims: the **A Brief History of the Metaverse** — a six-episode podcast series on The Next Billion Seconds (announced in the "session 1" interview, released 15 September) ([[sources/mark-pesce-session-1-wav]]); and the **co-inventor title** — at the "2035" event the host introduces Pesce as "one of the inventors of the metaverse" ([[sources/introduction-and-how-it-happened-mp4]]). The practical reading, from the simulation talk: the metaverse has run hype cycles for decades but resurges with the **Apple Vision Pro** and Meta; the core value is **simulation** (the spreadsheet parallel), and current fully immersive systems "cause motion sickness in the majority of women and men over the age of thirty" ([[sources/vr-simulation-future-of-work-mp4]]). The interoperability talk ("A House Built on Sand") is the most negative assessment: three decades on, the concept "remains largely empty and underdeveloped", virtual real-estate deeds are superficial, and the missing piece is the human agreement establishing a common origin ([[sources/a-house-built-on-sand-interoperability-in-the-me]]).

## Debates / Open Questions


- **Who invented the metaverse?** The 2035 host calls Pesce "one of the inventors"; the corpus's own etymology points to Snow Crash. The two are not reconciled in any source.

- **One metaverse or many?** Parisi's definition (one) vs the actual landscape (walled gardens). The corpus holds both; the interoperability talk is the only attempt at a resolution (human agreement) and finds it missing.
- **AR vs VR as the instrumentality.** The corpus consistently prefers AR (blending virtual and real) over full immersion, citing the motion-sickness problem — but the 2024/2025 Apple/Facebook predictions are left unverified in the corpus.

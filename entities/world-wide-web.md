# World Wide Web

## TL;DR

The longest arc in the corpus: the technology Pesce gave its first 3D interface in 1994, the credential he spends for three decades, the value system he preaches (open, shared, distributed), and the template he holds up for everything that follows it.

## Overview

In the corpus the Web's story begins in the corpus's own earliest dated sources. Pesce recounts meeting Tony Parisi in late 1993 and building the first 3D interface to the Web — the VRML browser, demonstrated as the cyber banana — and presenting it at the first international World Wide Web conference at CERN in May 1994, where he met Tim Berners-Lee ([[sources/mark-pesce-session-1-wav]], [[sources/reality-remix-wav]], [[sources/v20-june-2019-opening-mts]]). The 1996 avatars keynote expands the arc: the Web's move into social 3D cyberspace, the VRML Architecture Group's standards work against SGI and Microsoft, and the Foucault/Bentham reading of cyberspace as "panoptic self" ([[sources/avatars96-keynotes-pesce-parisi-512kb-mp4]]). The undated VRML interview states the medium's own terms — "a three-dimensional equivalent of HTML", ~2,000 community contributors, early adoption by chemists sharing molecular structures ([[sources/vrml-interview-mp4]]).

For three decades the Web is then Pesce's working credential, recited with slight variations from 2005's "brought virtual reality to the World Wide Web" ([[sources/artbell3-mp3]]) through 2010's Guestroom interviews ([[sources/guestroom-futurist-mark-pesce-mp3]]), 2018's ACMA address ("I'm one of the folks who helped bring you the World Wide Web", after X.25 firmware and the VRML ISO standards process) ([[sources/broadcast-and-autonomy-acma-30oct18-mp3]]), to 2026's SCA keynote, where "the current moment resembles the early growth of the World Wide Web in 1994" ([[sources/sca-wav]]). Alongside the credential sits a value system: the 2011 LCA keynote argues that open-sourcing VRML taught him "shared resources increase in value", warns that Facebook has captured social graphs in closed systems, recounts the 2010 WikiLeaks deplatforming, and offers a "design guide for anarchists" (distribute everything, transport independence, strong encryption, open source) plus his Plexus protocol for portable social graphs ([[sources/lca2011-keynote-mov]]). The 2010 IIF talk is the Web-as-medium argument: hypertext's centrifugal force, the TL;DR reading era, and the book's transition into an interconnected mass of text ([[sources/iif-2010-03-mark-pesce-1-mp4]]).

The Web also functions in the corpus as a template for every new technology. The 2016 BlockHack keynote opens with the parallel — blockchain's "1994 moment", a protocol for exchanging value the way IP enabled data ([[sources/blockhack16-talk-wav]], [[sources/zoom0013-tr2-wav]]); the 2019 V20 crypto-regulation summit opening casts the industry through the Web's "explorers, scouts, pioneers, and settlers" metaphor, with the first Web conference recalled from Geneva "25 years prior" and Gavin Bell's path from VRML standardization to chief scientist of the Bitcoin Foundation ([[sources/v20-june-2019-opening-mts]]). The 2017 OzWater talk makes the same move for open ROV: open source "similar to those powering the World Wide Web and Wikipedia" ([[sources/ozwater-wav]]).

Finally, the Web's *next form* is a standing argument: VRML → WebGL, and the Mixed Reality Service (MRS) — a W3C initiative Pesce co-develops that binds spatial coordinates to URIs so devices can query their physical environment for permissions, with Pokémon Go cited as the catalyst that finally gave the protocol a purpose ([[sources/web3d2017-mp3]], [[sources/web3d2017-m4a]], [[sources/internet-transformations-5oct21-mp4]]). In the "super tools" frame the Web is the second of three existing civilization-shapers (spreadsheet, Web, smartphone) whose reflections — AR, data populated into real space, and intelligence management — will be the next thirty years' tools ([[sources/supertools-mkv]], [[sources/supertools-anu-mp4]]). And in the 2023 Cybersamhain talk the Web receives the corpus's most unusual treatment: a cybernetic "structural coupling" of all humans, for which Pesce performed a blessing ritual on the new digital environment ([[sources/cybersamhain-to-chatgpt-1080p-mp4]]).

## Debates / Open Questions


- **Who co-created what, with whom.** The ASR renders Pesce's co-author variously as "Tony Parisi", "Tony Bogdanoff", and "Tony DeRose". The ingest keys the person as [[entities/tony-parisi]]; the "Bogdanoff" rendering is almost certainly recognition error, but the corpus contains no source that disambiguates.
- **The claim's boundary.** Pesce consistently claims the *first 3D interface* to the Web (VRML), never authorship of the Web itself — the "helped bring you the Web" phrasing is always scoped to X.25 firmware, VRML standards, and the 1994 conference. The entity page should not be read as a claim about the Web's invention.
- **Open source as moral argument.** The LCA2011 "anarchist design guide" and the Plexus project are the corpus's most normative statements about the Web; they sit uneasily with the closed-platform criticism of Apple and Facebook, which appears in the same talks.

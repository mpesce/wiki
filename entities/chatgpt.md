# ChatGPT

## TL;DR

The ChatGPT sources track the arc from novelty to restructuring force: a chatbot that passes medical exams in early 2023 becomes, by 2025, the watershed that repriced cognitive labour — with Pesce's consistent position that AI takes tasks rather than jobs, and that people and organisations who use it well will outperform those who don't.

## Overview

The earliest source is mainstream-curiosity coverage: a light The Project NZ segment defining ChatGPT as an advanced chatbot that drafts emails, disputes fines and passes medical-school entrance exams, with Pesce and the co-hosts concluding it will not directly take jobs but that people who use it will outperform those who don't — and that it still struggles with true creativity, common sense and humour [[sources/how-smart-is-chat-gpt-the-project-nz-original-mp]].

By The Futurists episodes, the framing has hardened into economics. Pesce dates the "watershed" to ChatGPT's late-2022 release, but locates the real shift when AI agents began operating autonomously for hours without error — moving humans from "in the loop" to "on the loop", supervising rather than executing, which fundamentally reprices cognitive labour [[sources/thefuturists-thewatershed-mkv]]. A parallel Futurists conversation adds his consultancy, Wisely AI, and the counter-narrative: AI tools are good at summarising and classifying but fail at complex process automation; "vibe coding" produces unreliable code needing expert oversight; and chatbots in education risk hindering critical thinking like "training wheels that prevent learning" [[sources/thefuturists-mp4]], [[sources/thefuturists-mp4-2]].

The 2025 "Facing the Machine" lecture to tertiary educators marks the most developed stage: 30 months after ChatGPT's release, weak signals — Intel outsourcing its marketing department to Accenture and AI tools, the bursting computer-science entry-level job bubble, graduate unemployment — argue current education models are "no longer fit for purpose"; he introduces a study showing reduced brain engagement when using ChatGPT and defines "resistance" as the degree to which a task, role or career can withstand automation [[sources/facing-the-machine-ibsuniversity-27jun25-mp4]]. The AI-and-work panel supplies the through-line: two years of ChatGPT showed AI is not a magic wand that eliminates jobs; it takes over rote tasks but requires a human in the loop, making the employee's role akin to a gardener tending automated systems — work that is more interesting, contingent on continuous learning [[sources/ai-and-the-future-of-work-mp4]].

Across all six sources the consistent claims are: ChatGPT-class AI takes tasks, not jobs; human-in/on-the-loop supervision is the emerging work pattern; and the disruptive burden falls on education and entry-level knowledge work. What changes over time is scale — from a party-trick chatbot to an agent layer that reprices cognitive labour.

## In the Corpus


The corpus holds ChatGPT as a four-stage arc from novelty to restructuring force:

- **Early 2023 — mainstream curiosity.** The Project NZ segment defines ChatGPT (drafts emails, disputes fines, passes medical-school entrance exams) and concludes it will not directly take jobs, but users who use it will outperform those who don't; it still struggles with true creativity, common sense, and humour ([[sources/how-smart-is-chat-gpt-the-project-nz-original-mp]]).
- **2022–2023 — the watershed and the counter-narrative.** The Futurists episodes: the "watershed" is ChatGPT's late-2022 release, but the real shift is autonomous agents moving humans "on the loop" (repricing cognitive labour); Wisely AI as the consultancy; "vibe coding" producing unreliable code; chatbots in education as "training wheels that prevent learning"; and "ChatGPT psychosis" ([[sources/thefuturists-thewatershed-mkv]], [[sources/thefuturists-mp4]]).
- **2025 — the education reckoning.** "Facing the Machine" (IBS University, June 2025): 30 months in, the weak signals (Intel outsourcing marketing to Accenture and AI, the CS entry-level job bubble, graduate unemployment) argue current education is "no longer fit for purpose"; the reduced-brain-engagement study; the "resistance" concept; the oral-exam recommendation ([[sources/facing-the-machine-ibsuniversity-27jun25-mp4]]).
- **The work through-line.** The AI-and-work panel: two years of ChatGPT show AI is not a magic wand; it takes rote tasks but requires a human in the loop, making the employee a gardener tending automated systems, contingent on continuous learning ([[sources/ai-and-the-future-of-work-mp4]]).

## Debates / Open Questions


- **The watershed date.** The corpus dates the "watershed" to ChatGPT's late-2022 release, but locates the *economic* shift in the autonomous agent capability (hours without error). The page holds both the release date and the agent-capability shift as the corpus's two-stage reading.
- **Tasks, not jobs.** The consistent claim across all six sources: ChatGPT-class AI takes tasks, not jobs, and requires a human in/on the loop. The page holds this as the corpus's standing position, noting the "AI doomer" counter as the narrative the corpus argues against.
- **Vibe coding.** The corpus warns that AI-generated code ("vibe coding") is often unreliable or insecure, needing expert oversight and potentially reducing productivity. The page holds the warning as the corpus's stance.
- **ChatGPT psychosis.** The corpus describes individuals entering feedback loops with sycophantic AI ("ChatGPT psychosis") and the erosion of consensus reality from fragmented media and plausible falsehoods. The page holds the phenomenon as the corpus's named term.
- **Resistance.** The 2025 lecture introduces "resistance" (the degree a task/role/career withstands automation) as the new organizing concept, with high-resistance sectors (trades, medicine, care) vs low-resistance sectors (marketing, administration, parts of law). The page holds the concept as the corpus's latest frame.
- **The weak signals.** The corpus cites Intel's Accenture/AI marketing outsourcing, the CS entry-level job bubble, and graduate unemployment as the empirical anchors for the education argument. The page holds these as the corpus's evidence and notes they are Pesce's cited weak signals, not independently verified in the corpus.

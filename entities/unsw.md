# UNSW

## TL;DR

The University of New South Wales — venue of the 2006 "Upon What Meat" talk, co-cited exemplar of open courseware, and employer of the BlockHack judge Nick Wales.

## Overview

UNSW (the University of New South Wales) holds three roles in the corpus. First, **the venue**: the 15 March 2006 talk "Upon What Meat" was delivered at UNSW, where Pesce's central idea is **externalization** — the calculator externalized our math ability ("we had to use a lot of log tables" before it), and the mobile phone is about to externalize our social brain ("it's already doing that, but poorly"); the Japanese **co-presence** phenomenon (15- and 16-year-olds in continuous SMS conversation with four or five friends from waking to sleeping) is the evidence, and a little Bluetooth program routing a phone's data traffic through a nearby internet-connected PC (circumventing the carrier) is the proof of concept — the phone as "a device for us to explore the connections that make us part of a community" ([[sources/uponwhatmeat-unsw-15mar06-mp3]]). Second, **the courseware exemplar**: in the eLearning 2008 keynote, UNSW (with Stanford and MIT) is the proof case — "If Stanford, MIT, and UNSW can share their full courseware online without diluting the education, no institution can refute that example" ([[sources/elearning08-mark-pesce-20081104-mp3]]). Third, **the affiliation**: in the BlockHack 16 keynote (Westpac, 2016), one of the hackathon judges is **Nick Wales from UNSW** ([[sources/zoom0013-tr2-wav]]). The corpus gives no other UNSW detail — no faculty, no specific building, no named staff beyond Wales.

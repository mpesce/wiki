# Ivan Sutherland

## TL;DR

The 1968 "Sword of Damocles" head-mounted display's creator, who in the corpus is the origin node of every VR/AR history talk Mark Pesce gives.

## Overview

Ivan Sutherland appears in the corpus exclusively as a figure inside Mark Pesce's origin narratives — never interviewed, never in his own words — but the account is remarkably stable across twenty-five years of talks (1995–2020). The consistent genealogy: **1963, Sketchpad**, the first interactive drawing program (also called "the first interactive computer program"), for which Pesce credits DARPA funding; **1965, the "Ultimate Display" paper**, which the AR Dilemmas talk identifies as the conceptual origin of augmented reality — real-time 3D graphics were, Pesce argues, "invented specifically to support this vision"; and **1968, the head-mounted display "Sword of Damocles"**, with positional tracking and stereoscopic vision, which Pesce calls the **first augmented reality system** because it blended computer-generated imagery with the user's real-world vision ([[sources/uq-wav]], [[sources/pesce-siggraph-frontiers-the-ar-dilemmas-mp4]], [[sources/mark-pesce-digital-depth-in-augmented-reality-mp]], [[sources/arin6904-5sep18-mp3]]).

The 1968 **Fall Joint Computer Conference** is the recurring scene: Sutherland presenting the headset while Douglas Engelbart gives the "Mother of All Demos" (mouse, hypertext) — the two technologies, Pesce says, were "born simultaneously and have continued to influence each other" ([[sources/reality-remix-wav]], [[sources/web3d2017-m4a]], [[sources/uq-wav]]). At the 2001 UQ lecture Sutherland is one of three "often-forgotten pioneers" (with Jason Reichert's *Cybernetic Serendipity* and Engelbart); in the 2018 ARIN lecture he is upgraded to "the most important figure in the field" ([[sources/uq-wav]], [[sources/arin6904-5sep18-mp3]]). The institutional frame is constant: IPTO/Licklider/DARPA as the money, the 1950s jet-fighter cockpit as the problem, and Sutherland as the solution's author. His name is the load-bearing wall of Pesce's larger argument — that AR is not a device but a thirty-year lineage of human-computer interaction design, and that the 1968 FJCC is the true origin moment of both the web and AR ([[sources/voices-of-vr-mark-pesce-20nov20-mp3]], [[sources/a-digital-reformation-wav]]). The corpus contains no Sutherland biography, no quotes from him, and no source other than Pesce's telling.

## Debates / Open Questions


- **First AR or first VR?** Pesce calls the 1968 headset the *first augmented reality system* (blended vision) while other corpus sources treat it as the first *VR* headset (Sword of Damocles, head tracking, stereoscopy). The corpus never reconciles the two readings — they coexist in adjacent talks.
- **Who is the "most important figure"?** The 2018 ARIN talk names Sutherland the field's most important figure; the 2001 UQ talk distributes the founding across Reichert, Sutherland and Engelbart. Both are defensible from the corpus; neither is.

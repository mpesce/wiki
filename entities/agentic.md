# agentic

## TL;DR

The 2026 corpus's core AI concept — the "watershed" at which a human moves from IN the loop to ON the loop — with verification design as its governing discipline.

## Overview

"Agentic" (agentic AI / agentic systems) enters the corpus through a set of 2026 clips (the F/A clip series), and it is the corpus's most fully argued-out AI concept. The central argument is the **watershed**: using AI agentic-ally was expensive because "a human had to be in the loop all the time"; once you cross the watershed, "the human is ON the loop, just checking in occasionally, making sure things stay on track", and that "changes the calculus of how much cognitive labor costs" — the watershed being "the point at which an agent completes a task before it falls over, giving high confidence it will finish". The stakes: "before it, for all of history, cognitive labor had one source — us" ([[sources/f2-cold-open-mp4]], [[sources/f2b-watershed-in-context-mp4]]).

The governing discipline is **verification design** — "the name for this whole new way of thinking about agentic systems holistically": given a business and its processes, how do you "design the agentic processes so they fail and learn and are constantly being audited so they cannot get off track" — with the caveat that "you can't just trust the supervisor agent either" ([[sources/f10-verification-design-mp4]]). The standing danger is the **greenfield competitor**: "there will be GREENFIELD competitors to every one of these businesses that form directly in post-watershed form — completely agentic, very different internally but producing the same outputs", and "a business that doesn't adapt gets overrun by them" ([[sources/a8b-optional-greenfields-danger-mp4]]). The clips name no speaker on camera, but the verification-design term matches the corpus's established Pesce concept (the wiki's [[concepts/verification-design]] page), and the agentic-systems concept page is the corpus's other home for the same argument.

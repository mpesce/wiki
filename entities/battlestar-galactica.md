# Battlestar Galactica

## TL;DR

The 2004 *Battlestar Galactica* revival, which in the corpus is a case study for two arguments: piracy-as-marketing and the end of centralized mass media.

## Overview

Battlestar Galactica (the 2004 revival) appears in the corpus exclusively as the lead example in two 2004–2005 talks: the AFTRS lecture **"Piracy is Good"** (18 October 2004) and the **"Welcome to the Swarm"** talk (delivered in San Francisco and elsewhere, early 2004–2005). In "Piracy is Good" the show is the core case: the UK broadcast on Sky One preceded the US airing on Sci-Fi Channel; the premiere was downloaded via BitTorrent in the US before the official broadcast; and the piracy **did not hurt ratings** — instead it generated word-of-mouth buzz among core fans and led to record US viewership. Pesce demonstrates the point with a modified BSG clip carrying a retailer's logo, illustrating his "hyperdistribution" model (producers partnering directly with advertisers, ads embedded as an on-screen bug rather than commercial breaks) ([[sources/piracyisgood-mp3]], [[sources/piracy-is-good-mp4]], [[sources/piracyisgood-smal-avi]], [[sources/piracyisgood-avi]], [[sources/piracy-is-good-2-mp4]]). The Doctor Who pilot leak is the parallel example — unauthorized distribution that boosted UK ratings ([[sources/piracyisgood-mp3]], [[sources/piracyisgood-smal-avi]]).

In "Welcome to the Swarm" the same show plays a different role: it is the opening proof that the **balkanization of epistemology** has arrived — the 2004 release was shared globally via BitTorrent before its official US broadcast, and BitTorrent's swarm mechanics (popularity increases distribution efficiency rather than crashing a server) mark "the end of centralized media control" ([[sources/hyperpeople-mp3]], [[sources/hyperpeople-mp4]], [[sources/hyperpeople-mobile-mp4]], [[sources/hyperpeople-ipod-mp4]]). The show's presence is otherwise absent — no review, no fandom, no episode detail — it is a distribution event, not a text. The corpus's BSG is the 2004 revival's first weeks: UK first, US second, torrent everywhere, ratings up.

## Debates / Open Questions


- **Piracy's direction of effect.** The corpus asserts the causal claim (piracy boosted ratings) from a single observation window; the counterfactual (what US viewership would have been without the torrent) is never addressed in any source.
- **BSG as text vs event.** No source treats the show's content, characters or writing — it is only its distribution. The entity page therefore reflects a distribution-only view, which is all the corpus offers.

# Tony Parisi

## TL;DR

Mark Pesce's co-inventor of VRML — the other half of the 1994 cyber-banana — and, per the corpus, the author of the "one metaverse" definition.

## Overview

Tony Parisi (ASR variants: Tony Parisi, "Tony Bogdanoff", "Tony DeRose") is Mark Pesce's co-inventor of VRML, and in the corpus he exists almost entirely inside Pesce's 1994 story. The stable account: in early 1994 the two built the first VRML browser, demonstrating a 3D model Pesce calls the **cyber-banana** ("the first VRML-based image"); the work was presented at the **first international conference on the World Wide Web** in Geneva, where Pesce met Tim Berners-Lee; VRML became a standard with SGI support, and in the 2019 V20 retrospective the collaboration is widened to include **Gavin Bell**, who later became chief scientist of the Bitcoin Foundation and worked on post-quantum cryptography ([[sources/reality-remix-wav]], [[sources/vr-creative-cluster-mark-pesce-9-aug-video1-mp4]], [[sources/v20-june-2019-opening-mts]]). One 2021 talk names the early interface **Labyrinth**, which "evolved into modern WebGL standards" ([[sources/arin-2610-internet-transformations-the-ar-dilemm-2]]).

The corpus's two most concrete Parisi artifacts: **Daniel's Story**, an early VRML world Pesce made with Parisi that was thought lost for decades and recently rediscovered on a server — the anecdote's point being the fragility of digital archives ([[sources/swarm-2021-mp4]]); and the **metaverse definition** — in the 2021 interoperability talk, "according to Tony Parisi's definition, there should be only one metaverse", the standard against which Decentraland, The Sandbox, Cryptovoxels and Horizon Worlds are found to be fragmented walled gardens ([[sources/a-house-built-on-sand-interoperability-in-the-me]]). The one non-Pesce voice is the 2023 Owen Rowley interview, where a former Autodesk systems administrator recalls working on early VR demos and VRML development, "collaborating with Mark Pesce and Tony Parisi" — the only third-party confirmation of the partnership in the corpus ([[sources/owen-interview-2023-mp4]]). Beyond the 1994–95 work, the corpus gives Parisi no identity, no institution, no quotes of his own.

## Debates / Open Questions


- **ASR identity drift.** The Web3D 2017 talk renders the co-inventor as "Tony Bogdanoff" and "Tony DeRose"; all other sources say Tony Parisi. The corpus never disambiguates — the name drift is a transcription artifact, but it means the entity's identity rests on the consistent majority of sources.
- **What did Parisi build vs Pesce?** Pesce claims "I and Tony Parisi developed the first VRML browser"; the Rowley interview confirms a collaboration but not a division of labor. The corpus has no Parisi-side account.

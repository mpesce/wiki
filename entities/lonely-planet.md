# Lonely Planet

## TL;DR

The travel publisher that, in the corpus, is the client Pesce advises in the "Where in the World" talk — on Google Earth, the swarm, and why the brand is a community, not a website.

## Overview

Lonely Planet appears in the corpus as the audience of a single talk: **"Where in the World"**, a presentation and Q&A by Mark Pesce "addressed to staff at Lonely Planet", "speaking from his position at the National Film School" ([[sources/whereintheworld-mp3]]). The talk's argument: digital distribution is reshaping travel publishing. Pesce outlines his background in VR and 3D earth modeling (the 1994 VRML protocol, early web-based earth models), and contrasts these with **Google Earth**, which "has established the world as a database and positioned Google as a potential threat to traditional publishers by aggregating geographically contextual information". He emphasizes the power of "swarms" and user-generated content (Wikipedia's rise against the Encyclopedia Britannica, Craigslist's disruption of classified advertising), and argues that traditional editorial models are being replaced by community-driven curation and social credit systems (similar to eBay). His advice: embrace **syndication and open content sharing** — the brand value lies in "curating a trusted community of expert authors and passionate travelers rather than hoarding content"; leverage social networks and mobile devices (the mobile phone is the primary device for the target demographic); warn against a "walled garden" approach. The Q&A covers the balance between expert authorship and user-generated content, the economic viability of free content, and the strategic threat of Google; the close: "define Lonely Planet not just as a website, but as a community and organizing principle for travel information", with "rapid, iterative development to keep pace with technological changes" ([[sources/whereintheworld-mp3]]). The corpus's Lonely Planet is the advised client; the page holds the talk as the corpus's Lonely Planet content.

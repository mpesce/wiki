# VR

## TL;DR

Pesce's lifelong field, told from the inside: consumer VR's rise (Sega, VRML), its "empty quarter", the 2014 smartphone-led renaissance — and his argument that VR's real future is simulation, videogrammetry, and augmented reality.

## Overview

The corpus contains a stable, self-consistent account of VR history that Pesce tells repeatedly with small variations: the 1968 twin breakthroughs of Sutherland's head-mounted display and Engelbart's mouse-and-hypertext demo; the 1950s fighter-cockpit origin of the problem (synthesizing information for pilots), funded by DARPA; NASA Ames's late-1980s virtual environment workstation (data gloves and a million-dollar supercomputer); Nintendo's Power Glove failure; his own Ono Sendai (founded after reading Jaron Lanier's claim that VR would be "the telephone of the future"), whose $1 automotive orientation sensor was licensed to Sega for the Virtua VR — a hit at the 1993 Consumer Electronics Show, cancelled July 1993 when user testing revealed motion sickness and Stanford Research Institute advised that the headset was unsafe for children ([[sources/reality-remix-wav]], [[sources/vr-creative-cluster-mark-pesce-9-aug-video1-mp4]], [[sources/web3d2017-m4a]], [[sources/theroadahead-17may18-mp3]], [[sources/arin6904-5sep18-mp3]]). The 2019 documentary interview adds the corpus's only physical relic — the Sega Genesis cartridge with the project's test code — and the origin of the company name ("Onosendai", from Gibson's *Neuromancer*) ([[sources/skype-interview-march-2019-mp4]]). From that failure comes VRML: the cyber banana, the first international Web conference in 1994, and ~2,000 community contributors building a standard "a three-dimensional equivalent of HTML" ([[sources/vrml-interview-mp4]]).

A second consistent thread is VR as a *safety* question. The 2010 7:30 Report on 3D TV's health risks puts Pesce on air as the historian who knows why Sega's headset was killed, citing the SRI advice that limited adult exposure to an hour a day ([[sources/7-30report-3dtv-3jun10-m4v-2]]); the 2015 ABC broadcast interview has him calling 3D TV "a gigantic unregulated medical experiment" ([[sources/0000000011abc207-iphone-m4v]]); and the 2018 lecture explains motion sickness as the conflict between visual perception and proprioception — "body-computer coupling" — which is why the PlayStation VR launch needed good content to avoid "alienating a new generation" ([[sources/arin6904-5sep18-mp3]], [[sources/vr-creative-cluster-mark-pesce-9-aug-video1-mp4]]).

The corpus's strangest VR material is its spiritual register. At the 2001 Esalen weekend with Rushkoff and Davis, Pesce walks through the VR artwork *Ephemer* — "a joyous art... inviting you into it not merely to observe but to participate in the being" — and predicts a twelve-year-old will build such work on a Nintendo 64 "and I will be able to stand inside of someone else's head" ([[sources/techno-pagans-at-the-end-of-history-9-esalen-mp3]], [[sources/techno-pagans-at-the-end-of-history-8-esalen-mp3]]). The 2010 RN *Encounter* program on cyberreligion records him as a gay Christian turned Wiccan whose "work in virtual reality became a mirror for this spiritual practice", with early VR pioneers cast as "often pagans who used the technology to create collaborative sacred spaces" ([[sources/rn-encounter-cyberreligion-22aug10-mp3]]).

The late corpus marks VR's second coming and its redirect. The 2016 VR Creative Cluster talks and the PwC Hong Kong talk ("thought VR was deader than a doornail, and then about 18 months ago it became very clear it was not, and I dived right back in") attribute the renaissance to smartphones collapsing display and sensor costs, WebGL, and graphics now "1,000 times faster than in 1996", with Google Cardboard as the pivot that democratized the field ([[sources/vr-creative-cluster-mark-pesce-9-aug-video1-mp4]], [[sources/pesce-pwc-hong-kong-july-2017-edited-mp3]]). The new vocabulary is *videogrammetry* — "photogrammetry plus a timeline", from the 1993 photogrammetry paper to 8Eye's 40-camera dome, the Joshua Bell PS VR experience, and Microsoft Research's polyportation ([[sources/ala-wav]], [[sources/theroadahead-17may18-mp3]]). And the destination is AR: the 2019 DECO lecture calls augmented reality "the central problem in design computing in the middle years of the 21st century" and insists that inscribing the world with data is "a SOCIAL ENGINEERING task" — "You would have wanted Mark Zuckerberg to think about that 15 years ago" ([[sources/the-world-and-its-double-deco-2200-mp3]]); the Mixed Reality Service (spatial coordinates bound to URIs) is the governance layer, with Pokémon Go as the forcing function ([[sources/web3d2017-m4a]], [[sources/internet-transformations-5oct21-mp4]]); and by 2020–2025 the futurist work has folded VR into simulation-as-business-skill ("VR is less about immersion and more about simulation") at the future-of-work panels, under his co-founder credit at Wisely AI ([[sources/common-factor-1-future-of-work-clean-mp4]], [[sources/introducing-mark-mp4]]).

## Debates / Open Questions


- **VR vs AR vs mixed reality.** The corpus keeps these as three entities (vr / ar / metaverse); Pesce's own late talks blur them ("mixed reality", "videogrammetry"). The split reflects the wiki's vocabulary more than a clean ontological boundary.
- **The self-count.** "27 years", "29 years", "30 years" in VR across 2017–2019 talks — counting from 1990/1991 (Ono Sendai) rather than 1994 (VRML). Consistent within the corpus.
- **Sega as entity.** Sega is central to the VR narrative but has no entity page (below the vocabulary threshold); it appears in 15+ source summaries. A candidate for a future vocabulary expansion.

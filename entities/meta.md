# Meta

## TL;DR

Meta appears in four sources as both the rebranded Facebook driving the second metaverse hype cycle and as Pesce's counter-example: a company whose walled-garden Horizon Worlds is the opposite of the interoperable metaverse he argues is the only kind worth building.

## Overview

The Meta references cluster in the recent corpus and are almost entirely metaverse-focused. In "A House Built on Sand" — delivered in VR — Pesce argues that after three decades the metaverse remains "largely empty": platforms including Decentraland, The Sandbox, Cryptovoxels, and Meta's Horizon Worlds each claim to be the metaverse, yet none is interoperable with any other, and Tony Parisi's definition implies there should be only one [[sources/a-house-built-on-sand-interoperability-in-the-me]]. He uses Meta's own platform as the exemplar of the problem: virtual real-estate deeds redeemable only inside walled gardens, with the film This Is Spinal Tap as his analogy for trivial unit mismatches causing outsized failures.

On the hardware side, he treats Meta as a driver of the current resurgent cycle — alongside the Apple Vision Pro — while noting fully immersive systems still cause motion sickness in a majority of adults over thirty, making them unwelcoming to broad demographics [[sources/vr-simulation-future-of-work-mp4]]. Earlier material adds texture: the 2020 Voices of VR interview credits Microsoft, not Facebook, with the HoloLens and Kinect as foundational to modern inside-out tracking, and reads Facebook's aggressive 2017 AR push as a counter-move against Snapchat [[sources/voices-of-vr-mark-pesce-20nov20-mp3]] — a reminder that Meta's AR/metaverse ambition predates the rebrand. Even the 2024-ish "barbell" market clip casts Meta AI at the low end of a commoditising AI market [[sources/f5-barbell-vacuum-cleaners-mp4]].

The synthesis is consistent: Pesce treats Meta as the metaverse's most visible champion and its most instructive failure mode — enormous investment, fragmenting rather than unifying the space, and (per his book-tour argument) building surveillance into the device layer through Project Aria's gaze tracking [[sources/voices-of-vr-mark-pesce-20nov20-mp3]]. What changed over time is the framing: pre-2021 sources discuss Facebook's AR strategy; post-rebrand sources discuss Meta's Horizon Worlds as one non-interoperable island among many, and the metaverse as "a set of human agreements rather than a purely technological achievement" — agreements Meta's platforms conspicuously lack.

## In the Corpus


The corpus holds Meta in three recent registers (the 1999 "libraries" source is a false positive on the word "meta-trends"):

- **The metaverse walled garden.** In "A House Built on Sand" (delivered in VR), Meta's Horizon Worlds is the exemplar of the problem: after three decades the metaverse remains "largely empty," and Decentraland, The Sandbox, Cryptovoxels, and Horizon Worlds each claim to be the metaverse yet none is interoperable with any other; virtual real-estate deeds are redeemable only inside walled gardens, and Tony Parisi's definition implies there should be only one ([[sources/a-house-built-on-sand-interoperability-in-the-me]]).
- **The hardware driver.** In the VR-simulation talk, Meta is a driver of the current resurgent cycle — alongside the Apple Vision Pro — while noting fully immersive systems still cause motion sickness in a majority of adults over thirty, making them unwelcoming to broad demographics ([[sources/vr-simulation-future-of-work-mp4]]).
- **The AI low end.** In the 2026 "barbell" clip, Meta AI sits at the low end of a commoditising AI market (the Walmart approach, with xAI's Grok), against the high end (Claude, GPT, Gemini) and the Chinese low end (DeepSeek, Qwen, Minimax) ([[sources/f5-barbell-vacuum-cleaners-mp4]]).

## Debates / Open Questions


- **Meta = the rebranded Facebook.** The corpus treats Meta as the 2023 rebrand of Facebook (the predecessor name, the Project Aria and Horizon Worlds lineage). The page holds the rebrand as the corpus's framing and does not fold pre-2021 Facebook sources into this page.
- **Horizon Worlds as the walled garden.** The corpus uses Meta's own platform as the exemplar of the metaverse's failure: enormous investment, fragmenting rather than unifying, and no interoperability. The page holds this as the corpus's standing critique.
- **The one-metaverse argument.** The corpus holds Tony Parisi's definition (there should be only one metaverse) against the fragmented walled-garden reality (Horizon Worlds, Decentraland, The Sandbox, Cryptovoxels). The page holds the one-metaverse argument as the corpus's stance.
- **The AI low end.** The 2026 barbell clip places Meta AI at the low end of a commoditising market (the Walmart approach). The page holds the placement and notes it is the corpus's latest Meta register.
- **The 1999 false positive.** The 1999 "Libraries in the Age of Infinite Media" source is tagged for the word "meta-trends," not the company. The page notes the false positive and does not count it as Meta content.
- **The accessibility caveat.** The VR-simulation talk credits Meta (and the Apple Vision Pro) as resurgent-cycle drivers but notes the motion-sickness majority over thirty as the accessibility barrier. The page holds the driver credit and the caveat together as the corpus's balanced stance.

# Meta

## TL;DR
Meta appears in four sources as both the rebranded Facebook driving the second metaverse hype cycle and as Pesce's counter-example: a company whose walled-garden Horizon Worlds is the opposite of the interoperable metaverse he argues is the only kind worth building.

## Overview
The Meta references cluster in the recent corpus and are almost entirely metaverse-focused. In "A House Built on Sand" — delivered in VR — Pesce argues that after three decades the metaverse remains "largely empty": platforms including Decentraland, The Sandbox, Cryptovoxels, and Meta's Horizon Worlds each claim to be the metaverse, yet none is interoperable with any other, and Tony Parisi's definition implies there should be only one [[sources/a-house-built-on-sand-interoperability-in-the-me]]. He uses Meta's own platform as the exemplar of the problem: virtual real-estate deeds redeemable only inside walled gardens, with the film This Is Spinal Tap as his analogy for trivial unit mismatches causing outsized failures.

On the hardware side, he treats Meta as a driver of the current resurgent cycle — alongside the Apple Vision Pro — while noting fully immersive systems still cause motion sickness in a majority of adults over thirty, making them unwelcoming to broad demographics [[sources/vr-simulation-future-of-work-mp4]]. Earlier material adds texture: the 2020 Voices of VR interview credits Microsoft, not Facebook, with the HoloLens and Kinect as foundational to modern inside-out tracking, and reads Facebook's aggressive 2017 AR push as a counter-move against Snapchat [[sources/voices-of-vr-mark-pesce-20nov20-mp3]] — a reminder that Meta's AR/metaverse ambition predates the rebrand. Even the 2024-ish "barbell" market clip casts Meta AI at the low end of a commoditising AI market [[sources/f5-barbell-vacuum-cleaners-mp4]].

The synthesis is consistent: Pesce treats Meta as the metaverse's most visible champion and its most instructive failure mode — enormous investment, fragmenting rather than unifying the space, and (per his book-tour argument) building surveillance into the device layer through Project Aria's gaze tracking [[sources/voices-of-vr-mark-pesce-20nov20-mp3]]. What changed over time is the framing: pre-2021 sources discuss Facebook's AR strategy; post-rebrand sources discuss Meta's Horizon Worlds as one non-interoperable island among many, and the metaverse as "a set of human agreements rather than a purely technological achievement" — agreements Meta's platforms conspicuously lack.

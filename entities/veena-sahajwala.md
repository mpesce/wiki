# Veena Sahajwala

## TL;DR

The engineering professor who rotates the *New Inventors* panel — the show's academic seat, and its most environmentally attuned vote.

## Overview

Veena Sahajwala (ASR variants: Veena/Venus/Venus) is the "engineering professor" of *The New Inventors* panel, one of its most consistent judges — 21 of the 2007–2009 season episodes carry her name, from the February 2007 Thompson coupling episode to the November 2009 safety-propeller episode ([[sources/the-new-inventors-20070221195900-mp4]], [[sources/the-new-inventors-20091111200000-mp4]]). She is the panel's only academic engineer (Bradfield Moody is simply "engineer"), and her votes track the engineering axis: safety and environment first. She is the one who argues the Conehead helmet liner "represents a revolutionary shift in safety standards", comparing two decades of helmet stagnation to the historical introduction of crumple zones in cars ([[sources/the-new-inventors-20070822200000-mp4]]); she votes for the solid-state LED with Russell on originality and market potential ([[sources/the-new-inventors-20070711200000-mp4]]); and she is the panel's environmental conscience — the Hydrosolar passive solar tracker (for its environmental benefits, against the FAS foot system), the Aqua Curve gutter-to-tree water device, the Benox blocks' reduced material use, and the Solomate solar heating/cooling system ([[sources/the-new-inventors-20070328195900-mp4]], [[sources/the-new-inventors-20081105200000-mp4]], [[sources/the-new-inventors-20090429200000-mp4]]).

Her safety vote is the other constant: the Sea Scooper water-rescue device ("critical safety application"), the Fume Tube carbon-monoxide exhaust system (the unanimous 2007 win she shares with Pesce and Vaughan on the safety ground), the ImmacuCut enclosed plasma cutter, the V100 man-overboard GPS mayday device (whose "integrated design" she highlights), and the Pedigree Matchmaker RFID lamb-identification system, whose originality she singles out in the 2009 win ([[sources/the-new-inventors-20081015200000-mp4]], [[sources/the-new-inventors-20070509200000-mp4]], [[sources/the-new-inventors-20091007200000-mp4]], [[sources/the-new-inventors-20090722200000-mp4]], [[sources/the-new-inventors-20091021200000-mp4]]). When her vote loses — the FAS foot-alignment episode, where she backs the solar tracker and Hobbs/Vaughan take the medical device — the deliberation is the standard format: her environmental case, their practical-need case, a two-to-one result ([[sources/the-new-inventors-20070328195900-mp4]]). The corpus gives her no identity beyond the chair — no university, no published work, no off-show interview — so her entity page is, like O'Loghlin's and Russell's, a role description drawn from the broadcast itself.

## Debates / Open Questions


- **Professor of what, at where?** The corpus credits her as "engineering professor" with no institution. (The wiki's [[entities/unsw]] page exists, but no source in the corpus links the two — the connection, if real, is outside the recorded material.)
- **The environmental vote as a stable persona.** Her votes are the most ideologically consistent of any judge in the corpus (environment + safety), which makes her a useful test case for whether the panel's criteria were genuinely plural or whether the "design/originality/market/need" rubric had a de facto environmental reading built in.

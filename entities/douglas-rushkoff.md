# Douglas Rushkoff

## TL;DR

Mark Pesce's longtime friend, co-panelist and fellow media theorist — co-host of Esalen's 2001 "Techno-Pagans at the End of History" trialogues with Pesce and Erik Davis, and later an interview subject in Pesce's 2021 "wisdom talks" on turning 60.

## Overview

Douglas Rushkoff appears in the corpus first as a collaborator and then as a reflective peer. The earliest cluster is the 2001 Esalen Institute weekend "Techno-Pagans at the End of History," a multi-day participatory trialogue co-hosted by Pesce, Rushkoff, and Erik Davis in the spirit of Terence McKenna's conversations with Rupert Sheldrake and Ralph Abraham ([[sources/friday-rm]], [[sources/techno-pagans-at-the-end-of-history-1-esalen-mp3]]). Across its sessions the three move between long-form conversation and open audience discussion, spanning virtual reality, psychedelics, nanotechnology, religion, and the "end of history" ([[sources/techno-pagans-at-the-end-of-history-5-esalen-mp3]], [[sources/techno-pagans-at-the-end-of-history-11-esalen-mp]]). Rushkoff frames the weekend around "beginnings" — he says he doesn't believe in them, only evolutions of form — and identifies as a media theorist with roots in theater and journalism, citing his early-1990s book on cyberculture and *Coercion*, and arguing that information culture has commodified communication, reducing rich dialogue to mere data transfer ([[sources/friday-mp3]]).

In 2009 the relationship reverses: Rushkoff hosts Pesce on his WFMU radio show The Media Squat, broadcast live from the Personal Democracy Forum at Lincoln Center, where Pesce discusses the collision between open systems like Wikipedia and closed hierarchies ([[sources/rushkoff-mediasquat-30jun09-mp3]]). By February 2021, Pesce interviews Rushkoff for a "wisdom talks" episode celebrating Rushkoff's sixtieth birthday — tracing his journey from early-1990s writings on hypertext and psychedelic culture, through his argument that the internet's promise of infinite play was co-opted by speculative capitalism, to his view that financialization has stripped reality of relational value and his return to community theater ([[sources/wisdom-douglas-rushkoff-cuts-us-some-slack-febru]]). As recently as January 2026, Rushkoff (together with Erik Davis) is cited by the host as one of the respected figures who recommended Pesce as a speaker for the dinner talk ([[sources/highcastledinner-17jan26-mp4]]).

Across three decades, the consistent thread is a shared intellectual project: both men came up through the 1990s cyberdelic counterculture, watched the open web get captured by commercial platforms, and turned to human-scale, relational practices (theater, mentoring, community) as the response. The corpus does not include Rushkoff's own books or broadcasts as standalone sources; he appears only alongside Pesce, so this page reflects the Pesce-facing slice of a longer career.

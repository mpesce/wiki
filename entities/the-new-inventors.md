# The New Inventors

## TL;DR

ABC television's weekly invention-competition program (late 2000s run), whose episodes and rotating expert judges — including Mark Pesce — form the largest single cluster in the corpus.

## Overview

The New Inventors was the Australian Broadcasting Corporation's weekly studio program in which three (sometimes four) Australian inventors pitched their creations to a rotating panel of experts. The format is consistent across the 64 dated episodes in the corpus (February 2007 – November 2009): each pitch was judged on design, originality, marketability, need, and safety; the winner of the night advanced in the running for the annual Inventor of the Year prize; and the studio audience plus viewers at home voted for a People's Choice Award by text or phone ([[sources/the-new-inventors-20070214195900-mp4]], [[sources/newinventors-mp4]], [[sources/the-new-inventors-20081029200000-mp4]]). The October 2008 broadcast was the show's 200th episode and opened with a retrospective on its five-year history — 597 Australian inventions and 717 inventors featured — placing the program's launch around 2004 ([[sources/the-new-inventors-20081029200000-mp4]]).

The judging panel is what the corpus mostly remembers. James O'Loughlin hosted most episodes, while the expert seats rotated among engineer James Bradfield Moody, science journalist Bernie Hobbs, agricultural scientist Chris Russell, interior designer Alison Page, designer-inventor Sally Deminks, woodworker Richard Vaughan, engineering professor Veena Sahajwala, journalist-inventor Christine Kininmonth, and surgeon-inventor Professor Fiona Wood ([[sources/the-new-inventors-20070221195900-mp4]], [[sources/the-new-inventors-20090325200000-mp4]], [[sources/the-new-inventors-20090401200000-mp4]]). Mark Pesce joined the panel as "author and futurist" and even hosted episodes himself ([[sources/the-new-inventors-20070829200000-mp4]], [[sources/the-new-inventors-20090408200000-mp4]], [[sources/the-new-inventors-20091104200000-mp4]]); every judge's own entity page in this wiki is built largely out of this cluster of episodes.

Thematically the show was a steady stream of practical, low-tech Australian ingenuity, tilted toward problems the country actually has: water scarcity (a dedicated 2007 special revisited the Every Drop shower and modular rain tanks, and the panel kept picking water inventions — the 2008 electronic water purifier won on "critical global need"), farm and rural safety (silo lids, shearing trolleys, bee hives, horse bits), workplace injury (fluorescent-lamp disposal units, pipe cutters, scaffold guards), and household ergonomics (wheelbarrows, doors, pegs) ([[sources/the-new-inventors-20070530200000-mp4]], [[sources/the-new-inventors-20070502200000-mp4]], [[sources/the-new-inventors-20081022200000-mp4]]). Occasionally the program ran a moonshot segment — quantum-communication entanglement, ANUGA flood modelling, UNSW micro aerial vehicles, a bushfire-response episode after the 2009 fires and floods ([[sources/the-new-inventors-20090812200000-mp4]], [[sources/the-new-inventors-20090610200000-mp4]]) — but the winning formula stayed consistent: a single clear need, an elegant mechanical fix, and a price a household or small business could pay.

Outside the episodes, the program survives in the corpus as Pesce's own biographical credential and a case study for his arguments. In his 2008 talk on Twitter and the media he used the show's hashtag as the example of television's new back-channel, turning passive viewing into a synchronous shared event ([[sources/01-twitter-and-the-media-m4a]]). In later talks he lists it in his bio — "judge on the ABC series The New Inventors" — alongside his VRML invention and *The Playful World* ([[sources/monash-wav]], [[sources/mind-share-hd-mov]]), and at the 2018 Pete board presentation he notes he was "labeled a futurist" because of hosting the show ([[sources/peet-m4a]], [[sources/recording-2-m4a]]). The 2005 telephone-repair research talk even frames a prediction Pesce made "on the New Inventors panel" ([[sources/telephone-repair-handbook-december-2005-mp3]]), confirming the program existed by the mid-2000s.

- The corpus ends the show's story at the 2009 season; no later episodes are recorded.

## Debates / Open Questions


- **The panel's stability vs the rotating brand.** Episodes shuffle experts weekly; the "New Inventors" brand is the constant, not any individual judge. The corpus can't say whether ABC intended the rotation (fresh perspectives) or cycled judges for budget reasons.
- **ASR identity noise.** Host and judge names arrive with many spellings (O'Loughlin / Lachlan / Loughlin; Hobbs / Hodges; Deminks / Deminks / Domingues; Kininmonth / Kninmonth / Kninmonks). These are almost certainly speech-recognition variants of the same recurring cast — the entity pages are keyed accordingly.
- **What happened after 2009.** The corpus contains no 2010-or-later episode; the show's end and any revival are unrecorded here. (Pesce's 2018 self-introduction still credits the show as his "futurist" origin, so it remained a live credential.)

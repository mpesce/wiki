# MIT

## TL;DR

The institution where Pesce spent the late 1970s–early 1980s (he says he was "expelled"), and the research establishment he returns to as a citation authority throughout his career.

## Overview

MIT (the Massachusetts Institute of Technology) holds two distinct roles in the corpus. First, **Pesce's own**: in the 2023 Cybersamhain talk he places his formative perspective — technology and spirituality as "completions of one another", developed under the influence of **Norbert Wiener's** cybernetics — at "MIT in the late 1970s and early 1980s" ([[sources/cybersamhain-to-chatgpt-1080p-mp4]]); in the 2012 Embiggen book launch he notes he was "expelled from MIT" ([[sources/embiggen-17aug12-mp4]]); and in a 2021 This Week in Startups interview a guest futurist and former startup executive recounts leaving MIT in 1982 to work at Contact Systems on the prototype for the Secure ID card ([[sources/mark-pesce-wtd1-28jun21-wav]]). Second, **MIT as institution**, cited repeatedly as the source of the field's ideas: the founding of the **AI lab** by McCarthy, Papert and Minsky ([[sources/rmit-mp3]]); **Sherry Turkle's** research showing children create a new ontological category for interactive toys like the Furby ([[sources/1-01-the-kids-are-alright-kincoppal-rose-bay-sch]]); the **Kismet and COG** robot projects that learn through interaction, the embodiment argument rooted in Piaget ([[sources/artbell2-mp3]]); **DARPA-funded nanotechnology** research at MIT and UCLA ([[sources/artbell3-mp3]]); **open courseware** (with Stanford and UNSW) as the example that "if they can share their full courseware online without diluting the education, no institution can refute that example" ([[sources/elearning08-mark-pesce-20081104-mp3]], [[sources/collisions-and-smash-repairs-mp4]]); and **Scratch**, "a drag-and-drop coding environment created at MIT", whose move to a web platform enabled what Pesce calls hypermimesis ([[sources/learning-in-the-connected-century-mov]]). The 2000 RMIT keynote is the one place where MIT's role is historical rather than institutional: the lineage from Babbage and the punched-card looms, through Turing and von Neumann as "the twin pillars of computer science", to the AI lab ([[sources/rmit-mp3]])

## Debates / Open Questions


- **Expelled vs graduated.** The 2012 talk says "expelled from MIT"; the 2021 guest account says he "left MIT in 1982" to work at Contact Systems. The corpus does not reconcile the two, and the Secure ID detail belongs to the guest's account, not Pesce's own retelling.
- **MIT as method vs MIT as place.** The 2000 RMIT talk uses MIT's AI lab as a historical node; the 2023 talk uses MIT as his formative home. Both are Pesce's own framings, and the entity page holds both without privileging one.

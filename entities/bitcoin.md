# Bitcoin

## TL;DR

Pesce credits Bitcoin with inventing a genuinely new kind of money — countable, transferable, non-duplicable — but doubts it as everyday currency, framing its real legacy as blockchain and the post-classical finance wave of stablecoins, CBDCs and smart contracts that followed.

## Overview

The early mentions are sidebars to his hyperconnectivity thesis. In the 2011 "Hypereconomics" lectures, currency appears among the domains friction-eliminating connectivity would transform — alongside Kerala fishermen and Kenya farmers using mobile phones to arbitrage markets — with the predicted endpoint a decentralised economy of direct API-level value exchange [[sources/hypereconomics-flv]], [[sources/hypereconomics-2-flv]]. Bitcoin itself enters as a case study: introduced in 2009 as a decentralized ledger solving the double-spend problem, it solved counterfeiting but its slow settlement times make it unsuitable for everyday commerce [[sources/asx-wav]].

2016 is a pivot year: opening the Westpac BlockHack16 hackathon, he heard bankers draw the same parallel he once used for VRML — that 2016 for blockchain is like 1994 for the web — with Ripple-based cross-border payment trials already running inside the bank [[sources/zoom0013-tr2-wav]].

2019 is the pivot to the argument he would repeat for two years: Facebook's June 2019 Libra proposal. Speaking on RN Drive, he argued Bitcoin was originally designed for the roughly four billion people who own smartphones but lack banking access, but its slow transactions disqualify it; Libra's basket-pegged stablecoin targets the unbanked at payments scale, with Visa, MasterCard, PayPal and Vodafone aboard — regulators worried about eroded money-creation monopolies and Facebook's unprecedented data reach [[sources/rnd-20190619-1906-fb-libra-mp3]], and the ASX "New Money" talk added the digital yuan as Libra's forced accelerant [[sources/asx-new-money-lbr-mp4]]. Chairing the V20 summit opening in June 2019, he cast crypto's move from "anarchic pioneering phase" to FATF-regulated settlement as the transition from pioneers to settlers [[sources/v20-june-2019-opening-mts]].

By the 2020–2021 CFO series talks, Bitcoin has become the first chapter of a larger story: PayPal's October 2020 crypto checkout as a regulated walled garden, China's digital yuan rollout targeting the 2022 Winter Olympics, and — in the post-classical finance frame — the 2008 whitepaper as the origin of smart contracts and DeFi alongside Diem, the digital euro and RBA pilots [[sources/cfo-series-melbourne-while-you-were-out-12nov20]]. The consistent through-line: Pesce credits Bitcoin with proving money can be countable, transferable and non-duplicable without a central issuer, while doubting that Bitcoin itself — slow, volatile, pioneer-phase — is the form that wins; the durable legacy is the technology stack, not the coin.

# Dr. George Greer

## TL;DR

The psychiatrist on the corpus's one panel — early MDMA legal research, the four-level spiritual framework, and the reading of the Griffiths psilocybin study.

## Overview

George Greer (Dr George Greer) appears in the corpus in exactly one source: a **panel discussion moderated by Mark Pesce** (the "3-04 Panel Discussion including Dr. George Greer" session, part of a larger conference that also referenced previous speakers John Hanna and Matt Baggett). The corpus identifies him as "a psychiatrist who conducted **early legal research on the psychotherapeutic use of MDMA in the 1980s**" — his work with approximately 80 subjects using MDMA "synthesized in a laboratory setting prior to its prohibition" ([[sources/3-04-panel-discussion-including-dr-george-greer]]). He presents a theoretical framework based on **Arthur Young's reflexive universe model**, dividing human experience into four levels — physical, mental, emotional, spiritual — and argues the primary purpose of psychedelic use is to "access the highest spiritual level to make conscious choices, a state he defines as being fully alive"; psychedelics "disrupt habitual neural patterns, forcing the user to actively choose their perception and purpose" ([[sources/3-04-panel-discussion-including-dr-george-greer]]). The panel turns to the **Griffiths psilocybin study** (significant mainstream media coverage): Greer's reading is that its most significant finding was not the immediate mystical experience but "50 percent of participants reported being fundamentally happier six months later, a change corroborated by their families". The panel also discusses making psychedelics "boring" or mundane through scientific description to reduce fear and stigma, the disconnect between the psychedelic community (which views such research as obvious) and the terrified general public, and the practical integration of insights (behavioral engagement, perseverance, support; discussing the experience with a close person; monitoring internal physiological states) — closing with the shamanic caution that "visions or epiphanies during altered states are not always truthful and should be treated with skepticism rather than absolute certainty" ([[sources/3-04-panel-discussion-including-dr-george-greer]]).

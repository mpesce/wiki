# Fiona Wood

## TL;DR

The "surgeon and inventor" — and, per the 2009 episode, "the inventor of spray-on skin" — who rotates the *New Inventors* panel through 2009.

## Overview

Fiona Wood (ASR variants: Professor Fiona Wood, surgeon and inventor Professor Fiona Wood) is one of the rotating judges on *The New Inventors*, credited in the 2009-03-25 episode as **"the inventor of spray-on skin"** ([[sources/the-new-inventors-20090325200000-mp4]]). In the corpus she is on the panel of seven 2009 episodes, always identified as **surgeon and inventor** (one source adds *professor*), which makes her the panel's only practicing clinician-inventor — the one judge whose own work is a medical technology. Her votes and remarks track the medical/safety axis: on the 2009-03-25 episode she highlights "the significant need for better food safety monitoring" (the episode's winner, Intelligent Plastic, a chemistry label scoring food freshness); on the 2009-08-26 episode she emphasizes "the safety and simplicity of using autologous cells" in the corneal stem-cell repair that wins the episode ([[sources/the-new-inventors-20090325200000-mp4]], [[sources/the-new-inventors-20090826200000-mp4]]). The other appearances are the standard rotating-panel format with the other judges (Bernie Hobbs, Chris Russell, James Bradfield Moody, Alison Page, Sally Deminks, Christine Keninmonth, Mark Pesce) and the episode winners (haptic gripper, Height Guard, the finger-saving door hinge, the internal bore casing cutter) ([[sources/the-new-inventors-20090401200000-mp4]], [[sources/the-new-inventors-20090617200000-mp4]], [[sources/the-new-inventors-20090624200000-mp4]], [[sources/the-new-inventors-20090923200000-mp4]], [[sources/the-new-inventors-20090930200000-mp4]]). As with the other judges, the corpus gives her no identity beyond the chair — no hospital, no publication, no off-show interview; the spray-on-skin credit is the one biographical fact it carries.

- **Spray-on skin: how far is the corpus's claim?** The 2009-03-25 episode credits her as "the inventor of spray-on skin"; no other source in the corpus elaborates. The entity page holds the claim as the corpus states it, without the hospital or paper trail the corpus does not contain.

# distributed ledger

## TL;DR

The enterprise-facing synonym for blockchain in the corpus — "a trusted, shared register of data" — appearing in the 2016–2019 financial and infrastructure talks.

## Overview

Distributed Ledger (distributed ledger technology, DLT) is the technical name the corpus uses for blockchain in its enterprise and infrastructure contexts, and it appears in three talks. (1) **BlockHack 16** (Westpac, 2016): the host predicts "distributed ledger technology will fundamentally change finance" — the term as the strategic frame for the bank's internal hackathon ([[sources/zoom0013-tr2-wav]]). (2) **PNG 2019**: "the potential of distributed ledger technology to secure land title registration, protecting rights for those who cannot afford legal defense" — the term as a land-rights infrastructure in Papua New Guinea ([[sources/png2019-mkv]]). (3) **Fintech Futures**: "Full Profile uses distributed ledger technology, or blockchain, to secure payment for grain farmers", and blockchain is defined as "a trusted, shared register of data that can be used for identity verification, securitizing loyalty points, and creating micro-insurance pools" ([[sources/fintech-futures-wav]]). The corpus holds Distributed Ledger and blockchain as the same technology under two names: "blockchain" is the popular/coin-adjacent term, and "distributed ledger" is the enterprise/infrastructure term (Westpac, PNG, the fintech accelerator). The consistent definition is the "trusted, shared register of data" — consensus-based, tamper-resistant, and applicable to identity, loyalty, insurance, and land title in addition to money.

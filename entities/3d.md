# 3D

## TL;DR

Across four decades of the corpus, 3D is Pesce's core technical and rhetorical territory: from co-creating VRML, the first 3D standard for the Web, in 1994, through the 2010 stereoscopic 3D-TV scare, to today's augmented-reality 'digital depth' and the persistent argument that the web should render in depth, not just flat pages.

## Overview

The 3D thread begins at the origin of the web's third dimension. Pesce's VRML work — described in [[sources/vrml-interview-mp4]] as a "three-dimensional equivalent of HTML" built by an open community of roughly 2,000 contributors — recurs across his 1990s recordings: Web Earth at the Avatars96 conference used live NASA satellite data to model the planet for climatology and epidemiology [[sources/avatars96-pesce-leonard-dipaola-512kb-mp4]], and he was presenting 3D web interfaces at the first World Wide Web conference by 1994 [[sources/mark-pesce-session-1-wav]]. At that stage 3D meant the promise of the web as a navigable, human-centred space; he argued then that 3D visualization is essential for subjects like landscapes and molecules that cannot be adequately represented in two dimensions.

The theme changes register in the late 2000s. By 2005 he was already pointing to the mobile phone — with its vector-map 3D chips — as an under-used node on multiple networks [[sources/telephone-repair-handbook-december-2005-mp3]], and by 2010 the public conversation had moved from VRML to stereoscopic 3D television. In 2010 appearances on 7:30 [[sources/7-30report-3dtv-3jun10-m4v]] and The 7pm Project [[sources/7pm-project-mov]] he drew directly on his 1990s Sega VR work to warn that stereo 3D "tricks the brain" with mismatched depth cues, citing motion-sickness study results and safety tests around children's developing nervous systems — the same safety concerns that, he notes, had led Sega to cancel its headset. Reality-check segments on JJJ Hack that year and in 2008 puncture the hype, noting that CNN's "hologram" interviews were not true volumetric capture [[sources/jjj-hack-future-3dec08-mp3]].

After the 3D-TV boom faded, 3D re-emerged in the 2010s as the substrate of augmented reality. His talks from 2016 to 2021 — the "Digital Depth in Augmented Reality" lecture [[sources/mark-pesce-digital-depth-in-augmented-reality-mp]], SIGGRAPH Frontiers "The AR Dilemmas" [[sources/pesce-siggraph-frontiers-the-ar-dilemmas-mp4]], and Web3D 2017 [[sources/web3d2017-mp3]] — recast 3D not as spectacle but as the rendering layer for metadata bound to physical space, with Pokémon Go as the mass-market proof that inscribing data into space changes behaviour. He argues the modern phone is already "an immersive platform in waiting" — ubiquitous 3D-capable hardware means the remaining problems are content and agreement, not silicon [[sources/web3d2017-mp3]]. The consistent through-line, from VRML to Decentraland-era virtual worlds [[sources/uptown-radio-recording-1-2021-04-26-t12-31-18am-2]], is that 3D is not a product category but an interface technology: the computer adapting to human spatial cognition rather than the reverse.

## In the Corpus


The corpus holds 3D as a four-decade thread that keeps changing register:

- **1993–1996 — 3D as the web's third dimension.** Pesce's co-creation of VRML (the "three-dimensional equivalent of HTML") and its presentation at the first WWW conference (CERN, May 1994) [[sources/mark-pesce-session-1-wav]], and the 1996 Avatars96 Web Earth built on live NASA satellite data for climatology/epidemiology [[sources/avatars96-pesce-leonard-dipaola-512kb-mp4]].
- **2005–2009 — 3D on the mobile phone.** The vector-map 3D chip in the handset as an under-used network node [[sources/telephone-repair-handbook-december-2005-mp3]], and The Scoop 2009 panel on AR, immersive 3D, and the "penny drop" moment [[sources/thescoop-3dec09-mp3]].
- **2010 — the stereoscopic 3D-TV scare.** The 7:30 reports [[sources/7-30report-3dtv-3jun10-m4v]], The 7pm Project [[sources/7pm-project-mov]], and Today Tonight all draw on the 1990s Sega VR safety work to warn that stereo 3D "tricks the brain"; the JJJ Hack 2008 segment punctures the "hologram" hype [[sources/jjj-hack-future-3dec08-mp3]].
- **2016–2021 — 3D as augmented-reality depth.** The "Digital Depth in AR" lecture [[sources/mark-pesce-digital-depth-in-augmented-reality-mp]], SIGGRAPH Frontiers "The AR Dilemmas" [[sources/pesce-siggraph-frontiers-the-ar-dilemmas-mp4]], Web3D 2017 [[sources/web3d2017-mp3]], and the ARIN / Internet Transformations talks recast 3D as the rendering layer for metadata bound to physical space (Pokémon Go as the mass-market proof) [[sources/arin-2610-internet-transformations-the-ar-dilemm-2]].
- **2023 — 3D as techno-spirit.** The Cybersamhain talk frames the 1994–95 3D-web co-creation as a structural-coupling of all humans and performs a blessing of the new digital space [[sources/cybersamhain-to-chatgpt-1080p-mp4]].

## Debates / Open Questions


- **VRML vs WebGL.** The corpus treats WebGL as VRML's direct successor (the 2010 VRML talk and the 2021 Internet Transformations lecture both frame it as the superseded predecessor). The page holds that lineage as the corpus states it; the 3D web is now a WebGL/WebXR matter, not a VRML matter.
- **The 3D-TV safety thread.** The 2010 3D-TV scare is anchored on Pesce's first-hand Sega VR experience and the Stanford Research Institute's safety advice. The corpus presents the motion-sickness warnings (Macquarie University's 20%-of-150 study) and the "gigantic unregulated medical experiment" framing as the safety argument; the page holds the Sega/SRI safety advice as the corpus's anchor and notes the same concern recurs in the AR "right to write" ethics of 2016–2021.
- **The slug is a category, not a product.** "3D" in the corpus lumps together VR, stereoscopic 3D television, augmented reality, and 3D graphics under one technology slug. The page holds the slug as the shared technical substrate (the web rendering in depth) and notes the register shifts (VRML → 3D-TV → AR depth) are the entity's actual evolution.
- **Sega VirtuaVR.** The corpus names the cancelled Sega headset and its motion-sickness failure (1993 CES, cancelled July 1993) as the first VR era's end. Sega has no entity page in the corpus; the page holds the headset under the 3D/Virtual Reality thread and the safety thread.

# Microsoft

## TL;DR

Microsoft appears in the corpus as a recurring foil across three eras: the 1990s antagonist of open web standards, the 2009–2011 search-and-security laggard chasing Google, and the 2016–2021 quiet enabler of augmented reality and cloud transformation.

## Overview

In the mid-1990s Microsoft appears as the corporate threat to an open 3D web: Pesce recounts the VRML community's standards battle against Silicon Graphics and Microsoft during the "frontier days" of 1995 [[sources/avatars96-keynotes-pesce-parisi-512kb-mp4]]. By 2004 the tone had shifted to grudging respect — his DIGIMART talk credits Microsoft with a counter-intuitive insight about sharing economics, paying the Halo machinima makers of Red vs. Blue rather than suing them, having worked out that "the more something is shared, the more valuable it becomes" [[sources/digimart-when-the-going-gets-weird-mp3]].

2009 and 2010 are dominated by Microsoft's search and security struggles. On JJJ Hack he analysed the Microsoft–Yahoo search deal as Microsoft "effectively buying its way into second place" against Google [[sources/hack-thurs-2009-07-30-mp3]], and months later he walked through the Internet Explorer 6 zero-day that had breached Google and other major corporations, advising listeners to switch to open-source browsers and framing the intrusions as "a form of military action by other means" [[sources/hack-tues-2010-01-19-mp3]].

From 2016 onward, Microsoft is recast as the under-appreciated foundation layer of modern AR. Pesce credits the Kinect and HoloLens — not Facebook — as foundational to modern inside-out tracking [[sources/voices-of-vr-mark-pesce-20nov20-mp3]], treats holoportation on HoloLens as the visible edge of videogrammetry [[sources/accanet-12sep18-mp3]], and in "The AR Dilemmas" criticises Microsoft alongside Google and Apple for treating physical space as a writable canvas without respecting property boundaries [[sources/pesce-siggraph-frontiers-the-ar-dilemmas-mp4]]. In the pandemic-era talks, Microsoft Teams appears as emblematic of the forced shift to remote work — whose human costs Microsoft itself studied [[sources/at-the-crossroads-cfo-series-wa-23jul20-mp4]] — and by mid-2021 he holds Microsoft up as the exemplar of a trillion-dollar company restructuring from single-user software licenses to cloud services, a slow, human-scale transformation rather than a sudden pivot [[sources/mark-pesce-alchemist-23jun21-wav]]. The constant across all three eras: Microsoft as a bellwether of platform power — first challenged, then copied, then quietly indispensable.

## In the Corpus


The corpus holds Microsoft as a recurring foil across three eras, with the register shifting from antagonist to enabler:

- **The 1990s open-web antagonist.** The VRML community's standards battle against Silicon Graphics and Microsoft during the "frontier days" of 1995 [[sources/avatars96-keynotes-pesce-parisi-512kb-mp4]], and the Render Morphix Reality Lab that became Direct3D (the rendering engine behind the first VRML browser) [[sources/mark-pesce-session-1-wav]].
- **The 2004 sharing-economics insight.** DIGIMART credits Microsoft with the counter-intuitive Red vs. Blue decision — paying the Halo machinima makers rather than suing them, after working out that "the more something is shared, the more valuable it becomes" [[sources/digimart-when-the-going-gets-weird-mp3]].
- **The 2009–2011 search-and-security laggard.** The Microsoft–Yahoo search deal ("buying its way into second place" against Google) [[sources/hack-thurs-2009-07-30-mp3]], the Internet Explorer 6 zero-day that breached Google and other corporations ("military action by other means") [[sources/hack-tues-2010-01-19-mp3]], and the Kinect (The Drum, 2011) as the predicted standard [[sources/the-drum-youtube-1-m4v]].
- **The 2016–2021 AR and cloud enabler.** The Kinect and HoloLens credited (not Facebook) as foundational to modern inside-out tracking [[sources/voices-of-vr-mark-pesce-20nov20-mp3]], holoportation on HoloLens as the visible edge of videogrammetry [[sources/accanet-12sep18-mp3]], Microsoft in the AR "right to write" critique alongside Google and Apple [[sources/pesce-siggraph-frontiers-the-ar-dilemmas-mp4]], Teams as the pandemic remote-work emblem (whose human costs Microsoft itself studied) [[sources/at-the-crossroads-cfo-series-wa-23jul20-mp4]], and the trillion-dollar cloud pivot (Alchemist, 2021) [[sources/mark-pesce-alchemist-23jun21-wav]].

## Debates / Open Questions


- **The three-era foil.** The corpus holds the arc: 1990s open-web antagonist → 2009–2011 search-and-security laggard → 2016–2021 AR-and-cloud enabler. The page holds the three registers and notes the shift from "challenged, then copied, then quietly indispensable."
- **The Direct3D lineage.** The corpus credits Render Morphix Reality Lab as the software rendering engine that became Direct3D and underpinned the first VRML browser. The page holds the lineage as the corpus states it.
- **Red vs. Blue.** The 2004 DIGIMART talk credits Microsoft with the sharing-economics insight (paying the machinima makers, not suing). The page holds the Red vs. Blue story as the corpus's exemplar of hyperdistribution and the "WEIRD turn pro" moment.
- **The IE6 zero-day.** The 2010 Hack segment walks the Internet Explorer 6 zero-day (breaching Google and others) and advises open-source browsers; the intrusions are framed as "military action by other means." The page holds the security framing and the open-source recommendation.
- **The Kinect as AR foundation.** The 2020 Voices of VR interview credits Microsoft's HoloLens and Kinect (not Facebook) as foundational to modern inside-out tracking. The page holds the Microsoft-over-Facebook credit and notes the Kinect's 3D monitoring as the surveillance thread.
- **The cloud pivot.** The 2021 Alchemist interview holds Microsoft up as the exemplar of a trillion-dollar company restructuring from single-user software to cloud services — a slow, human-scale transformation. The page holds the pivot as the corpus's latest Microsoft register.
- **The right-to-write inclusion.** The 2016–2021 AR Dilemmas lectures include Microsoft (with Google and Apple) in the critique of treating physical space as a writable canvas without consent. The page holds the inclusion as the corpus's stance.

# Microsoft

## TL;DR

Microsoft appears in the corpus as a recurring foil across three eras: the 1990s antagonist of open web standards, the 2009–2011 search-and-security laggard chasing Google, and the 2016–2021 quiet enabler of augmented reality and cloud transformation.

## Overview

In the mid-1990s Microsoft appears as the corporate threat to an open 3D web: Pesce recounts the VRML community's standards battle against Silicon Graphics and Microsoft during the "frontier days" of 1995 [[sources/avatars96-keynotes-pesce-parisi-512kb-mp4]]. By 2004 the tone had shifted to grudging respect — his DIGIMART talk credits Microsoft with a counter-intuitive insight about sharing economics, paying the Halo machinima makers of Red vs. Blue rather than suing them, having worked out that "the more something is shared, the more valuable it becomes" [[sources/digimart-when-the-going-gets-weird-mp3]].

2009 and 2010 are dominated by Microsoft's search and security struggles. On JJJ Hack he analysed the Microsoft–Yahoo search deal as Microsoft "effectively buying its way into second place" against Google [[sources/hack-thurs-2009-07-30-mp3]], and months later he walked through the Internet Explorer 6 zero-day that had breached Google and other major corporations, advising listeners to switch to open-source browsers and framing the intrusions as "a form of military action by other means" [[sources/hack-tues-2010-01-19-mp3]].

From 2016 onward, Microsoft is recast as the under-appreciated foundation layer of modern AR. Pesce credits the Kinect and HoloLens — not Facebook — as foundational to modern inside-out tracking [[sources/voices-of-vr-mark-pesce-20nov20-mp3]], treats holoportation on HoloLens as the visible edge of videogrammetry [[sources/accanet-12sep18-mp3]], and in "The AR Dilemmas" criticises Microsoft alongside Google and Apple for treating physical space as a writable canvas without respecting property boundaries [[sources/pesce-siggraph-frontiers-the-ar-dilemmas-mp4]]. In the pandemic-era talks, Microsoft Teams appears as emblematic of the forced shift to remote work — whose human costs Microsoft itself studied [[sources/at-the-crossroads-cfo-series-wa-23jul20-mp4]] — and by mid-2021 he holds Microsoft up as the exemplar of a trillion-dollar company restructuring from single-user software licenses to cloud services, a slow, human-scale transformation rather than a sudden pivot [[sources/mark-pesce-alchemist-23jun21-wav]]. The constant across all three eras: Microsoft as a bellwether of platform power — first challenged, then copied, then quietly indispensable.

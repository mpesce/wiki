# smart contracts

## TL;DR

The application layer of the blockchain argument in the corpus — "computer programs wrapped in blockchain technology that can operate on digital currencies" — with the DAO hack as its standing cautionary tale.

## Overview

Smart contracts enter the corpus as part of Mark Pesce's financial-future toolkit, and the definition is stable: "computer programs wrapped in blockchain technology that can operate on digital currencies, allowing for automated and verifiable financial transactions" (Post-Classical Finance, May 2021) ([[sources/post-classical-finance-cfo-series-sydney-11may21]]). The argument recurs in the same financial-tour venues: the CFO talks (2016: smart contracts and "smart money", the ASX migration to blockchain settlement, self-executing contracts in trade finance), the Smarter Money and Grant Thornton talks (the DAO — a $150M code-governed venture fund hacked for $50M, forcing a controversial blockchain rollback), the Trench Talk podcast (efficiency gains from smart contracts), and the CFO series (the RBA's wholesale digital currency built on Ethereum smart contracts) ([[sources/cfo-wav]], [[sources/smarter-money-19oct16-mp3]], [[sources/gt-mel-wav]], [[sources/trench-talk-ep57-mark-pesce-mp3]], [[sources/cfo-series-melbourne-while-you-were-out-12nov20]]).

The 2021 framing is the most developed: smart contracts are the mechanism by which **decentralized finance** lets "individuals manage investments and loans directly on their devices", and the endpoint is a "late 2020s landscape of financialization where code and value are inseparable", an ecosystem of **active assets** that "continuously seek to maximize their worth through smart contracts" — every piece of data and physical item wrapped in blockchain becomes an insurable, securitizable, tradable financial instrument ([[sources/post-classical-finance-cfo-series-sydney-11may21]]). The Queensland CFO talk (September 2020) adds the DeFi scale: Ethereum-based smart contracts "creating over-collateralized loan pools worth billions", forecast to "disintermediate traditional business banking" ([[sources/beyond-the-horizon-cfo-series-qld-10sep20-mp4]]). The cautionary constant is the DAO incident: the bug, the $50M, and the rollback are repeated in every talk that includes smart contracts — the corpus's standing warning that code-governed finance is not yet trustworthy ([[sources/smarter-money-19oct16-mp3]], [[sources/gt-mel-wav]]).

## Debates / Open Questions


- **DAO: bug or design?** The corpus repeats the DAO story (bug, $50M, rollback) but never argues whether the rollback was the right call — it is held as a cautionary tale, not a settled judgment.
- **Are smart contracts the technology or the application?** The corpus treats them as the layer on which DeFi and the financialized future rest, but the underlying blockchain page holds the mechanism. The two pages are complementary, not competing.

# Libra

## TL;DR

Facebook's 2019 stablecoin that, in the corpus, functions less as a product than as the triggering event for the global central-bank digital-currency race.

## Overview

Libra (Facebook's proposed cryptocurrency, announced June 2019) enters the corpus in the days after its announcement — the RN Drive interview of 19 June 2019 is the closest thing to a first report ([[sources/rnd-20190619-1906-fb-libra-mp3]]). Pesce's consistent reading across the 2019–2020 talks: Libra is a **stablecoin pegged to a basket of currencies**, designed as a payments layer, not an investment vehicle — for the gap that smartphones created: roughly four billion people own mobile devices, but fewer than one billion have access to credit cards or traditional banking. Bitcoin was designed for that purpose but its slow settlement times make it unsuitable for everyday low-value transactions ([[sources/rnd-20190619-1906-fb-libra-mp3]], [[sources/asx-new-money-lbr-mp4]]). The partnerships (Visa, MasterCard, PayPal, Vodafone) are described as Facebook bringing its "900 pound gorilla" friends into the space; the two government worries are the erosion of the state's monopoly on money creation and the unprecedented data collection Libra would grant Facebook — a permanent, auditable trail of transactions, despite the white paper's pseudonymity features ([[sources/rnd-20190619-1906-fb-libra-mp3]]).

The corpus's larger claim, repeated through the year, is that **Libra's real effect is on the rest of the world**: despite regulatory pushback (Maxine Waters's request to pause; Visa and MasterCard withdrawals noted in the Citi talk), Libra is "unstoppable" because it targets the unbanked of the developing world where Facebook is the primary internet, and it forced the People's Bank of China to accelerate the Digital Yuan (DCEP) — 50 million users, full rollout aimed at the 2022 Winter Olympics, and a tool of state surveillance and financial soft power ([[sources/asx-wav]], [[sources/asx-new-money-hvec-480p-mp4]], [[sources/citi-wav]], [[sources/cfo-series-melbourne-while-you-were-out-12nov20]], [[sources/mark-pesce-interview-9dec20-mp3]]). At the Papua New Guinea conference the frame becomes "currency war": Libra, digital yuan and Binance's Venus as three forces PNG must plan against ([[sources/png2019-mkv]]); at the V20 summit the Libra announcement is what intensified the FATF compliance push ([[sources/v20-june-2019-opening-mts]]). By November 2020 Libra is background: PayPal's crypto walled garden and the RBA's wholesale currency on Ethereum smart contracts are the news, with Libra's announcement as the catalyst that started the clock ([[sources/cfo-series-melbourne-while-you-were-out-12nov20]]). The corpus never reports Libra's eventual renaming or abandonment.

## Debates / Open Questions


- **The date-metadata problem.** The asx-wav file carries a 2009 date but its content is unambiguously post-June 2019 (it discusses the Libra announcement). The file-system date is unreliable for this recording.
- **What happened to Libra?** The corpus stops treating Libra as news by late 2020; no source mentions the later renaming (Diem) or the project's collapse. The entity's endpoint in the corpus is 2020-12.

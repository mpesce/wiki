# Stanford

## TL;DR

The university that, in the corpus, is the open-courseware exemplar, the venue of one lecture, and — via Stanford Research Institute — the safety advisor that killed Sega's VR.

## Overview

Stanford (Stanford University; ASR/variant: "Stanford Research Institute") holds four roles in the corpus. First, the **open-courseware exemplar**: in the eLearning 2008 keynote and the TAFE "Collisions and Smash Repairs" talk, Stanford (with MIT and UNSW/Melbourne) is the proof case that "if Stanford, MIT, and UNSW can share their full courseware online without diluting the education, no institution can refute that example" — the value of education is in the experience, "you can't download an experience" ([[sources/elearning08-mark-pesce-20081104-mp3]], [[sources/collisions-and-smash-repairs-mp4]]). Second, **iTunes University**: in the three variants of "The New New", Stanford is the institution "sharing courses online via iTunes University and YouTube", "further corroding the structure of higher education" and "melting" under the pressure of a generation that shares rather than buys ([[sources/the-new-new-mp3]], [[sources/the-new-new-m4v]], [[sources/the-new-new-mov]]). Third, **the venue**: "Terror and Transhumanism" is a lecture Pesce delivered to **Stanford University students**, a critical deprogramming session following visits by transhumanist figures Eric Drexler and John Smart — transhumanism as a religious belief system with unquestioned dogmas, analyzed through McLuhan's ingression (intelligence into matter) and Jung's shadow, closing with the critique of the extropian upload as "physically impossible and psychologically unsound" ([[sources/terror-and-transhumanism-mp3]]). Fourth, **Stanford Research Institute**: in the 730 report on 3D TV safety (June 2010), Pesce recalls that **SRI advised Sega that the VR product was unsafe for children** and recommended limiting adult exposure to an hour a day, "leading Sega to kill the product despite significant marketing efforts" — the corpus's origin account for the Sega VR cancellation ([[sources/7-30report-3dtv-3jun10-m4v]], [[sources/7-30report-3dtv-3jun10-m4v-2]]).

## Debates / Open Questions


- **SRI vs Stanford.** The 730 report names "Stanford Research Institute" (SRI), which the corpus maps to this Stanford entity. The other Stanford appearances are the university proper. The page holds both under one entity because the corpus treats them as one.

- **Who killed Sega VR?** The corpus's account attributes the cancellation to SRI's safety advice (unsafe for children; one-hour adult limit). Other corpus sources attribute it to motion sickness / processing power / legal concerns. The corpus does not reconcile the two — both are held as Pesce's own shifting retellings.

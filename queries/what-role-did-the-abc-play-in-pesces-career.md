# What role did the Australian Broadcasting Corporation (ABC) play in Pesce's career and thinking?

## Answer
The ABC gave Pesce his most visible public platform: The New Inventors. He served as a panelist on the long-running ABC TV program, and the archive preserves dozens of full episodes from 2007–2009 in which the panel judges inventions from around Australia — for example [[sources/the-new-inventors-20070919200000-mp4]] and [[sources/the-new-inventors-20090930200000-mp4]]. The corpus contains 93 New Inventors episode recordings, and the program followed him into other work: in [[sources/telephone-repair-handbook-december-2005-mp3]] he opens a research talk by citing a prediction he made on the New Inventors panel — that within five years people would use their mobile phones ten times more — and both [[sources/mark-pesce-wtd1-28jun21-wav]] and [[sources/uq-wav]] introduce him by that panel role, indicating it remained a signature credential long after the show.

ABC radio made him a regular expert commentator across at least a decade of programs. On Triple J he explained BitTorrent and digital distribution to a youth audience ([[sources/abc-jjj-14mar06-interview-mp3]]) and returned for the network's Future Week to discuss mobile technology, ISP-level filtering and the NBN ([[sources/jjj-hack-future-3dec08-mp3]]). On Radio National he discussed anonymity and hacktivism on Common Knowledge ([[sources/rn-common-knowledge-19feb12-mp3]]) and game narrative on The Book Show ([[sources/4-03-the-book-show-23-june-2006-mp3]]); RN Drive carried his analysis of the Cambridge Analytica scandal and Facebook's Libra ([[sources/rnd-20180319-1825-mp3]], [[sources/rnd-20190619-1906-fb-libra-mp3]]); and he fronted a Nightlife panel on Twitter ([[sources/nightlife-twitter-11mar09-mp3]]) and ABC TV's The Drum during the WikiLeaks controversy ([[sources/the-drum-youtube-m4v]]).

The relationship was more than promotional: the ABC functioned as both stage and test-bed for his arguments about broadcasting's own future. His 2020 presentation to ABC staff, [[sources/abc-14jul20-video-first-mp4]], tells the national broadcaster directly that hypercasting — YouTube, livestreaming, TikTok, and social platforms as social learning systems — has outpaced traditional broadcasters. On ABC shows he repeatedly made the case that the media institutions hosting him were being transformed: on [[sources/jjj-hack-future-3dec08-mp3]] he criticised ISP-level filtering and NBN costings, and on [[sources/01-twitter-and-the-media-m4a]] he argued Twitter was breaking news before the broadcasters could. The relationship ran the other way too — ABC programming surfaced inside his own work, as when he announced ABC's Background Briefing among the nominees on his Next Billion Seconds podcast ([[sources/pesce-best-current-affairs-podcast-mp4]]).

The overall pattern is that the ABC was Pesce's longest-running institutional partnership and public amplifier — 65 sources in the corpus reference the organisation — while he simultaneously argued, on the ABC's own air, that the broadcast model the ABC embodied was being displaced by hyperdistribution.

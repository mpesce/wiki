# What is Pesce's history with and argument about podcasting?

## Answer
Pesce is both a podcasting practitioner and one of the early theorists of why podcasting matters. His theoretical case flows straight out of his hyperdistribution work: in a serialized audio lecture recorded for download, he traced how Napster's 1999 "superdistribution" and BitTorrent's 2002 "hyperdistribution" removed the gatekeepers between creators and audiences, letting one person reach the world at no cost [[sources/4-02-hyperdistribution-mp3]]. In "Welcome to the Swarm" he argued that peer-to-peer distribution ends centralized media control and empowers amateur creators who no longer need mass-market tastes, while warning that the same personal devices (the iPod era) can trap listeners in self-created sensory bubbles [[sources/hyperpeople-mp3]]. He later named podcasting as one of four simultaneous revolutions — alongside YouTube, Wikipedia, and the mobile iPhone — that allowed low-cost content distribution to wrench control away from institutions [[sources/collisions-and-smash-repairs-mp4]], and told Triple J in 2006 that the real disruption was audiences becoming creators themselves [[sources/abc-jjj-14mar06-interview-mp3]].

As a practitioner, Pesce has spent two decades on both sides of the microphone. He has been a frequent podcast guest — discussing the future of the web on The Scoop [[sources/thescoop-3dec09-mp3]], innovation and remote work on the Alchemist podcast [[sources/mark-pesce-alchemist-23jun21-wav]], disruption for Trench Talk [[sources/trench-talk-ep57-mark-pesce-mp3]], and Delta-variant science through the pandemic on the Chaser podcast [[sources/chaser-podcast-11jul21-wav]] [[sources/mark-chaser-podcast-21jul21-wav]]. He has also hosted: The Next Billion Seconds, produced on Podcast One Australia, where he interviewed guests such as Genevieve Bell — with visible craft, briefing her beforehand to avoid academic jargon and shaping the episode's structure [[sources/genevieve-bell-mp3]] — and where he presented the Australian Podcast Awards' best current affairs category, praising the winner's "complex storytelling and beautiful sound design" [[sources/pesce-best-current-affairs-podcast-mp4]].

The through-line is that Pesce treats podcasting as the audio expression of his core thesis: when distribution is free and audiences swarm, the professional advantage shifts from controlling channels to earning attention and trust — whether as a host curating conversations [[sources/sca-wav]] or as a theorist explaining why the swarm rewards creators who serve communities rather than gatekeepers [[sources/hyperpeople-mp3]] [[sources/4-02-hyperdistribution-mp3]].

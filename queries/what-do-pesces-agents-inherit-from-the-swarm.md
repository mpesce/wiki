# What do Pesce's AI agents inherit from his "swarm" and "hyperpeople" theses?

## Answer
The 2004–2005 "swarm" thesis and the 2025–2026 agent thesis are one continuous argument: in both, the scarce, intelligent layer is *not* the individual node but the pattern of the network — and both require a governance vocabulary to keep that layer trustworthy.

The swarm thesis. In the "Welcome to the Swarm" talks ([[sources/hyperpeople-mp3]] and [[sources/hyperpeople-mobile-mp4]], 2004) he names the convergence: peer-to-peer file sharing, social networks, and personal media culture, which he calls *hyperpeople*. The economics were already visible in 1999: [[sources/1-02-libraries-in-the-age-of-infinite-media-mp3]] explains BitTorrent's efficiency — files split into pieces shared by a *swarm* of users, devastating the broadcast model of distribution. By 2005 ([[sources/the-swarm-parts-i-ii-and-iii-mp3]]) the conclusion is in: the channel is flattened, money gives way to attention as the primary currency, and audiences depend on *social networks* to filter the noise. The human-use-of-human-networks talks ([[sources/the-human-use-of-human-networks-mp3]], from his AFTRS role) preview the *Hyper People* book, and the PDF-era talk ([[sources/pdf-pesce-mov]]) extends the thesis into politics ("hyper politics and hyper people"). The swarm's key claim: intelligence and curation are emergent properties of the connected whole, not of any one participant.

The agent thesis. Twenty years later the same structure reappears with the nodes replaced by models. An agent, in [[sources/f1-agent-definition-mp4]] (2026), is "an AI plus three things": memory (a scratchpad), tools (file, email, spreadsheet I/O), and goal logic — "the part that is like the Terminator" — and the first real agents appeared in 2023. Capability is tracked as the *task horizon* — how long an agent can go before it falls over — with METR's roughly seven-month doubling cadence and the November 2025 threshold where all three frontier labs shipped major releases at once ([[sources/f3a-task-horizon-mp4]]). But he refuses the absolute: *relative* superintelligence is the working concept — a system brighter than you and brighter than the people who could judge it, in its domain ([[sources/d1-brighter-than-you-mp4]], [[sources/f8-relative-superintelligence-mp4]] — absolute superintelligence is, in Australian terms, "a furphy").

What is inherited is the governance layer. In the swarm era his answer to emergent collective behavior was decentralization and curation. In the agent era it is *verification*: formal verification from pacemakers and aircrafts ([[sources/d6-pacemakers-mp4]]), the scramble for formal methods, the cost of not being thorough ([[sources/d17-cost-of-not-being-thorough-mp4]], [[sources/d3-scramble-for-formal-methods-mp4]]). His book *AI in Practice* is half governance, because what wasn't recognized three years earlier is now the whole conversation ([[sources/d14-ai-in-practice-governance-mp4]]). Two trust regimes emerge for "mythos-class" systems ([[sources/f14-delphic-oracle-mp4]]): trust only what is formally provable, or go back to an oracle-like Delphic mode. The human stays at the *set point*: systems may detect that the world has changed but may not alter their own behavior — a human must inspect, interrogate, verify, and sign the change ([[sources/f16-set-point-human-signature-mp4]]). And the economics of trust get a market: the telematics-insurance analogy — just as car insurers price risk from vehicle data, agent insurers will price risk from inspectable, verifiable systems, spawning an industry of verification design ([[sources/f17-f19-insurance-thesis-combo-mp4]]). Most people will be *on* the loop, not in it ([[sources/f19-room-on-the-loop-mp4]]), and the failure mode is mundane rather than apocalyptic: Waze once gave him directions that worked in aggregate and created a jam — "This is how Skynet wipes us out" ([[sources/d12-waze-skynet-mp4]]).

The thread is explicit in the corpus's own symbols: the 1999 Burning Man event he helped build was the *Speaker's Corner at the Oracle at Arrowhead* ([[sources/3-01-the-audio-tour-mp3]]), and the 2026 agent talk proposes a *Delphic oracle* as one of two trust regimes ([[sources/f14-delphic-oracle-mp4]]). The swarm was the network's intelligence; the agent is the machine's intelligence; the oracle is how both get trusted.

# What did Pesce get right and wrong about the 2010s?

## Answer
This page is an evaluative retrospective, drawn from the corpus's own dated record: predictions made 2005–2019, checked against what the later recordings show actually happened.

**What he got right.** The central 2005 call — that hyperdistribution flattens the channels, that "the primary currency of the 21st century has shifted from money to attention," and that blogs and social networks are replacing traditional news media ([[sources/the-swarm-parts-i-ii-and-iii-mp3]]) — proved to be the decade's actual economic center. The corpus's later recordings keep confirming it: BitTorrent buzz converting into record ABC ratings in 2006 ([[sources/abc-jjj-14mar06-interview-mp3]]), news sites restructuring their link economics for ad attention by 2010 ([[sources/iif-2010-03-mark-pesce-mp3]]), the TikTok attention platform with its economics and geopolitics intertwined by 2019 ([[sources/ppm-2019-10-21-tik-tok-mp3]]). His platform-economics call is in [[sources/hypereconomics-flv]] (2011): the 1995–2026 window as a pivotal era because connectivity is eliminating market frictions — Kerala fishermen's phones, Kenyan SMS market data, Kogan and Uber. The decade's outcome — platform companies eating the taxi, retail and airline industries — matches. His Facebook forecast ([[sources/mark-pesce-futurist-the-last-days-of-reality-mp4]], 2017): the 2011 shift to curation was behavioral modification, not filtering — the 2015 mood-alteration reveal and the 2017 *Australian* targeting report are cited as evidence, and the corpus's 2020 Social Dilemma discussion ([[sources/weekdays-28sep20-the-social-dilemma-mp3]]) confirms the engagement-AI mechanism he described. And the quiet one: in [[sources/four-day-week-mp4]] (2019) he argues Australians were *already* working the four-day week — 40 hours in 1984, 34 in 2019, 33 post-pandemic — a data-driven claim the later corpus never contradicts.

**What he got wrong or missed.** His mid-2020s AR-glasses forecast, made in [[sources/accanet-12sep18-mp3]] (September 2018, building on HoloLens-era Microsoft Research work and videogrammetry), does not survive the corpus's later record: by 2023–2026 his own talks ([[sources/cybersamhain-to-chatgpt-1080p-mp4]], [[sources/thefuturists-mp4]]) center on ChatGPT and AI agents, with AR absent — the immersive-media bet lost the decade's narrative to large language models. The 2011 bandwidth forecast in the same recording (60 Mbps per household by 2020, NBN "may be insufficient") has no confirming outcome in the corpus; the later talks simply stop discussing Australian broadband. And the 2005 "blogs replace news media" call was directionally right but overstated: by 2008 he was already asking how *professional* journalists differentiate from "a potential billion users" ([[sources/01-twitter-and-the-media-m4a]]) — the decade's outcome was newsrooms shrinking and re-differentiating, not disappearing.

**The pivot the 2010s produced.** The decade ends in the corpus with his own consultancy (Wisely AI) advising organizations to implement AI "safely," his warning that companies adopt AI on hype rather than workflow understanding, and his "vibe coding" critique of AI-generated code ([[sources/thefuturists-mp4]], 2023). The 2010s attention-economy thesis did not die — it became the substrate on which the 2025–2026 agent era landed, with governance (verification, oracles, insurance) replacing distribution as the scarce skill. That is the retrospective the corpus itself supports: the 2010s predictions about *distribution* held; the ones about *form factors* (glasses, bandwidth) did not.

# Weekdays 28Sep20 The Social Dilemma.mp3

## Summary

This recording is a radio interview from the ABC, dated September 28, 2020, featuring host Josh and guest Mark Pesci, a futurist and technologist. The discussion centers on the Netflix documentary The Social Dilemma, which features interviews with former Facebook and Google employees, including Tristan Harris, who discuss the ethical implications of social media algorithms. Pesci explains that these platforms use artificial intelligence to track user behavior, such as dwell time and emotional reactions, to build predictive models of individual users. The primary goal of these systems is to maximize engagement and time on site by serving content that triggers strong reactions, effectively creating personalized feedback loops that can be addictive. Pesci argues that this model differs fundamentally from traditional media because it tailors content to individual weaknesses rather than strengths, leading to the creation of information silos or rabbit holes. He notes that this can radicalize users into extreme political or conspiracy theories, such as QAnon or anti-vaxxer movements, by continuously feeding them similar content. The conversation addresses listener concerns about whether companies are listening to voice conversations. While Pesci states that direct voice recognition is not currently a standard practice, he explains that the algorithms are sophisticated enough to infer interests from background data and user interactions, making it appear as though the platform is eavesdropping. He also discusses the impact on adolescents, noting that the constant social comparison and status anxiety exacerbated by social media negatively affect concentration and mental health in young people whose social skills are still developing. The segment concludes with a discussion on the societal consequences of fragmented realities, where different groups consume different versions of the truth, potentially leading to civil unrest. Pesci suggests that while individual users can mitigate some effects by turning off notifications and avoiding algorithmic recommendations, systemic change requires legislation to regulate the use of artificial intelligence for mass profiling. He emphasizes that the current business model, which prioritizes engagement over user well-being, has unintended side effects that threaten social cohesion. The interview ends with Pesci advising listeners to critically evaluate their reliance on these platforms and to advocate for stricter regulatory frameworks to protect public discourse.

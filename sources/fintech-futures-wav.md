# Fintech Futures.wav

## Summary

This recording is a conference presentation delivered by Mark Pesce, a technology commentator and former Apple and Mozilla engineer, focusing on the rise of financial technology, or FinTech. The talk, which appears to be part of a larger event in Australia given references to local institutions, outlines the shifting power dynamics between traditional banks and technology companies. Pesce begins by discussing the Australian "big four" banks' recent petition to the ACCC to restrict mobile payment services like Apple Pay, Android Pay, and Samsung Pay. He argues that the banks are attempting to form a monopoly to protect their revenue streams from the small transaction fees taken by smartphone processors, despite the fact that Apple’s profits exceed the combined profits of the major banks. The speaker contrasts this resistance with the success of mobile money in developing nations, citing Kenya’s M-Pesa as a prime example of how simple SMS-based transfers transformed the economy by removing friction from cash transactions. He highlights the rapid adoption of smartphones, noting that 85% of Australians own one and predicting that 80% of global adults will by the end of the decade. This ubiquity allows for the emergence of FinTech startups that offer specialized banking services, such as loans, investments, and remittances, often more efficiently than traditional institutions. Pesce explains that while banks view these startups as threats, many are actually becoming partners or acquisition targets to help banks streamline operations and comply with regulations like Know Your Customer (KYC). Pesce then details three specific startups he mentors through the Stone & Chalk accelerator in Sydney. EasyShare provides a payments platform for share-house residents to manage bills and rent seamlessly. Spriggy offers a debit card and app for children to learn financial management under parental control. Full Profile uses distributed ledger technology, or blockchain, to secure payment for grain farmers, ensuring they are paid at every step of the supply chain to eliminate counterparty risk. The talk concludes with an explanation of blockchain technology as a trusted, shared register of data that can be used for identity verification, securitizing loyalty points, and creating micro-insurance pools.

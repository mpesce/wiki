# Transition without wrecking economy.mp4

## Summary

This recording appears to be a segment from a talk or lecture by Mark Pesce, focusing on the intersection of environmental sustainability and economic viability. The speaker addresses the challenge of implementing green technologies without disrupting existing economic structures or civilization. He highlights the significant headwinds faced by businesses across various industries as they respond to increasing pressure from customers who demand environmentally responsible products. To illustrate this dynamic, Pesce uses the example of an appliance manufacturer developing a new electric dishwasher. While the product represents a step toward the electrification of homes, the speaker notes that consumers are increasingly reluctant to purchase such items if they are not manufactured using sustainable materials. Specifically, he points out that customers may refuse to buy the dishwasher if it is not made with green steel, as they do not want their purchase to contribute to environmental damage. This example underscores the broader trend where consumer expectations now include the entire supply chain and material sourcing, not just the end product's energy efficiency. The core message is that businesses must navigate these complex demands to remain successful in a market where environmental impact is a primary purchasing criterion.

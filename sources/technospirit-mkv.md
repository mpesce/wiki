# technospirit.mkv

## Summary

This recording is a panel discussion hosted by Marina, featuring speakers Ines, Ingrid Lafleur, and Mark Pesce, exploring the intersection of spirituality, technology, and social justice. The conversation begins with introductions, where Lafleur identifies herself as an Afrofuture theorist and visiting fellow at the Modern Ancient Brown Foundation in Detroit, while Pesce, speaking from Sydney, acknowledges the traditional lands of the local Indigenous people. The central theme is the rejection of the opposition between technology and spirituality, framing them instead as complementary forces. Pesce draws on the cybernetics of Norbert Wiener and the structural coupling concepts of Maturana and Varela to argue that human-machine interactions are inherently relational and can be blessed through intentional ritual. He recounts performing a Wiccan ritual in 1994 to consecrate the early web, suggesting that modern users should similarly "bless" their interactions with artificial intelligence to maintain a sacred framing. The panelists discuss the commoditization of spiritual practices in digital spaces, noting the tension between transactional offerings in West African religions and the opaque data harvesting of social media platforms. Lafleur highlights the importance of diverse creators in technology to mitigate bias, citing the failure of facial recognition systems to accurately identify Black women as a critical example of systemic harm. She introduces her "Dinknesh method," a four-phase foresight framework that integrates identity, systemic analysis, ancestral spirituality, and emerging technology to create culturally informed futures. The group also addresses surveillance, agreeing that while spirituality has historically helped communities evade state control, it can also make individuals more visible to peer-based monitoring. In the closing segment, the speakers emphasize agency and the need for continuous ethical design rather than one-off compliance. Pesce compares technology to fire, requiring constant attention and feedback loops to prevent harm. The discussion concludes with a call for digital literacy and the integration of diverse spiritual traditions into tech development to ensure that emerging technologies serve human flourishing rather than control. The host thanks the participants and the supporting organizations, Feminist Internet and CCI, for facilitating the dialogue.

# A Series of Tubes WPC 11Sep19.mp3

## Summary

This recording is the opening keynote address delivered by futurist Mark Pesce at the 12th World Plumbing Congress in Melbourne. Pesce, who identifies himself as a media and technology commentator rather than a plumbing professional, frames his talk around the concept that the future is already present but unevenly distributed. He argues that futurists do not predict the future but analyze current trends to understand likely outcomes, using the Consumer Electronics Show in Las Vegas as a primary source for identifying emerging technologies. The main topic of the speech is the integration of artificial intelligence and the Internet of Things into the plumbing and construction trades. Pesce highlights the Kohler Connect smart faucet as a case study, explaining that its value lies not in the hardware, but in its connection to cloud-based voice assistants and machine learning systems. He details how these systems require billions of data points to learn and improve, effectively connecting the faucet to a global network of information. He warns that this connectivity introduces new security risks, such as the potential for hackers to manipulate water systems, necessitating the development of antivirus protections for plumbing infrastructure. Pesce then shifts focus to project management and workforce participation, citing the Australian startup Takeoffs, which uses AI and human oversight to automate bill of materials generation from construction plans. He uses the example of Tesla’s initial failure with fully robotic assembly lines to illustrate that automation works best when humans and machines are paired, with each handling tasks where they excel. Finally, he argues that the plumbing profession’s traditional apprenticeship model is a significant advantage in an era of accelerating technological change. He predicts that by 2030, most organizations will shift from static job roles to continuous apprenticeship models, similar to the plumbing trade, to keep pace with new tools. He encourages plumbers to leverage their expertise in experiential learning to help other industries adapt, emphasizing that the future of work will rely on continuous, peer-to-peer learning.

# A4_repricing-cognition.mp4

## Summary

This recording is a segment from a talk or lecture by Mark Pesce, a media and technology commentator and former engineer at Apple and Mozilla. The specific date of the recording is not mentioned in the provided transcript. The main topic is the economic impact of artificial intelligence on the cost of cognitive labor. Pesce argues that cognition is being commoditized for the first time in history because machines can now perform cognitive tasks with sufficient quality to allow for pricing. He notes that while many observers predicted the cost of cognition would drop to zero, this has not occurred. Instead, the cost has been repriced. Pesce explains that even when organizations generate their own tokens or use them extensively, they still incur costs. Therefore, the cost of cognition remains a tangible expense rather than a free resource. He concludes the segment by stating that every organization must now evaluate all areas where they currently rely on human cognitive effort, implying that these areas need to be reassessed in light of the new, non-zero cost structure of machine-based cognition. The transcript ends abruptly as Pesce begins to list the specific places where human cognition is utilized.

# Open State.wav

## Summary

This recording captures a lunchtime talk by Mark Pesce, a futurist, inventor, and writer, delivered at an event in South Australia. The session is introduced by a moderator who outlines the agenda, which includes a discussion on future cities, IoT, and AI, with subsequent speakers including Robert Wickham from Salesforce. Pesce’s presentation focuses on demystifying machine learning, explicitly rejecting the notion that it is "magic pixie dust." He argues that machine learning is fundamentally about systems learning from their mistakes to improve performance toward a specific goal. To illustrate this, Pesce details the development of AlphaGo, explaining how the program learned to play the ancient Chinese game of Go by analyzing its own errors and playing against itself until it defeated the world’s top human player. He then applies this concept to Facebook, describing how the platform uses machine learning to curate news feeds based on user engagement data, effectively feeding mistakes back into the system to maximize user addiction and advertising revenue. Pesce transitions to the concept of the "smart city," using a Sydney-based startup called Premonition as a case study. Premonition uses machine learning to optimize vehicle fleet routes by analyzing driver data, traffic, and weather, achieving efficiency gains of 6 to 20 percent. He posits that this principle of extracting efficiencies will expand across the economy, raising the critical question of what goals society chooses to optimize for. The talk concludes with a focus on energy grids, specifically in South Australia. Pesce envisions a future where the power grid operates like a "Premonition for power," using machine learning to balance decentralized generation, storage, and consumption in real-time. He warns that granting such systems autonomy creates a zero-sum dynamic where individual control is traded for collective stability. He argues that if these systems are perceived as restricting rather than encouraging, they may be viewed as authoritarian, turning the smart city into a "smart prison." Pesce emphasizes that while futurists do not predict the future, the winds are blowing toward pervasive autonomy, and society must now decide what kind of city and world it wants to build before these technologies are broadly applied.

# lms_20100531_0905 (1).mp3

## Summary

This recording is a segment from the radio program Life Matters, featuring an interview with social network strategist Laurel Papworth and digital cultures researcher Mark Pesce. The discussion centers on the "Quick Campaign," a protest movement urging users to quit Facebook due to privacy concerns and the platform's opaque handling of user data. Mark Pesce, who recently deleted his account, argues that Facebook has become an untrustworthy custodian of the social graph, which he describes as a valuable personal asset that has been exposed to the real world. He highlights the lack of negotiation in the user agreement, noting that Facebook dictates terms unilaterally and has been opaque about its data practices. Pesce also points out that Facebook is backed by Peter Thiel and the CIA’s investment arm, In-Q-Tel, raising further questions about data security and surveillance. Laurel Papworth, a long-time Facebook user, offers a contrasting perspective, stating that she does not intend to quit the platform. She argues that targeted advertising, while controversial, provides contextually relevant information that is often more useful than generic broadcast ads. Papworth defends Facebook’s decision to retain deleted user data for a period, citing the need to track predators and organized crime, and asserts that she trusts the company more than law enforcement agencies due to the latter's potential for overreach. She also addresses the complexity of privacy settings, explaining that while the controls are technically simple to adjust, users often struggle to navigate the menus. The conversation concludes with a debate on the future of social networking. Pesce advocates for decentralized alternatives to Facebook’s centralized model, warning that centralization creates vulnerabilities for hackers and governments.

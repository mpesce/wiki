# The New Inventors-20070822200000.mp4

## Summary

This recording is an episode of the Australian television program The New Inventors, hosted by James O'Loughlin. The broadcast features a panel of experts, including engineering professor Veena Sahajwala, agricultural scientist Chris Russell, and designer Sally Deminks, who evaluate three distinct inventions. The primary focus of the segment is the Conehead liner, a shock-absorbing helmet technology developed by Don Morgan from Brisbane. Morgan explains that one in four motorcycle or bicycle accidents results in death or brain damage, often because standard single-density foam liners fail to absorb impact effectively. His innovation embeds low-density cone-shaped foam structures into a high-density lining; these cones compress readily upon initial impact and then harden to absorb force more effectively. The panel discusses the engineering challenges of fusing the two foam densities and notes that the product has passed European double-impact standards. Following the technical presentation, the panel debates the relative merits of the three inventions: the Conehead liner, the Lil' Jim exercise device, and the Aqua Genie fish tank cleaner. While Sally Deminks initially favors the Aqua Genie for its clever application of existing reverse-flushing technology and strong marketability to hobbyist fish owners, and Chris Russell raises concerns about the high cost of the Conehead liner, the group ultimately converges on the helmet technology. Veena Sahajwala argues that the Conehead represents a revolutionary shift in safety standards, comparing the stagnation of helmet design to the historical addition of crumple zones in automobiles. The panel unanimously selects the Conehead liner as the winner of the episode, with Morgan becoming a candidate for the annual Inventor of the Year award. The episode concludes with a prompt for viewers to vote for the People's Choice Award

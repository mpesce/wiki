# The New Inventors-20070829200000.mp4

## Summary

This recording is an episode of the Australian television program The New Inventors, hosted by Mark Pesce. The show features three inventors presenting their creations to a panel of judges consisting of agricultural scientist Chris Russell, interior designer Alison Page, and engineer James Bradfield Moody. The episode begins with Tony Kennedy from Cundaberg, New South Wales, who presents the AJ's Beetle Eater, a non-chemical trap designed to protect beehives from small hive beetles. The device uses vegetable oil to trap the beetles, which are attracted to dark crevices, and Kennedy demonstrates its simple installation and maintenance. The second segment features Mark Bridgeman and Ian Jackson, who introduce the Phase Changer, a microprocessor-controlled rotary phase converter. This invention converts single-phase electricity into stable three-phase power for rural areas where three-phase supply is unavailable. The judges discuss the device's improved voltage stability, energy efficiency, and safety diagnostics compared to traditional converters. The final inventor is Roy McCutchenson from Perth, who presents Checkmate, a glow-in-the-dark visual aid for truck drivers to verify that wheel nuts are secure. The device clamps onto the nuts and indicates if they have loosened, addressing a critical safety issue for heavy vehicles. The judges deliberate on the merits of each invention, debating criteria such as design, marketability, and safety. James Bradfield Moody initially favors the Phase Changer for its technical innovation, while Chris Russell supports the Beetle Eater for its simplicity and biological approach. Ultimately, Chris Russell selects the AJ's Beetle Eater as the winner of the night, with Tony Kennedy advancing to the competition for Inventor of the Year. The episode concludes with a prompt for viewers to vote for the People's Choice Award and a preview of upcoming segments, including a discussion on vampire bats and stroke treatment.

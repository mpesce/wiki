# Techno-Pagans at the End of History 6(Esalen).mp3

## Summary

Part of 'Techno-Pagans at the End of History' (Esalen Institute, 2001) — a multi-day participatory weekend co-hosted by Mark Pesce, Douglas Rushkoff, and Erik Davis. The group moves between long-form conversation and open discussion with the audience, spanning virtual reality, psychedelics, nanotechnology, religion, and the end of history. (Episode 6.) Mark continues the nanotechnology thread: with the nano-manipulator you can 'push atoms into place' and 'watch the quantum tunneling as the atoms leapt out of place very suddenly' — 'already an extant device... the prototype for the systems that will follow in the next 14 years.' He then raises nanotechnology's largest discontent, 'the thing that keeps me worried at night and makes me afraid of the dark': the GRAY GOO PROBLEM. A 'nano-assembler' (a tiny machine with a tiny computer) has two instructions that matter: it eats (and 'is not all that picky') and it makes copies of itself. 'If such a device were released into the environment, it's believed the surface of the planet would be nothing but a sea of these nano-manipulators in 72 hours.' He notes the nanotechnologists are more frightened of it than he is ('which makes me happy'). He closes by plotting exponential curves (data compression, architecture density, population, energy release) and arguing that 'somewhere between 2010 and 2025 we possess all information, move at infinite speeds, release infinite amounts of energy, live forever' — and that 'the fulfillment of these things will occur in dimensions very difficult for us to grok or imagine.'

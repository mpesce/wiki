# Pesce Business On The Edge --Essential 19May21.mp4

## Summary

This recording is a professional futurist presentation delivered by a speaker to an audience at Essential, an Australian energy company, with Josh, a representative of the organization, facilitating interactive segments. The talk focuses on long-term trends affecting work, energy, and technology over the next 15 years, specifically tailored to Essential’s business context. The speaker introduces the Johari window framework to categorize global challenges, identifying China as a known known, climate change as a known unknown, the pandemic as an unknown known, and UFOs as a shifting unknown unknown. The core of the presentation argues that the pandemic has permanently altered the nature of work, creating three distinct worker classes: remote, office-bound, and hybrid. The speaker predicts a significant shift toward hybrid work, which will drive population migration from urban centers to regional and rural Australia, aided by new satellite broadband technologies like Starlink. This demographic shift is supported by statistics showing increased net migration to regional areas and a surge in mortgage applications. The speaker posits that this decentralization of the workforce will reshape the energy sector, moving the grid from a centralized model to a distributed, edge-centric system. The final section details the electrification of transport and its impact on the power grid. The speaker highlights the rapid adoption of electric vehicles, noting that by 2035, a majority of new cars in major markets will be electric. These vehicles, equipped with bidirectional charging, will act as massive distributed storage assets, fundamentally changing how energy is generated and consumed. The presentation concludes by drawing parallels between the historical evolution of computing from mainframes to the cloud and the future of electricity, predicting a hybrid grid where small-scale generation and storage at the edge become as significant as centralized power plants. The speaker ends with a prompt for the audience to consider how their business model must adapt to becoming a buyer from many small generators rather than a seller to many customers.

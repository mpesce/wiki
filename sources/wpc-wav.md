# WPC.wav

## Summary

This recording captures the opening keynote address by Mark Pesce at the 12th World Plumbing Congress in Melbourne. Pesce, introduced as a futurist, inventor, and co-inventor of VRML, frames his talk around the concept that the future is already present but unevenly distributed. He begins by demonstrating the Kohler Connect smart faucet, a device featured at the Consumer Electronics Show, to illustrate how plumbing is becoming integrated with the internet and artificial intelligence. He explains that voice commands are processed via cloud computing, effectively connecting the faucet to a vast network of data and AI systems. Pesce highlights the security risks of such connected systems, noting that hackers could potentially weaponize plumbing infrastructure, and argues that the industry must develop antivirus-like protections for these new digital threats. The speaker then shifts focus to project management and the changing nature of work. He introduces Takeoffs, a Brisbane-based startup that uses AI and human oversight to automate the creation of bills of materials from construction plans. Using the Tesla Model 3 production line as a cautionary tale, Pesce argues that automation does not replace humans but rather changes their roles. He posits that the future of construction involves a partnership between humans and machines, where AI handles detailed data processing while humans provide sanity checks and creative oversight. He predicts that within the next thirty years, apps will use machine vision to scan materials, optimize layouts, and perform real-time quality inspections on job sites. In the final segment, Pesce discusses the future of professional participation, arguing that the plumbing profession’s traditional apprenticeship model is a significant advantage in an era of accelerating technological change. He suggests that most organizations will need to adopt similar continuous learning structures to keep pace with new tools. He contrasts this with current corporate practices, such as AT&T’s upskilling programs versus Telstra’s layoffs, and urges plumbers to leverage their expertise in mentorship to advise other industries. The talk concludes with a Q&A session where Pesce addresses questions regarding broadband infrastructure, the role of hydrogen in energy, and the importance of measurement in creating a data-driven civilization.

# 01 Twitter and the Media.m4a

## Summary

This recording is a talk by Mark Pesce, a media and technology commentator, discussing the transformative impact of Twitter on journalism and media consumption. The presentation begins with a critique of a quote by Bob Woodward, who described social media as noise and a diversion. Pesce counters this by arguing that traditional media has long served powerful interests and that Twitter has already proven its value in breaking news. He cites the May 12, 2008, Sichuan earthquake as a primary example, noting that Twitter provided real-time information and images from the disaster zone approximately thirty minutes before major news services could report on it. He also references the 2007 California wildfires as an earlier instance where Twitter served as a powerful organizational and media-sharing tool. Pesce argues that the rapid growth of Twitter, which he notes had reached 18 million users at the time of the talk, signals the impending collapse of traditional wire services such as the AP and Reuters. He posits that these agencies were built for an era of information scarcity, whereas the current era is defined by hyper-connectivity and an abundance of raw reportage. Consequently, newspapers that rely heavily on wire service content are building their foundations on sand. He raises the difficult question of how professional journalists will differentiate themselves from a potential billion users who are constantly reporting events in their own voices, suggesting that the traditional model of critical distancing is being replaced by a new form of participatory media. The speaker then broadens his focus to television, explaining how Twitter has become an interactive back channel that transforms passive viewing into a synchronous shared event.

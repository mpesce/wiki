# The New Inventors-20090325200000.mp4

## Summary

This recording is an episode of the Australian television program The New Inventors, broadcast on ABC One. The show features three teams presenting their inventions to a panel of judges, including engineer James Bradfield Moody, inventor Sally Domingues, and Professor Fiona Wood, the inventor of spray-on skin. The host introduces the contestants and guides the presentations, which cover a variety of practical and creative solutions. The first presentation is from Richard and Simon Noble, who demonstrate a hydraulic attachment for farm tractors designed to thin trees in plantations. Their device, called the gripper snipper, allows operators to cut, lift, and place trees safely from the tractor seat, replacing dangerous chainsaw work. They explain that the unit costs between twenty and twenty-five thousand dollars and is intended for small-scale forestry contractors. The judges discuss the device's safety benefits and its maneuverability, noting that it can be paired with a chipper to remove debris from the forest. The second invention is Intelligent Plastic, developed by Dr. Paul Brockwell and Dr. Bob Hollow. This technology uses a chemistry-based label that measures the freshness of food by detecting chemical changes as it deteriorates, displaying a score from zero to one00. The team argues that this removes guesswork from the food supply chain, benefiting suppliers, retailers, and consumers by reducing waste and ensuring quality. The judges praise the originality of the smart material and its broad market potential, with Fiona Wood highlighting the significant need for better food safety monitoring. The final presentation is from Michael and Rory, who introduce Cat Attack, a patch applied to the back of a cricket bat. The device is designed to reduce vibration, thereby increasing the sweet spot and potentially reducing arm strain for players. They note the product costs around forty-five to fifty dollars and has received positive feedback from players, though its legality in professional matches remains uncertain. The judges question whether the performance improvement is physical or psychological, suggesting a placebo test. Ultimately, the panel unanimously selects Intelligent Plastic as the winner of the episode, citing its high potential and widespread need, while the audience is invited to vote for the People's Choice Award.

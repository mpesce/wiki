# The New Inventors-20070919200000.mp4

## Summary

This recording is an episode of the television program The New Inventors, hosted by James O'Loughlin. The show features three inventors presenting their creations to a panel of judges consisting of agricultural scientist Chris Russell, engineering professor Veena Sahajwala, and interior designer Alison Page. The first invention presented is the Orbi One Oven by Alan Jones from Lithgow, New South Wales. Jones argues that traditional rectangular ovens create uneven heat distribution, leading to issues like sunken cakes. His dome-shaped gas oven uses a central burner and removable hot plates made of different materials to circulate heat efficiently. The judges discuss the aerodynamic benefits of the dome shape and the potential for the design to become a sculptural kitchen appliance, though they request comparative efficiency data. The second invention is the Unreal Ice sculpture by Tim Gersonday from Highland Park, Queensland. Gersonday, a former ice carver, created a reusable acrylic resin shell filled with a dense brine solution. This device mimics the appearance of real ice, including condensation effects, but does not melt or require drip trays. It is designed to keep food and drinks cold for four to five hours, targeting markets such as caterers, cruise ships, and large buffets where hand-carved ice is impractical. The judges note the practical utility of the device for food safety but debate the aesthetic appeal of artificial ice compared to the romance of traditional carving. The final invention is the Easy Vein device by Dr. Dane Horsfall. This small plastic tool is designed to stabilize a patient's vein during the insertion of an intravenous cannula, a procedure that fails on the first attempt approximately one in five times. The device adheres to the skin, straddles the vein, and guides the needle while moving the doctor's thumb out of the way to allow for a direct approach. Horsfall demonstrates the device on a volunteer, explaining that it reduces patient stress and bruising. The judges highlight the significant need for such a solution in hospital settings, noting the high volume of cannulation procedures and the potential for cost reduction in mass production. The judges ultimately select the Easy Vein as the winner of the night, citing its practical necessity and innovative solution

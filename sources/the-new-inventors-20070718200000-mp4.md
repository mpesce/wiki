# The New Inventors-20070718200000.mp4

## Summary

This recording is a segment from the Australian Broadcasting Corporation television program The New Inventors, featuring a panel of judges including engineer James Bradfield Moody, science journalist Bernie Hobbs, and inventor Sally Deminks. The episode focuses on practical innovations, beginning with a discussion on water conservation. The first invention presented is Aquatain, a silicone-based liquid developed by an inventor named Graham. Aquatain is poured onto the surface of farm dams to create a wafer-thin, insoluble film that prevents evaporation. The segment notes that up to 40 percent of water in farm dams can be lost to evaporation, and Aquatain is designed to mitigate this loss. The product is described as easily degradable into common soil silicates and capable of covering large bodies of water, such as a one-hectare dam, in minutes. Following the water conservation segment, the program introduces a new trap for octopus. Traditional methods involve attaching shelter pots to long lines, which requires thousands of pots for a commercial catch because octopus are clever and escape when they feel the line being pulled. The new invention, referred to as the octopus trigger pipe, uses an LED lure to attract the creature. The trap is designed so that only an octopus has the strength to pull the lure and trigger the trap door, allowing for targeted fishing that is safe for reefs and marine parks by avoiding the capture of other sea creatures like crayfish. The transcript concludes with a brief mention of the previous week’s panel pick, Keith Higgins and his solid-state lighting, and the beginning of a discussion on the people’s choice award, which included options such as the solid-state lighting and a gannet swing-away device.

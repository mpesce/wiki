# Last Days of Reality Toronto 17Oct17.mov

## Summary

This recording is a keynote address delivered by Mark Pesce, a media and technology commentator, on the 500th anniversary of Martin Luther’s 95 Theses. Pesce draws a parallel between Luther’s challenge to the Catholic Church and the need to confront what he terms digital colonialism. He argues that the internet has been captured by massive monopolies, specifically Facebook, which colonized public digital space by offering community tools that users adopted without considering the cost. This adoption led to a business model reliant on pervasive surveillance and machine learning algorithms designed to maximize user engagement by curating content that reinforces individual biases. Pesce traces the technological progression from desktop computing to smartphones, which de-territorialized digital monopolies, and now to augmented reality (AR). He highlights that AR technologies, such as those in the iPhone X and Google’s Project Tango, require constant locative awareness and facial tracking, making them inherently technologies of surveillance. He cites Pokemon Go as an early example of AR treating the physical world as a colony, noting incidents where players disrupted local communities and visited sensitive historical sites like Auschwitz. Pesce warns that the convergence of Facebook’s profiling algorithms, smartphone hardware, and AR spectacles will create a fully curated, personalized reality that isolates users from shared objective truth. In his conclusion, Pesce calls for a digital reformation to counter this colonialism. He proposes three principles: judging technologies by their social good and harm, ensuring private value accrues only after social costs are accounted for, and rejecting the philosophy of asking forgiveness rather than permission. He references his own work on mixed reality services and permission frameworks, arguing that limits are necessary protections for the common world. The talk ends with a quote from Martin Luther, emphasizing the moral imperative to stand against conscience-violating systems, even if success is uncertain.

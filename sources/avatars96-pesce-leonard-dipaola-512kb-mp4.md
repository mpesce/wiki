# avatars96-pesce-leonard-dipaola_512kb.mp4

## Summary

This recording captures a panel discussion from an Avatar conference, featuring Mark Pesce, Brett Leonard, and Steve from OnLive Technologies. Mark Pesce opens the session by presenting Web Earth, a three-dimensional model of the planet that utilizes live satellite data from NASA and Switzerland. He explains that the project aims to visualize climatology and epidemiology to aid in planetary management, arguing that open-sourcing the code is essential to prevent the technology from becoming a tool for state surveillance. Pesce announces the Web Earth Initiative, which partners with Digital Equipment Corporation for computing power, the World Health Organization for data, and Electric Minds to host VRML University, a free training program for visualization techniques. Brett Leonard follows by discussing his work with L-Squared Communications and the launch of isTV, an interactive sandbox designed to bring virtual worlds to a mass audience. He argues that Hollywood talent is necessary to bridge the gap between niche virtual communities and the general public, using the "viral infiltration" metaphor to describe how interactive media can empower users to become creators rather than passive consumers. Leonard highlights a project with Intel and the CIA creative agency that features Danny DeVito as a motion-captured avatar, emphasizing the need for high production value to attract mainstream adoption. He stresses the importance of breaking down the passive audience paradigm to ensure the technology serves a positive social function. Steve from OnLive Technologies concludes the panel by demonstrating a 3D voice chat application. He distinguishes his product from standard text chat by focusing on group communication and telepresence, where users interact through avatars in a shared virtual space with distance-based audio attenuation. The demonstration shows users forming natural social clusters and engaging in serendipitous conversations, illustrating the potential for collaborative learning and community building. The session ends with a brief Q&A addressing the importance of persistent identity and social context in virtual environments, with the moderator noting that the next session is ready to begin.

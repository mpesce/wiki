# Guestroom - Futurist Mark Pesce.mp3

## Summary

This recording is an interview from the ABC Local Online radio program The Guest Room, hosted by Leon Compton. The guest is Mark Pesce, identified as a futurist, author, and former engineer who previously worked on virtual reality and the World Wide Web. The conversation takes place in 2010, as Pesce references the current year and predicts trends for the following decade. The discussion begins with the rapid adoption of mobile phones in Australia, where Pesce notes that the device has become the central nexus for human communication, creating a strong psychological attachment. He illustrates this dependency with an anecdote about a mother struggling to cross a busy street while glued to her phone, arguing that technology has already altered human behavior and culture. The conversation explores the trade-offs of this connectivity, specifically the rising value of unconnected time and the social cost of unplugging. Pesce also addresses the concept of helicopter parenting, noting that while GPS tracking in children’s phones can feel Orwellian, it also serves as a vital link to support networks. Later topics include the evolution of technology drivers, where Pesce argues that consumer electronics, rather than military needs, now lead innovation. He discusses the future of books, predicting that physical and digital formats will coexist, but that digital texts will become more interactive and connected like Wikipedia. The interview also covers the history of virtual reality, explaining why head-mounted displays failed due to health concerns for children, and how the Nintendo Wii successfully brought physical interaction to gaming. The segment concludes with a discussion on cybersecurity and national sovereignty, with Pesce comparing modern corporate and state hacking to an arms race, warning that networks inherently dissolve traditional boundaries.

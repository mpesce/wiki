## Summary

This recording is a television interview segment featuring tech correspondent Mark Pesci and host Catherine. The discussion centers on Google's aggressive expansion into health data and artificial intelligence, specifically addressing a recent whistleblower report that Google possesses the full, non-anonymized medical records of approximately 50 million US patients from the Ascension health care group. Pesci explains that while medical data is typically de-identified for statistical use, Google holds detailed surgical and treatment records, raising significant privacy concerns. He notes that Google’s stated goal of improving health outcomes is vague, as the company lacks an obvious consumer health product to justify the data collection. The conversation then shifts to Google’s acquisition of Fitbit for 2.1 billion US dollars. Pesci shares his personal experience as a Fitbit user, noting that the purchase means Google now has access to his daily fitness and biometric data, which is uploaded continuously. He argues that this data grab is a strategic move to feed AI models that require massive datasets to predict health conditions such as heart attacks or cancer. While acknowledging the potential benefits of AI in prolonging wellness and reducing long-term care costs, Pesci questions whether private corporate control is the most effective or ethical path, drawing parallels to the pharmaceutical industry and the need for antitrust regulation to prevent market dominance. The final portion of the interview addresses surveillance and digital currencies. Pesci discusses the concept of "technologies of obfuscation," citing examples from Hong Kong where citizens use makeup and clothing to confuse facial recognition systems. He then covers the People's Bank of China’s development of a digital yuan, intended to replace physical banknotes within the next 12 months.

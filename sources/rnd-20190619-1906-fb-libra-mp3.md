# rnd_20190619_1906_fb_libra.mp3

## Summary

This recording is an interview segment from the Australian radio program RN Drive, featuring futurist and author Mark Pesce discussing Facebook's planned launch of the Libra cryptocurrency in 2020. The host introduces the topic by noting Facebook's entry into the blockchain marketplace and Pesce's claim that the technology could be as significant as the smartphone. Pesce explains that Libra addresses a gap created by the proliferation of smartphones, as approximately four billion people own mobile devices but fewer than one billion have access to credit cards or traditional banking systems. He argues that while Bitcoin was originally designed for this purpose, its slow transaction times make it unsuitable for everyday payments, whereas Libra is designed as a stablecoin pegged to a basket of currencies to facilitate quick, low-value transactions. Pesce highlights the strategic advantage of Libra's partnerships with major financial entities like Visa, MasterCard, PayPal, and Vodafone, describing Facebook as a dominant force bringing its "900 pound gorilla" friends into the space. He clarifies that Libra is intended as a payments layer rather than an investment vehicle, distinguishing it from volatile cryptocurrencies like Bitcoin. The conversation shifts to regulatory concerns, specifically referencing U.S. Congresswoman Maxine Waters' request to pause the project. Pesce notes two primary government worries: the erosion of the state's monopoly on money creation and the unprecedented data collection capabilities Libra would grant Facebook. He warns that Libra would provide a permanent, auditable trail of financial transactions, allowing Facebook to deepen its understanding of user behavior beyond what is currently possible, despite pseudonymity features in the project's white paper.

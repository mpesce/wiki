# 7-30Report-eBooks-18Mar10.mp4.m4v

## Summary

This recording is a news report by Deb Cornwall examining the rapid adoption of electronic books and the resulting pressure on the traditional publishing industry. The segment highlights how the global release of Amazon's Kindle and the upcoming launch of the Apple iPad have accelerated the shift toward digital reading. Industry predictions suggest that up to 50 percent of book sales in major markets will be digital within the next decade. Publishers, who previously resisted the format, are now scrambling to go digital to avoid the fate of the music and movie industries. Current data indicates that while digital books account for only 6 to 7 percent of the market in the US and Britain, a significant portion of those sales occur within the first 24 hours of a release, making delayed digital launches financially detrimental. The report features commentary from Australian publisher Louise Adler, who notes that the current ebook catalog is limited and primarily targeted at American audiences, leaving many contemporary fiction and nonfiction titles unavailable. She attributes this to publisher resistance against Amazon's attempt to cap ebook prices below ten US dollars and a lack of sufficient information for buyers. Contrary to expectations, the primary demographic driving this market is not young digital natives but middle-aged women. Sydney nurse Candice Gray is profiled as an example of this trend; she purchased a Kindle for her husband but found the device instantly appealing, reading three books in less than a month compared to her previous pace. She also notes that the device has changed her reading habits, allowing her to read in shorter sessions throughout the day and access an online shopping catalog. The segment concludes by discussing the impact on local libraries, which are preparing for a transformation where ebooks can be accessed 24/7 without late fees or overdue issues.

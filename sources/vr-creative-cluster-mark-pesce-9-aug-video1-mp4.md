# VR Creative Cluster Mark Pesce 9 Aug video1.MP4

## Summary

This recording is a talk by Mark Pesce, a media and technology commentator and former Apple and Mozilla engineer, recounting his history with virtual reality and the World Wide Web. Pesce begins by describing his founding of Ono Sendai in 1991, the first consumer VR company in the United States. He details how the company solved orientation tracking by combining automotive sensors to reduce component costs from fifty dollars to one dollar. This technology was licensed to Sega for their VR project, which was secretly demonstrated at the 1993 Consumer Electronics Show. Despite initial interest from major retailers, the project was cancelled in July 1993, likely due to user discomfort and legal concerns, causing Pesce’s company to collapse. Following this failure, Pesce discovered the nascent World Wide Web in July 1993. He and collaborator Tony Parisi developed the first VRML browser in early 1994, presenting their work at the first international conference on the World Wide Web in Geneva. Pesce spent the next several years helping standardize VRML and exploring real-time 3D streaming, culminating in a motion-capture demonstration at SIGGRAPH 1997. However, VRML and VR subsequently declined due to high hardware costs, lack of browser support, and the public’s unfamiliarity with real-time 3D graphics. Pesce attributes the eventual resurgence of VR to the ubiquity of smartphones, which drove down the cost of displays and sensors, and the advancement of WebGL, which enabled high-performance 3D graphics in web browsers. Pesce argues that VR is returning because computer graphics are now a thousand times faster than in 1996, and real-time 3D is no longer alien to the general public. He notes that the price of high-end VR systems has dropped rapidly, with Amazon selling capable systems for around two thousand Australian dollars. The talk concludes with a warning about the upcoming release of PlayStation VR. Pesce expresses concern that if major titles cause users to feel sick due to poor design, the industry could lose a generation of potential users.

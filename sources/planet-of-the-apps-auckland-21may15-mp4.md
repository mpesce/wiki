# Planet of the Apps Auckland 21May15.mp4

## Summary

This recording is a keynote speech delivered by a technology commentator in New Zealand, likely in 2015, as indicated by references to current events and future projections. The speaker, who thanks Mark Pesce for the invitation, discusses the rapid global adoption of smartphones and their role in creating the "planet of the apps." He argues that by 2020, 80% of adults will own smartphones, transforming markets from fixed locations into ubiquitous, connected ecosystems. Using Uber as a primary case study, he illustrates how app-based middlemen aggregate supply and demand, creating new economic efficiencies and disrupting traditional industries like taxi services. He notes that this connectivity fosters complex ecosystems where competitors collaborate, such as Amazon providing infrastructure to Netflix, and where suppliers and customers optimize their roles through data. A significant portion of the talk focuses on the transformation of money and payment systems. The speaker highlights Kenya’s M-Pesa as a successful model of mobile money that bypassed traditional banking, enabling financial inclusion and supporting new services like micro-loans and solar energy purchases. He contrasts this with the developed world’s reliance on credit cards and argues that digital currencies are the necessary next step. He urges New Zealand to lead in developing a digital New Zealand dollar to facilitate app-to-app interactions and create a parallel financial system. The speech concludes with a look at India, where Prime Minister Narendra Modi’s initiatives to provide biometric IDs and bank accounts to the unbanked population are poised to integrate a billion people into the global financial system. The speaker predicts this will trigger massive economic growth and innovation, similar to what occurred in East Africa. He encourages New Zealand businesses to prepare for this shift by pioneering connected market solutions that can be exported to emerging economies, emphasizing that the future of commerce is already unfolding in various parts of the world.

# The New Inventors-20071010200000.mp4

## Summary

This recording is a segment from the Australian television program The New Inventors, hosted by Adam Hills. The episode features three inventors presenting their products to a panel of judges, including James, Richard, and Bernie. The first invention is Clearmax, a portable water distiller developed by Joe Lagan and Steve Sutton. The device uses a dual-pot system where steam from boiling contaminated water is condensed in a top pot, utilizing the cooling water to pre-heat the next batch for efficiency. The inventors demonstrate the unit, noting it produces two liters of clean water in approximately 39 minutes for the first batch and 22 minutes for subsequent ones. They target markets in developing countries, specifically China, as well as recreational users like boat owners, with a retail price of around $700. The second invention is the Cocoa Split, created by Mike Fould. Inspired by the difficulty of opening coconuts, the device uses a double arrowhead blade to split a coconut evenly in half with one or two hits from a mallet. Fould demonstrates the tool, showing how it allows for easy removal of the flesh and juice. The product is priced between $120 and $150 and is marketed for both home use and retail environments like supermarkets, where it can be used for display purposes. The judges noted its effectiveness and safety, though they suggested adding a cradle to collect juice and improve stability. The final invention is the Holes Precision Self-Centering Hole Punch, presented by Barry Hull from Perth. This spring-loaded tool transfers the exact center of a pre-bored hole to another surface, ensuring 100% accuracy for drilling. Hull demonstrates that the device can mark 110 holes in 10 minutes, a task that typically takes an hour. Priced between $26 and $43 depending on size, the tool is manufactured in Australia. The judges praised its utility for tradespeople and its ability to save time and money. After deliberation, the panel selects the Holes Precision Self-Centering Hole Punch as the winner of the episode, placing Barry Hull in the running for the Inventor of the Year award.

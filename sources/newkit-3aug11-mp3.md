# NewKit-3Aug11.mp3

## Summary

This recording is a lecture delivered by Mark Pesce, a digital ethnographer, to a classroom audience of approximately seventy students. The talk, which appears to take place around 2011 based on references to current events and technology, focuses on the cultural and economic impact of mobile connectivity. Pesce begins by conducting a live survey of the audience’s ages and the age at which they first acquired a mobile phone, establishing a baseline for his discussion on the rapid shift toward hyperconnectivity. He argues that mobile devices have become essential social lubricants and economic amplifiers, citing examples from fishermen in Kerala, farmers in Kenya, and barbers in Karachi who used mobile phones to significantly increase their incomes. Pesce explores the psychological implications of this connectivity, noting a growing correlation between being connected and feeling emotionally close to others. He references the work of anthropologists Genevieve Bell and Sherry Turkle to discuss how devices serve as proxies for human intimacy. The lecture also covers the evolution of phone technology, describing the migration from basic and feature phones to app phones, which facilitate the rapid sharing of ideas and behaviors. He illustrates this with the example of the "missed call" signaling system in India, which became so widespread that telecom companies began charging for it. The latter portion of the talk addresses the concept of the "interest graph" and the age of omniscience, where situational awareness is shared globally through networks. Pesce discusses how sharing expertise creates nano-celebrities and allows individuals to access collective knowledge, using the case of a hacker who learned skills through online communities. He concludes with a thought experiment involving an alien device that provides real-time environmental data, illustrating how hyperconnected networks allow individuals to make informed decisions and avoid fatal mistakes, effectively granting them superpowers of expanded awareness.

# hyperpeople.mp4

## Summary

This recording is a lecture titled "Welcome to the Swarm," delivered by Mark Pesce, a media commentator and former Apple engineer. The talk appears to be a homecoming address to a community he describes as family, likely at a technology or media conference, given the reference to a previous talk on the same stage four years ago. Pesce begins by discussing the "balkanization of epistemology" and the bewildering social milieu created by technology, arguing that traditional mass media is collapsing under the weight of new distribution models. The core of the presentation focuses on the concept of the "swarm," illustrated through three primary examples. First, Pesce explains BitTorrent using the 2004 release of the Battlestar Galactica revival as a case study, arguing that peer-to-peer file sharing is more effective than broadcasting because it scales with demand, effectively ending the era of centralized media control. Second, he details the rise of Wikipedia, contrasting it with the failed paywall strategy of the Encyclopedia Britannica. He describes Wikipedia as a "knowledge swarm" where collective expertise creates a growing, open encyclopedia, citing the real-time update of Camilla Parker Bowles' title during her wedding as an example of this emergent behavior. Third, he addresses the "swarming of understanding" through personal media consumption, such as the iPod, and new social tools like Outfoxed and Isellipedia, which leverage trust networks to filter information. Pesce warns that while these technologies empower individuals, they risk creating a "tunnel" of personalized experience that isolates users from shared reality, leading to a fragmentation of society akin to the red-state versus blue-state divide. He argues that the solution lies in maintaining "perfect trust" with others to navigate this new landscape. The lecture concludes with a Q&A session where Pesce discusses the I Ching, the necessity of "dietary guidelines" for media consumption to prevent mental gluttony, and the future of the blogosphere and public media in an age of infinite choice.

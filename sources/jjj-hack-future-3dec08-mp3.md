# JJJ_HACK_FUTURE_3Dec08.mp3

## Summary

This recording is an episode of the Australian radio program Hack, hosted by Kate O'Toole, featuring a segment from Triple J's Future Week. The broadcast includes interviews with several experts and a discussion with Mark Pesce, a media commentator, lecturer, and consultant. The program covers a range of emerging technologies, including quantum physics, mobile communications, crime prevention design, and agricultural innovation. The first guest is Joe Hope, a physicist from the Australian National University, who explains the theoretical and practical limitations of human teleportation. He details how quantum mechanics prevents perfect copying of a person and notes that while quantum teleportation of small particles is possible, it is currently used for quantum computing and unbreakable encryption codes. Following Hope, Mark Pesce discusses the future of mobile technology, noting the shift toward touch-based interfaces and the availability of video broadcasting apps on 3G phones. Pesce also critiques the Australian government's plans for ISP-level internet filtering, arguing it is impractical and ineffective, and comments on the National Broadband Network, suggesting the $4.7 billion budget is likely insufficient for the project's full cost. The show then features Frank Defratus, a holography expert, who clarifies that CNN's recent election interviews using Will.i.am were not true holograms but traditional video projections. Defratus predicts that true 3D holographic displays will become common within five to ten years. Next, a researcher from the Designing Out Crime Center at UTS discusses urban design solutions to reduce theft, such as signage to alter pedestrian behavior and the "text message bomb" technology, which allows police to remotely disable stolen mobile phones. The final segment addresses vertical farming, with Dr. Dixon de Pommier proposing skyscraper farms to meet future food demands, while Professor Bruce Bugbee argues for more efficient land use and reduced meat consumption. The episode concludes with Pesce predicting the creation of synthetic life by early 2009 and reviewing listener suggestions for future technologies.

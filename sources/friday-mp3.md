# Friday.mp3

## Summary

This recording is the opening session of a three-day workshop titled "Trilogues for a New Millennium," held at the Esalen Institute. The event features a dialogue between three speakers: Mark Pesce, Douglas Rushkoff, and Eric Reiss. The session serves as an introduction to the weekend’s themes, which explore the intersection of science, magic, spirituality, and technology. The speakers explain that the event was inspired by the work of the late Terence McKenna, who previously hosted similar conversations with Rupert Sheldrake and Ralph Abraham. The format is designed as an interactive, jazz-like conversation rather than a passive lecture, encouraging audience participation and vulnerability. The speakers outline their individual backgrounds and perspectives. Mark Pesce, a former Apple and Mozilla engineer, discusses his work on VRML and his book The Playful World, focusing on the plasticity of reality and the importance of the body in digital spaces. Douglas Rushkoff, a media theorist, shares his history in theater and journalism, referencing his books Siberia and Coercion. He argues that the rise of information culture has commodified communication, reducing rich dialogue to mere data transfer and eroding the value of eloquence and process. Eric Reiss, a journalist and author of Technosis, approaches the topic anthropologically, examining how magical and religious impulses persist in new media, gaming, and subcultures. He highlights the influence of Tolkien on computer games and the role of technology in democratizing the creation of shared imaginary worlds. The session concludes with an open forum where audience members share their personal hopes, fears, and dreams regarding the future of technology and society. Participants discuss themes such as the tension between control and surrender, the fear of corporate hegemony, and the desire for deeper human connection amidst rapid technological change. The speakers respond to these comments, emphasizing that technology is not inherently good or bad but leans toward specific uses depending on its design and application. They frame the weekend as an opportunity to examine the nature, cause, and influence of these changes, aiming to empower participants to engage critically with the evolving digital landscape.

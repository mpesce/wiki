## Summary

This recording is a presentation by Mark Pesce, a futurist and former Apple engineer, delivered to an audience in Australia, likely at a media or marketing industry event. The talk, which references events from May of the previous year and the recent release of Apple’s HomePod, suggests a timeframe around late 2017 or early 2018. Pesce begins by defining his role not as a predictor of specific events, but as an analyst of the operating environment, using the launch of Tesla’s rocket as a hook to discuss the intersection of marketing and technology. He argues that human dissatisfaction is the engine of progress, a concept he applies to Amazon’s evolution from a bookstore to a vast logistics network designed to fulfill consumer desires instantly. The core of the presentation focuses on the rise of voice interfaces like Alexa, Google Assistant, and Siri. Pesce explains that artificial intelligence is not magic but a process of learning from mistakes, illustrated by the story of AlphaGo mastering the game of Go through billions of simulated games. He notes that while these devices are becoming the hottest selling consumer electronics, they currently suffer from "digital disappointment" because they cannot yet execute complex commands or integrate fully with brand content. He contrasts the strategic approaches of the major tech companies: Google focuses on data harvesting and search, while Amazon aims to narrow the gap between a voice command and physical delivery, potentially making brands an enveloping presence in daily life. Pesce then critiques the current web advertising model, which he argues has played itself out through excessive profiling, citing Facebook’s internal documents that revealed they targeted users at their most vulnerable emotional moments. He proposes a shift toward direct relationships between brands and consumers, facilitated by new technologies like the Basic Attention Token (BAT) created by Brendan Eich. This system allows advertisers to pay audiences directly for their attention via cryptocurrency, reversing the dynamic of surveillance capitalism. Pesce concludes that the future belongs to brands that can manage these relationships with human sensitivity and intent, moving away from the exploitative data models of the past toward a system where power is returned to the individual consumer.

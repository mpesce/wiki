# Saturday Afternoon.rm

## Summary

Erik Davis opens by reading (and skimming) a short story he calls 'Speaking of Shocks: The Electric Ant' by Philip K. Dick — the tale of T. Garson Pool, who wakes in a hospital ward with no right hand and feels no pain, in a world where 'splunked' orders and mercantile squids dominate — using it as a lens into the weekend's themes of narrative, programming, and 'shocks.' The conversation then turns to AGENCY: whether the agent is an illusion, a Möbius strip of surrender where letting go of the egoic agent opens onto a different kind of agency ('agent of compassion, agent of productive human relationships'). Mark affirms they still need to name the programming being shoved into young people's minds — 'that's how I'm constituted, to respond to that ethically' — even while recognizing the deeper unraveling. They discuss the subversive PBS/NPR documentary 'Merchants of Cool' (how corporations set the agenda and create the 'culture of cool' and feed it back to kids before they can form their own), and end by examining urban planning — how we build the world we live in, how the loss of parks (now malls) and neighbors quietly reverses the social structures that would buffer the effect of advertising.

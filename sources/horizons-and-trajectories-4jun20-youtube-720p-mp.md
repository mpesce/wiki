# Horizons and Trajectories 4Jun20 YouTube 720p.mp4

## Summary

This recording is a lecture or talk delivered by Mark Pesce, a futurist and former Apple and Mozilla engineer, who discusses the economic and social implications of the COVID-19 pandemic. Pesce explains that he was labeled a futurist by ABC News and uses the concept of exponential growth to analyze the virus's spread. He argues that the pandemic collapsed the standard forward planning horizon for businesses from years to hours, forcing a global response to flatten the curve. Pesce highlights that Australia, New Zealand, and other nations successfully lowered the reproduction rate below one, allowing them to regain a longer planning horizon compared to the United States and the EU, which remain in a state of unpredictability. The speaker outlines four major trends that will dominate business activity for the next 30 weeks. The first is the rise of the sterile economy, illustrated by the NRL’s use of biosecurity teams to create safe environments for players and staff. This trend creates tension between safety and profitability, as seen in the AFL’s handling of drug testers and New Zealand’s entry of the Avatar film crew. The second trend is that every business has effectively become a health business, necessitating the hiring of biosecurity officers who will evolve into Chief Health Officers within the C-suite. These officers will redesign high-touch processes to reduce transmission risks. The third trend is the rise of contactless culture, where practices such as cashless payments and remote work become standard to minimize disease vectors. The final trend involves the paradox of the protective bubble, where domestic safety contrasts with the economic danger of isolation, requiring businesses to endure a period of limited international trade. Pesce concludes by advising leaders to shift from role-based organizational structures to capacity-based planning. He suggests that organizations should inventory the diverse skills of their workforce to adapt to rapidly changing circumstances, revisiting their strategic trajectories monthly rather than annually. He emphasizes that humility and empathy are essential strengths for leaders navigating this chaotic period, citing examples of community unrest in the United States to underscore the need for resilient and compassionate leadership.

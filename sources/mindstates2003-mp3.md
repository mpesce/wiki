# Mindstates2003.mp3

## Summary

This recording is a lecture by Mark Pesce, delivered at a conference where he references a previous talk titled "Becoming Transhuman" given two years prior. The presentation is structured in three parts and explores the intersection of technology, biology, and culture through the lens of memetics. Pesce begins by discussing the concept of "noise" and natural selection, citing physicist Anton Zeilinger’s observation that the quantum level of the universe is random noise. He counters this with John Joe McFadden’s theory of quantum evolution, which suggests that DNA exploits quantum superposition to generate biological variety, thereby restoring a sense of order and purpose to the universe. He further examines Stephen Wolfram’s "The New Kind of Science," arguing that complexity emerges from simple rules and that the universe functions as a computational system where natural selection operates on simple traits. In the second section, Pesce analyzes the emergence of language as a parasitic process that exploits the brain’s innate ability to process temporal sequences. He posits that language and memes are inextricably linked, with memes serving as the material of consciousness that are subject to natural selection. He illustrates this with the story of the Maasai tribe, which regained its social standing by leveraging conservation efforts to reprogram cultural perceptions. Pesce also discusses the biological drives for dominance and survival, noting how memes reinforce these innate tendencies. He highlights the ongoing scientific debate between nanotechnologists Richard Smalley and Eric Drexler as a "war of memes" that is currently dictating the direction of government-funded research, despite a lack of definitive scientific data. The final part of the talk focuses on the accelerating feedback loop between human memes and technology. Pesce argues that as technology becomes finer, moving from stone tools to atomic-scale assembly, the noise level in culture is rising. He predicts that this increasing complexity will lead to an overflow in human consciousness, similar to the emergence of language. Drawing on a realization he had while in Lucerne, he proposes that the mechanisms of meme transmission are evolving rapidly and that nanotechnology will eventually allow memes to break free from the human substrate. He envisions a future where memes become physical entities in the material world, leading to a transformation he terms an eschaton, comparable to the Cambrian explosion in biological history.

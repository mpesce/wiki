# Trench_Talk_EP57_-_Mark_Pesce.mp3

## Summary

This recording is an episode of the Trench Talk podcast, hosted by Matt Reynolds, featuring an interview with Mark Pesce, a futurist, author, and keynote speaker for the 2019 World Plumbing Conference. The conversation takes place via Zoom in July 2019, with Reynolds in Melbourne and Pesce in Sydney. The discussion centers on how various industries, particularly the trades, can navigate technological disruption by understanding the distinct strengths of humans and machines. Pesce begins by defining his role as a futurist, comparing it to that of a climate scientist who predicts long-term trends rather than specific events. He argues that the future is negotiated through current decisions and emphasizes the need to balance excitement with caution. A major theme is the overestimation of short-term technological impact versus the underestimation of long-term transformation. Using the example of Tesla’s Model 3 assembly line, Pesce illustrates that full automation is difficult because robots lack the sensory and subtle mechanical skills of humans. He suggests that the future of work involves a hybrid model where machines handle repetitive tasks while humans focus on complex, integrative, and creative problem-solving. He also discusses the role of augmented reality in mapping workspaces and the importance of digitizing the implicit knowledge held by trade professionals. The interview shifts to broader economic and social topics, including the rise of digital currencies and platform economics. Pesce predicts that central banks will issue digital currencies to compete with private initiatives like Facebook’s Libra, which he views as a potential driver of global commerce, particularly in developing nations. He highlights the efficiency gains from smart contracts and the evolution of banking into app-based services. The conversation concludes with a discussion on voice technology, the importance of soft skills like mentoring in an automated world, and Pesce’s personal views on living in Australia. He recommends the book Team Human by Doug Rushkoff and advises businesses to engage early in the design of new tools to ensure they meet practical needs.

# SLV.wav

## Summary

This recording is a public lecture delivered by Mark Pesce at the State Library of Victoria, hosted by the literary journal The Engine. The event, which marked the journal's 77th anniversary, took place in late 2017, as indicated by references to recent events and the current user base of Facebook. Pesce, described as an inventor, writer, and entrepreneur, discusses the societal impacts of surveillance capitalism and artificial intelligence. He is joined for a Q&A session by Jonathan Green, the editor of The Engine. Pesce begins by highlighting Facebook’s controversial advertising practices, specifically a 2017 report that the company targeted teenagers experiencing emotional distress with commercial ads. He connects this to a 2014 study on emotional contagion, arguing that Facebook’s news feed algorithms function as a "global cognitive bias amplifier." By profiling users through extensive data collection and machine learning, the platform curates content that reinforces existing beliefs, thereby eroding the public sphere and accelerating the fragmentation of reality. Pesce warns that this technology has been weaponized for political influence, citing Cambridge Analytica’s role in the Brexit and US elections, and notes that similar surveillance systems are being developed in China through its social credit system. The talk further explores the impending integration of augmented reality, which Pesce characterizes as a new layer of surveillance that will allow companies to curate users' physical experiences. He argues that the Enlightenment’s emphasis on knowledge has produced a shadow side where information is used to manipulate rather than liberate. In the concluding Q&A, Pesce and Green discuss the ethical responsibilities of technology designers, the importance of teaching critical distance to children, and the challenge of maintaining human agency in a world where digital systems are designed to gratify users rather than challenge them.

# mind_share_hd.mov

## Summary

This recording is a public lecture delivered by Mark Pesce, a media commentator and technology writer, at the Royal Institution in Adelaide. The event was introduced by host Lisa, who outlined Pesce’s background as an inventor of VRML, author of The Playful World, and judge on the ABC series The New Inventors. Pesce’s talk, titled "The Next Billion Seconds," explores the profound societal shifts caused by global mobile connectivity. He argues that the greatest changes in life are often invisible because they are too profound, using the metaphor of a "gigasecond" (approximately 32 years) to frame the current era of hyper-connectivity. Pesce illustrates the economic and social impact of mobile technology through anecdotes from India, Kenya, and Pakistan, showing how connectivity acts as a potent economic amplifier. He discusses the Fukushima nuclear disaster and the subsequent creation of Safecast, a crowdsourced radiation monitoring website, as an example of "hyper-intelligence" emerging from connected individuals. He also references the use of apps to locate police checkpoints during London riots and the collaborative nature of Wikipedia. To demonstrate these concepts, Pesce conducted a live audience experiment where participants shared facts about Adelaide on post-it notes, with some notes intentionally containing lies to test the crowd’s ability to discern truth from fiction. In the subsequent Q&A session, Pesce addressed concerns regarding privacy, the loss of solitude, and the potential for "hyper-stupidity" or misuse of technology. He emphasized that while connectivity offers immense empowerment, it also requires new ethical frameworks and "connected hygiene." He noted that the human brain is outsourcing cognitive functions to devices, creating a new form of social coordination that is distinct from biological hive minds. The lecture concluded with Pesce mentioning his upcoming book, The Next Billion Seconds, and a humorous anecdote about a woman who desired an implanted mobile phone, highlighting the deep integration of these devices into modern life.

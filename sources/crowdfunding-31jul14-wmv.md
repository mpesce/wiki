# crowdfunding-31Jul14.wmv

## Summary

This recording is a workshop and lecture on crowdfunding delivered by Mark Pesce, a media commentator and former Apple engineer, to a small group of startup founders. The session begins with an introduction by a host who notes Pesce’s experience running two crowdfunding campaigns. Pesce frames the talk as an interactive discussion rather than a one-way lecture, aiming to help the audience apply crowdfunding strategies to their own projects. He begins by recounting his own experience launching the "Lite by Morris Cloud," a Wi-Fi connected LED device, on Kickstarter in October 2012. Although the campaign raised $275,000, it failed to meet its $700,000 goal, resulting in the team receiving no funds. Pesce uses this failure to illustrate key lessons about campaign psychology, specifically the importance of setting a lower goal to ensure success and generate momentum. The main body of the talk covers the strategic and operational aspects of crowdfunding. Pesce argues that crowdfunding is not a shortcut to investment but a rigorous marketing and customer acquisition process. He explains that backers are essentially fans, and the relationship is emotional rather than purely transactional. He details the structure of reward tiers, advising creators to offer five distinct levels ranging from low-cost acknowledgment to high-end aspirational experiences. He emphasizes the need for detailed planning, including a 120-day pre-launch period for building relationships and a 30-day campaign duration to maintain intensity. Pesce also discusses the platforms available, comparing Kickstarter’s all-or-nothing model with Indiegogo’s flexible funding options, and highlights the importance of transparent communication with backers during the fulfillment phase to maintain trust. In the final segment, Pesce addresses the realities of hardware manufacturing, noting that physical products face significant delays and compliance challenges that digital projects do not. He advises that a successful campaign is not a business in itself but serves as market validation that can attract traditional venture capital. The workshop concludes with a Q&A session where attendees discuss specific projects, including a modular baby pram and a smart cooler. Pesce advises these founders to use crowdfunding primarily as a go-to-market strategy to build brand awareness and customer base, rather than solely as a funding mechanism, and encourages them to learn from the mistakes of previous campaigns to avoid common pitfalls in fulfillment and expectation management.

# dense-and-thick.mp4

## Summary

This recording is a closing keynote address delivered by Mark Pesce at the Webstock conference in Wellington, New Zealand. The event was introduced by a host who noted Pesce’s previous participation and the audience’s enthusiasm. Pesce begins by thanking the organizers and praising the local tech community, specifically highlighting the work of SilverStripe and the National Library of New Zealand’s ambitious digital sharing initiatives. He frames the conference as a nexus for human connection and sharing, setting the stage for his vision of the future web. Pesce traces the history of the web from his first experience with NCSA Mosaic in October 1993 to the explosive growth of 1994, arguing that the web succeeded because it acted as a "perfect love machine" that reflected individual desires. He identifies the digitization of books and the release of the Apple iPad as the bookends of the "golden age" of the web, marking the complete translation of human culture into a universally accessible digital form. However, he argues that this era is closing, and the next phase involves making implicit human meaning explicit through augmented reality and ubiquitous connectivity. The core of the talk focuses on three thought experiments illustrating how handheld devices will bridge the physical and digital worlds. First, he envisions books dissolving into personalized, hyperlinked networks where the device acts as a lens for reassembling meaning. Second, he describes using a device to trace the supply chain of beef mince, providing real-time data on health, ethics, and carbon footprint to optimize consumer choices. Third, he discusses the integration of personal genomic data with medication management, allowing individuals to instantly verify drug interactions and efficacy. Pesce concludes that this transition represents a new semantic web driven by human desire rather than machine-to-machine communication. He predicts that this shift will create industries larger than current tech giants and encourages the Wellington community to position itself as the epicenter of this next technological revolution.

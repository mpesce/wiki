# ASX.wav

## Summary

This recording is a keynote address delivered at an event hosted by the Australian Securities Exchange (ASX), focusing on the global transition from traditional digital payments to digital currencies. The speaker, a technology commentator, begins by acknowledging the ASX’s role in providing reliable financial infrastructure, highlighting achievements such as the first fully electronic market and the Connectivity ARC. The main topic shifts to the inadequacy of current credit and debit card systems for the developing world, where billions of people own smartphones but lack bank accounts or card access. The speaker argues that this gap requires a new form of money: digital currency, which must be countable, transferable, and secure against double-spending. The talk details the evolution of digital currency, starting with Bitcoin’s 2009 introduction as a decentralized ledger that solved the double-spend problem but suffered from slow settlement times. The speaker then discusses Facebook’s Libra proposal, announced in June 2019, which aims to create a stable, fast-settling currency backed by a basket of securities. Despite regulatory pushback in developed nations, the speaker contends that Libra is unstoppable because it targets the unbanked populations in the developing world, where Facebook has massive user bases. This move has forced central banks, particularly the People’s Bank of China, to accelerate the development of their own digital currencies, such as the digital yuan, to compete for data and economic influence. The speaker predicts that the adoption of digital currencies will be rapid, potentially taking five years or less, driven by the existing smartphone infrastructure. This shift will enable microtransactions, allowing for new business models such as pay-per-view media and on-demand services that are currently hindered by the high costs and friction of traditional payment systems. Using Kenya’s M-Pesa and the startup CopoCopo as examples, the speaker illustrates how digital transaction data can unlock credit and insights for small businesses. The address concludes by urging Australian businesses to prepare for this inevitable change, as digital currencies will transform global commerce and create new opportunities for growth and innovation.

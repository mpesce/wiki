## Summary

This recording is a philosophical lecture or talk by Mark Pesce, exploring the concept of the transhuman condition and the technological paths to it. The speaker frames the current era as a "Bardo" state, a liminal space between human and post-human existence, characterized by existential terror and the need to confront reality without the comfort of traditional cultural structures. Pesce argues that humanity is currently in a state of denial, ignoring both the potential for utopian advancement and the specter of self-destruction. The first technological path discussed is the ancient practice of shamanism, which Pesce describes as a method for accessing the "ineffable" or transhuman realm through entheogens and altered states of consciousness. He references Terrence McKenna to define the shaman as an individual who has stepped outside ordinary space-time to gain knowledge and return to the community. Pesce connects this to modern witchcraft and the Celtic legends of the fairy folk, suggesting these are cultural echoes of a future where humans might merge with a higher-dimensional reality. He emphasizes that while anyone can attempt this transition, it carries a high cost to personal identity and often results in madness or isolation. The second path is electronic brain stimulation, a concept Pesce traces back to his childhood reading of Michael Crichton's novel The Terminal Man in 1973. He discusses the work of neurophysiologists James Olds and Jose Delgado, who demonstrated that direct electrical stimulation of the brain's limbic system could override natural drives and create dependency. Pesce highlights Delgado's 1969 book, Physical Control of the Mind, which warned of the ethical and philosophical dangers of controlling personal identity. He further cites Dr. Michael Persinger

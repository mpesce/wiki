# vrml-interview.mp4

## Summary

This recording is a talk by Mark Pesce discussing the development and implications of the Virtual Reality Modeling Language, or VRML. Pesce describes VRML as a three-dimensional equivalent of HTML that allows users to create interactive 3D worlds accessible via the World Wide Web. He notes that the standard was created by a global community of approximately 2,000 contributors and is designed to be open and available on any platform that supports web access. Pesce addresses concerns about performance, explaining that latency is the primary issue rather than bandwidth, and that VRML environments will often combine local CD-ROM content with web-based arrangements to optimize communication. The speaker argues that VR is fundamentally an interface technology that enables human-centered computing, where the computer adapts to the user rather than the reverse. He predicts that the widespread adoption of 3D graphics cards and CD-quality sound in personal computers over the next year or two will make virtual reality accessible to the average user. Pesce highlights that 3D visualization is essential for understanding certain subjects, such as landscapes and sculptures, which cannot be adequately represented in two dimensions. He cites an unexpected early adoption of VRML by chemists worldwide, who began using the technology to share and annotate molecular structures, demonstrating its utility beyond entertainment or gaming. Pesce further explores the social dimensions of the technology, contrasting the visual nature of Western culture with the auditory concept of place and community. He suggests that while the internet can facilitate connection, it also risks isolating users, comparing it to a social condom that allows interaction without the risks of face-to-face contact. He anticipates that people will increasingly use mediated environments for social interaction and that VRML will be used to model cities and the world for management purposes.

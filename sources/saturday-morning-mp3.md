# Saturday Morning.mp3

## Summary

This recording is a talk and subsequent open discussion led by Mark Pesce, a media and technology commentator, likely delivered at a retreat or conference given the reference to a "white tent" and a ritual. Pesce opens by arguing that society is currently experiencing a renaissance comparable in magnitude to the 15th and 16th centuries. He draws parallels between historical innovations and modern ones, equating perspective painting with holography, the discovery of the round earth with the atomic bomb and space exploration, calculus with chaos theory, the printing press with the internet, and the introduction of coffee with the use of psychedelic drugs. He posits that a renaissance is fundamentally a shift in perspective, moving from playing the game to playing the metagame, where the models and language used to understand reality are up for grabs. Pesce introduces the concept of "open source reality," suggesting that much of what is perceived as fixed is actually a collaborative and arbitrary process. He critiques the "closed source" nature of traditional media, such as television, which he argues was designed to keep audiences passive. He details how the remote control, the video game joystick, and the computer keyboard progressively demystified media, allowing users to deconstruct content and create their own. However, he argues that from 1996 to 2000, institutions launched a campaign to undo this participatory culture. This was achieved through "stickiness" techniques to capture attention, the remystification of computer interfaces, and the declaration that "content is king," which commodified the internet. He notes that the dot-com bubble burst, but the underlying communications infrastructure remained strong, leaving behind open source intuitions that can be applied to other realms, including religion and politics. The discussion expands to cover the tension between the desire for autonomy and the comfort of established hierarchies. Participants debate whether the "masses" can handle the responsibility of participating in reality creation or if they prefer the safety of pre-packaged narratives. Topics include the nature of "shocks" as a way to break complacency, the role of constraints in creativity, and the psychological appeal of reality television as a fantasy of encountering the "real." The conversation also touches on the historical context of religious texts, arguing that scriptures are collaborative stories rather than divine dictates, and the ethical implications of open source principles in governance and spirituality. The session concludes with a reflection on the need for a culture that encourages voluntary engagement and autonomy rather than passive consumption.

## Summary

This recording is an episode of the podcast The Great COVID Reset, featuring an interview between host Max and guest Mark Pesce. The conversation takes place during a second wave of the pandemic in Melbourne, Australia, with the host noting that the show’s title feels ironic given the lack of significant societal reset. The discussion begins with technical audio checks before shifting to the current state of the crisis, where both speakers express concern that society has not yet found a sustainable solution for living with the virus and remains overly reliant on the promise of a future vaccine. Mark Pesce, speaking from Melbourne, describes the atmosphere as one of defeat, comparing the city to a fallen state where the invaders are present in the streets. He highlights the economic impact of the lockdown, noting that the Victorian restrictions are estimated to cost the national economy 1.2 billion dollars. The conversation touches on the failure of earlier optimistic predictions regarding local travel and border closures, with Pesce recalling a specific moment on June 11 when the reopening of Melbourne felt like a temporary pause in a horror film rather than a true recovery. He questions whether the public has the mental resilience for a prolonged second lockdown and critiques the government’s staged response, suggesting that the current measures are insufficient to stop the spread. The dialogue also addresses the confusion caused by fluctuating daily infection numbers, which make it difficult to discern a clear trend. When asked to predict the situation in 2022, Pesce expresses deep pessimism, arguing that political and societal structures have not changed enough to make the current sacrifices worthwhile.

# Business on the Edge 21Apr21.mp4

## Summary

This recording is a keynote address delivered by Mark Pesce, a technology commentator and former Apple and Mozilla engineer, likely to an Australian business audience. Speaking in the early post-pandemic period, Pesce outlines major societal and technological shifts expected over the next 15 years, referencing his podcast The Next Billion Seconds. He begins by analyzing the permanent restructuring of work, noting that the pandemic has divided the workforce into three distinct groups: permanent remote workers, who now comprise about 25% of the labor force; extroverts who eagerly returned to offices; and hybrid workers, making up roughly 40 to 45% of the workforce. This shift has altered management requirements and, combined with the launch of Starlink satellite internet in rural Australia, has accelerated a migration from cities to regional areas. Pesce predicts that by 2035, the population balance between regional and urban Australia will reset from 20/80 to 30/70. The second half of the talk focuses on the rapid electrification of civilization. Pesce argues that electric motors are replacing internal combustion engines not just for environmental reasons, but because they are significantly simpler and more resilient, citing the contrast between 2,000 engine components and 20 motor components. He projects that by 2035, 20 to 30% of new vehicles in Australia will be electric, with major manufacturers like Ford, GM, and Volkswagen ceasing production of petrol vehicles. A key insight is the potential for vehicle-to-grid technology, where millions of electric cars with two-way charging will act as distributed battery storage, solving grid stability issues. Finally, Pesce frames these changes through the lens of computing history, describing a recurring "heartbeat" where innovation shifts from central mainframes to the edge, such as personal computers and smartphones. He argues that the energy grid is undergoing a similar reversal. Instead of a centralized model with few generators serving many customers, the future will feature a decentralized network where millions of edge devices, including rooftop solar panels and electric vehicles, generate and store power. This transition will require new business models and infrastructure designs to manage a grid where the edge becomes the primary source of generation

# 2-03 The Pathetic Fallacy _ Poems.mp3

## Summary

This recording is a spoken word performance and lecture delivered at the Burning Man festival, likely within the Oracular Madness camp. The speaker, a poet and author, begins by reflecting on a visit to the Upper Paleolithic caves in the Dordogne, contrasting the optimism of prehistoric art with the current state of the world. He introduces his upcoming book, The Great Bay, California After the Collapse, which explores a future where the Sacramento Valley becomes a massive bay after a global collapse. The speaker discusses the concept of the "pathetic fallacy" in poetry, arguing for a "cosmic fallacy" that emphasizes interconnectedness and the gift economy as a more authentic human mode of existence compared to the "temporary blip" of modern civilization, which he characterizes by standing armies and slavery. The presentation includes the reading of several original poems. "Last Specimen" explores the connection between nature and human suffering, while a narrative poem recounts a surreal encounter with bees in Death Valley who claim to be old friends seeking rain. Another poem, "Death Valley," describes a meditative hike through the desert landscape. The speaker also reads "The Condor Spirits Song" from his new book, a piece where a spirit entity laments the environmental destruction and violence of modern humanity, vowing to send plagues and earthquakes. He briefly mentions a dream about swimming with a whale and reads "In Gratitude," a poem honoring Albert Hofmann that celebrates the beauty of illusion and perception. The talk concludes with a promotional mention of his new book on Burning Man, Inspired Madness, and an invitation to attend evening improv events at the Oraculon. The speaker outlines the schedule for the following day, which includes a discussion on playa artwork by Peggy Nelson, a talk on entheogenic issues by John Hannah and Sylvia Tyson, and a final panel discussion. The tone is conversational and philosophical, blending literary analysis with speculative fiction and personal anecdotes to critique contemporary society and envision a post-collapse future.

# Panel discussion, facilitated by host Mark Pesce.mp4

## Summary

This recording is a panel discussion focused on the theme of democratizing technology, featuring a moderator and five panelists: Alex Gushka, COO of Startup Australia; Jen George, Managing Director of TAMI; Ben Wong, CEO of Academy XI; Liesl Yearsley, CEO of Akin; and Angie Abdilla, founder of Old Ways New. The session addresses the ethical, educational, and economic challenges of integrating artificial intelligence into society. Liesl Yearsley opens by discussing the pervasive influence of AI on human behavior, citing her experience with Cognia and IBM Watson. She argues that current optimization parameters often prioritize engagement over well-being, leading to addictive behaviors and externalized societal costs. She advocates for embedding ethical rules and parameters into next-generation AI to ensure it improves quality of life rather than exploiting attention. Angie Abdilla then introduces indigenous knowledge systems, specifically the concept of Lore, as a framework for AI governance. She suggests applying principles of custodial ethics and pattern thinking to create holistic, sustainable AI systems that respect interconnections between people and the environment. Ben Wong addresses the need for democratizing education, noting that traditional models are failing to keep pace with technological change. He emphasizes the necessity of lifelong learning and highlights how ed-tech and virtual labs are making skills more accessible. Alex Gushka discusses the Australian startup ecosystem, arguing that the country must convert its high talent pool into economic value to remain competitive globally. He notes that while Australia has world-class education, it lags behind the US in tech market capitalization and needs to encourage entrepreneurship among young people. Jen George shares the origin of the Like-Minded Bitches Drinking Wine community, which supports women in tech, and discusses the importance of representation and mentorship. The panel concludes by debating the tension between corporate profit and ethical responsibility, referencing Google’s removal of the phrase "don't be evil." They discuss how market forces and cultural shifts can reward good behavior, with Yearsley expressing hope for a new class of tech companies that optimize for human flourishing rather than pure capitalist gain. The final segment offers advice for Sydney entrepreneurs, urging them to take audacious risks, focus on solving real problems, and leverage Australia’s unique position to foster a thriving tech culture.

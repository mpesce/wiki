# Recording 7.wav

## Summary

This recording is a talk titled "Property Over the Next Billion Seconds" delivered by Mark Pesce, a media and technology commentator. The presentation focuses on how emerging automation and technology will influence property investment decisions over the next several decades, specifically looking toward the year 2050. Pesce begins by distinguishing futurism from psychic prediction, framing it instead as a climate-science-like analysis of long-term trends to guide investment strategies. The first major topic is the gradual rollout of autonomous vehicles. Pesce argues that fully self-driving cars in complex urban environments, such as navigating Sydney’s George Street, are decades away due to the difficulty of handling exceptions and dynamic road conditions. He predicts that automation will first appear in interstate trucking on highways like the Hume and Sturt, creating high-value logistics hubs in outer ring areas such as Liverpool. He advises investors to evaluate property based on how easily autonomous vehicles can navigate the location, noting that outer zones will see automation benefits before inner city areas. The second section addresses the future of retail. Pesce uses the example of IKEA to illustrate that physical retail is evolving into an experience-based destination, similar to a theme park, rather than just a place to buy goods. He contrasts this with the rise of home-based shopping through platforms like Amazon and Alibaba, where voice assistants like Alexa turn the home into a store. He suggests that traditional retailers must offer unique, engaging experiences to compete with the convenience of online delivery. Finally, Pesce discusses the impact of AI on the workforce, using the example of AlphaGo defeating the world champion in Go to show how machines can serve as tools for human improvement rather than just replacements. He explains that automation moves the "goalposts" of jobs, requiring continuous learning and flexibility. This shift is driving demand for flexible workspaces, such as coworking spaces, and temporary living arrangements like WeLive. The talk concludes with a call for investors to develop or seek data-driven tools that can predict property value changes based on these technological and demographic shifts.

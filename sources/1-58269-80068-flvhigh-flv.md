## Summary

This recording is a business presentation titled "Using the Network for Business Success," delivered by Mark Pesce to an Australian audience. Pesce, a media and technology commentator, outlines the evolution of business communication from traditional methods like postal mail and telephones to the data-driven era of the internet and Web 2.0. He notes that while email initially became the dominant business tool due to its asynchronous nature, the rise of social media has fundamentally altered how companies interact with customers. Pesce argues that the web has transformed from a one-way publishing medium into a two-way interactive platform, enabling unprecedented connectivity through platforms like Facebook, Twitter, and YouTube. The speaker emphasizes that human cognitive limits, historically capped at managing around 150 social relationships, have been expanded by digital networks that act as "supercharged Rolodexes." These platforms allow individuals to maintain broader connections and share content, which creates a new dynamic where customers are constantly engaged in conversations about brands. Pesce illustrates this with two case studies: Virgin Australia, which failed to monitor positive organic praise on Twitter while running a disjointed marketing campaign, and American Express, which ignored a public complaint from a well-connected customer regarding a credit card issue, resulting in significant reputational damage. He argues that traditional mass marketing is failing in this environment because it does not account for the decentralized, peer-to-peer nature of social influence. Pesce advises businesses to adopt a proactive listening strategy rather than relying on one-off marketing campaigns. He criticizes companies that block social media on corporate networks, suggesting instead that employers should trust employees and utilize them as a distributed network of monitors to identify both positive and negative conversations. He cites the ABC’s social media guidelines as a model for establishing mutual respect and clear expectations. The talk concludes with the assertion that social media is not an advertising channel but a relationship-building tool based on authenticity and expertise. Pesce encourages businesses to create accounts that invite genuine conversation, noting that while the process requires time and effort, the long-term value of building authentic customer relationships through the network far exceeds traditional advertising methods. The session ends with a brief Q&A regarding monitoring tools and the use of social media in educational contexts.

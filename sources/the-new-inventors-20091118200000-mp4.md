# The New Inventors-20091118200000.mp4

## Summary

This recording is an episode of the Australian television program The New Inventors, hosted by James O'Loughlin. The show features three inventors presenting their creations to a panel of judges, including engineer James Bradford Moody, journalist Christine Kidinmonth, and woodworker Richard Vaughan. The first segment introduces Darren Visser from Melbourne, who presents a rotating ice tunnel designed for year-round snowboarding. The device uses refrigerated glycol to maintain a cold surface, allowing riders to practice on a continuous track. Visser discusses the engineering challenges of rotating plumbing and the specific board designs required for the ice surface. The second invention is the Live Lens, presented by Roland Butcher. This technology utilizes a liquid crystal-on-silicon chip containing one million independent pixels that act as a spatial aperture. Unlike standard camera lenses that adjust exposure for the entire frame, the Live Lens adjusts light intake for each pixel individually, allowing for clear images in scenes with high contrast between bright and dark areas. Butcher demonstrates the chip's effectiveness using security camera footage and discusses its potential applications in broadcast television and wildlife photography. The final invention is the Drain Wave, created by David Fisher and Quentin Davenport from Hobart. This device addresses the problem of blocked sewer pipes caused by the widespread adoption of low-flush toilets. By collecting grey water from sinks and showers in an underground bucket, the system releases a large surge of water into the sewer line when the toilet is flushed, ensuring sufficient flow to prevent blockages. The judges deliberate on the market viability and originality of the three inventions. James Bradford Moody selects the Live Lens as his winner due to its significant market potential, while Christine Kidinmonth favors the Drain Wave for its immediate practical application. The episode concludes with the announcement of the judges' choice and an invitation for viewers to vote for the People's Choice Award. The broadcast includes brief news segments and promotional trailers for other ABC programs.

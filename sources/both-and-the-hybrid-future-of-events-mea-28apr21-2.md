# Both-And The Hybrid Future of Events MEA 28Apr21.mp4

## Summary

This recording is a professional talk delivered by Mark Pesce, a futurist and former engineer, to an audience of meetings and events professionals. Introduced by Beverly, Pesce discusses the structural changes in work and events resulting from the pandemic. He notes that the global shift to remote work revealed that the office is no longer the primary locus of labor but rather a space for connection and serendipity. Pesce categorizes the modern workforce into three groups: permanent remote workers, office-based extroverts, and a new class of hybrid workers. He argues that hybrid workers require continuous negotiation with management regarding their flexible schedules, creating a new management challenge for organizations. The speaker then applies these workforce dynamics to the events industry, observing that large-scale conferences such as Microsoft Build and Google I/O have successfully transitioned to virtual formats, achieving significantly higher attendance than their physical counterparts. He posits that the future of events will consist of roughly 35 percent fully real, 25 percent fully virtual, and 40 percent hybrid events. Pesce emphasizes that hybrid events currently suffer from inequitable experiences between in-room and remote attendees, a gap that the industry must close to succeed. He outlines the necessary hard skills, including media production, lighting, and sound design, as well as soft skills such as performing for a camera and moderating simultaneous real and virtual interactions. Pesce concludes by highlighting the new global reach afforded by virtual technology, using his local meditation group as an example of how virtual participation expanded their community internationally. He suggests that even as in-person events resume, there is a demand for hybrid delivery to maintain access for remote participants. The talk ends with the guiding principle that the future favors flexibility, requiring event professionals to master the balance between technological capability and human connection to create effective hybrid experiences.

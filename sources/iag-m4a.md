# IAG.m4a

## Summary

This recording is a futurist talk delivered by Mark Pesce, likely at a Blue Sky event in Brisbane, Australia, given references to local geography and the audience. The presentation, themed around the "weird" nature of the future, explores three primary areas: autonomous vehicles, blockchain technology, and energy systems. Pesce begins by discussing the limitations of self-driving cars, arguing that while highway autonomy is achievable, full autonomy in complex urban environments is decades away due to the vast number of edge cases. He predicts that cities will redesign infrastructure to lower decision loads for vehicles, leading to a collapse in logistics costs and the emergence of mobile services like driverless hotel rooms and autonomous grocery stores. He also highlights the potential of autonomous drones to transform logistics in remote areas like Papua New Guinea, where ground transport is prohibitively expensive. The second section addresses blockchain and cryptocurrencies. Pesce explains the mechanics of blockchain using a travel expense analogy, emphasizing its role in creating transparent, tamper-proof ledgers. He distinguishes between speculative cryptocurrency trading and practical applications, citing Australian startup Agridigital for agricultural settlements and the ASX’s move toward blockchain-based stock settlements. In a notable segment, Pesce announces the "Mark Pesce Token," a personal coin offering that tokenizes his professional labor, allowing individuals to purchase units of his time. He frames this as an experiment to understand the economics of a future where professionals may issue their own currencies. The final topic covers energy, focusing on the rapid decline in solar panel costs and the rise of battery storage. Pesce discusses the Tesla battery in South Australia and predicts a future where autonomous electric trucks act as mobile power plants, hauling charged batteries to areas with high demand. He highlights Power Ledger, a Perth-based firm using blockchain for peer-to-peer energy trading, as a key example of this convergence. The talk concludes with a vision of a network of autonomous batteries smoothing out national energy demands, ending with a Hunter S. Thompson quote encouraging the audience to embrace the professionalization of weirdness.

# digirupt-preview.mp4

## Summary

This recording is a keynote speech delivered by a technology commentator in New Zealand, thanking the local community and Mark Pesce for the invitation. The speaker, who identifies as a former Apple and Mozilla engineer, discusses the rapid global adoption of smartphones, predicting that 80 percent of adult humans will own one within 60 months. He argues that this infrastructure has created a "planet of the apps," where markets are no longer bound by physical location or time. The talk uses Travis Kalanick and Uber as a primary case study, explaining how the company aggregated supply and demand through smartphone apps to disrupt traditional taxi services. The speaker highlights how connected marketplaces generate ecosystem effects, allowing suppliers and customers to optimize their activities and leading to the emergence of new competitors like Lyft and ancillary services like SherpaShare. The second half of the presentation shifts focus to the transformation of money and financial services. The speaker contrasts the developed world’s reliance on credit cards with the success of mobile money in East Africa, specifically citing Kenya’s M-Pesa system. He details how M-Pesa enabled new economic activities, such as M-Kopa selling solar panels via micro-payments and Koppo Koppo using transaction data to help small businesses access bank loans. The speaker advocates for the adoption of digital currencies, noting that smartphones provide the necessary storage and interface for such systems. He urges New Zealand to lead in creating a digital New Zealand dollar to facilitate app-to-app interactions and value chains. The speech concludes with a look at India, where Prime Minister Narendra Modi’s initiatives to provide biometric ID cards and universal bank accounts are poised to bring a billion unbanked citizens into the global financial system. The speaker predicts that India will leapfrog other nations into a connected economy, driven by investments from companies like Alibaba. He encourages New Zealand to position itself as a hub for developing connected market products and services that can be exported to this emerging South Asian market, emphasizing that the future of commerce is already unfolding in various parts of the world.

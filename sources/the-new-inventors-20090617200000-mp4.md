# The New Inventors-20090617200000.mp4

## Summary

This recording is an episode of the Australian television program The New Inventors, hosted by James O'Loughlin. The show features a panel of judges, including agricultural scientist Chris Russell, surgeon and inventor Professor Fiona Wood, and inventor and journalist Christine Kenimont, who evaluate several new inventions. No specific air date is mentioned in the transcript, though the news segment at the end references the 2009 Iranian presidential election protests and the swine flu pandemic, placing the broadcast in late 2009. The first invention presented is a self-propelled, three-wheeled machine by Kevin Witkowski from Adelaide that uses infrared heat generated by LPG gas to repair and recycle asphalt in road potholes. The device heats the existing road surface for five to ten minutes, allowing it to be raked, rejuvenated with oils, and topped up with a small amount of new bitumen. Witkowski claims the machine costs around $80,000 but pays for itself within a year by reducing the need for expensive full road replacements. The judges discuss its maneuverability on slopes and its potential for preventative maintenance, with Chris Russell noting its practicality for local councils. The second invention is the Height Guard, a safety barrier for scaffolders developed by Miles Guess and Russell Hughes from Queensland. This device solves the dilemma of working at heights without protection by allowing a guardrail to be hooked onto the scaffold frame before a worker climbs up to the next level. It is a single component with no moving parts, tested to support 175 kilograms, and costs approximately $190. The judges praise its originality and safety benefits, noting it is quicker and safer than traditional harnesses. Other brief segments include a 15-year-old named David presenting a PC standby smart device that cuts power to computer accessories when the main unit is off, and a husband presenting a bed slip cover to prevent dust accumulation for his allergic wife. The final major invention is the Geo Brick by Rory Stanton, a non-fired brick made from clay, gravel, and eco-cement containing 30% slag, which produces 20 percent of the carbon dioxide emissions of traditional bricks. The judges ultimately select the Height Guard

## Summary

This recording is a keynote lecture delivered by Mark Pesce to an audience in New Zealand, likely at a technology conference hosted by the Hotel de Brette. The talk, which appears to be from around 2015 based on references to current events and future projections, focuses on the emergence of the "planet of the apps" and its profound impact on global economics. Pesce argues that the smartphone has become the primary infrastructure for modern commerce, predicting that within 60 months, 80% of the world's adult population will own one. He posits that this shift transforms markets from fixed locations into connected ecosystems where middlemen thrive by aggregating supply and demand. The main points of the lecture center on the disruption of traditional industries through app-based marketplaces. Pesce uses Uber as a primary example, detailing how it bootstrapped into a massive business by connecting limousine drivers with customers via smartphone apps, a model that traditional taxi companies failed to replicate due to their reliance on outdated business structures. He explains that once a market is connected, it spawns secondary economic relationships, such as drivers using multiple apps to optimize earnings and customers comparing services in real-time. The talk then shifts to the future of finance, contrasting the sophisticated but disconnected payment systems of the developed world with the mobile money success in Kenya. Pesce advocates for the adoption of digital currencies, suggesting that a digital New Zealand dollar could unlock new app ecosystems and value chains. In the final segment, Pesce discusses the impending financial inclusion of India. He notes that Prime Minister Narendra Modi’s implementation of biometric ID cards and bank accounts will allow a billion unbanked Indians to access mobile money services by 2019. This development is expected to create a massive new market, prompting global tech giants like Alibaba to invest heavily in Indian smartphone manufacturers to secure their place in this emerging economy. Pesce concludes by urging New Zealand to position itself as a pioneer in developing products and services for this connected global economy, emphasizing that the talent and opportunity to lead this transformation are currently available in the region.

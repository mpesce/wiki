# Beyond the Horizon CFO Series QLD 10Sep20.mp4

## Summary

This recording is a talk delivered by Mark Pesce, a futurist and host of the podcast The Next Billion Seconds, to an online audience in Melbourne. The presentation, part of an online CFO series, examines the distinct economic and social lessons learned by Australia’s three eastern states during the pandemic. Pesce begins by discussing Victoria’s second lockdown, using the effective reproduction rate to explain how rising virus transmission collapses forward planning horizons. He argues that Victoria’s experience provides critical national knowledge on how to successfully hibernate and resume businesses, a skill that will be essential in the 2020s due to ongoing service interruptions driven by climate factors like the Indian Ocean Dipole and bushfires. The speaker then contrasts this with New South Wales, which has adapted to a high level of daily uncertainty through rigorous contact tracing and testing, a system that became the national gold standard after the initial failure involving the Ruby Princess cruise ship. Pesce highlights the shift to remote work in Sydney, noting that while it has emptied CBDs, it has forced a renegotiation of workplace dynamics, requiring managers to provide more structure to prevent employee burnout and to address the financial costs now borne by workers. He emphasizes that this resilience in the face of uncertainty is a valuable asset for the broader economy as government financial support like JobKeeper is withdrawn. Finally, Pesce analyzes Queensland’s position as the state that has remained largely open, using the AFL Grand Final at the Gabba as a symbol of its functional economy. He predicts that the post-pandemic world will see a permanent shift toward digital payments, a step change accelerated by the crisis. He also details the rise of decentralized finance, specifically Ethereum-based smart contracts, which are creating over-collateralized loan pools worth billions. He forecasts that these technologies will eventually disintermediate traditional business banking. Additionally, he discusses the collapse of commercial air travel and the simultaneous explosion of package delivery, suggesting that airlines will pivot to cargo or merge with logistics providers, while tourism will remain predominantly local and interstate for the foreseeable future.

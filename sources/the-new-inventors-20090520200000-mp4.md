# The New Inventors-20090520200000.mp4

## Summary

This recording is an episode of the Australian television program The New Inventors, broadcast on ABC1. The show features three inventors presenting their creations to a panel of judges consisting of agricultural scientist Chris Russell, science journalist Bernie Hobbs, and designer Sally Deminks. The first inventor, Clive Thomas from Melbourne, presents a portable food heating and cooling unit designed for catering. The device is flat-packed for easy transport and assembly, utilizing a fan-forced electric heater for warming food to 70 degrees Celsius and a standard commercial refrigeration unit for cooling. Thomas notes the unit is constructed from stainless steel to prevent bacterial growth and requires only a wipe-down for cleaning. The judges discuss the high cost of forty-two thousand dollars, with Russell questioning the hygiene of the assembly clips and Deminks suggesting the use of waste heat from the cooling unit to improve energy efficiency. Thomas explains that the modular design allows the unit to function as either a heater or a cooler and is intended for use in restaurants, hospitals, and outdoor events. The second inventor, Tony Abrahams, also from Melbourne, presents a bed bug barrier for hotel beds. The device is a small unit attached to bed legs containing a cavity filled with non-drying, non-toxic glue. Bed bugs attempting to climb the legs become trapped and die, preventing infestations. Abrahams highlights that the solution is low-cost, costing between three and four dollars per unit, and effective against the problem of bed bugs traveling on luggage. The judges praise the simplicity and low-tech nature of the solution, with Russell noting that the glue is already used in the horticultural industry. They discuss the potential for dead bugs to form a bridge, which Abrahams dismisses as unlikely given the surface area. The judges unanimously select Abrahams as the winner of the episode, awarding him the title of Inventor of the Year.

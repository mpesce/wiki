# CFO Series Melbourne While You Were Out 12Nov20.mp4

## Summary

This recording is a talk delivered by Mark Pesce to the CFO series in Melbourne, referencing a previous appearance on June 10. The speaker opens by acknowledging the recent success of Victoria in controlling the second wave of COVID-19, noting that this public health crisis consumed national attention while significant shifts occurred in the global financial landscape. The main topic of the presentation is the rapid transformation of money through the adoption of digital currencies, central bank digital currencies, and cryptocurrency integration by major financial institutions. Pesce outlines these changes in chronological order, beginning with events from the previous year. He discusses Facebook’s announcement of the Libra cryptocurrency, which, despite regulatory pushback, catalyzed the People’s Bank of China to launch its Digital Currency Electronic Payment system. The speaker explains that China is aggressively rolling out this digital yuan, aiming for full integration by the 2022 Winter Olympics, and notes that this move serves both as a tool for state surveillance and a mechanism for expanding Chinese financial soft power globally. He highlights that China already possesses the world’s most sophisticated digital payments infrastructure, positioning it ahead of other nations. Moving to the previous month, the talk covers PayPal’s announcement on October 21 that it would allow users to buy and pay with four cryptocurrencies, including Bitcoin and Ethereum. Pesce argues that PayPal is creating a regulated, walled garden for crypto transactions to prepare its infrastructure for the inevitable arrival of central bank digital currencies. By using email addresses rather than complex wallet addresses for transfers, PayPal is lowering the barrier to entry for its half-billion users. Finally, the speaker addresses events from the previous week, specifically the Reserve Bank of Australia’s announcement to create a wholesale digital currency using Ethereum smart contracts to modernize bank settlements. He concludes by noting that the European Central Bank has also begun designing its own digital currency, asserting that the 2020s will be defined by a pervasive shift toward a digitalized money economy, representing the most significant change in monetary systems since the invention of paper money.

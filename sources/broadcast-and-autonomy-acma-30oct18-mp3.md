# Broadcast_and_Autonomy_ACMA_30Oct18.mp3

## Summary

Address to the ACMA (Australian Communications and Media Authority, 30 Oct 2018) on broadcast and autonomy. He opens with a note about his phone in airplane mode not turning off Bluetooth (an iOS 12 'convenience') — 'which tells us something about the subtlety of the radio spectrum in 2018.' He lays out his bona fides: writing firmware for X.25 packet assembler/disassemblers in 1984, CSU/DSUs, pioneering dial-up at Shiva Corporation, then working with Tim Berners-Lee — 'I'm one of the folks who helped bring you the World Wide Web' — and taking VRML through the ISO standards process. The thesis is two 'twin drivers' for the next 15 years / half billion seconds: (1) the very HUMAN driver into better fidelity, more reality, more immersion in rich 3D videogrammetry that will 'overwhelm what we think of as television broadcasting today'; and (2) the MACHINES clamoring for 'as much consistency, low latency, error recovery and fallback as possible' to do their job with assurance. The key question for a carrier: 'this is the area where a carrier doesn't become a commodity' — devices will preferentially connect to the network that provides greater assurance to the autonomous systems on it, and 'those autonomous systems will probably pay for that privilege.'

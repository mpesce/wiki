## Summary

This recording is a segment from the Australian television program 730, focusing on the rapid expansion of 3D technology in cinema and television and the associated health risks. The report highlights the shift from novelty to mainstream adoption, noting that major films like Avatar utilize the format and that 3D television is entering Australian households. It mentions that SBS and Foxtel plan to broadcast World Cup soccer matches in 3D using Sony cameras, with Sony projecting the sale of 100 million 3D televisions globally within three years. The segment details concerns regarding the long-term effects of 3D viewing, particularly for children. Macquarie University computer researcher Manolia Kavakli is cited for a study in which 20 percent of 150 participants reported motion sickness after viewing 3D content for ten minutes. Kavakli emphasizes the need for further research into high-frequency image repetition and its impact on perception.

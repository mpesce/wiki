# swf2010-pesce.wav

## Summary

This recording is a panel discussion from the Sydney Writers Festival, featuring Mark Pesce and Marcus Whitting. The session, titled "Who Needs the Publisher Anyway," addresses the evolving landscape of publishing and digital media. Pesce begins by recounting his initial pitch to the festival, which was intended to showcase a completed open-source book project but evolved into a broader exploration of how things change. He notes that traditional New York publishers were uninterested in his concept, preferring celebrity cookbooks, which led him to self-publish a collection of essays. By distributing the work as a free PDF and EPUB on his website, Pesce demonstrated that the distribution function of a publisher can be easily replicated by the author. Pesce then details the "Share This Book" project, launched in November of the previous year, which aimed to create a book through a collaborative, community-driven process. He read a manifesto proposing that the creative process itself should embody the book's theme of sharing. However, the project stalled when Pesce experienced writer's block and withdrew from the community for several months, a period during which he realized that the traditional book format is dissolving. He argues that nonfiction is merging into a vast corpus of factual knowledge, while fiction is diverging into three types: immersive narratives, universe-creating works, and hypertextual pieces like James Joyce's Ulysses. The discussion concludes with a redefinition of authorship. Pesce asserts that the author is no longer a singular figure but a "thought leader" who plants ideas in a shared garden, while others act as gardeners who prune and maintain the work. He suggests that the boundaries between author, reader, and publisher are melting, replaced by a fluid zone of connectivity. The panelists are working on a new format that functions as both a wiki and a book, allowing users to switch between reader and author modes. This approach seeks to refit the traditional titles of book, author, and publishing to the digital age, where creation is an ongoing, open-ended process rather than a finished product.

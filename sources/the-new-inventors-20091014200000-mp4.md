# The New Inventors-20091014200000.mp4

## Summary

This recording is an episode of the Australian television program The New Inventors, hosted by James O'Loughlin. The show features a panel of judges, including engineer James Bradfield Moody, journalist Christine Kinenmonth, and woodworker Richard Vaughan, who evaluate three distinct inventions. The first segment introduces a snake trap designed by Graham Lloyd, which uses scented sawdust to lure snakes into a box where a one-way door mechanism secures them safely. The inventor explains that the device is intended for both professional snake catchers and domestic use, though the current prototype price of $299 is noted as high for individual households. The second invention is a wool bale fastener gun presented by Shane Rees from Ivanhoe, New South Wales. This tool allows workers to attach staples to wool bales without using a dangerous manual bale hook. Rees demonstrates the device, which loads fasteners like a magazine, and discusses plans to sell the gun for free to manufacturers who purchase large quantities of the fasteners. The judges praise the practicality of the solution for the wool industry, noting it is an early prototype that requires further refinement in ergonomics and loading mechanisms. The final invention is a water-recycling high-pressure cleaner created by John Tanner of Sydney. Designed to wash cars and floors using only about 20 liters of water, the device sprays water under pressure and simultaneously vacuums it back through a filter. Tanner explains the dual-motor system that allows for powerful cleaning while conserving water, making it suitable for use during water restrictions. The panel debates the marketability of the three inventions, with the judges ultimately selecting the wool bale fastener gun as the winner of the episode. The winner, Shane Rees, advances to the competition for the title of Inventor of the Year. The episode concludes with a public vote option and a closing quote from psychologist Rollo May.

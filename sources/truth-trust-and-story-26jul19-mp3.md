# Truth Trust and Story 26Jul19.mp3

## Summary

This recording is a talk delivered by Mark Pesce, a media and technology commentator, at an event for NITV, the Indigenous Australian television network. The speech begins with an acknowledgment of the Gadigal people, the traditional owners of the land. Pesce defines futurists as storytellers who use narratives about the present to shape visions of the future, citing William Gibson’s observation that the future is already here but unevenly distributed. He argues that the past twenty-five years of global connectivity have created a paradox: while the internet has enabled unprecedented sharing and learning, it has also amplified ignorance and misinformation. Pesce emphasizes that in this era of information oversupply, individuals subconsciously select facts that align with their existing beliefs, eroding trust in institutions. He identifies NITV as a rare source of trusted truth for Indigenous Australia and urges the organization to leverage this trust to empower its community, framing trust as a fundamental, bidirectional resource that is increasingly scarce. The second half of the talk shifts focus to emerging technology, specifically augmented reality (AR). Pesce distinguishes AR from virtual reality, describing AR as a technology that overlays synthetic content onto the real world using devices like the Magic Leap headset. He predicts that AR glasses will eventually replace smartphones as the primary interface for digital interaction, offering a way to engage with digital content without the isolation of staring at a screen. He highlights the potential of AR to transform broadcasting, allowing viewers to experience events like the AFL Grand Final from any angle in three dimensions. Pesce connects this technological shift to Indigenous storytelling traditions, noting that AR returns narrative to a specific place, a concept central to Australian culture for thousands of years. He concludes by warning that AR allows for the manipulation of reality and emotion, making transparency and trust essential. He asserts that while he would not hand the keys to his perception to major tech corporations, he would trust NITV to use this powerful medium responsibly.

# The New Inventors-20090211200000.mp4

## Summary

This recording is an episode of the Australian Broadcasting Corporation television program The New Inventors, hosted by James Bradfield Moody. The show features three inventors presenting their creations to a panel of judges, which includes science journalist Bernie Hobbs and inventor Christine Canin. The first segment introduces Paul Kinney from Walcha, near Tamworth, who presents a guitar designed to produce true stereo sound. Kinney explains that his instrument features two sound holes positioned to allow the player to hear the music in stereo, creating a more immersive experience for both the player and the listener. He is joined on stage by guitarist Bruce Matiski to demonstrate the acoustic differences compared to a standard acoustic guitar. The second invention is the GasTech system, developed by brothers Derek and Bruce Watkins from Dungog, north of Newcastle. This device is a retrofit LPG injection system for diesel engines that uses a computer and multiple sensors to inject the precise amount of gas needed for efficient combustion. The brothers claim the system reduces fuel consumption by one-third, lowers emissions, and increases engine power, with a payback period of three to six months for heavy vehicles. The final invention is Dry Eyes, created by Rod Casser, a device that seals over a child's forehead during bath time to keep water and shampoo out of their eyes. Casser demonstrates the product with his children, Jack and Lucy, highlighting how it gives the child a sense of control while freeing the parent's hands. Following the presentations, the judges deliberate on the best invention based on design, originality, and marketability. James Bradfield Moody announces that the GasTech system is the winner of the night, citing its strong market potential and efficient design. The episode concludes with an announcement for the People's Choice Award, inviting viewers to vote for their favorite invention via text, phone, or the ABC website. The broadcast also includes promotional segments for other ABC programs and a call for donations to the Victorian Bushfire Appeal.

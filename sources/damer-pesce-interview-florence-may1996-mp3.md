# damer-pesce-interview-florence-may1996.mp3

## Summary

This recording is an interview featuring Mark Pesce, a media and technology commentator, discussing his upcoming book on virtual worlds and the future of digital space. The conversation begins with Pesce describing the book as a starter kit for virtual environments, including guides on social etiquette and a companion website. He introduces his theory of structural coupling, arguing that informational relationships are always multi-way, contrasting this with top-down systems like fascism. Pesce posits that digital media will invert the relationship between physical distance and social proximity, creating a Cambrian explosion of new social forms. He notes that current technologies are merely the starting point of a revolution, similar to early electrical inventions, and emphasizes that the true impact lies in how these tools affect human emotion and perception rather than just their functional inventory. The discussion shifts to the social dynamics of virtual environments, referencing a virtual wedding Pesce participated in, which was mentally exhausting due to the intense focus required to maintain avatars. He predicts that virtual worlds will subsume traditional media and that synthetic organisms will evolve within these spaces to act as independent knowledge seekers. Addressing the concept of ownership, Pesce counters John Perry Barlow’s view that the internet should be purely interactive by arguing that matter and ownership are necessary components of a complete universe. He identifies himself as a radical ecologist who views cyberspace as the preeminent environment for planetary management, allowing for a panoptic mechanism that can monitor pollution and enforce social mores. He warns that while this technology has momentum, it lacks direction, and society must provide a vector to avoid pathological outcomes.

# sat-20100213-1110-Mark_Pesce_-_future_gaze-048.mp3

## Summary

This recording is a radio interview featuring Mark Pesce, identified as an author, futurist, and honorary associate in the Digital Cultures Programme at the University of Sydney. The host, Kim, interviews Pesce from Sydney ahead of his scheduled appearance at the Webstock 2010 technology conference. The conversation begins with a discussion on the unreliability of long-term predictions, with Pesce noting that technological timelines have compressed significantly. Pesce identifies augmented reality as the most exciting recent development, explaining how mobile phones with GPS, cameras, and fast network links allow users to overlay digital information onto the physical world. The host challenges this, arguing that tourists may spend more time staring at their devices than observing their surroundings, effectively substituting reality rather than augmenting it. Pesce acknowledges this concern but counters that such filtering provides a sense of security and that new tools will eventually emerge to manage the increasing information pressure, similar to how search engines evolved to handle the early web. The dialogue shifts to social media, where Pesce describes humans as inherently social beings whose capabilities have been rapidly amplified by platforms like Twitter and Facebook. He predicts that generic social networks will evolve into highly customized segments for specific groups, such as aging peers or academics. The interview then covers the future of books, with Pesce arguing that nonfiction texts will become more interconnected and searchable, dissolving the traditional linear format. He also discusses live streaming and continuous recording, noting that while it offers a complete record of life, it challenges human memory and editing habits. Further topics include the impact of technology on democracy, citing examples like Iran and China where governments are engaged in an arms race to block or monitor digital communication. Pesce reflects on the failure of early 3D web interfaces in the 1990s, attributing their demise to market unpreparedness. The interview concludes with a discussion on music consumption, where Pesce defends the album format against the trend of cherry-picking individual tracks, and a final reflection on the accelerating human attachment to mobile devices.

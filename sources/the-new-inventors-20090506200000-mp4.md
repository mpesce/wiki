# The New Inventors-20090506200000.mp4

## Summary

This recording is an episode of the Australian Broadcasting Corporation program The New Inventors, hosted by James O'Loughlin. The broadcast features a panel of judges, including engineer James Bradford Moody, science journalist Bernie Hobbs, and inventor and journalist Christine Kininmoth, who evaluate three distinct inventions. The episode begins with a segment on the Kool-Aid horse blanket, invented by Dave MacDonald from Koonia in South Tasmania. MacDonald explains that traditional horse rugs inhibit the animal's natural ability to regulate body temperature through pilo-erection, the raising and lowering of hair. His design uses soft rubber insulators to lift the blanket twelve millimeters off the horse's back, allowing for air circulation and sweat wicking while still providing protection from wind and rain. The panel discusses the thermal properties of the device, noting that it allows the horse to maintain its healthy core temperature of 37.8 degrees Celsius without overheating. The second invention presented is Cubio, a school bag organizer developed by Catherine Ryan and Gail Rose from Perth. The device is a soft plastic cube that attaches to a child's backpack, featuring static-cling stickers for daily essentials like hats and water bottles on the exterior, and daily cards for specific activities on the interior. The inventors demonstrate the system with a young child, showing how it helps children independently pack the correct items for their schedule, thereby reducing morning stress for parents and preventing distress for children who arrive at school without necessary items. The panel notes the product's robustness and food-grade safety, while also discussing the potential for children to peel the stickers, a feature the inventors explain is intentional to allow for customization and reusability. The final invention is the Tyre Down motorbike tie-down system, created by Rob Purcell and Rene Van Keken. This device addresses the common issue of damaging a motorcycle's suspension when compressing the forks to secure the bike to a trailer. The system uses a steel cradle and ratchet straps that grip the rear tire, allowing the bike to be secured without compressing the suspension.

## Summary

This recording is a lecture or talk delivered by Mark Pesce, a media and technology commentator and former engineer. The presentation explores the intersection of human evolutionary history, neuroscience, and modern technology, arguing that human weakness, rather than exceptionalism, is our primary defense against manipulative systems. Pesce begins by discussing the unique human relationship with time and reproduction, noting that unlike other mammals, humans lack a specific breeding season, leading to a constant state of sexual readiness. He connects this biological trait to the creation of early symbolic art, citing Paleolithic sculptures such as the Venus of Hohle Fels, the Venus of Willendorf, and the Venus of Laussel. These artifacts, created between 40,000 and 25,000 years ago, demonstrate an emerging culture where sex and time intersect, with the Venus of Laussel specifically showing evidence of lunar counting. Pesce then transitions to modern neuroscience, referencing Jerome Letwin’s 1959 paper on frog vision and the subsequent work of Humberto Maturana and Francisco Varela. He explains their concept of structural coupling, where systems interact and co-evolve through the exchange of information, summarized by the phrase "all doing is knowing and all knowing is doing." Applying this framework to the human eye, which he describes as an external part of the brain, Pesce argues that early artists used this coupling to create forms that mirrored human desire. He posits that culture serves as a management system for these innate, timeless sensual impulses.

# Piracy_is_Good 2.mp4

## Summary

This recording is a lecture delivered by Mark Pesce, a media and technology commentator, likely at the Australian Film, Television and Radio School (AFTRS) in late 2004. The talk, titled "Piracy is Good," argues that file-sharing technologies like BitTorrent have fundamentally altered the economics of television distribution. Pesce begins by explaining how BitTorrent works, noting that unlike traditional server-based distribution, the popularity of a file increases its availability rather than crashing the system. He cites the 2004 premiere of Battlestar Galactica and the leaked pilot of Doctor Who as examples where unauthorized downloads actually boosted viewership and cultural interest, suggesting that piracy acts as a marketing tool rather than a threat. Pesce critiques the traditional television value chain, which relies on an oligopoly of broadcasters in Australia to aggregate audiences for advertisers. He proposes a new model of "hyperdistribution" where producers bypass broadcasters and work directly with advertisers. In this model, advertising is embedded directly into the video stream via a subtle "bug" or watermark, rather than through traditional commercial breaks. This allows for a more seamless viewing experience and enables producers to monetize content globally without the high costs of traditional distribution. He argues that this model is more cost-effective for both producers and advertisers, as it leverages the existing desire of audiences to access content on demand across various devices. The speaker addresses potential objections, such as the difficulty of downloading files and the loss of DVD sales revenue, by predicting that user interfaces will improve and that the DVD market for TV shows is a temporary phenomenon. He also discusses the role of broadcasters, suggesting they will survive by focusing on live content like sports, news, and reality TV, which cannot be easily hyperdistributed. Pesce concludes by urging Australian producers to adapt to this new landscape, creating content that is valuable globally and suitable for fragmented viewing moments. He predicts that within the year, a successful hyperdistributed television program will emerge, marking the beginning of a new era in media economics where piracy is harnessed for commercial gain.

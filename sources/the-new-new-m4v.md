# The New New.m4v

## Summary

This recording is a talk titled "The New New" delivered by Mark Pesce, a media and technology commentator. Although no specific date is provided in the transcript, the speaker references recent events such as a "last week" announcement by Fairfax Media and a "Friday afternoon" front page story in the Sydney Morning Herald. The audience appears to be government officials or public sector employees, whom Pesce addresses as the interface between institutional control and external sharing. Pesce argues that technology has created a "new neurosis" characterized by low thresholds for anxiety and rapid information propagation. He illustrates this using the example of a controversial t-shirt from the brand Cotton On, which originated on the parenting blog Mama Mia, spread via email and Twitter, and eventually became front-page news in Australia within hours. He posits that networks amplify novelty, causing outrage to propagate wildly. The talk then shifts to the concept of "hyper-connectivity," which leads to "hyper-intelligence" and "hyper-empowerment" when communities pool their knowledge. Pesce uses RateMyProfessors.com as a primary example, explaining how student ratings destabilized university tenure systems by exposing poor teaching, a process he describes as inevitable and destabilizing. He notes that institutions like universities and newspapers are "melting" under the pressure of this sharing, leading to initiatives like Stanford’s iTunes University and paywalls at major media companies. Addressing the audience directly, Pesce warns that they are caught between internal pressures to lock down information and external pressures to share it immediately. He advises that resisting sharing only builds pressure, suggesting that when a community forms around shared information, it becomes impossible to control. He introduces the concept of "Government 2.0" and describes the audience as the "shock troops" of this revolution. The talk concludes with Pesce discussing his own book project, "Share This Book," which he is developing collaboratively to embody the principles he preaches. He emphasizes that sharing is not a single act but a continuous, constructive process that redefines authorship and responsibility, urging the audience to expect chaos and community engagement as they navigate this new landscape.

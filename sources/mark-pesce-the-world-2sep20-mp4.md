# Mark Pesce The World 2Sep20.mp4

## Summary

This recording is a news interview featuring Mark Pesce, identified as a futurist, discussing recent developments in social media moderation and digital regulation. The segment opens with a report on Facebook dismantling a Russian-linked network called Peace Data, which was targeting left-leaning readers with misinformation about the Biden campaign. Pesce analyzes this action as a subtle continuation of Russian interference tactics aimed at polarizing the American left, noting that such methods are likely to be copied by other groups. He argues that the political environment in the United States is hungry for slanted information, acting as an accelerant for disinformation. Pesce questions Facebook's ability to maintain vigilance across two billion users with only ten weeks remaining until the election, suggesting that the platform faces a whack-a-mole scenario against agile disinformation networks. The conversation then shifts to the QAnon movement, which Pesce describes as a form of emerging 21st-century religion or belief system. He explains that these groups evade bans by code-switching their language and re-establishing themselves, allowing them to move faster than large social networks can react. The discussion also touches on the dark web, where Pesce highlights the paradox of tools designed to protect political dissidents being misused by those seeking to dismantle the state. Finally, the interview addresses Facebook's threat to block news sharing in Australia in response to government pressure to pay for local news content. Pesce criticizes this strategy, arguing that Facebook has misread the room by publicly claiming monopoly power while facing scrutiny from the Australian Competition and Consumer Commission.

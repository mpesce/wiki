# EGA.wav

## Summary

This recording captures a talk by Mark Pesce at the Electronic Frontier Australia (EGA) conference, where he returns after a thirteen-year break. Pesce, a media and technology commentator, frames his presentation as "technology as a drug," contrasting it with the conference's broader theme of drugs as technology. He issues a warning to attendees with paranoid disorders, describing the talk as "paranoid, cray-cray, and dystopian," and distributes tinfoil to the audience as a symbolic prop. The core of his argument centers on Facebook’s evolution into a global cognitive bias amplifier. He cites a 2017 report in The Australian alleging that Facebook pitched advertisers on targeting stressed teenagers, and references a 2014 study demonstrating emotional contagion through news feed manipulation. Pesce argues that Facebook’s machine learning algorithms profile users to maximize engagement by confirming their existing beliefs, effectively creating personalized realities that erode the public sphere and accelerate political polarization. Pesce extends this critique to the broader era of surveillance capitalism, noting that the techniques used by Facebook are now being applied across connected systems, from casinos to political campaigns. He highlights the emergence of augmented reality as a next-stage surveillance tool, predicting that devices like smart glasses will allow corporations to curate a user’s entire visual reality based on their behavioral profiles. He references Cambridge Analytica’s role in the Brexit and US elections as evidence of weaponized influence. In response to these threats, Pesce offers three pieces of advice: know your substance by maintaining healthy skepticism of free digital services, know your source by verifying information independently, and know yourself by practicing mindfulness regarding one’s relationship with social media. He emphasizes that the problem is not just technological but deeply human, rooted in innate cognitive biases that are now being amplified by artificial intelligence. The recording concludes with a Q&A session moderated by Kat. Audience members ask about using technology for good, the potential for market alternatives to Facebook, and the impact of social media on language. Pesce discusses the dangers of state regulation, comparing it to China’s social credit system, and advocates for commons-based thinking over market-driven solutions. He mentions emerging decentralized technologies like Solid and P2P networks as potential tools for reclaiming data privacy. He also addresses practical security measures, such as using password managers, VPNs, and Tor, while acknowledging that security is a moving target. The session ends with a discussion on parenting in the digital age, where Pesce advises de-emphasizing virtual experiences in favor of real-world social connections for young children.

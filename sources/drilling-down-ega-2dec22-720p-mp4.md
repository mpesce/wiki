# Drilling Down EGA 2Dec22 720p.mp4

## Summary

This recording is a personal talk delivered by Mark Pesce, a media and technology commentator, at an event referred to as EGA. Pesce begins with an acknowledgment of the Gadigal lands of the Eora nation and notes that he is approaching his sixtieth birthday. He frames his narrative through the lens of a man entering his third turn of life, emphasizing that the outcome of his words is more important than credit. The central topic is a detailed account of his experience with a severe dental abscess and the subsequent failed attempt to save tooth number 14 via a root canal, which ultimately required extraction. Pesce describes the intense pain he endured starting in early August, leading to a visit to an endodontist in Chatswood. During the procedure, he experienced a moment of metaphysical illumination while under local anesthesia, realizing that his presence in the chair was the result of a long sweep of civilizational choices aimed at alleviating human suffering. He argues that modern dentistry and medicine represent a collective effort to reduce misery, a progress he views as a positive aspect of human karma. However, he posits that this same drive to alleviate suffering through material culture has inadvertently led to environmental feedback loops that threaten human sustainability. He contends that the planet is being damaged not by selfishness, but by an excess of human caring and the material manifestations of that care. The talk concludes with the difficult extraction of the tooth in September, which Pesce uses as a metaphor for the challenges facing humanity. He references the temporary environmental improvements observed during the 2020 pandemic lockdowns, suggesting that people witnessed a different way of being that was quickly forgotten. Pesce argues that humanity possesses more agency than it admits, having already demonstrated the capacity to change through collective action. He rejects nihilism, asserting that while the current path is unsustainable, the opportunity for transformation is present, and that individuals must accept the responsibility of their actions to navigate this crisis.

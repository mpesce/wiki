# The New Inventors-20090218200000.mp4

## Summary

This recording is an episode of the Australian television program The New Inventors, hosted by James O'Loughlin. The show features a panel of judges, including agricultural scientist Chris Russell, science journalist Bernie Hobbs, and interior designer Alison Page, who evaluate various practical inventions submitted by members of the public. The episode begins with a preview of upcoming ABC programming, including a wildlife documentary about the Caribbean and a segment on chimpanzees, before transitioning to the main content. The first invention presented is the Cracker, a removable cargo tray system for utility vehicles designed by David Herman. The system allows tradespeople to unload their entire tool tray at once rather than removing items individually, reducing physical strain and theft risk. The tray uses roof racks that invert into legs with jacking screws and wheels, allowing it to be maneuvered off the vehicle and used as a mobile workbench. The judges discuss the cost, approximately $2,500 to $2,700, and the necessity of operating on level ground. Subsequent segments feature Max, an 18-year-old who invented the scuba sleeve, a neoprene accessory holder that keeps diving gear secure and accessible while keeping hands free. Karen Gordon and Greg Raditch from Perth present a reusable barricade tape dispenser that winds and retracts tape to reduce waste on mining and construction sites. Another inventor demonstrates a steel fire guard designed to contain embers and prevent people from rolling into campfires. The final invention is a guidance system for mechanical weeders that uses magnetic markers to detect underground drip irrigation pipes, automatically steering the machine to avoid damaging the lines. The episode concludes with promotional clips for other ABC shows, including Love, Cranford, and The Scruffy and the Unloved.

## Summary

This recording is a lecture delivered by Mark Pesce, a futurist and former Apple engineer, via Zoom to an audience at the Australian National University, likely associated with the 3AI initiative. Pesce begins by acknowledging the Gadigal people of the Eora Nation and notes that this is his first day teaching remotely, a shift from his previous in-person sessions. He frames his work as helping people understand time scales to make reasonable decisions, a skill he argues is critical during the pandemic. He references a conversation with a friend in Massachusetts, an epicenter of the US pandemic, who expressed fear that his daughter’s future had become unknowable. Pesce uses this anecdote to introduce the concept of "dense time," a period where significant events occur so rapidly that the future becomes unpredictable. Pesce draws a parallel between the pandemic and the AI concept of the "singularity," which he defines not as a machine takeover, but as a state where exponential change causes the forward planning horizon to contract. He cites a Wired article and a personal observation from colleague Sally Dominguez to explain that when exponential growth hits its peak, the horizon bends into a wall, rendering standard predictive models useless. He identifies the peak of this dense time in the US and Australia around March 11-12, 2020, marked by political addresses and panic buying. During this phase, he argues, the forward planning horizon shrank to approximately 30 minutes, triggering survival instincts and a loss of sanity based on the expectation of a stable future. As the curve flattened in Australia, Pesce describes the gradual expansion of the planning horizon from 30 minutes to days, and eventually to weeks, allowing for a sense of normalcy and boredom. However, he warns that dense time is not over, particularly in the United States, where the pandemic remains acute. He posits that the world now operates on multiple time scales, creating a need for empathy between those exiting lockdown and those still in crisis.

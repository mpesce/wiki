# Smartsheet - Reimagining the Future of Work.mp4

## Summary

This recording is a talk delivered by a speaker who references a recent column in IEEE Spectrum, addressing the future of work in the post-pandemic era. The speaker begins by analyzing a chart of United States productivity since 1947, noting that while productivity plateaued around 2000 despite massive IT investments, the March 2020 lockdown revealed the true utility of that infrastructure. The speaker argues that the pandemic flipped a switch, allowing office workers to realize they had been using "chainsaws" to do work previously done with "stone knives," fundamentally changing how workers perceive their tools and capabilities. The main topic is the fracturing of the workforce into three distinct cohorts: those who prefer full-time office work, those who prefer full-time remote work, and the emerging hybrid workforce. The speaker estimates that pre-pandemic office workers now represent only about 25 percent of the workforce, while remote workers may reach 25 percent, and hybrid workers will comprise approximately 40 percent. The talk details the specific challenges for managers in each category. For office-loving extroverts, managers must help scaffold relationships in a less crowded environment. For remote workers, who are fighting for permanent flexibility, managers must provide regular check-ins to prevent overwork and maintain connection, a dynamic described as the "no normal." For hybrid workers, the speaker emphasizes that policy must be a continuous negotiation rather than a fixed rule, as every hybrid worker has unique needs. The speaker concludes by stating that the future favors flexibility, noting that 40 percent of American workers intend to change jobs within 12 months to find more flexible organizations. Consequently, the physical office is being redesigned to focus on team building, serendipity, and collaboration rather than individual desk work. Managers are urged to take on new roles as facilitators of these connections and negotiators of hybrid policies, supported by new digital tools. The speaker ends by urging organizations to support their managers, who now face a dramatically increased workload in managing these diverse and evolving workforce structures.

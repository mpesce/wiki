## Summary

This recording is a segment from the Australian radio program Background Briefing, featuring a discussion on the state of the global technology investment market and whether a new dot-com bubble is forming. The host introduces the topic by noting that while the Global Financial Crisis and the 2000 internet crash should have made investors cautious, recent data suggests a resurgence in venture capital, with over $5 billion flowing into new web companies in the first four months of the year. The segment features three guests: Jody Rich, CEO of the San Francisco-based company People Browser; Violet Ho, managing director at Kroll Consulting in Beijing; and Mark Pesce, a web entrepreneur and honorary associate for the Digital Cultures Program at the University of Sydney. Jody Rich argues against the bubble narrative, suggesting that the current tech landscape is more viable than the late 1990s because companies like Facebook and Twitter are achieving revenue positivity quickly. He describes the investment environment as a Darwinian process where some startups fail while others succeed, noting that venture capital is currently abundant for social media startups. He highlights the ability of social data to provide real-time intelligence on global events, such as political revolutions and natural disasters, which drives corporate interest in sentiment analysis. Mark Pesce, who recently served as a judge at a startup competition in San Francisco, describes the atmosphere as one of exuberance, with bidding wars breaking out for promising ideas. He cites a company called Green Goose, which creates cheap movement sensors, as an example of a startup that secured angel investment. Pesce notes that the angel investor community has become more open and competitive, driving up deal values. However, he argues that a true bubble is unlikely because major companies like Facebook and Twitter are not rushing to go public, unlike during the 2000 crash. Instead, capital is flowing into private companies, leading to job creation rather than a speculative frenzy that would harm the broader economy. Violet Ho provides a perspective from China, noting that foreign investment in Chinese internet companies has been ongoing for a decade, with successful examples like Alibaba and Baidu.

# The New Inventors-20070926200000.mp4

## Summary

This recording is an episode of the Australian television program The New Inventors, hosted by James Mathison. The show features a panel of experts, including agricultural scientist Chris Russell, designer Sally Deminks, and woodworker Richard Vaughan, who evaluate three new inventions. The first invention presented is the Will Save power line detector, developed by Ken and Peter Wilson from Victoria. This device is designed for tipper trucks and lifting equipment to prevent electrocution by detecting overhead power lines and automatically shutting down the hydraulics. The inventors explain that the system uses a wireless sensor to avoid the hazards of wiring along the truck tray, and the panel discusses the device's failsafe nature and estimated cost of five to eleven thousand dollars. The second invention is the Stubby Cooler, created by Nathan Jones from New South Wales. This device uses a thermoelectric Peltier element and a cooling fan to keep beverages cold in a standard stubby holder. Jones demonstrates that the unit can maintain a drink at refrigerator temperature for extended periods and notes that the technology can be reversed to keep drinks hot. The panel praises the portability and originality of the concept, suggesting it could be sold as a standalone insert for various cup holders. The final invention is Panelform, a permanent formwork system for building concrete structures, presented by Paul Blazevic from the Gold Coast. Made from high-impact plastic, the panels clip together to form a mold for walls, eliminating the need to remove formwork after concrete is poured. Blazevic highlights the speed and cost savings of the system, which he developed after helping build his family home in the 1970s. The panel debates the structural integrity and aesthetic perception of plastic walls. After a three-way tie in the judges' votes, the panel reaches a consensus to award the win to Panelfov, placing Blazevic in the running for the Inventor of the Year award. The episode concludes with a prompt for viewers to vote for the People's Choice Award via text or phone.

## Summary

This recording is a keynote talk delivered by Mark Pesce at the Australian Social Innovation Exchange, sponsored by COFA, Headship, and Cisco. The event took place on a Saturday morning in 2010, as indicated by Pesce’s reference to the current year and his recent approval for Australian citizenship, which he received just two weeks prior to the speech. Pesce opens by sharing his personal pride in becoming a citizen, noting he scored a perfect score on the citizenship test, before pivoting to a broader critique of Australian materialism. The central theme of the talk is the paradox of abundance in Australia, which Pesce describes as a "lucky country" where citizens possess an excessive amount of wealth and technology. He illustrates this point by displaying a collection of obsolete mobile phones he has accumulated, arguing that these devices represent a "miniaturized Australia" that drives economic growth in the developing world. While acknowledging the positive impact of mobile technology on global poverty, Pesce highlights the environmental and existential costs of constant consumption. He argues that the more material objects people own, the more they are possessed by the need to acquire, maintain, and upgrade them, leading to a spiritual redirection away from personal needs. Pesce extends this critique to the housing market, comparing the current real estate bubble to the historical Tulip Mania. He suggests that Australians are effectively selling themselves into thirty-year slavery to banks to maintain their lifestyle, living in a state of constant anxiety over economic stability. He posits that the nation is stretched to a breaking point, facing a choice between continued material servitude or a return to pre-industrial scarcity.

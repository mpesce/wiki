# Citi How to Thrive in the 2020s 16Oct19.mp3

## Summary

This recording is a keynote talk delivered by Mark Pesce, a media and technology commentator, addressing an audience in Australia. The speech, likely delivered in late 2019 given the references to upcoming anniversaries and recent announcements, explores the intersection of automation, artificial intelligence, and human capability in the financial sector. Pesce opens by noting the rapid pace of change, citing the recent shift to zero-cost online trading by major US firms like Robinhood, Charles Schwab, and E-Trade as a catalyst for increased automation. The main body of the talk focuses on the emergence of digital currencies, specifically Facebook’s proposed Libra, and its potential to unlock commerce for billions of unbanked individuals in developing nations. Pesce argues that the transition to digital assets will lead to a massive increase in transaction volume and data, necessitating the use of machine learning to process this information. He draws a parallel to the spreadsheet, describing digital wallets and trading tools as "super tools" that will become indispensable. However, he highlights a fundamental flaw in machine learning: it is designed to recognize patterns from the past and cannot identify novel opportunities or "the future." Consequently, while algorithms excel at looking backward, humans possess a unique evolutionary advantage in looking forward, often described as intuition or a "spidey sense." Pesce concludes by arguing that the future of work and finance depends on humans leveraging their forward-looking capabilities while using automated tools for backward-looking analysis. He posits that organizations must evolve from static structures to dynamic trajectories that prioritize identifying, mentoring, and sharing talent. He predicts that by 2030, work, education, and mentoring will merge, with professionals spending half their time learning new skills and the other half teaching others. The talk ends with the assertion that human cooperation and the sharing of excellence are the only sustainable competitive advantages against the scale and speed of automated systems.

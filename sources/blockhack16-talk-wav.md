# BlockHack16 talk.WAV

## Summary

This recording captures the opening keynote and panel discussion for BlockHack 16, a blockchain hackathon hosted by Westpac in late May 2016. The event, held on the traditional lands of Indigenous Australians, was organized following an internal survey that revealed significant employee interest in blockchain technology. The host, Mark Pesce, introduced the event by comparing the current moment to the first World Wide Web conference in 1994, suggesting that blockchain is poised to fundamentally change finance. He emphasized that the participating teams are developing solutions that challenge existing legal and financial systems, indicating a need for more resilient infrastructure. The panel featured three Westpac executives: Rob Wilson, the CTO; Mike Baldwin, Head of Transaction Solutions; and Ian Hill, Head of Innovation. Wilson described blockchain as part of a broader disruption wave aimed at removing friction from customer lives, noting that while many fintechs use the technology, the "killer app" has yet to be found. Baldwin highlighted a successful proof of concept using Ripple for international payments to Fiji and New Zealand, which demonstrated faster and cheaper transactions compared to traditional telegraphic transfers. He stressed the importance of building a network of participants for blockchain solutions to be effective. Hill focused on the concept of "build to think," advising teams to prototype quickly to clarify ideas and solve real-world problems rather than just building for the sake of using blockchain. The panelists also addressed questions regarding regulation and trust. They agreed that while regulations may not need to change drastically, regulators must broaden their view of what constitutes a financial service to accommodate new technologies. They noted that trust is built empirically through safe, incident-free usage over time. The session concluded with housekeeping instructions, including emergency procedures, meal times, and the assignment of mentors to various teams. Participants were directed to their respective rooms on the 27th and 28th floors to begin their two-day hacking period, with a pitching competition scheduled for the following afternoon.

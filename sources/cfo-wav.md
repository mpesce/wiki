# CFO.wav

## Summary

This recording is a talk delivered by Mark Pesce, a professional futurist, to an audience of Chief Financial Officers. The session focuses on the evolving landscape of banking, the practical limits of artificial intelligence, and the strategic implications of blockchain technology. Pesce begins by defining his role as someone who identifies the arc of the future rather than predicting specific outcomes, using William Gibson’s quote about the future being unevenly distributed as a guiding principle. He illustrates this concept by discussing the "opportunity crisis" in Sydney’s fintech sector, specifically highlighting a new bank being built from scratch without legacy systems. He contrasts this agile startup with traditional Australian banks that rely on outdated COBOL code, arguing that the new model uses blockchain and open banking APIs to create a platform for third-party financial services rather than a siloed institution. Pesce addresses the fear of job displacement by machines, refuting the idea that automation will replace human roles in the near term. He uses the example of self-driving Teslas to show that while machines excel in predictable environments like highways, they struggle with the complex, exception-heavy nature of city driving, such as navigating Sydney’s George Street. He argues that the future of work involves "pair play," where humans and machines collaborate to outperform either alone, a concept demonstrated by the game of Go. For CFOs, this means machines will handle routine tasks, allowing humans to focus on complex strategic problems and edge cases. The final segment of the talk explores the rise of smart contracts and "smart money." Pesce details how blockchain technology will automate financial processes, citing the upcoming migration of the Australian Securities Exchange to a blockchain-based settlement system. He discusses the potential for self-executing contracts in trade finance and the disruptive potential of decentralized applications, while also noting the security risks illustrated by the 2016 DAO hack. He concludes by advising that the future role of the CFO will shift toward strategic guidance, overseeing the deployment of smart contracts and ensuring that automated financial systems align with business goals. He recommends that aspiring professionals pursue dual degrees in finance or law combined with computer science to navigate this new landscape.

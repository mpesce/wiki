# HowNotToBeSeen.mp4

## Summary

This recording is a keynote address delivered by Mark Pesce at the first Python convention. Pesce, a media and technology commentator, begins by reflecting on his four-decade history with programming languages, tracing his path from RPG and assembly to C++ and JavaScript. He explains that he recently adopted Python, finding its power and community ethos compelling, which led him to install it on all his devices. This technical shift coincided with his growing frustration with centralized social networks, particularly Facebook, which he views as a tool for corporate data extraction that compromises user privacy. Pesce details his decision to leave Facebook, describing it as a "digital refugee" fleeing a regime that prioritizes its own interests over user autonomy. He argues that individuals must reclaim their social graphs, which he considers their most important possession, by moving away from monolithic platforms. To address this, he introduces Plexus, a second-generation, decentralized social network architecture written entirely in Python. The system is designed to be personal, portable, and private, allowing users to run their own instance and manage connections through a modular database called the Plex. The core of the talk is a live demonstration of Plexus, where Pesce shows how the system can share a URL across multiple services, including Twitter, Posterous, Tumblr, and SMTP, using separate Python modules for sharing and listening. He emphasizes that Plexus is open source and intended to be a grassroots, bottom-up alternative to corporate social media. Pesce concludes by comparing the project to the early days of Napster, arguing that decentralized tools empower users to control their own communication channels. He frames the project as a social experiment that requires community collaboration to succeed, highlighting Python’s culture of sharing as the ideal foundation for this new infrastructure.

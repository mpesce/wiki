# The New Inventors-20090715200000.mp4

## Summary

This recording is an episode of the Australian television program The New Inventors, hosted by James O'Loughlin. The show features three inventors presenting their creations to a panel of judges, which includes engineer James Bradford Moody, science journalist Bernie Hobbes, and futurist Mark Pesci. The first segment introduces Matthew Walker from Brisbane, who presents the Pass Window, a credit card with a transparent window and printed pattern designed to combat online fraud. Walker explains that the card superimposes over a digital pattern on a screen to reveal a unique, dynamic four-digit password for each transaction, offering a cheaper and more secure alternative to electronic tokens. The judges praise the design and originality of the solution. The second invention is the CPEN, presented by Alan Barber, which is a floating pen that shrink-wraps a boat's hull to prevent barnacle growth and eliminate the need for toxic anti-fouling agents. Barber demonstrates how the pen isolates the boat in a pool of water, which is then pumped out, allowing external water pressure to press a skin and rope mat tightly against the hull. The system costs between $12,000 and $50,000 depending on boat size and includes an automatic bilge pump to handle rain or splashing. The third invention is the PENOGUARD, a safety device for construction sites that covers access holes in floors. It functions as a mesh gate when closed to signal danger and converts into a solid guardrail when opened for material transport, preventing workers from falling through holes. The judges ultimately select Matthew Walker's Pass Window as the winner of the episode, citing its strength in design, massive potential market, and originality. The episode concludes with a mention of a People's Choice Award for viewers to vote on, followed by a news segment covering various topics including a missing British backpacker found in the Blue Mountains, an Iranian plane crash, a corruption conviction in Queensland, and swine flu preparations. The broadcast ends with promotional segments for upcoming ABC television programs.

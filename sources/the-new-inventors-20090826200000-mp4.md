# The New Inventors-20090826200000.mp4

## Summary

This recording is an episode of the Australian television program The New Inventors, hosted by James O'Loughlin. The show features three inventors presenting their creations to a panel of judges, which includes interior designer Alison Page, surgeon Professor Fiona Wood, and futurist Mark Pesce. The first invention presented is the RoboCam Pro, a motorized camera jib developed by Christopher Collins. Collins explains that his device combines a jib arm, track, tripod, and motion control rig into a single, lightweight unit that is significantly cheaper and easier to set up than traditional heavy equipment. He demonstrates its ability to perform complex, sweeping camera movements in confined spaces, noting that a software version allowing for precise, repeatable motion paths is currently in development. The second invention involves a medical procedure for repairing corneas damaged by stem cell deficiency, presented by Dr. Stephanie Watson and Dr. Nick DiGirolamo. They describe a process where healthy stem cells are harvested from a patient's eye, cultured on a contact lens for ten days, and then implanted into the damaged eye to restore sight. The doctors highlight that this outpatient procedure avoids the need for corneal transplants and could potentially be adapted to treat other conditions, such as retinal disorders or skin wounds. The final invention is the Blue Point system, created by Graham Givens, which recycles worn mining bucket teeth by welding a hardened steel fitting onto them. This process reduces the cost of replacement teeth by approximately fifty percent and provides work for local welders. The judges deliberate on the merits of the three inventions, with Alison Page praising the originality of the stem cell technique and the elegant design of the camera rig. Professor Wood emphasizes the safety and simplicity of using autologous cells for eye repair. Ultimately, the panel selects the stem cell eye repair method as the winner of the episode. The program concludes by inviting viewers to vote for the people's choice award via text or phone, and teases upcoming segments from other ABC programs, including a special edition of Q and A live from the Melbourne Writers Festival.

# Final Thoughts.mp3

## Summary

This recording is the final episode of a six-part podcast series hosted by Mark Pesce, a media commentator and former engineer. In this concluding segment, Pesce revisits the statistics of atmospheric carbon dioxide to correct a previous claim made in the first episode. He explains that while CO2 levels rose by a third since his birth nearly 59 years ago, the more significant metric is the increase from pre-industrial levels. He notes that before the mid-18th century, atmospheric CO2 stood at 270 parts per million. It took approximately 200 years to rise by 49 parts per million to reach the level present when he was born. By the time he turns 60 at the end of the next year, levels are projected to reach 417 parts per million, representing a total increase of 98 parts per million from pre-industrial times. Pesce argues that this means his lifetime has seen twice as much CO2 added to the atmosphere as all of prior human history combined, effectively doubling the pre-modern baseline rather than merely increasing it by a third. Pesce reflects on the prosperity and technological advances of his generation, acknowledging that while wealth has not been evenly distributed, he has been a beneficiary of this era. He frames the current moment as a time for those in positions of power, such as CEOs, deans, and entrepreneurs, to assess what they owe to society for the opportunities they have received. He references the IPCC’s sixth report from August 2021 and its use of the term shared socio-economic pathways, interpreting this language as a recognition that climate change is a collective problem requiring a shared project.

# supertools.mkv

## Summary

This recording is a keynote talk delivered by Mark Pesce, a media and technology commentator, at an event hosted by Dr. Bell. Pesce opens by acknowledging the Ngunnawal people and frames the presentation as a personal exploration of his lifelong passion for technology. He traces the evolution of "super tools" that have fundamentally shaped modern civilization, beginning with the spreadsheet, which he identifies as the first killer app that enabled the personal computing revolution and the financial management of late capitalism. He then discusses the World Wide Web, noting its 30th anniversary and its role in amplifying collective intelligence and creating a new form of knowledge formation, exemplified by Wikipedia. The third historical super tool is the smartphone, which he describes as a postmodern equivalent to the metal ax head, achieving universal ubiquity in just 12 years by detaching the web from specific space and time. Pesce argues that these existing tools have created a new environment and predicts the emergence of three corresponding "reflection" super tools for the next 30 years. The first is augmented reality, which he views as a response to the attention-hogging nature of smartphones. He describes AR as a means to blend computer-generated environments with the real world, effectively rewriting consensus reality. This technology will reveal the "digital depth" of the physical world, where every material object has a data double. The second reflection is the population of real space with this data, a project he compares to the early web's population of digital space. The third and final super tool is a new form of intelligence that manages the overwhelming density of this data. Using the example of AlphaGo and the game of Go, Pesce illustrates a model of "pair play," where human and artificial intelligence form a complementary partnership. He argues that neither human nor machine intelligence alone is sufficient; together, they amplify each other's capacity to handle complexity. The talk concludes with Pesce connecting these technological developments to the visions of Buckminster Fuller and Douglas Engelbart. He cites Fuller’s concept of "Spaceship Earth" and Engelbart’s 1968 "Mother of All Demos" as foundational works that anticipated the need for tools to manage global complexity. Pesce posits that the ultimate goal of these super tools is to provide humanity with the sight to understand and act at a global scale, ensuring a sustainable civilization. He frames this not as a rejection of AI, but as a human story of artificial intelligence, where the partnership between human and machine intelligence is necessary to navigate the blinding light of digital depth.

# The New Inventors-20091028200000.mp4

## Summary

This recording is an episode of the Australian television show New Inventors, hosted by James Bradfield Moody. The program features three inventors presenting their creations to a panel of judges, which includes science journalist Bernie Hobbs and inventor and journalist Christine Kanimon. The first invention presented is the More Safe Rail by Dan Morby, a flexible racetrack barrier designed to protect jockeys and horses from injury. The rail uses a mechanism where posts pop out upon impact, allowing a rider to pass through without hitting a solid steel or aluminum post. Morby explains that the system also reduces the time required to move rails between race meetings from two days to five hours. The second invention is Braille Oz by Jim Tapscott, a machine that automates the creation of Braille signage. Tapscott demonstrates how the device uses a modified engraver to drill holes and insert individual beads, a process that currently takes hours when done by hand with tweezers. He notes that the machine costs approximately 1,550 dollars and is multilingual, as the characters are determined by the software program. The third invention is the Anti Vortex Suction Cover by Gary Hall, a retrofit for spa and pool suction inlets. Hall’s design uses tapered fins to reduce the whirlpool effect that causes hair entanglement, a leading cause of drowning for children under 14. The cover costs about 22 dollars and can be installed by a pool specialist in half an hour. The judges evaluate the inventions based on criteria such as need, safety, originality, design, and marketability. While the panel acknowledges the strong safety benefits of all three inventions, they debate the relative urgency of protecting children versus professional athletes. Ultimately, the judges select the Anti Vortex Suction Cover as the winner of the episode, placing Gary Hall in the running for the Inventor of the Year award. The episode concludes with a prompt for viewers to vote for the People’s Choice Award and a preview of upcoming ABC One programs.

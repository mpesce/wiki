# TheFuturists.mp4

## Summary

This recording is an episode of the podcast The Futurist, hosted by Rob Tercek, featuring an interview with Mark Pesce, a futurist and founder of the AI consultancy Wisely AI. Tercek notes that Pesce is visiting Los Angeles from Sydney, and the conversation takes place shortly before a futurist conference scheduled for September 22 and 23 in Dubai. The discussion centers on the practical application of artificial intelligence in business, challenging the prevailing narrative that AI will immediately replace human workers. Pesce argues that most organizations are using AI incorrectly, treating it as a magic wand rather than a tool that requires a deep understanding of existing business processes. He highlights that while AI excels at summarizing and classifying data, it often fails at complex process automation, with some studies showing that developers using AI coding tools actually experience a decline in productivity due to the time required to verify and debug the generated code. The dialogue expands to address the societal and educational impacts of AI dependency. Pesce and Tercek discuss how the use of chatbots in education may hinder the development of critical thinking and cognitive skills, comparing it to using training wheels that prevent learning. They also explore the concept of "vibe coding," where non-experts generate software through prompts, noting that while this creates functional prototypes, it often results in unreliable code with significant security vulnerabilities. The conversation touches on the reliability of AI agents, explaining that because agents decompose goals into multiple steps, small error rates compound, making autonomous execution unreliable without constant human oversight. Finally, the hosts address the political and cultural implications of AI, including the "woke" controversy surrounding Elon Musk’s Grok model and the potential for AI to reinforce echo chambers or spread disinformation. Pesce references the phenomenon of "ChatGPT psychosis," where individuals become trapped in feedback loops with sycophantic AI systems. The interview concludes with a historical perspective on job displacement, noting that while automation will continue to eliminate roles in information shuffling and middle management, it has historically led to economic growth and the creation of new sectors, particularly in care and trades. Pesce advises that the focus should be on reevaluating the value of human labor and maintaining organizational resilience rather than panicking about mass unemployment.

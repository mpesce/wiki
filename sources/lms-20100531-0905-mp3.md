# lms_20100531_0905.mp3

## Summary

This recording is a segment from the radio program Life Matters, featuring an interview with social network strategist Laurel Papworth and Mark Pesce, a writer and honorary associate in digital cultures at the University of Sydney. The discussion centers on the "Quick Campaign," a protest movement urging users to quit Facebook due to privacy concerns and the platform's opaque data practices. The host notes that while the movement started in Canada with thousands of sign-ups, Facebook recently gained ten million new users, suggesting the exodus is minor in scale. Mark Pesce argues that Facebook has gathered the largest wealth of marketing data in history and cannot be trusted with user information. He describes the social graph as a valuable asset that has been extracted from users' minds and placed in the real world, requiring protection. Pesce criticizes Facebook for lacking transparency and negotiating terms with users, instead imposing rules unilaterally. He also highlights the involvement of investors like Peter Thiel and In-Q-Tel, the CIA’s technology investment arm, raising concerns about data access by intelligence agencies. Laurel Papworth counters that quitting is a recurring, ineffective tactic and that targeted advertising can be beneficial by providing contextually relevant information rather than generic broadcasts. She argues that users often misunderstand the utility of data sharing and that privacy settings are manageable with a few clicks. The conversation shifts to data retention, where Pesce asserts that users should have the right to delete their information upon leaving. Papworth disagrees, arguing that retaining data is necessary for law enforcement to track predators and organized crime who delete accounts after making connections. She states she trusts Facebook more than law enforcement agencies regarding data handling.

# hyperpeople.mp3

## Summary

This recording is a talk by Mark Pesce, a media and technology commentator, delivered to an audience he describes as a "homecoming" and "family." The presentation, titled "Welcome to the Swarm," explores the shift from centralized mass media to decentralized, peer-to-peer information systems. Pesce begins by discussing the 2004 release of the Battlestar Galactica revival, noting how the show was shared globally via BitTorrent before its official US broadcast. He explains BitTorrent as a mechanism that allows information to "swarm," arguing that this technology marks the end of centralized media control and the rise of amateur creators who can produce meaningful content without catering to mass market tastes. Pesce then transitions to the concept of a "knowledge swarm" using Wikipedia as a primary example. He contrasts the commercial failure of the Encyclopedia Britannica’s online launch with the exponential growth of Wikipedia, which was created in 2001 by combining Wiki technology with an open encyclopedia model. He describes how Wikipedia aggregates human expertise through a simple act of seduction, where users feel ownership over the content they edit and share. He illustrates this with a personal anecdote about updating the Wikipedia page for Camilla Parker Bowles during her 2005 wedding to Prince Charles. The talk further examines the personalization of media consumption through devices like the iPod, warning that while these tools offer freedom, they risk isolating individuals in self-created sensory bubbles and eroding the shared reality that binds communities. In the final section, Pesce introduces emerging tools designed to restore social trust and understanding, such as the Outfoxed browser plugin and the Isellipedia mobile application. These platforms allow users to share ratings and ask questions within their personal social networks, moving beyond the factual data of Wikipedia to a "swarm of understanding." He concludes by challenging the audience to develop an "ecology of the common mind," suggesting that we need dietary guidelines and exercise for the mind to navigate the overwhelming abundance of media. The session ends with a Q&A covering topics like the I Ching, the limitations of social networks like MySpace, and the role of trust in a fragmented digital world.

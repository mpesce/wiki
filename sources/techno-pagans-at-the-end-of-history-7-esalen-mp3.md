# Techno-Pagans at the End of History 7(Esalen).mp3

## Summary

Part of 'Techno-Pagans at the End of History' (Esalen Institute, 2001) — a multi-day participatory weekend co-hosted by Mark Pesce, Douglas Rushkoff, and Erik Davis. The group moves between long-form conversation and open discussion with the audience, spanning virtual reality, psychedelics, nanotechnology, religion, and the end of history. (Episode 7.) Mark continues on the strange physics of time and computation: 'if you could digitize yourself at 400 megahertz, an hour is long enough for you to live your life 50 times over.' He draws the black-hole analogy — from the exterior observer's view, falling into the event horizon is a terminal event at time zero, but from the falling person's frame, 'the distance it takes to fall from the event horizon into the core of the black hole is forever... time itself breaks down as a concept.' As 'reality gets more and more bizarre and more and more unmanageable' and technology 'hurls open more and more doorways onto nano-worlds, virtual worlds, telepathic worlds, artificial life, AI,' 'reality is mutating before our very eyes.' He frames the job of people like them as 'to assuage other people's anxiety.' The conversation then turns to a very practical problem: OVERPOPULATION, which 'tends to erode every social justice program, every effort to clean up water, every effort to deal with AIDS in the third world, every effort to expand the educational base or expand connectivity — there are too many people.' It's 'not a technological problem... it's a political and social problem,' and 'if every person would parent only one child, population would drop 50% in 30 years.'

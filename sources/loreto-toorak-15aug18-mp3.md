# Loreto Toorak 15Aug18.mp3

## Summary

This recording is a talk by Mark Pesce, a media and technology commentator, delivered to an audience of educators. The presentation focuses on the role of mentoring in preparing students for a rapidly changing future, arguing that peer mentoring is the cornerstone of 21st-century education. Pesce begins by distinguishing futurists from psychics, comparing the former to climate scientists who predict long-term trends rather than specific events. He notes that he entered first grade fifty years prior, highlighting the shift from linear life paths to flexible, non-linear trajectories for younger generations. Pesce addresses common fears regarding artificial intelligence, using examples such as Google’s AI demo and self-driving cars to argue that machines are currently best suited for routine tasks, while humans excel at handling exceptions. He cites the case of Go champion Kei Jie, who used AlphaGo to improve his own skills, and the concept of "advanced chess" where human-computer teams outperform standalone machines, to demonstrate that technology amplifies human capability rather than replacing it. He predicts that by 2033, the average worker will spend half their time learning new skills and half practicing existing ones, a trend already visible in companies like AT&T. The speaker emphasizes that education must move away from industrial, didactic models toward peer-oriented networks. He advocates for introducing peer mentoring and assessment from kindergarten, allowing students to share expertise and assess one another continuously rather than through static snapshots. Pesce acknowledges the challenges of this transition, including resistance from parents and the need to adapt assessment methods. He concludes by reinforcing the idea that the future is undeniably human, requiring individuals to become better at learning from one another to navigate a world where both humans and machines are constantly evolving.

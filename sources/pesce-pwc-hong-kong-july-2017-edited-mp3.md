# Pesce - PwC Hong Kong July 2017 Edited.mp3

## Summary

Talk at PwC Hong Kong (July 2017, edited) reviewing what he sees as a dominant trend in VR. He notes he has done VR for 27 years and 'thought VR was deader than a doornail, and then about 18 months ago it became very clear it was not, and I dived right back in.' He explains VIDEOPHAMETRY: starting with the 1993 photogrammetry paper (taking many photographs of a subject from many angles and using software + math to compose a 3D image — 'almost considered magic' in 1993). The modern outside-in system puts a subject in the middle, fires ~100 digital SLRs at once, and post-processes into a 3D model. The deeper point (from Q&A): this is about a 'flexible simulation environment' — 'you couldn't utter the phrase run the numbers before there was a spreadsheet... that's a spreadsheet frame of mind.' What's about to happen around business processes: 'once you've got a business process charted out, you can start to apply AI rules.' 'This isn't just about people creating detailed models — it's about people creating these detailed models in order to teach the intelligences how they work. We are already using VR to teach AIs.' He closes with Marshall McLuhan: 'When information overload occurs, pattern recognition is how you determine truth.'

# The New Inventors-20090708200000.mp4

## Summary

This recording is a transcript from an episode of the Australian television program The New Inventors, hosted by James O'Loughlin. The show features three inventors presenting their creations to a panel of judges, which includes futurist and author Mark Pesce, alongside Sally Deminks and Richard Vaughan. The first invention is a floating solar energy array developed by Phil Connor. This device uses a thin plastic lens to concentrate sunlight onto a small area of silicon cells while floating on water. The water serves to cool the system and allows the structure to submerge during high winds to avoid damage, aiming to produce electricity at a cost comparable to fossil fuels. The second segment introduces a circuit board technology by David Teal and Madhu that utilizes recycled and biodegradable plastics. This method eliminates the need for toxic etching chemicals and allows for easier mechanical disassembly and recycling at the end of the device's life. The inventors argue that this approach reduces the environmental impact of electronic waste and could be more durable and potentially cheaper than traditional manufacturing methods. The final invention is a convertible arm wrestling table created by a young inventor named Alan. The table functions as a standard desk or coffee table but can be flipped over using brass rods to become a competitive arm wrestling station, featuring padded surfaces and sturdy handles. After the presentations, the judges debate the merits of each invention. Mark Pesce highlights the potential of the plastic circuit boards to transform electronics manufacturing and reduce toxic waste. The panel unanimously selects the circuits in plastic technology as the winner of the episode, placing David Teal and Madhu in the running for the annual inventor of the year award. The broadcast concludes with a prompt for viewers to vote for their favorite invention via text message or the show's website. The transcript also includes brief news segments regarding a Rio Tinto employee arrested in China, a ban on bottled water in Bundanoon, and a police fine-free week in New South Wales.

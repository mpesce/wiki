# Friday.rm

## Summary

Opening session of 'Trialogues for a New Millennium' (Esalen, 2001) — a multi-day participatory conversation between Mark Pesce, Douglas Rushkoff, and Erik Davis, conceived in the spirit of Terence McKenna's conversations with Rupert Sheldrake and Ralph Abraham. (The prior year's Esalen memorial for McKenna inspired Nancy Lunney, who runs Esalen programs, to ask the three to carry that work forward.) The session frames the weekend around 'beginnings': Douglas says he doesn't believe in beginnings at all, only evolutions of form; Mark is 'very conscious about beginnings,' placing a Ganesha by the door (Ganesha guards beginnings and blocks bad demons) and reflecting on the film Magnolia and its 'Wise Up' chorus. Mark outlines the three of them as very different from their predecessors but delighted to work with 'two of the brightest people I've ever met.' He sets up an evening ritual to invoke the 'Holy Guardian Angel' (the higher self) and, asserting that 'reality is plastic,' turns the dials over to the audience, passing the mic around the room to collect hopes, fears, and dreams for the 21st century.

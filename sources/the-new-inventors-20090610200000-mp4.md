# The New Inventors-20090610200000.mp4

## Summary

This recording is an episode of the Australian television program The New Inventors, hosted by James O'Loughlin. The broadcast features a panel discussion and demonstrations focused on technologies designed to mitigate the impact of natural disasters, particularly in the context of recent bushfires and floods in Australia. The panel includes science journalist Bernie Hobbes, engineer James Bradford Moody, and futurist Mark Pesce. The episode begins with a segment on bushfire-resistant architecture, featuring Dr. Ian Weir, who presents a house designed with structural steel stumps, fire-resistant glazing, and perforated shutters to protect against ember attack. Weir is joined by Justin Leonard, who demonstrates a simulation tool that models how different house designs perform under bushfire conditions, allowing architects to identify weak points such as specific windows before construction. The program then shifts to flood prediction, introducing ANUGA, an open-source computer program developed to simulate water flow for tsunami inundation, dam breaks, and riverine flooding. The presenters explain how the software uses topographical data to help town planners and emergency managers build safer communities and plan evacuations, noting its application in analyzing the 2004 Boxing Day tsunami. Following this, the show highlights micro aerial vehicles, or MAVs, developed at the University of New South Wales. These miniature helicopters are equipped with cameras and sensors for search, tracking, and reconnaissance tasks, allowing operators to conduct missions safely from a distance. The final segment addresses the welfare of pets during evacuations, featuring Matt Guest and Scott Haran from the Arc organization. They demonstrate their mobile unit, designed in response to the 2003 Canberra bushfires, which provides temporary accommodation and veterinary care for animals displaced by disasters. The unit includes facilities for logging, tagging, and housing various animals, as well as equipment for biosecurity responses. The episode concludes with a quote from Dostoevsky and a preview of upcoming ABC One programs, including a news report on protests by Indian students in Sydney and a helicopter crash incident.

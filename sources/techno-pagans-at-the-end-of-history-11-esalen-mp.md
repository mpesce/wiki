# Techno-Pagans at the End of History 11(Esalen).mp3

## Summary

Part of 'Techno-Pagans at the End of History' (Esalen Institute, 2001) — a multi-day participatory weekend co-hosted by Mark Pesce, Douglas Rushkoff, and Erik Davis. The group moves between long-form conversation and open discussion with the audience, spanning virtual reality, psychedelics, nanotechnology, religion, and the end of history. (Episode 11, economics of the web.) Mark argues that 'where's the value?' in the web is the key question: 'you can't charge for advertising — forget advertising. How about creating some other value? Markets, like destination sites, are as valuable as dollars.' He sees a shift occurring as the technology makes it easier to deliver services. In the background, 'the web still does best what it was originally designed to do, which is facilitate esoteric research. And if you're trying to free yourself from the illusions of society, this is just an enormous, unclosed loophole that they've left open' — where once a literature department at a girl's school in Massachusetts would undertake a massive project (studying the Dunciad) that would fade out of existence when finished, 'now all that stuff is uploaded and exists for anybody to [find].' The discussion turns to learning: 'the web, branching off from what was brought up initially... language assists or defines content or affects content. You get a more elaborate language or simpler language, and it might create better ways to communicate content — because it's a fact that what we're doing doesn't look like it's working. The way that society has evolved on the planet doesn't look self-sustaining. And VR and the web is probably our best shot to amp up the learning of everyone.' (With the caveat that 'it's not going to be worth it if it becomes all Disney.') Mark notes that one of the main things children learn growing up is 'what Steven Spielberg buries within the theme of a movie. And maybe that's the way it always will be.'

# Horizons and Trajectories 4Jun20.mp4

## Summary

This recording is a talk delivered by Mark Pesce, a futurist and former Apple engineer, to the Young Presidents Organization (YPO) in Australia. The presentation takes place in June, shortly after the peak of the initial pandemic lockdowns in April. Pesce begins by reflecting on the rapid return of public speaking events as a sign of economic recovery. He explains the mechanics of exponential growth and the reproduction rate, or R factor, which caused the global shutdown when it exceeded one. He contrasts the collapsed forward planning horizon during the peak of the crisis with the current ability in Australia to plan for the remainder of the year, thanks to successful containment efforts that kept the R factor below one. Pesce outlines four major trends defining the next thirty weeks of business activity. The first is the rise of the sterile economy, illustrated by the NRL’s use of biosecurity teams to create safe environments for players and staff. He notes the tension between maintaining sterility and profitability, citing examples from the AFL and New Zealand’s film industry. The second trend is that every business has become a health business, requiring the appointment of biosecurity officers or chief health officers to redesign processes and manage risk. The third trend is the rise of contactless culture, accelerated by the abandonment of cash and the implementation of strict hygiene protocols in retail and offices, such as at Apple stores. The final trend is the paradox of the bubble, where Australia’s safe internal environment highlights the danger of the outside world, creating a dilemma for a trading nation dependent on international connections. In his conclusion, Pesce advises leaders to adopt trajectory-based planning, shifting from static job roles to a dynamic inventory of employee capacities. This approach allows organizations to adapt quickly to shifting circumstances by identifying skill gaps and upskilling staff. He emphasizes that humility and empathy are essential strengths for leaders navigating this period of chaos and change, urging them to remain resilient and adaptable as they endure the chronic phase of the pandemic.

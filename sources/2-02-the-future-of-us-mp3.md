# 2-02 The Future of Us.mp3

## Summary

This recording is the second part of a three-day talk series titled The Future of Us, delivered by Mark Pesce at the Speaker's Corner during the Burning Man event. The first day's theme was the future of me, while this session focuses on collective human development. Pesce begins by tracing human origins back to the dawn of man, noting that while indigenous Australians record their dreamings to 60,000 years ago, Homo sapiens are approximately 200,000 years old with artifacts dating back 105,000 years. He argues that the drive to create artifice, such as decorated pottery or large sculptures, is a constant form of communication that defines humanity. Pesce posits that although humans are genetically similar to chimpanzees, the key difference is that humans are entirely defined by communication. He describes communication as a form of magic or neuro-linguistic programming that allows individuals to program the beliefs of themselves and others, thereby shaping both the physical and cultural worlds. This power creates a reality where rival groups of shamans fight to establish dominance, a dynamic visible in the acceleration of human history. He references Robert Anton Wilson's view of reality as a standstill between these rival groups and suggests that the future depends on how this communicative power is used to dominate or liberate. The speaker frames the Burning Man playa as a training ground or military encampment where participants test and refine their communicative tools, comparing the event to a weapons expo where successful ideas are integrated into a broader arsenal. He acknowledges that Burning Man functions as a cult, similar to early Mormonism or Scientology, but emphasizes that the community must decide what kind of help it wants to provide.

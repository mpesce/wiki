# Pesce-TheSymposium-9Dec18.mp4

## Summary

This recording is a panel discussion featuring Mark Pesce, Wendy Hall, and Peter Norvig, moderated by Pesce. The session appears to be part of a conference or symposium commemorating the 50th anniversary of Douglas Engelbart’s 1968 demo, focusing on the evolution of hypermedia and the web. The speakers reflect on how the original vision of intelligence augmentation has been shaped by the rise of search engines and the current state of digital culture. Wendy Hall discusses the history of hypermedia authoring, noting that early systems like NLS and Microcosm required users to actively create links and structure. She argues that the web shifted from a system of creation to one of consumption, largely because manual authoring was too difficult without AI support. She recalls meeting Tim Berners-Lee in the early 1990s and her initial skepticism about embedding links in documents, a perspective she later revised as the web grew. Hall emphasizes that the semantic web ideas were ahead of their time, hindered by the lack of advanced AI at the time, and suggests that modern intelligent agents could now enable the personalized knowledge environments envisioned by Engelbart and Vannevar Bush. Peter Norvig addresses the transition from human-curated taxonomies to machine-driven search. He explains that as the web expanded beyond human capacity to organize, search engines like Google became essential for navigating the "long tail" of information. Norvig highlights the importance of provenance and trust, arguing that open systems like Wikipedia allow for community verification, whereas closed algorithms can obscure intent. He also touches on the future of interfaces, including voice, augmented reality, and federated learning, suggesting that while technology is advancing, the social and cultural challenges of online citizenship and education remain significant. The panel concludes with both speakers expressing a desire for a world where technology facilitates personal connections and good digital citizenship rather than just data retrieval.

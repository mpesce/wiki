## Summary

This recording is a conference talk delivered by Mark Pesce, a media and technology commentator, discussing the shifting dynamics of power in the digital age. The speaker references a previous talk given in the same room one year prior, noting that the current date is shortly after the 2008 US presidential election, specifically mentioning November 4, 2008, as a turning point. The central thesis is that society is experiencing an acceleration of power distribution that has moved beyond simple mobilization to a state of "hyper-connectivity," where traditional hierarchical organizations are colliding with decentralized, ad-hoc communities or "adhocracies." Pesce argues that this transition creates a loss of control for established institutions, comparing the current era to the chaotic collisions inside the Large Hadron Collider. He cites the conflict between the Church of Scientology and Wikipedia as a primary example of this mismatch. While Scientology operates as a rigid hierarchy that uses legal threats to maintain control, Wikipedia functions as a social contract driven by participation and consensus. The speaker notes that when these two structures interact, the hierarchy’s tools become ineffective against the smooth, transparent nature of the adhocracy. He further illustrates this incompatibility using the 2008 Obama campaign, specifically the failures of Project Houdini and the New York state campaign. In these instances, the hierarchical structure of the campaign was overwhelmed by the empowered, decentralized actions of supporters, causing the systems to "short out" because they could not process the volume of distributed information. The talk concludes with a warning that the next decade will be difficult for politicians and institutions as they attempt to integrate with these new hyper-intelligent communities.

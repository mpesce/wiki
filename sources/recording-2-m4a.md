# Recording 2.m4a

## Summary

This recording is a presentation and Q&A session delivered by Mark Pesce to the board of Pete, a home builder, in Perth. Introduced by chairman Tony Lennon, Pesce, who notes he is often labeled a futurist due to his work on the ABC show The New Inventors, outlines three primary trends shaping the future of housing: increased human capacity through connectivity, advances in machine learning, and the rise of simulation technologies like augmented reality. He argues that Pete is well-positioned to leverage these trends rather than fight them. Pesce begins by explaining how smartphones have connected populations, allowing for new stages of social interaction including sharing, learning, and doing. He then details the rapid progress of artificial intelligence, citing the self-driving car fatality in Tempe, Arizona, and Google’s AlphaGo as examples of systems that learn from mistakes. He suggests that Amazon’s Alexa, which understands customer preferences deeply, could theoretically recommend where a person should live based on their lifestyle data. Pesce proposes that Pete could partner with Amazon to use this data for co-designing homes that fit specific buyer needs, turning big data into a benefit for customers rather than just a tool for corporate profit. The talk shifts to augmented reality, using Pokémon Go and the IKEA Place app as examples of how technology can help customers visualize future spaces. Pesce suggests Pete could use AR to let buyers design their homes and see the broader community master plan before construction begins. He also emphasizes the importance of social media for community building, advising Pete to create local social networks that foster connections among residents, similar to how global platforms operate. He recommends that the board request regular social media reports to monitor public sentiment. In the final section, Pesce discusses emerging housing models driven by millennial desires for flexibility and density. He highlights startups like WeLive, Star City, and Loftium, which offer co-living and shared ownership models. He also mentions Power Ledger, a West Australian company using blockchain to facilitate peer-to-peer solar energy trading, as a potential partner for sustainable community development. The session concludes with a Q&A where Pesce discusses the challenges of millennial mobility, the role of banks in digital privacy, and the need for new financial instruments to support flexible housing arrangements.

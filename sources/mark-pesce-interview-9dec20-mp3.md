# Mark Pesce interview 9Dec20.mp3

## Summary

This recording is a podcast interview featuring host Jon and guest Mark Pesce, who is speaking from Sydney, Australia. The conversation begins with a discussion on the future of events, where Pesce argues that the pandemic has revealed the limitations of virtual conferences, particularly the loss of serendipitous in-person interactions. He predicts that while augmented reality headsets may provide an 80 percent substitute for physical presence by the end of the decade, the embodied experience of face-to-face contact remains irreplaceable. He notes that this shift has been a learning experience for younger generations who have never known a world without online connectivity. The majority of the interview focuses on digital currencies and their societal implications. Pesce distinguishes between digital cash and traditional bank money, explaining that digital cash functions like physical bills but exists on smartphones. He highlights the rollout of the Digital Currency Electronic Payment system by the People's Bank of China, noting that 50 million users currently employ it and that it is designed with central recording of all transactions, effectively turning currency into a surveillance tool. In contrast, he suggests that Western nations can design digital currencies with stronger privacy features. The discussion also covers the impact of Facebook’s Libra announcement, which spurred major economies to accelerate their own digital currency projects to maintain monetary sovereignty. Pesce further explores the concept of DeFi, or decentralized finance, describing it as money that thinks and can automatically move to maximize yields. However, he warns that these systems are prone to bugs and security vulnerabilities, citing recent hacks of hundreds of millions of dollars. He envisions a future where non-human entities, such as Teslas and automated dairy cows, hold digital wallets to pay for services and earn revenue independently. The interview concludes with Pesce mentioning his podcast, The Next Billion Seconds, and a planned series on cryptonomics, before a brief, humorous exchange with his assistant Siri.

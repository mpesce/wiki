# The Last Days of Reality - EGA 8Dec17.mkv

## Summary

Mark Pesce delivered a talk at the EGA conference, noting his return after a thirteen-year absence. He framed his presentation as "technology as a drug," issuing a warning to attendees with paranoid disorders before discussing the psychological and societal impacts of social media. Pesce cited a 2018 report from The Australian alleging that Facebook had pitched advertisers on its ability to detect the emotional states of teenagers to target them with confidence-boosting ads. He also referenced a 2014 study in the Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences where Facebook manipulated the news feeds of 689,000 users to demonstrate emotional contagion without their consent. The speaker argued that Facebook’s machine learning algorithms function as a global cognitive bias amplifier, curating content to maximize user engagement by confirming existing beliefs. This process, he claimed, erodes the public sphere and creates isolated realities, as illustrated by a BuzzFeed analysis of a mother and daughter seeing different versions of the news. Pesce described this dynamic as surveillance capitalism, where pervasive data collection and artificial intelligence deprive users of free will. He extended this critique to emerging augmented reality technologies, which he viewed as tools for constant surveillance that would allow corporations to curate physical reality. He also discussed the weaponization of dopamine reward systems in gambling machines and video game loot boxes, drawing parallels to social media addiction. In the concluding segment, Pesce offered three pieces of advice: know your substance, know your source, and know yourself. He urged the audience to maintain a healthy paranoia regarding free digital services, verify the provenance of information, and practice mindfulness regarding their relationship with social media. During the Q&A, he addressed questions about regulation, suggesting that while monopolies like Facebook should be regulated, there is a risk of state control similar to China’s social credit system. He also provided privacy tips such as using password managers and VPNs, and advised parents to limit children’s screen time in favor of real-world experiences. The talk was based on an essay published in The Engine by Melbourne University Press.

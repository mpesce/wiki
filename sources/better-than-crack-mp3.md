# Better-Than-Crack.mp3

## Summary

This recording is a keynote lecture delivered by Mark Pesce, an honorary associate with the Digital Culture Program, likely at a university in Sydney, Australia. The talk, titled "Better Than Crap," explores the psychological and sociological impact of mobile technology on human behavior. Pesce begins with an anecdote about a mother nearly dropping her child while distracted by her iPhone, using it to illustrate how compelling digital devices have become. He argues that this seduction is not a new phenomenon but follows a historical pattern of technological adoption, tracing the lineage from the Altair 8800 in 1975 to the early World Wide Web in 1993. Pesce details his personal history with computing, including his first encounter with a teletype and his work on hypertext systems prior to the web. He describes the "seduction" of the web in the mid-1990s, noting how early adopters spread the technology through social networks, much like the viral adoption of mobile phones in developing nations. He cites examples from India, Nigeria, Pakistan, and Kenya, where mobile phones have transformed economies by enabling fishermen, farmers, and barbers to optimize their work and where mobile minutes have become a form of currency. He highlights the direct correlation between mobile ownership and GDP growth, arguing that these devices have become essential tools for wealth creation and social connection. The lecture concludes with a discussion on the current state of connectivity, comparing the mobile phone to a "secret lover" that reshapes social behaviors. Pesce uses the concept of the Schwarzschild radius to suggest that society has passed the point of no return regarding digital integration. He engages the audience in a Q&A session, discussing the trade-offs between constant connectivity and disconnection, and predicts that younger generations will view constant connection as a normal state of being, potentially leading to even earlier ages for device ownership.

# Pesce Pandemic Time ANU 3Ai 15May20.mp4

## Summary

This recording is a lecture delivered by Mark Pesce, a futurist and former Apple and Mozilla engineer, to an audience at the Australian National University’s 3AI institute. Speaking via Zoom on his first day of teaching, Pesce acknowledges the Gadigal people of the Eora Nation before addressing the concept of "pandemic time." He explains that his role as a futurist is to help people understand the timescales they operate in, a skill that has become critical during the global health crisis. Pesce argues that the pandemic represents a period of "dense time," a state where significant events occur so rapidly that the future becomes unpredictable. Pesce draws parallels between the pandemic and the "singularity," a term he generally dislikes but finds justified in this context because it describes an exponential change process where the horizon of predictability bends upward into a wall. He cites a Wired article and personal anecdotes, including a call with a distressed friend in Massachusetts, to illustrate how individuals struggle when their forward planning horizon contracts. At the peak of the crisis in March 2020, Pesce notes that this horizon shrank to approximately 30 minutes, triggering survival instincts such as panic buying and lockdown. He contrasts this with the current situation in Australia, where the curve has been flattened, allowing the planning horizon to extend to several weeks or months. The speaker emphasizes that while Australia is exiting the acute phase, other nations remain in dense time, creating a global landscape of multiple, conflicting timescales. Pesce warns that this experience is not over, as future exponential events may recur, and traditional long-term planning skills are useless or dangerous during such periods. He concludes by advocating for the development of new cognitive tools and empathy to navigate this "radically foreshortened now," suggesting the need for a "Short Now Foundation" to complement existing long-term thinking initiatives. The talk serves as a reflection on how humanity can learn from this unprecedented disruption to better prepare for future uncertainties.

# CFO Series Sydney 24Jun20 Same Time Next Year.mp4

## Summary

This recording is a presentation by Mark Pesce, a professional futurist and former Apple and Mozilla engineer, discussing the economic and social outlook for Australia for the upcoming financial year. Speaking from his home studio, Pesce notes that the pandemic has made long-term prediction difficult, with the world becoming unpredictable around March 12, 2020. He argues that the dominant theme for the next year will be the recurring question of whether individuals, businesses, and governments are okay, a sentiment amplified by the lingering trauma of the 2019-2020 bushfires. Pesce identifies biosecurity as the new priority, superseding traditional profitability. He cites examples such as Apple’s strict safety protocols in Sydney, the NRL’s appointment of a biosecurity officer, and the AFL’s cancellation of a game due to a positive test, illustrating how health mandates now directly impact business operations. He predicts that biosecurity will evolve into a C-suite role, such as Chief Health Officer, and that national security will increasingly focus on preventing viral entry, potentially involving military enforcement of quarantine as seen in New Zealand. Pesce describes the current state as living under a bubble, where borders between Australian states remain policed to prevent virus transmission. This isolation has driven a shift toward touchless consumer behaviors, including digital payments and neo-banking, and has normalized remote work, particularly in finance. He warns that while the bubble provides safety, it contains a finite amount of economic air, creating tension between the desire for economic growth and the need for safety. He highlights the planned trans-Tasman travel bubble with New Zealand as a critical experiment for learning how to safely reopen international borders. The presentation concludes with a risk analysis. Pesce identifies the moving goalposts of the pandemic as a known known, the potential insolvency of small businesses when JobKeeper ends in September as a known unknown, and the inevitability of a second wave, currently visible in Victoria, as an unknown known. He labels the political instability in the United States as the primary unknown unknown.

## Summary

This recording is a television interview segment featuring Mark Pesce, identified in the transcript as a virtual reality pioneer and judge on the ABC show New Inventors. The host introduces Pesce to discuss the health concerns associated with 3D technology, specifically referencing the movie Avatar and home 3D television systems. The discussion takes place in a studio setting, with the host and Pesce engaging in a dialogue about the physiological effects of stereoscopic displays. Pesce explains that 3D systems, including virtual reality headsets, 3D movies, and televisions, trick the brain by presenting slightly different images to each eye. This process causes the brain to ignore real-world information, creating a temporary disorientation similar to having "sea legs" when the device is removed. During this adjustment period, users lack proper depth perception, which poses safety risks if they immediately engage in activities like driving or cycling. Pesce highlights a specific concern regarding children, noting that their developing nervous systems may be susceptible to permanent damage from such exposure. He cites research from fifteen years prior, when he worked with Sega on a virtual reality headset for the Master System. Although Sega initially buried test results indicating the device was unsafe for children, the testing lab later re-ran and released the data, confirming the risks for virtual reality. Pesce argues that similar testing is now required to determine if these risks apply to 3D movies and television. When asked if he would watch Avatar in 3D at home, Pesce suggests that occasional viewing, such as two hours a month, is likely safe, but nightly viewing is not.

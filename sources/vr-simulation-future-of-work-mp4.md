## Summary

This recording appears to be an excerpt from a talk or interview featuring Mark Pesce, a media and technology commentator. The primary topic is the current state of virtual reality and the broader concept of simulation in the workplace. Pesce notes that the metaverse has experienced multiple cycles of hype and decline throughout his career but is currently resurging due to hardware developments such as the Apple Vision Pro and initiatives by Meta. He suggests that while these technologies may eventually serve work environments, current fully immersive systems have significant accessibility issues. Specifically, he points out that most such systems cause motion sickness in the majority of women and men over the age of thirty, as they are generally designed for young, able-bodied users with more robust vestibular systems. Consequently, the technology is not currently welcoming to a broad demographic, though he assumes this may improve over time. Pesce argues that the core value of this emerging technology is not the virtual reality aspect itself, but the ability to simulate things. He draws a parallel to spreadsheets, which have been used for nearly fifty years as financial simulations. He posits that modern simulations are becoming increasingly sophisticated and useful. When this simulation capability is paired with artificial intelligence, it transforms into a core business skill that employees should master.

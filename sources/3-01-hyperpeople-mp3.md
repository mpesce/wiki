# 3-01 Hyperpeople.mp3

## Summary

This recording is a keynote speech delivered by Mark Pesce, likely at a technology conference, discussing the societal and intellectual implications of global network connectivity. The talk begins by contrasting the one-way mass communication of the 20th century with the multi-directional, decentralized communication of the modern internet. Pesce argues that connecting billions of individuals creates a state of hyperconnectivity, which he compares to the emergent order of the Great Barrier Reef, where individual actions collectively create a new environment without central direction. The speaker posits that this hyperconnectivity leads to the emergence of hyperintelligence, defined as the collective amplification of individual knowledge. He uses the history of the Vatican Library and the rise of Wikipedia as primary examples. Pesce details how Wikipedia evolved from the failed expert-driven Nupedia into a massive, user-generated repository of human knowledge, growing from 15,000 to two million articles in five years. He argues that this shift moves the repository of information from static books to the living minds of connected individuals, making knowledge instantly available and continuously updated. Pesce distinguishes between knowledge and wisdom, noting that while hyperintelligence provides vast access to facts, it does not automatically confer understanding. He suggests that hyperconnectivity serves as a counterbalance, allowing individuals to check their actions against peers and mentors, thereby transforming raw data into embodied understanding. The speech concludes with the concept of the hyper person, an individual who leverages both hyperintelligence and hyperconnectivity to become more effective than isolated individuals. Pesce predicts a rapid cultural evolution over the next two generations as humanity transitions into these hyperconnected communities, urging the audience to embrace this shift despite the challenges it poses to privacy, individuality, and traditional beliefs.

# 0000000011abc207.iPhone.m4v

## Summary

A 30-minute recording of an Australian TV 7 news program (7:30, hosted by Samantha Armitage) covering several consumer and media stories. The lead story examines the 3D TV boom: the first 3D broadcast in Australian television, the shutter-glasses technology (the same principle Mark Pesce worked on for Sega's VR headset), and expert warnings about visual health risks. Media and technology guru Mark Pesce is interviewed and calls 3D TV a "gigantic unregulated medical experiment," noting that while vision experts foresee no daily-watching problems, Samsung's own warnings list epileptic seizures, dizziness and possible eye strain, and predicts the US will own nearly half the world's 3D sets (9.2 million) by 2015. The second story is an investigation into smartphone "bill shock": the telecommunications ombudsman is receiving over 100 complaints a day, with one-off charges of $9,000-$26,000 from a single data-heavy download (one man was billed $26,000 for a $12 iTunes album; a 13-year-old's game downloads cost an MP $10,500). Telstra responded by waiving the extraordinary charges and reviewing its billing procedures. Other segments: a food-safety investigation finding 4 in 5 sushi samples in Sydney and nearly half in Brisbane contaminated with staphylococcus, Bacillus cereus or (worst case) Listeria, triggering a health department crackdown (Melbourne passed clean); a profile of late-blooming acts in the Australia's Got Talent semi-finals (an 84-year-old swing-dancing couple); a legal dispute at a Queensland Pets Paradise that left pets locked in the store; and a feature on TV game-show regulars and the psychology of game-show appeal. The episode ends with standard advertising and programme teasers (KFC, Sensodyne, Uncle Tobys, Home and Away, Grey's Anatomy).

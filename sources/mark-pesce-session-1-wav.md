## Summary

This recording is a conversation between Mark Pesce and an interviewer, likely Robert, discussing the history of the World Wide Web, virtual reality, and current climate and political issues in Australia. The speakers note that it has been nearly 19 years since a previous interaction, with the conversation taking place in Sydney. They discuss Australia’s status as a climate canary, referencing recent extreme weather events such as a hillside collapse in Wellington, New Zealand, and record rainfall and flooding in Sydney. The dialogue touches on a recent change of government in Australia and the formal commitment to the Paris Agreement, which has begun to align capital investment toward renewable energy solutions. The core of the discussion focuses on the origins of the Web and 3D interfaces. Pesce recounts meeting Tony in late 1993 in San Francisco, where they developed a prototype for a 3D interface to the World Wide Web. They presented this work at the first international World Wide Web conference at CERN in May 1994, an event Pesce describes as having a unique sense of shared purpose. He explains that their project relied on software rendering engines, specifically Render Morphix Reality Lab, which was later acquired by Microsoft and became Direct3D. The first application built on this technology was the VRML browser, allowing fully realized 3D worlds to run on ordinary PCs. Pesce highlights a significant early demo at SIGGRAPH in 1994, which digitized the Daniel’s Story exhibition from the US Holocaust Memorial Museum.

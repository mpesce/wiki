## Summary

This recording is an interview with Mark Pesce, a media and technology commentator, discussing the collapse of the cryptocurrency exchange FTX and its founder, Sam Bankman-Fried. The conversation takes place around the time of Bankman-Fried's arrest, with the host noting that the former multibillionaire was likely facing charges in the US the following morning. Pesce describes the FTX empire as an internal Ponzi scheme where the company’s own coin, FTT, was loaned to its investment arm, Alameda Research, to artificially inflate the exchange’s valuation. When the stablecoin peg collapsed, FTX could not meet depositor requirements, triggering a bank run on assets that were essentially worthless. Pesce highlights the lack of due diligence by major investors like Sequoia and the absence of basic accounting controls at FTX, which allowed for the commingling of depositor and investment funds. He anticipates that wire fraud charges will be filed, arguing that taking money over the wire and failing to return it constitutes fraud regardless of the underlying asset. The discussion also touches on the regulatory vacuum in the crypto industry, noting that while US regulators have been slow to act, other publicly traded crypto exchanges are now clamoring for regulation to restore customer trust. Pesce predicts that crypto exchanges will soon adopt banking-style prudential regulations, such as capital reserves and regular audits, to survive. The interview further explores the broader context of the "crypto winter," drawing parallels between the FTX collapse and historical financial crises like the 1997 Asian financial crisis, suggesting that similar mistakes are being repeated in a new medium.

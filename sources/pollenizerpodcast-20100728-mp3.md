## Summary

This recording is an episode of the Pollenizer podcast, featuring an interview with Mark Pesce, a media and technology commentator and former Apple and Mozilla engineer. The primary topic of the discussion is Plexus, a new open-source software project Pesce is developing to manage social media interactions. Pesce explains that the project originated from his desire to leave Facebook due to privacy concerns regarding his social graph. He describes Plexus as a personal, portable, and private piece of software that acts as a traffic cop for communications, allowing users to interact with friends across various platforms like Facebook, Twitter, and Flickr without needing to be members of each service. The system is designed to run in the background of a mobile device, automatically routing messages and media through the appropriate channels based on user preferences. Pesce elaborates on the technical and philosophical underpinnings of Plexus, comparing its open-source nature to the Linux kernel. He emphasizes that the core software provides an API that allows third parties to build diverse front-end applications, creating a commercial ecology around the open platform. He discusses the importance of the social graph, noting that it is a valuable asset that Facebook currently owns and does not allow users to export easily. To address this, Plexus aims to let users maintain their social connections privately by creating isolated accounts for specific contacts, ensuring that no single platform holds a complete map of their relationships. The interview also touches on the creative process behind the invention. Pesce describes the experience of having a persistent idea that obsessively occupies his thoughts until it is implemented.

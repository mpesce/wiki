# S01E01 Mark.wav

## Summary

This recording is an episode of the podcast This Week in Startups Australia, featuring host Mark Pesce and guest Jason Calacanis. The interview marks the first guest appearance for series six of the show. Calacanis, a prominent angel investor and entrepreneur, discusses his career trajectory from growing up in Brooklyn to founding the magazine Silicon Valley Reporter in 1995. He details how the publication became a significant business during the early internet boom, eventually leading to the creation of the blog network Engadget, which was sold to AOL for 30 million dollars eighteen months after its launch. Calacanis reflects on his transition from journalism to angel investing, noting that he began as a Sequoia Scout and later built a successful syndicate through AngelList and his own platform. He highlights major investments, including Uber, and discusses the psychological aspects of risk-taking and the importance of investing in passionate, skilled founders. The conversation shifts to Calacanis’s current ventures, specifically his newsletter Inside, which aggregates top news stories, and his efforts to grow the Australian startup ecosystem. He advocates for training local investors through initiatives like Angel University and suggests that the government could support the industry by implementing knowledge-based tests for sophisticated investors, similar to driver’s license requirements. Calacanis also addresses the rise of Initial Coin Offerings, expressing skepticism about the majority of crypto projects while acknowledging the potential for compliant blockchain tools to eventually integrate with traditional venture capital. He emphasizes that while ICOs pose a competitive threat, the fundamental value of angel investing lies in direct diligence and relationships. The interview concludes with advice for Australian entrepreneurs, urging them to be bold and ambitious rather than meek. Calacanis points to local successes like Atlassian and Canva as proof that world-class companies can emerge from Sydney. He encourages founders to seek large funding and maintain a relentless vision, asserting that the next major global tech company could originate in Australia. The discussion ends with mutual appreciation for the growth of the Australian startup community and the potential for expanding the podcast format to other international startup hubs.

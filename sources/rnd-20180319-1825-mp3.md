# rnd_20180319_1825.mp3

## Summary

This recording is an interview segment from the news program RN Drive, featuring Mark Pesci, an author and futurist. The discussion centers on the revelation that voter profiling firm Cambridge Analytica harvested data from over 50 million Facebook profiles without permission, a breach linked to Donald Trump’s 2016 presidential campaign. Pesci explains that Cambridge Analytica was founded by Robert Mercer and his daughter Rebecca, who also backed far-right organizations such as Breitbart. The firm utilized sophisticated software to target voters with specific messages designed to suppress opposition or encourage marginal voters, having previously been involved in the 2016 Brexit and Kenyan elections. Pesci details the data harvesting process, which began when a Russian researcher created a personality quiz on Facebook. By agreeing to participate, users granted the researcher access to their profile data. The researcher then traversed the social graph, collecting data from the friends of those who took the quiz. Although framed as legitimate research, the data was ultimately provided to Cambridge Analytica. This information allowed the firm to profile voters by analyzing their social connections, interests, and group memberships to predict political leanings and tailor messaging. Pesci argues that this practice is not distinct from Facebook’s standard advertising model, as the platform functions fundamentally as a data collection engine that weaponizes user information to drive engagement and sales. The interview addresses the implications for privacy and regulation. Pesci notes that Facebook was aware of the breach years prior but downplayed it, highlighting the lack of protection for user data. He argues that strict regulations would conflict with Facebook’s business model, which relies on selling detailed profile data to advertisers. Pesci describes Facebook as a surveillance engine that constantly monitors user behavior to tune the news feed for maximum engagement

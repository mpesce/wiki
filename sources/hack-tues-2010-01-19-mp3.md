# hack_tues_2010_01_19.mp3

## Summary

This recording is an episode of the Australian radio program Hack, hosted by Kate O'Toole. The broadcast features a segment with technology writer Mark Pesce discussing recent security vulnerabilities in Microsoft Internet Explorer. Pesce explains that hackers exploited a flaw in Internet Explorer 6 to breach major corporations, including Google, Yahoo, and Northrop Grumman. He advises users to upgrade to Internet Explorer 8 or switch to open-source browsers like Firefox, arguing that open-source code allows for greater transparency and faster identification of security bugs compared to Microsoft's proprietary model. Pesce characterizes these intrusions as a form of military action by other means, noting the severe risk posed to data stored in the cloud. The program also covers Prince William's visit to the Redfern Aboriginal community in Sydney, his first official trip overseas. Reporter Tom Tilly describes the prince's arrival and his meeting with local elders, including Mick Mundine and Rob Welsh. The elders raised the issue of returning the head of Pemulwuy, an Eora warrior killed in 1802, which is believed to be held in England. While no immediate agreement was reached, the elders reported that the prince listened respectfully. The segment concludes with a phone-in debate regarding whether Australia should remain a monarchy or become a republic, featuring arguments from both supporters and critics of the Crown.

# The Knew Normal CFO Series Melbourne 10Jun20.mp4

## Summary

This recording is a business presentation or talk delivered by a futurist and host of the podcast The Next Billion Seconds, addressing an audience in Australia. The speaker discusses the transition from the acute phase of the pandemic to what he terms the chronic phase, noting that the exponential growth of the virus in March 2020 collapsed the traditional forward planning horizon. He argues that the primary goal for the remainder of 2020 is to keep the reproduction rate below one to prevent a second wave, a metric that will frame business strategy. The speaker highlights that Australia recorded no community transmissions of COVID-19 the day prior to the talk and that New Zealand had moved to level one restrictions, though he warns that such isolation creates economic bubbles with significant costs. The main topic covers four key trends for 2020. The first is the shift from a sharing economy to a sterile economy, illustrated by the National Rugby League’s use of a biosecurity officer to create safe playing conditions and Apple Stores implementing visible safety measures like temperature checks and social distancing. The speaker predicts that the biosecurity officer role will evolve into a C-suite position, such as a Chief Health or Wellness Officer, responsible for transforming business processes to ensure safety. The second trend is the rise of a contactless culture, driven by the pandemic’s acceleration of digital payments, digital banking, and remote work. He notes that remote work has proven to increase productivity by 10 to 20 percent, challenging previous assumptions about employee slacking. The third trend involves the tension between safety bubbles and economic necessity, using New Zealand’s isolation as an example of a bubble that protects health but harms tourism and requires exceptions for major film productions. The speaker concludes by emphasizing that the current period is a gigantic experiment where goalposts constantly move. He advises organizations to adopt continuous planning rather than annual cycles, stating that companies that adapt through experimentation and learning will be best positioned to thrive when the pandemic eventually ends.

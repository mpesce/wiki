# Reality Remix.wav

## Summary

This recording is a conference talk delivered by Mark Pesce, a media and technology commentator and former engineer, to an audience that includes Scott Fisher. Pesce outlines the history and future of virtual reality (VR), arguing that its evolution is inextricably linked to the development of the web. He begins by tracing the origins of VR to 1968, citing Ivan Sutherland’s head-mounted display and Douglas Engelbart’s "mother of all demos," which introduced the mouse and hypertext. Pesce notes that these two technologies were born simultaneously and have continued to influence each other. The speaker then details his own career, starting in 1990 when he read Jaron Lanier’s assertion that VR would be the "telephone of the future" rather than a television. This inspired Pesce to found Ono Sendai Corporation to create affordable, networked consumer VR systems. He describes the company’s work on low-cost sensors and a partnership with Sega for a Genesis-based VR headset, which ultimately failed due to motion sickness issues caused by limited processing power. Simultaneously, Pesce collaborated with Tony Parisi to apply VR concepts to the web, resulting in the first VRML-based image, a banana, in 1994. This work helped establish VRML as a standard, bridging the gap between virtual environments and the World Wide Web. Pesce discusses the "empty quarter" of VR development, noting that real-time 3D graphics advanced through video game consoles like the PlayStation, while the iPhone brought the web to mobile devices. He identifies the introduction of Google Cardboard as the pivotal moment that democratized VR by turning smartphones into headsets. The talk concludes with Pesce’s proposal for a "Mixed Reality Service," a protocol that maps physical coordinates to URLs, allowing the physical world to provide context and information to users. He envisions a future where the web and the real world are seamlessly integrated into a single mixed reality experience, urging the audience to use these tools to create positive, empathetic, and informative environments.

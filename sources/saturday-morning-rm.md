# Saturday Morning.rm

## Summary

Mark gives a 30-40 minute keynote before opening to discussion: he believes society is 'in the midst of a renaissance of perhaps unprecedented magnitude,' bigger than the 1500s-1600s. He maps the original Renaissance to its modern corollaries. The original gave perspective painting (representing 3D in 2D — relating one dimension to the next), the realization the world is round (an increase in dimensionality), calculus and limits (approximating curves — 'taking lines and approximating curves'), the printing press (transforming literature and information distribution), and coffee (imported to Paris and Italy, which let people stay up late and gave birth to Bohemian culture — 'the first real counterculture, the first culture in a different circadian rhythm'). The modern equivalent of perspective painting, he says, is the HOLOGRAPH: not only does it show 3D in 2D, it carries an extra dimension — and if you smash a hologram, each little piece holds a faint version of the whole. The discussion then covers 'shocks' (valuable only insofar as you can experience them) and a three-phase model (literal → metaphorical → recapitulatory: 'I am God'), the cultural 'trickster game' of how to embed the potential for openings in your media, and the impulse to transference and regression (giving parental authority to scientists or spiritual leaders — acceptable so long as it stays fluid, voluntary, and autonomous).

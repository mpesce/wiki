# Genevieve Bell.mp3

## Summary

This recording is a pre-interview briefing and partial interview for the podcast The Next Billion Seconds, featuring host Mark Pesce and guest Dr. Genevieve Bell. The session begins with informal production notes where Pesce advises Bell to avoid academic jargon, followed by a discussion of the episode structure, which is divided into two parts: connected animals and a new emerging discipline. The interview itself focuses on the Internet of Things and its impact on animal behavior. Bell explains how automatic milking machines, using RFID tags and lasers, allow cows to milk themselves on demand rather than on a fixed twice-daily schedule. This shift revealed that cows have specific desires and social preferences, such as having friends, which were previously invisible to farmers. The conversation then shifts to domestic cats, citing studies from the UK and US that used GPS and RFID tracking to map their movements. These studies found that cats travel much farther than expected, engage in territorial negotiations, and that a third of cats are fed by people other than their primary owners. Bell also discusses the darker side of animal tracking, noting that geotagging and trackers can expose endangered species like rhinos and gorillas to poachers. She highlights the case of albatrosses, which have become accustomed to being tagged and bled by researchers, creating a reciprocal relationship that mirrors human interactions with online service providers. In the final segment, the discussion turns to human data privacy and the ethical implications of algorithmic decision-making. Bell uses the example of a robotic vacuum cleaner mapping a home to illustrate how intimate data is being collected and potentially sold. She argues that we are at a critical historical moment where technology is forming a complex system, similar to the rise of the railway or industrial capitalism, requiring a new set of tools and practitioners to manage it. She warns that algorithms trained on historical data can entrench existing biases, such as gender pay gaps, and notes that the current public discourse often lacks nuance, oscillating between utopian and dystopian views without addressing the practical ethical questions of autonomous systems and data ownership.

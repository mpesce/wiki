# Mark Pesce Alchemist 23Jun21.wav

## Summary

This recording is an interview featuring Mark Pesce, a media and technology commentator and former Apple and Mozilla engineer, speaking with host Rachel. The conversation takes place on June 23, with Pesce located in Australia and the host in the United States. Pesce opens by discussing the nature of innovation, arguing that it often occurs slowly before appearing suddenly, citing the recent transformations in banking and the automotive industry as primary examples. He explains that large corporations, such as Microsoft, are undergoing significant structural shifts, moving from selling single-user software to delivering cloud-based services. Pesce notes that while innovators often see solutions a generation ahead of their organizations, the scale of trillion-dollar entities makes rapid change difficult, leading to a slow, human-scale transformation. The discussion shifts to the challenges faced by institutions and entrepreneurs. Pesce reflects on his time as an entrepreneur in residence at Sydney University, highlighting the dilemmas between institutional intellectual property ownership and the personal financial security of tenured professors. He argues that organizations often fail to integrate successful innovations created within their "sandboxes," forcing entrepreneurs to leave to scale their work. The conversation also covers the conservative nature of massive capital accumulations, using Intel as a case study of a company that missed the mobile revolution due to its focus on the x86 architecture. Pesce also touches on his personal history, including his role in writing the VRML specification and his connection to Gavin Bell, who later became the first chief scientist of the Bitcoin Foundation. In the final segment, Pesce addresses the cultural and psychological impacts of the pandemic, noting the stark difference in experience between Australia’s eradication strategy and the United States’ approach. He suggests that the pandemic has broken the traditional Prussian model of education, offering an opportunity to rethink how children are taught in a flexible, remote-work era. Looking toward the future, Pesce predicts that by the 2030s, the concepts of value and code will become inseparable, fundamentally altering the financial sector. He concludes by promoting his recent book, Augmented Reality Unboxing Text, and his podcast, The Next Billion Seconds, while reflecting on his own aging process and the importance of working from love to leave the world better.

# Both-And The Hybrid Future of Events MEA 28Apr21 720p.mp4

## Summary

This recording is a professional talk delivered by Mark Pesce, a futurist and media commentator, likely at a meetings and events industry conference. Introducing himself as a speaker who works in this sector, Pesce outlines how the post-pandemic landscape has fundamentally altered work structures and, consequently, the events industry. He begins by noting that the massive IT investments of the last decade finally yielded productivity gains when lockdowns forced a global shift to remote work, proving that technology enabled work from anywhere rather than just improving office efficiency. Pesce argues that the workforce has divided into three distinct groups: permanent remote workers, who now comprise about 25 percent of the labor force; office-centric workers who thrive on in-person interaction; and a new class of hybrid workers who require flexible arrangements. He highlights a management paradox where remote workers actually require more frequent face-to-face check-ins with managers, making them an increased management responsibility. For hybrid employees, organizations must shift from static policies to continuous negotiation regarding work schedules. Consequently, the office is no longer the primary locus of daily work but has become a space for connection, serendipity, and relationship building, similar to the mailroom at Pixar designed to encourage accidental interactions. Turning to the events sector, Pesce details how major tech conferences like Microsoft Build and Google I/O moved online during the pandemic and discovered that virtual formats allowed for unprecedented scale, with attendance figures jumping from thousands to hundreds of thousands. He predicts a future where events are split into three categories: fully physical, fully virtual, and hybrid. He identifies hybrid events as the largest emerging category, requiring new hard skills in media production, such as lighting, sound, and live streaming, to ensure virtual participants have an experience equivalent to those in the room. He also emphasizes soft skills, including the ability to moderate both physical and digital audiences simultaneously and to foster serendipity across different platforms. Pesce concludes by advocating for flexibility, noting that the pandemic has granted event professionals global reach, allowing them to blend local and global talent in ways previously impossible.

# The New Inventors-20081008200000.mp4

## Summary

This recording is an episode of the Australian television program The New Inventors, hosted by James O'Loughlin, focusing on the future of transport. The episode opens with a discussion on the rising costs of petroleum and the environmental impact of transport, noting that over 40 percent of Australia's energy consumption is due to vehicles. The first featured invention is the Trev, a lightweight, three-wheeled electric vehicle developed by Dr. Peter Pudney from Adelaide. Designed for one or two passengers, the Trev weighs only 300 kilograms, uses no fossil fuels, and costs approximately one cent per kilometre to operate. It features a battery range of 120 kilometres, a top speed of 120 kilometres per hour, and requires minimal maintenance due to the lack of complex combustion engine parts. The second segment introduces a renewable hydrogen boat system presented by Colin and Sam. This technology uses wind and solar energy to separate hydrogen from seawater, which is then stored and used in fuel cells to power the vessel's propellers and refrigeration. The system is designed as a retrofit for trawlers, costing over half a million dollars but paying for itself within five years by eliminating fuel costs. The presenters discuss the safety of hydrogen, noting it is less flammable than gasoline, and the energy efficiency of using renewable sources for on-board generation. The final invention is a coaxial rotor helicopter developed by Sean Herbert in Canberra. This electric aircraft uses counter-rotating blades to eliminate the need for a tail rotor, making it 25 percent more energy efficient and easier to control. The design includes a single-stick control system and is available in various sizes, with the cheapest model priced at 180,000 dollars. The episode concludes with a segment where the panel members discuss their personal carbon emissions from driving and flying, emphasizing the need for infrastructure changes and diverse solutions to reduce transport's environmental footprint. The broadcast ends with a teaser for a behind-the-scenes special of the music quiz show Spicks and Specks.

# Techno-Pagans at the End of History 9(Esalen).mp3

## Summary

Part of 'Techno-Pagans at the End of History' (Esalen Institute, 2001) — a multi-day participatory weekend co-hosted by Mark Pesce, Douglas Rushkoff, and Erik Davis. The group moves between long-form conversation and open discussion with the audience, spanning virtual reality, psychedelics, nanotechnology, religion, and the end of history. (Episode 9, VR art continued.) Mark reflects on entering that VR work 'Ephemer' a few weeks earlier and coming out 'not with the feeling of longing that I'd had with Osmosis but incredibly joyous, because here was art — you can't be called by any other name. It's art, and it's a joyous art, it's a playful art, it's an art that is inviting you into it not merely to observe but to participate in the being.' He calls the artist 'the first' and 'really an exemplar,' and notes that everything she has done 'will be able to be done on a Nintendo 64 in two years time, and with good enough tools a twelve-year-old should be able to do it and send it across the internet, and I will be able to stand inside of someone else's head.' The interface 'comes out of a desire to be simple and effective and in the body and costs five dollars — none of this is rocket science. It's always a question of intent.' The session then moves into a guided chakra/sound exercise: energy moving up to 'everyone's favorite chakra, the heart' (green, surrounding the heart), then to the throat — 'the place where we say the things that we mean, the place where we sing from and cry from.'

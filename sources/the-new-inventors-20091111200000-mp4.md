# The New Inventors-20091111200000.mp4

## Summary

This recording is an episode of the Australian Broadcasting Corporation television program Inventors, hosted by James O'Loughlin. The show features three inventors presenting their creations to a panel of judges, including science journalist Bernie Hobbs, engineering professor Venus Sahajwala, and woodworker Richard Vaughan. The first invention is a safety boat propeller designed by Colin Chamberlain from Mackay. Chamberlain explains that his propeller features a rounded leading edge and additional slots to release water pressure, preventing the severe lacerations typically caused by standard blades. He demonstrates the safety by placing his arm in the spinning propeller, noting that while it may cause a bruise, it will not sever limbs. The judges praise the design for challenging the notion that efficiency must be prioritized over safety, with Chamberlain noting the speed loss is less than one percent. The second invention is the Scoot, a single-wheeled kick scooter created by Colin Redmond from Melbourne. The vehicle utilizes a hubless wheel with a low-friction nano material and pedals for steering, providing a low center of gravity. Redmond developed the scooter after observing children on traditional scooters struggling with small wheels on uneven surfaces. The panel discusses the potential for modular components and the durability of the polyurethane tires, which are expected to last five years. The third invention is the Check Clip, a medical device developed by paramedic Michael with assistance from Eileen and Neil Stead. This disposable plastic clip attaches an ampule directly to a syringe, ensuring that the medication source is never separated from the needle. This prevents dangerous mix-ups in emergency situations where multiple IV medications are being prepared. The judges highlight the device's ability to standardize practice and protect healthcare workers from sharps injuries. The episode concludes with the panel selecting the safety propeller as the winner of the night, placing Chamberlain in the running for the Inventor of the Year award. Viewers are invited to vote for the People's Choice Award via text or phone. The broadcast ends with a quote from French writer Andre Gide and a news segment covering the asylum seekers on the Oceanic Viking, World War One commemorations, and Australian birth rates.

# Common Factor 1_Future of Work_CLEAN.mp4

## Summary

This recording is a panel discussion from the Common Factor series, hosted by Madeline Palmer, editor in chief at Factor. The session explores the future of work, focusing on hybrid models, employee wellbeing, and the role of technology. The panel includes Stuart Munro, a change management leader; Belinda Morgan, a flexible work adviser and author; Mark Pesce, a futurist and co-founder of Wisely AI; and Nina Lucky Last, a workplace strategy specialist. The discussion references the pandemic as a catalyst for change, specifically noting that on March 25, 2020, organizations successfully transitioned to remote work, revealing hidden capacities for transformation. The speakers debate the shift toward shorter workweeks, with Pesce arguing that Australians have effectively adopted a four-day week due to digitization, with average hours dropping from 40 in 1984 to 33 post-pandemic. He predicts a move toward a 20-hour workweek within 20 years to support care cultures and an aging population. The conversation addresses challenges in mentoring younger generations, noting that while flexibility is valued, in-person interaction remains crucial for learning and building capabilities. Panelists discuss how organizations must intentionally design hybrid environments to facilitate these moments, using examples like Atlassian’s remote-first model to show how connection and productivity can be maintained. Technology is identified as both a tool and a barrier. Pesce explains that AI automates rote tasks, requiring a human-in-the-loop approach where employees act as gardeners of automated systems. He also notes that VR is less about immersion and more about simulation, which is becoming a core business skill. Other speakers highlight the importance of workplace design, including sound management, round spaces for creativity, and the need for quality amenities to make offices attractive. The discussion concludes with advice for engaging Gen Z workers, emphasizing fairness, achievement, and camaraderie as key drivers of engagement, and a prediction that the transition to flexible work will ultimately be seen as a manageable and beneficial evolution.

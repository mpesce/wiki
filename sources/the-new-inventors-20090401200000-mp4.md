# The New Inventors-20090401200000.mp4

## Summary

This recording is an episode of the Australian television program The New Inventors, hosted by James O'Loughlin. The panel of judges includes science journalist Bernie Hobbs, surgeon and inventor Professor Fiona Wood, and futurist and author Mark Pesce. The show features several inventors presenting their creations to the panel for feedback and potential funding. The first invention presented is a mobile metal arm designed to serve as a secure lifeline for workers in confined spaces, such as oil tanks or sewerage plants. The device allows for easier entry and exit and enables a worker to be extracted in an emergency without requiring a rescuer to enter the dangerous space. The inventors explain that the arm fits onto standard flanges and has been tested in training scenarios, though it has not yet been used in a real-life rescue. Next, Dr. Hamish Styler presents a low-cost emergency alarm system for medical offices. The device, which costs approximately sixty dollars, connects to a computer via USB and alerts other computers on the network when a doctor feels threatened or a patient collapses. The system includes software that monitors network connectivity to ensure the alarm is functional. The panel discusses the device's discreet design and its utility in de-escalating aggressive situations or calling for help during medical emergencies. The final invention is a portable stem aligner created by Doug Tillinghast and Nemi Orvina of Sydney City Council. This device resembles a car jack and is used to straighten bent street sign poles, reducing the physical strain and safety risks associated with manual repair methods. The inventors demonstrate the tool on set, explaining that it saves the council significant time and money by avoiding the need to jackhammer out concrete foundations. The episode concludes with a recap of the previous week's people's choice award and a brief news segment regarding severe weather and economic trends.

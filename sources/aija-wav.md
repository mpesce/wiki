# AIJA.wav

## Summary

This recording features a keynote address by Mark Pesce, a futurist, author, and former Apple and Mozilla engineer, delivered to an audience in Australia. The talk, titled "The Next Billion Seconds," projects societal, technological, and economic trends from the present day to January 2050. Pesce opens by defining his role as a trend analyst rather than a psychic, noting that a billion seconds from the time of speaking lands in late January 2050. He discusses demographic shifts, predicting Australia’s population will reach 38 million, comparable to California’s current size, while addressing climate change impacts such as rising sea levels displacing Pacific Islanders and creating climate refugees from Bangladesh. Pesce addresses the limitations of autonomous vehicles, citing a recent fatal accident in Arizona where emergency braking software was disabled, and argues that full self-driving cars are a "pipe dream" due to the complexity of urban environments. He contrasts this with the rapid adoption of autonomous drones and interstate trucking. The discussion then shifts to the future of work, describing a shift from linear career ladders to flexible, project-based teams where continuous learning and mentoring are central. He emphasizes that robots will handle repetitive tasks but lack the agility for skilled trades like plumbing or surgery, ensuring human expertise remains vital. Education is reimagined as a lifelong process focused on capacity building rather than rote fact acquisition, with assessments replaced by peer evaluation. The latter portion of the talk explores the role of artificial intelligence, warning that AI amplifies human biases, as seen in biased image search results and criminal justice algorithms. Pesce advocates for human oversight to "prune" these algorithms. He also touches on the transition to an experiential economy, the rise of smart money and digital currencies, and the importance of maintaining human connection in an increasingly automated world. The session concludes with a Q&A covering wealth inequality, screen addiction for children, the implementation of the EU’s GDPR, and the future of digital currencies and remote collaboration tools.

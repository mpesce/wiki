# The Swarm (Parts I, II, and III).mp3

## Summary

This recording is a lecture delivered by Mark Pesce, a media and technology commentator, to an audience in Australia. Although no specific date is provided in the transcript, Pesce references events from 2005, including the viral spread of the "This Land Is Your Land" mashup and the rise of social networking sites like Friendster and MySpace. The talk, titled "The Swarm," explores the evolution of media distribution from traditional broadcasting to peer-to-peer networks and the emergent behaviors of human social networks. Pesce begins by contrasting the fixed-cost economics of radio wave broadcasting with the linear cost increases of netcasting and the superior efficiency of BitTorrent, which he terms hyper-distribution. He argues that this shift has eliminated the scarcity of distribution channels that previously allowed studios and broadcasters to maintain monopolies. Consequently, the primary currency of the 21st century has shifted from money to attention. Because the distribution channel is now flattened, mass-produced studio content cannot compete with the sheer volume of user-generated material, forcing audiences to rely on social networks to filter noise and find relevant experiences. The latter half of the lecture focuses on the concept of the "human swarm," using Wikipedia as the primary example. Pesce describes Wikipedia’s growth through the "stone soup" metaphor, where individual contributions create a repository that attracts more knowledge until it becomes a gravitational force for human expertise. He notes that while Wikipedia is not perfectly accurate, it converges on "good enough" rather than perfection, reflecting a broader cultural shift toward accepting subjectivity and imperfection in media. The talk concludes with a discussion on how blogs and social networks are replacing traditional news media, suggesting that the future of information lies in collaborative, swarming behaviors that prioritize relevance and community over centralized control.

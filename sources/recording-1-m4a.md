# Recording 1.m4a

## Summary

This recording is a futurist talk by Mark Pesce, likely delivered at a Blue Sky event, where he explores the "weird" unintended consequences of emerging technologies. The presentation is structured around three main themes: autonomous vehicles, blockchain and cryptocurrency, and energy systems. Pesce begins by discussing self-driving cars, noting that while highway autonomy is achievable, full street-level autonomy is further away than expected due to complex edge cases like weather and traffic. He predicts a gradual rise in autonomy that will lower logistics costs, potentially leading to mobile retail concepts like the Robomart and driverless hotel rooms. He also highlights the potential of autonomous drones to transform logistics in remote areas like Papua New Guinea, where ground transport is expensive. The second section addresses blockchain technology, distinguishing it from the speculative frenzy of cryptocurrencies. Pesce explains the mechanics of blockchain using a travel expense analogy, emphasizing its utility for transparent, tamper-proof ledgers. He cites Australian examples such as Agridigital, which uses blockchain for agricultural settlements, and the ASX, which is moving to blockchain-based settlements. He then announces the "Mark Pesce Token," a personal coin offering representing his professional time, as an experiment in tokenizing labor. This concept is framed as a way to understand the future of personal currencies and professional services. The final segment focuses on energy, arguing that renewables, particularly solar, are becoming cheaper and more central to the grid. Pesce discusses the role of battery storage, referencing the Tesla battery in South Australia, and envisions a future where autonomous electric trucks transport charged batteries to balance regional power demands. He highlights Power Ledger, a Perth-based company that uses blockchain for peer-to-peer energy trading, as a key example. The talk concludes with a synthesis of these technologies, imagining a network of autonomous batteries trading energy via cryptocurrency, and ends with a call to embrace the professionalization of the "weird" future.

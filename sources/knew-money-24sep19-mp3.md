# Knew Money 24Sep19.mp3

## Summary

Talk on the convergence of the internet and economics ('the future of the Internet is the future of economics and vice versa'). Mark gives a 'crash course' in money: in a small village you don't need money as a medium of exchange because trust and socialization replace it; as you scale, money becomes 'a marker that replaces the trust.' He traces trading cultures back ~3,000 years and the pivotal role of the country at the crossroads of Africa, Europe, and Asia. The core argument is a widening gap: a transaction has to happen perfectly in 2-3 seconds across the card, processor, bank, and network — and only ~700 million people in the world have credit cards, versus ~4.5 billion with smartphones. 'There is a huge gap' between a smartphone-enabled civilization and a financial civilization. He discusses CRYPTONOMICS (his step-by-step introduction to crypto for non-experts) and wired money, and ends on geopolitics: by the end of the next decade, China's satellite states (Myanmar, Laos, Cambodia) will use some version of the digital yuan — not an amplification of the US-China trade war but 'a battle between a private company, Facebook, and China.' It reveals where the locus of authority has moved in the US and hasn't in China: 'the world we're moving into can be described as one world, two systems.'

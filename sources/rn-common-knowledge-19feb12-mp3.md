# RN_Common_Knowledge-19Feb12.mp3

## Summary

This recording is an episode of the ABC Radio National program Common Knowledge, hosted by Cassie McCullough and Jason De Rosso. The episode features two main guests: futurist and writer Mark Pesce, an honorary associate at the University of Sydney, and Edwina McCann, editor of Harper's Bazaar Australia. The broadcast discusses the power of anonymity in the digital age and the commercial value of red carpet events. The first segment focuses on the hacktivist group Anonymous and their recent actions regarding the Greek financial crisis. Mark Pesce explains that Anonymous took down key Greek government websites, including those of the prime minister and the Ministry of Finance, using distributed denial of service attacks. He characterizes the group as a leaderless, horizontally integrated collective that operates from a position of anonymity to avoid the vulnerabilities faced by named leaders, such as Julian Assange. Pesce notes that while the group’s rhetoric is often high drama and camp, referencing V for Vendetta, their actions represent a significant shift in how power is reconfigured by the network. He also mentions a threat posted on Paste Bin to shut down the global domain name service on March 31, a claim he views with caution but acknowledges as a display of the group's evolving social justice agenda. The second segment shifts to the fashion industry, where Edwina McCann discusses how the red carpet has become the most valuable advertising real estate, surpassing the traditional catwalk. She credits Anna Wintour and the shift toward dressing celebrities, such as Uma Thurman in Prada in 1996, for this change. McCann explains that fashion houses now invest heavily in styling suites for major awards like the Oscars and Grammys, leveraging social media to amplify brand visibility. Mark Pesce adds that social media acts as a force multiplier for these images, making the red carpet a dangerous place for celebrities due to the pervasive scrutiny and criticism from shows like Fashion Police. The episode concludes with the hosts sharing personal obsessions, including the TV show Kitchen Rules and Roger Waters’ multimedia performance of Pink Floyd’s The Wall in Sydney.

# At the Crossroads - CFO Series WA - 23Jul20.mp4

## Summary

This recording is a futurist talk delivered to an audience of Chief Financial Officers in Western Australia, likely in late 2020. The speaker, who identifies as a futurist with fifteen years of experience, discusses how the pandemic has altered economic and social landscapes, specifically referencing his recent work with Synergy in Perth and a prior address to CFOs in Melbourne. He emphasizes that the virus reproduction rate is the primary metric governing life and business planning, noting that Western Australia’s near-zero rate provides a clearer forward horizon compared to the lockdowns in Victoria and the uncertainty in Sydney. The speaker outlines three major crossroads facing businesses and individuals. The first is the shift of biosecurity to the status of national security, resulting in the multiplication of borders between states and regions. This creates economic bubbles that require careful management to grow safely without compromising health. The second crossroad concerns the transition to remote work, citing Microsoft’s findings that shorter meetings, blurred work-life boundaries, and the need for structured socialization are key challenges. He argues that managers, including CFOs, must increase one-on-one interactions to prevent employee burnout and maintain team cohesion. The third crossroad involves the acceleration of digital payments and the rise of neo-banks. The speaker notes that the pandemic forced a rapid move away from cash, with Perth already leading in digital adoption prior to the crisis. He highlights the growth of digital banks like Judo Bank, which are entering the business lending market, and predicts that individuals will increasingly use digital tools to optimize their finances similarly to corporate treasury management. He concludes by describing Australia as a three-speed economy, with Western Australia moving fastest, New South Wales at a moderate pace, and Victoria lagging due to lockdowns. He advises organizations to prioritize flexibility and balance to navigate this uneven global economic landscape until a vaccine is widely available.

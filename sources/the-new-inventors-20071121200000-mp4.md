# The New Inventors-20071121200000.mp4

## Summary

This recording is a transcript from an episode of the Australian television program The New Inventors, hosted by James O'Loughlin. The broadcast features a segment from a political interview with Kevin Rudd, who discusses his commitment to evidence-based policy and consultation regarding issues such as homelessness and Indigenous life expectancy. Following a brief commercial break, the main program segment introduces three inventors and their creations to a panel of judges including engineer James Bradfield Moody, science journalist Bernie Hobbs, and inventor Christine Kninmonks. The first inventor, Vilo Nimatolu from Adelaide, presents an improved shock absorber that uses a spring as a valve to allow for analog adjustment of damping, offering a more efficient and affordable alternative to traditional digital systems. The second inventor, Charlie Carrello, demonstrates a pneumatically operated machine that cuts and melts holes in plastic mulch for strawberry farming, significantly reducing labor time compared to manual methods. The third inventor, Troy O'Bree, showcases the Midget Solo Bush Trekker, a bicycle trailer that converts into a tent, designed for long-distance cyclists who want to avoid sleeping on the ground. The judges deliberate on the merits of the inventions, with James Bradfield Moody favoring the shock absorber for its originality and design, while Bernie Hobbs supports the strawberry hole puncher for its immediate marketability and practical design. The judges ultimately declare Charlie Carrello and his strawberry hole puncher the winner of the episode. The program concludes with a promotion for the upcoming grand final, where the top inventions of the year will compete for the 2007 Invention of the Year prize, and notes that the People's Choice award results will be published online on November 26.

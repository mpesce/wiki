# VR Creative Cluster Mark Pesce 9 Aug16 video2.MP4

## Summary

This recording is a talk by Mark Pesce, a media and technology commentator, discussing the emerging potential of virtual and augmented reality for education, business, and social interaction. While no specific date is stated, the content references recent events such as the release of Pokemon Go and an Atlantic article, situating the speech in the mid-2010s. Pesce begins by expressing enthusiasm for a new molecular assembly tool called Nano One for the Vibe, a virtual reality device. He argues that such tools can transform traditional "stick and ball" chemistry models into immersive experiences that allow students to visualize atomic orbitals and quantum mechanics, aligning with constructivist educational theories championed by Jean Piaget and Seymour Papert. Pesce transitions to the technical infrastructure of WebVR, noting its compatibility with mobile devices and desktop browsers, including Google’s Daydream platform. He predicts that by the following August, hundreds of millions of devices will support WebVR, shifting the industry’s focus from hardware access to content creation. He frames the web as a tool for sharing knowledge and VR as a tool for sharing experience, emphasizing that embodied learning is crucial for understanding complex systems. To illustrate this, he discusses the VR application Soundstage, which allows users to build electronic music setups in virtual space. He proposes that similar immersive simulation tools could revolutionize business process management, allowing organizations to model and test complex hierarchies and failure points in real-time, much like spreadsheets did for accounting decades ago. The speaker then addresses augmented reality, using Pokemon Go as a case study for the need for spatial zoning and property rights in shared virtual spaces. He recalls his own work from 1991 on "cyberspace protocol," a standard for managing virtual interactions, which he believes is now urgently needed to regulate how AR overlays interact with physical locations. He concludes by stating his intention to develop an internet-wide protocol for AR to ensure interoperability and respect for user space. In a Q&A session, he addresses questions about the balance between business and entertainment use cases, the future of room-scale VR sensors, and the transitional nature of current headsets compared to upcoming mixed reality devices like HoloLens and Magic Leap.

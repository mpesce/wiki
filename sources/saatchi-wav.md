# Saatchi.wav

## Summary

This recording is a lecture or presentation by Mark Pesce, a futurist and former Apple and Mozilla engineer, delivered to an audience likely involved in marketing or media. The talk opens with a critique of Elon Musk’s Tesla launch into orbit, which Pesce describes as a marketing genius but scientific failure. He defines his role not as a predictor of specific events, but as an analyst of the operating environment, comparing his work to that of a climatologist. Pesce argues that human dissatisfaction is the engine of progress, a concept he applies to the evolution of Amazon from a bookstore to a comprehensive provider of needs, culminating in the one-click purchase patent and the Alexa voice assistant. The core of the presentation focuses on the nature of artificial intelligence, which Pesce demystifies by explaining it as a process of learning from mistakes through repetition. He uses Google’s AlphaGo as a primary example, detailing how the program achieved godlike proficiency in the game of Go by playing billions of games against itself and learning from every error. This same learning mechanism explains the rapid improvement of voice assistants like Alexa, Google Assistant, and Siri, which now process billions of words daily. Pesce highlights a gap between the current state of these devices and their potential, noting that brands are currently invisible on these new voice channels. He contrasts the strategic approaches of Google, which operates as an intangible cloud of data, and Amazon, which is grounded in physical logistics and aims to narrow the gap between a voice command and the delivery of goods. Pesce warns against the Facebook model of advertising, citing internal documents that reveal the company exploits users’ vulnerable emotional states for profit. He argues that this approach has led to a loss of trust, particularly among younger demographics. Instead, he advocates for a new model based on direct relationships between brands and consumers, facilitated by voice interfaces that allow for sensitive, human-like interactions. The talk concludes with an introduction to the Basic Attention Token, a cryptocurrency created by Brendan Eich, which allows advertisers to pay audiences directly for their attention rather than through publishers. Pesce suggests this technology, already available in the Brave browser, shifts power to the consumer and offers a sustainable alternative to the exhausted impression-based advertising model.

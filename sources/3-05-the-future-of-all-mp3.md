# 3-05 The Future of All.mp3

## Summary

This recording is the third and final talk in a series delivered by Mark Pesce at the Speaker's Corner, titled "The Future of All." The lecture addresses the cultural fixation on the 2012 Mayan calendar prediction of the world's end, which Pesce refers to as the eschaton. He argues that while the concept of an expiration date can be empowering, the public's uncritical acceptance of the 2012 prophecy is a form of "received knowledge" that lacks personal verification. Pesce draws a historical parallel to the Millerites of the 1830s, a group that predicted the second coming in 1843 and 1844, leading to the Great Disappointment. He notes that unlike these historical groups, modern society has not altered its behavior or abandoned attachments in preparation for 2012, suggesting a dissonance between belief and action. Pesce distinguishes his views from those of Terence McKenna, clarifying that he does not endorse the idea of a sudden, catastrophic end. Instead, he aligns the concept of the eschaton with the technological singularity, a term coined by his friend Vernor Vinge. However, Pesce rejects the notion of a sudden, discontinuous leap in intelligence. He argues against the idea of artificial intelligence as a separate, alien entity that would overwhelm humanity, citing Ray Kurzweil's earlier work as an example of this misapprehension. Pesce posits that intelligence is not created but grown through subsumption and emergence, a process he calls hyperintelligence.

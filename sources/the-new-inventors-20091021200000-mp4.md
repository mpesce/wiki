# The New Inventors-20091021200000.mp4

## Summary

This recording is an episode of the Australian Broadcasting Corporation program The New Inventors, hosted by James A. Loughlin. The show features three inventors presenting their creations to a panel of judges consisting of agricultural scientist Chris Russell, engineering professor Veena Sahajwala, and futurist and author Mark Pesce. The first invention is the Vaxac, a wearable device created by Kenneth Miles from Rookle, New South Wales, designed to keep veterinary vaccines cool while freeing up a farmer's hands for handling cattle. Miles demonstrated the device with a live cow named Rosie, explaining that it maintains vaccine temperatures between two and eight degrees Celsius using ice packs, thereby preventing the loss of efficacy and reducing the risk of accidental needle sticks. The second invention is the Nova Tank, a portable water storage system that holds five tons of water and can be assembled through a standard doorway. The inventor, John, explained that the tank uses a strong, flexible liner protected by a solid top and base, allowing for easy transport and rapid deployment in disaster zones, farms, or construction sites. The panel discussed the advantages of its small footprint and the potential for adding shielding to protect the liner from vandalism or accidental damage. The third invention is the Pedigree Matchmaker, developed by Steve Semple and Mark Mortimer from Central West New South Wales. This system uses radio frequency identification tags and a data reader in a laneway to record the order in which lambs and ewes pass through. By analyzing the strong maternal bond where lambs follow their mothers for six to eight weeks, the software identifies the mother of each lamb with high accuracy, saving breeders significant time and labor compared to manual tracking. The judges deliberated on design, originality, and marketability. Chris Russell praised the Vaxac for its elegant simplicity, while Veena Sahajwala highlighted the originality of the Pedigree Matchmaker. Mark Pesce favored the Vaxac for its practical market appeal. Ultimately, the panel selected the Pedigree Matchmaker as the winner for solving a previously unsolved problem.

# Techno-Pagans at the End of History 8(Esalen).mp3

## Summary

Part of 'Techno-Pagans at the End of History' (Esalen Institute, 2001) — a multi-day participatory weekend co-hosted by Mark Pesce, Douglas Rushkoff, and Erik Davis. The group moves between long-form conversation and open discussion with the audience, spanning virtual reality, psychedelics, nanotechnology, religion, and the end of history. (Episode 8, the most politically charged.) Mark returns to the overpopulation/misery argument: 'fundamentalist Islam, the Catholic Church, have population policies in place which shove more people into disease, degradation, and early death every year than suffered under the Holocaust' — and the people who represent those views 'fly around in 747s and are joyously received by democratic governments... a collapse of moral perception of staggering proportion.' He admits 'this is the part of my spiel that most often gets people walking out on me. Because usually I end it by calling for hanging the Pope.' He asks how we square our connivance in dehumanizing policies that 'make it very, very difficult to offer hope to people in the third world.' The session then shifts to art in virtual space, describing an interactive VR work ('Ephemer'): you look into an object and it changes — 'you might look into a rock and see the forest inside it' — and travel further down through strata (tectonic plates in motion, active rocks) to the final substructure, which is a body (blood, bones, sinews, all in motion).

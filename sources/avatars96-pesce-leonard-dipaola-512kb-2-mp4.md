# avatars96-pesce-leonard-dipaola_512kb 2.mp4

## Summary

This recording is a panel discussion from a conference focused on virtual worlds and avatars, featuring Mark Pesce, Brett Leonard, and Steve from OnLive Technologies. Mark Pesce opens by presenting Web Earth, a VRML 2.0 visualization of the planet using live satellite data. He discusses the potential for this technology to become a powerful surveillance tool, arguing that the best way to prevent abuse is to open-source the code and empower the community. He announces the Web Earth Initiative, which partners with Digital Equipment Corporation and the World Health Organization to visualize climatological and epidemiological data. Pesce also introduces Vermal University, a free training program hosted by Electric Minds to teach VRML and visualization techniques to researchers and the public. Brett Leonard, a filmmaker and co-chairman of L-Squared Communications, follows by discussing the challenge of bringing virtual media to a mass audience. He argues that Hollywood talent is essential for creating high-production-value content that can attract mainstream users who are currently accustomed to passive media. Leonard describes his company’s project, isTV, which aims to empower users to create interactive content, and mentions a collaboration with Intel and the CIA creative agency to install a virtual studio in Hollywood. He emphasizes the need to break down the passive audience paradigm and uses cautionary tales in his films to address the societal risks of immersive technology. Steve from OnLive Technologies demonstrates a 3D voice chat application called Aztec World, which he describes as a tool for group communication rather than simple peer-to-peer chat. He highlights features such as distance attenuation, spatial audio, and natural social behaviors like personal space and group formation. The session concludes with a Q&A where the panelists discuss the importance of social context, persistent identity, and reputation systems in virtual environments. They agree that while the technology is advancing, significant social engineering is required to transform virtual spaces from empty plazas into functional communities.

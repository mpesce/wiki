## Summary

This recording is an interview featuring Mark Pesce, a media and technology commentator, discussing the intersection of history, technology, and geopolitics. The conversation begins with a reference to Mark Twain’s observation that the future rhymes, using Norman Angel’s 1909 book The Great Illusion as a historical parallel to Francis Fukuyama’s end of history thesis and Tom Friedman’s Golden Arches theory. Pesce argues that these optimistic frameworks failed to account for human selfishness and illogic, a lesson he believes the web pioneers of 1994 missed by building an ignorance amplifier alongside the global library. The discussion shifts to modern military technology, specifically the rapid price drop of autonomous drones and the emergence of semi-autonomous electric vertical takeoff and landing vehicles. Pesce highlights the remixing of off-the-shelf technologies, such as commercial drones and facial recognition, into lethal weapons systems, citing Ukrainian use of these tools to target Russian generals. He also details a deal between Palmer Luckey and the Australian Defence Force to build extra-large autonomous underwater vehicles to secure Australia’s coastline, a necessity driven by slow domestic submarine procurement and the threat posed by East Asian powers. The conversation touches on cyber warfare and electronic countermeasures, noting that war serves as a massive technological accelerator, a dynamic reminiscent of World War II. In the final segment, Pesce reflects on Australia’s status as a resource-rich but nervous nation, heavily dependent on China as a trading partner. He concludes by discussing the transition to a renewable economy, arguing that while the path is complex, it represents trillion-dollar opportunities rather than just roadblocks.

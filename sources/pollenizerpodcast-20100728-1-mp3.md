# PollenizerPodcast-20100728 (1).mp3

## Summary

This recording is an episode of the Pollenizer podcast, featuring an interview with Mark Pesce, a media and technology commentator and former Apple and Mozilla engineer. The conversation centers on Pesce’s new project, Plexus, an open-source software tool designed to unify social media interactions. Pesce explains that the project originated from his decision to leave Facebook due to privacy concerns regarding his social graph. He describes the invention process as an obsessive drive that does not leave him alone, noting that true inventions colonize an inventor's life until they are realized. He argues that successful innovation requires the right idea falling on the right person with the necessary skills and vision. Pesce details Plexus as a personal, portable, and private piece of software intended to run in the background of a mobile device. It acts as a traffic cop for communications, allowing users to interact with contacts across various platforms such as Facebook, Twitter, Flickr, and Yammer without needing to manage each service individually. A key feature discussed is the ability to communicate with specific Facebook friends without maintaining a full Facebook account, thereby preventing the company from accessing the user’s broader social graph. Pesce emphasizes that social graphs are valuable assets that individuals should retain ownership of, criticizing Facebook for making it difficult to export this data. He envisions Plexus as plumbing that connects disparate social networks, similar to how email protocols allow for universal communication. The interview also covers the development and distribution model of Plexus. Pesce states that the core kernel will be open source, comparable to the Linux kernel, providing an API that allows third parties to build various front-end applications and services. He mentions that while he is currently the primary developer, he has a community of followers on his blog and Twitter feed

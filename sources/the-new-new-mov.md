# The New New.mov

## Summary

This recording is a talk titled The New New, delivered by Mark Pesce to an audience of government employees. Pesce states that he will not discuss specific hardware but rather the social implications of technology, specifically focusing on the phenomenon he calls the new neurosis. He begins by analyzing the blog Mama Mia, a community for mothers, to illustrate how online networks amplify outrage. He traces the viral spread of a controversy regarding a Cotton On t-shirt, showing how it moved from a blog post to email, then Twitter, and finally to the front page of the Sydney Morning Herald within hours. Pesce argues that networks thrive on novelty, and human desire for new information causes outrage to propagate wildly, creating a state of constant anxiety for institutions. Pesce then discusses the concept of hyper-connectivity, using the website RateMyProfessors as a primary example. He explains that when students share their experiences with lecturers, they create a form of hyper-intelligence that empowers them to choose better teachers, thereby destabilizing traditional university tenure systems. He notes that institutions like Stanford University and the University of New South Wales are responding by sharing course materials online through platforms like iTunes University and YouTube, further eroding the exclusive control universities have over education. He argues that this sharing is inevitable and that institutions are melting under the pressure of a generation that naturally shares media rather than buying it. Addressing the audience directly, Pesce identifies them as the interface between government institutions and the outside world, acting as the shock troops of Government 2.0. He warns that they will face pressure from both outside communities demanding immediate information sharing and inside bureaucracies trying to lock it down. He advises that when they do share, they should expect a neurotic reaction from bloggers, which is actually a sign of successful connection. Finally, Pesce discusses his upcoming book, Share This Book, which he is developing using the very collaborative and open processes he advocates. He explains that the book will be a product of hyper-connectivity, with the author’s role evolving into that of a facilitator who herds the community’s creative efforts, rather than a sole creator.

# CFO Series NZ 18Jun20 The Bubble.mp4

## Summary

This recording is a talk delivered by Mark Pesce, a futurist and host of the podcast The Next Billion Seconds, to an audience in New Zealand. Although the specific date is not stated, the content references events from March and April of the current year, the appointment of the military to handle quarantine in New Zealand, and the fiscal year 2020-2021, indicating the speech took place in late 2020. Pesce begins by acknowledging the unprecedented nature of the pandemic, noting that the exponential growth of the virus initially obscured the planning horizon. He highlights New Zealand’s successful lockdown strategy, which flattened the curve and granted the nation a clearer view for planning through the end of the fiscal year compared to other countries. The main topic of the talk is the emerging economic and social landscape defined by the concept of a "dome." Pesce argues that New Zealand is living under a protective dome with functionally closed borders, creating a persistent tension between maintaining safety and sustaining economic activity. He discusses the potential for a trans-Tasman bubble with Australia as a prototype for future international travel, emphasizing the need for trust and gradual expansion. A central thesis of the presentation is that biosecurity has become the new national security. Pesce points to the New Zealand Prime Minister’s decision to appoint the military to manage quarantine as evidence of this shift, noting that the Chief Health Officer has effectively become the most powerful figure in the country due to the critical importance of preserving biosecurity. Pesce concludes by addressing the role of the Chief Financial Officer in this new environment. He explains that the pandemic has accelerated the shift toward contactless business practices, including digital payments and remote work. Finance is particularly amenable to remote work, but this requires CFOs to act as social glue for distributed teams. The talk ends with the assertion that traditional annual planning cycles are insufficient. Organizations must adopt agile, weekly, or even daily planning cycles to adapt to moving goalposts. Pesce suggests that companies that master this flexibility and learn from rapid changes will thrive long after the pandemic ends.

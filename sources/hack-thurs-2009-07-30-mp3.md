# hack_thurs_2009_07_30.mp3

## Summary

This recording is an episode of the Australian radio program Hack, hosted by Kato Tool. The broadcast covers several distinct news segments, beginning with a report on the aftermath of terrorist bombings in Jakarta, Indonesia. The segment notes that two Australians, Craig Senga and Nathan Verity, were killed in the attacks, and funerals were held for them. It highlights a new youth movement called Indonesia Unite, which uses social media to project a message of resilience against terrorism. The program then features an interview with technology writer and futurist Mark Pesce regarding a new deal between Microsoft and Yahoo to create a co-managed search engine to rival Google. Pesce explains that Microsoft effectively bought its way into second place in the market, combining its Bing search technology with Yahoo’s advertising capabilities. He discusses the competitive dynamics, noting that while Google remains the dominant leader, the partnership allows Microsoft to gather user data and compete for the future of search across mobile and cloud platforms. Subsequent segments include a report by John Barrington on the potential decline of traditional petrol stations as electric vehicle infrastructure, such as battery swap stations, becomes more prevalent. Another report by Alice Brennan details the controversy in Alice Springs, where the Australian government is acquiring town camps through 40-year lease agreements to secure tenure for a 100 million dollar housing investment, a move criticized by some as a land grab. The episode concludes with a piece on the difficulty victims of sexual assault face in communicating their experiences to family, prompted by a controversial lie detector stunt on a commercial radio station. The segment features a victim who was assaulted at age nine and has not told her parents twelve years later, illustrating the long-term psychological impact of such trauma.

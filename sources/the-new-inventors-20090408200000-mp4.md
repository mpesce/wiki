# The New Inventors-20090408200000.mp4

## Summary

This recording is an episode of the Australian television program The New Inventors, hosted by Mark Pesce and Christine O'Loughlin. The show features three inventors presenting their creations to a panel of judges, with the winner selected for a chance to be named Inventor of the Year. The first segment features Jason Schaller, who presents HeliTech, a modular water tank system that can be attached to standard helicopters in under thirty minutes to convert them into firefighting aircraft. Schaller explains that the system uses a high-powered electric pump to fill an extendable drop bag with up to 4,000 liters of water in approximately 26 seconds. He notes that the system has been trialed in Victorian bushfires and allows military helicopters to return to their bases once the fire threat subsides, unlike dedicated firefighting aircraft. The second invention is the Gripper Lace, presented by Eugene Fong from Melbourne. This device is a fabric loop and gripping plate system that secures shoelaces without the need for traditional knotting, preventing them from coming undone during activity. Fong demonstrates how the laces are threaded through the central aperture and secured with side clips. The panel discusses the potential market for the product, noting its utility for children and individuals with limited dexterity, while also questioning the durability of the attachment method on leather shoes. The final segment introduces Brendan, who presents a wireless infrared gaming gun system. The device allows players to engage in live-action shooting games in outdoor environments without a central computer, using infrared beams to detect hits on targets up to 100 meters away. The system provides instant audio and visual feedback on a small monitor attached to the gun. The judges debate the merits of the three inventions, with Mark Pesce and the other panelists ultimately selecting HeliTech as the winner of the night due to its significant innovation and practical application in emergency response. The broadcast concludes with a prompt for viewers to vote for the People's Choice award and a brief news segment covering police raids on drug syndicates and bank interest rate decisions.

# 3-04 Panel Discussion including Dr. George Greer.mp3

## Summary

This recording captures a panel discussion moderated by Mark Pesce, featuring Dr. George Greer, a psychiatrist who conducted early legal research on the psychotherapeutic use of MDMA in the 1980s. The session appears to be part of a larger conference, with references to previous speakers such as John Hanna and Matt Baggett. Dr. Greer opens by describing his work with approximately 80 subjects using MDMA synthesized in a laboratory setting prior to its prohibition. He then presents a theoretical framework based on Arthur Young’s reflexive universe model, dividing human experience into four levels: physical, mental, emotional, and spiritual. Greer argues that the primary purpose of psychedelic use is to access the highest spiritual level to make conscious choices, a state he defines as being fully alive. He explains that psychedelics disrupt habitual neural patterns, forcing the user to actively choose their perception and purpose, which can lead to profound learning if the individual can integrate the experience. The discussion shifts to the recent Griffiths psilocybin study, which received significant mainstream media coverage. Greer highlights that the study’s most significant finding was not the immediate mystical experience, but the fact that 50 percent of participants reported being fundamentally happier six months later, a change corroborated by their families. The panel discusses the concept of making psychedelics "boring" or mundane through scientific description to reduce fear and stigma. They note the disconnect between the psychedelic community, which often views such research as obvious, and the general public, who are terrified by the loss of control associated with these substances. In the final segment, the focus turns to the practical integration of psychedelic insights into daily life. A psychotherapist on the panel emphasizes that a powerful internal experience does not automatically translate into life change; it requires behavioral engagement, perseverance, and support. Greer suggests that discussing the experience with a close person helps reinforce the insights, while another speaker notes the importance of monitoring internal physiological states to access the benefits of the experience without the drug. The conversation concludes with a cautionary note from a reference to shamanic traditions, suggesting that visions or epiphanies during altered states are not always truthful and should be treated with skepticism rather than absolute certainty

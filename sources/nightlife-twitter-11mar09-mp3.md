# nightlife-twitter-11Mar09.mp3

## Summary

ABC Radio 'Night Live' (Tony Delroy, 11 Mar 2009) panel on Twitter, with futurist Mark Pesce and business-tech expert Paul Wallbank. Mark explains Twitter grew ~400% the prior year and offers two metaphors: a SWITCHBOARD (a conference call where one 140-character 'tweet' is broadcast to everyone connected) and a giant array of SMS messages patched back and forth. He distinguishes FOLLOWERS (people who receive your tweets) from FOLLOWEES (people you elect to receive tweets from) — 'they don't have to be the same people' (you may follow an ex-girlfriend who's stalking you, or someone trying to sell you something). He's been on it a year, has ~3,000 followers and follows ~2,300 (mostly Australians). Callers weigh in: one deliberately avoids Twitter and describes meeting strangers in a bar by avatar; another notes the cycle of popularity (he's 'cracked 11,000 posts'); others describe the 140-character limit, cheating it into two 280s, and how the network has to be built up before you 'fall into the hole.' The consensus: you only see what Twitter is once you've built up your network.

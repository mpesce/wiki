# 7-30Report-eBooks-18Mar10.mp4.m4v

## Summary

This recording is a news report by Deb Cornwall discussing the rapid adoption of electronic books and the subsequent shift in the publishing industry. The segment highlights how publishers, previously resistant to digital formats, are now embracing ebooks to survive, mirroring the earlier transition in the music and movie industries. With predictions that up to 50% of books will be sold digitally within a decade, the report notes that demand has surged following the global release of Amazon’s Kindle and the upcoming launch of the Apple iPad. Industry insiders argue that delaying digital releases to protect hardback sales is no longer viable, as a significant portion of digital sales occur within the first 24 hours of a book’s release. The report features several perspectives on the changing landscape. Australian publisher Louise Adler expresses disappointment in the limited catalogue available, noting that most contemporary fiction and nonfiction is missing and that many titles are targeted at American audiences. She attributes this partly to publisher resistance against Amazon’s attempt to cap ebook prices below ten US dollars. Contrary to expectations, the primary demographic driving ebook sales is not young digital natives but middle-aged women. Sydney nurse Candice Gray is profiled as an example of this trend; she purchased a Kindle for her husband but found the device instantly appealing, reading three books in less than a month and utilizing the built-in shopping catalogue. Gray notes that the device has changed her reading habits, allowing her to read in shorter bursts throughout the day. The segment also touches on the impact on local libraries, which are preparing for a transformation where ebooks can be borrowed without late fees or overdue issues, with digital libraries available 24/7.

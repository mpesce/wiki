# Citi.wav

## Summary

This recording captures a 35-minute keynote presentation by Mark Pesce, a media and technology commentator, delivered at a conference in Australia. The talk, titled around the theme of the "next billion seconds," explores the intersection of automation, artificial intelligence, and human capability in the evolving financial landscape. Pesce begins by noting the rapid pace of change, citing the recent shift to zero-cost trading in the United States by firms like Robinhood, Charles Schwab, and E-Trade as a catalyst for increased automation. He argues that while computers excel at predictable tasks, they fail at the unpredictable, leaving unique value in human judgment and intuition. A significant portion of the talk is dedicated to the emergence of digital currencies, specifically Facebook’s proposed Libra. Pesce explains that unlike digital payments which rely on existing banking infrastructure, digital currencies can function anywhere with smartphone access, potentially unlocking commerce for billions of unbanked individuals in developing nations like Nigeria and Indonesia. He discusses the Libra Association, noting recent withdrawals by major partners like Visa and MasterCard, but predicts the project will proceed due to the value of transaction data and geopolitical dynamics between the US and China. He contrasts this with the limitations of current mobile money systems and highlights the potential for digital assets to transform trading into a high-frequency, data-driven environment. Pesce then shifts to the implications for workers and organizations, arguing that machine learning tools, while powerful, are inherently backward-looking and blind to new patterns. He posits that the future advantage, or alpha, lies in human forward-thinking and intuition, which must be paired with sophisticated tools. He advocates for a new organizational model focused on identifying, mentoring, and sharing talent, predicting that by 2030, work, education, and mentoring will merge into a single continuous process. The recording concludes with a Q&A session where Pesce addresses questions regarding the competitive threats to Libra, the impact on developing economies, and the role of regulatory compliance in the adoption of digital currencies.

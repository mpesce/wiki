# ABCTheWorld 13dec22.m4v

## Summary

This recording is an interview with Mark Pesce, a media and technology commentator, discussing the collapse of the cryptocurrency exchange FTX and its founder, Sam Bankman-Fried. The conversation takes place around the time of Bankman-Fried's arrest, with the host noting that charges were expected to be filed in the US the following morning. Pesce describes the FTX empire as an internal Ponzi scheme built on the FTT coin, which was loaned to the investment arm Alameda Research to inflate valuations. When the peg of the stablecoin collapsed, FTX could not meet depositor demands, triggering a bank run on assets that were essentially worthless. Pesce highlights the lack of due diligence by major investors like Sequoia and the complete absence of accounting controls, which allowed for the commingling of depositor and investment funds. The discussion shifts to the legal implications, noting that while wire fraud charges are likely, it remains unclear how existing securities and banking laws apply to cryptocurrencies. Pesce observes that other crypto exchanges, previously antagonistic to regulation, are now clamoring for oversight to restore customer trust through audits and capital reserves. He draws parallels between the current "crypto winter" and historical financial crises, such as the 1997 Asian financial crisis triggered by Long-Term Capital Management, suggesting that similar mistakes are being repeated in a new medium. The interview also addresses the role of celebrity endorsements, with Pesce noting that figures like Matt Damon, Jimmy Fallon, and Kim Kardashian face lawsuits for promoting investments they could not inspect for financial prudence. Finally, the conversation addresses the recovery of lost funds. While cryptocurrency transactions are theoretically traceable, the poor record-keeping at FTX has complicated the process.

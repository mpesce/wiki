## Summary

This recording is a radio interview featuring Mark Pesce, a media and technology commentator, discussing the societal and ethical implications of emerging technologies, specifically nanotechnology and artificial intelligence. The broadcast begins with a commercial for an HGH supplement and a hand-cranked emergency radio, followed by a discussion with a host and callers. A listener from Denver asks whether new technologies will be abused, to which Pesce responds that society must rationally prepare for potential misuse while acknowledging the benefits. The conversation then shifts to the energy requirements of nanotechnology, with Pesce noting that biological systems offer models for efficient energy use, comparing human energy consumption to a 120-watt light bulb. Callers subsequently inquire about the future of entertainment and the role of computers in raising children on other planets. Pesce argues that a computer raised by humans would possess a broader, more human intellect, whereas a purely rational AI might make alien and dangerous ethical decisions, such as lacking mercy in justice systems. The discussion moves to immersive media, referencing the video game Majestic, which blurs the line between reality and virtual experience by contacting players through personal devices. Pesce compares the potential addictive nature of such immersive technologies to the historical fear of novels, suggesting that society will adapt to new storytelling forms. The final segment addresses the economic impact of nanotechnology. A caller asks how the ability to fabricate goods on demand would affect the global economy. Pesce posits that this technology could lead to freedom from want by producing food from raw soil and removing the scarcity that currently drives material value and conflict. He predicts a shift from an economy based on material consumption to one based on the consumption of designs and creativity, fundamentally altering the nature of money and productivity. The transcript ends as the host prepares to take more phone calls. No specific date is mentioned in the transcript, though the reference to the game Majestic suggests a timeframe around its release in the early 2000s.

# The New Inventors-20070221195900.mp4

## Summary

This recording is an episode of the Australian television program The New Inventors, hosted by James O'Loughlin. The show features a panel of experts, including engineering professor Veena Sahajwala, agricultural scientist Chris Russell, and designer Sally Deminks, who evaluate three distinct inventions. The first presenter, Glenn Thompson from Orange, New South Wales, introduces the Thompson coupling, a new constant velocity joint designed to reduce friction and energy loss in vehicles and machinery. The panel demonstrates that the device allows shafts to rotate at an angle with minimal resistance compared to traditional steel-ball joints, noting its potential for significant fuel savings and improved safety. The second invention is the Variety adjustable golfing platform, presented by John Crossley from Sydney. This device allows golfers to practice on uneven terrain by tilting a practice mat up to 12 degrees using a system of rotating wedges. The panel discusses the stability of the steel construction and the practicality of the design for driving ranges, acknowledging that while it is a niche product, it offers a superior solution for practicing shots on slopes. The final invention is the No Sweat guttering system, presented by Jock McNeish and Liam Rosney from Melbourne. This automated system uses electric actuators to tilt roof gutters, allowing debris to fall out for easy cleanup without the need for ladders. The presenters explain that the system can be programmed to empty regularly or flip upside down during bushfire season to prevent ember accumulation. The panel praises the lateral thinking involved in changing the problem from prevention to removal. The episode concludes with the panel unanimously selecting the Thompson coupling as the winner of the night, noting its originality and massive potential market impact, while also inviting viewers to vote for the People's Choice Award.

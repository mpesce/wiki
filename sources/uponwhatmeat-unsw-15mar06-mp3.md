# UponWhatMeat-UNSW-15Mar06.mp3

## Summary

UNSW talk (15 Mar 2006) on how expectations of technology change over time, opening with a Wikipedia poll and noting that for anyone under 21 the web is younger than they are. His question: 'what the hell did people do with computers before the web?' (Before 1994 there was no web and little email, so people did other things with computers.) The central idea is EXTERNALIZATION: the calculator externalized our math ability (before it, 'we had to use a lot of log tables'); the mobile phone is about to externalize our brain for our SOCIAL life — 'it's already doing that, but poorly, because no one's really designed it to do that.' He cites the Japanese phenomenon of CO-PRESENCE: 15- and 16-year-olds who are in continuous SMS conversation with four or five friends from waking to sleeping — not detailing every moment, but maintaining a continuous social presence. He describes a little program to route a phone's data traffic through a nearby internet-connected PC via Bluetooth (circumventing the carrier), so phones can support services we couldn't have before — 'maintaining and exploring our social networks.' The mobile phone, he argues, is not just a voice device (as in America) or a voice-and-text device (as in Australia) — 'it's a device for us to explore the connections that make us part of a community.'

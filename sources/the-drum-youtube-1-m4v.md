# The Drum-YouTube (1).m4v

## Summary

This recording is an interview from ABC Radio National's The Drum, featuring Mark Pesce, an author, futurist, and virtual reality expert, discussing the state of social media and emerging technology. The conversation takes place in the context of the early 2011 WikiLeaks controversy, with the host noting that Twitter has grown to over 175 million users in just four years. The dialogue opens with a lighthearted anecdote about the confusion between corn starch and corn flour in Australia, which serves as a segue into the central theme of the episode: the fragility of trust in an era of ubiquitous online information. Pesce argues that as reliance on social media increases, the need to forge and reinforce bonds of trust becomes critical. He describes the current political climate as a period of negotiation between governments and citizens, where both sides are testing the boundaries of power in a "vast room" filled with dangerous red buttons. He predicts that rather than relaxing control, governments will become more sclerotic, citing the U.S. military's ban on USB sticks as an example. The discussion also touches on information warfare, where disinformation campaigns muddy the waters around events like the WikiLeaks leak, making it difficult to distinguish truth from state-sponsored or hacktivist interference. Pesce suggests that media organizations must use social media to rebuild trust, noting that individuals often rely on trusted personal connections to verify news rather than traditional outlets. In the final segment, Pesce shifts focus to the Microsoft Kinect, a 3D motion-sensing device for the Xbox 360. He predicts that this technology will become standard in all computers and tablets within five years, allowing machines to monitor user mood, posture, and identity in three dimensions

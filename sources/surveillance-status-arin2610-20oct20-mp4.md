## Summary

This recording is a lecture by Mark Pesce, a media and technology commentator, discussing the implications of augmented reality. Pesce states that he is promoting a book titled Augmented Reality, published by Polity Press, which he notes was facilitated by a connection made by Fiona. The talk argues that augmented reality is not a future technology but one already deployed at scale, using the 2016 release of Pokemon Go as a primary example. Pesce opens with an anecdote about a gathering in Peg Patterson Park in Rhodes, Sydney, on the evening of July 13, 2016. Hundreds of people congregated in the park at 11 p.m. to play Pokemon Go, causing a disturbance that required police intervention. He attributes this behavior to Niantic, the game’s creator, inscribing meaning into the physical space through PokeStops and PokeGyms. Pesce posits that when data is written into space, it alters human behavior because people act according to the metadata they perceive in their environment. The lecture then traces the historical origins of this technology to the 1990s and early 2000s, focusing on John Hanke. Hanke founded Keyhole, a company providing high-resolution Earth imagery that was notably used by CNN during the 2003 Iraq War. Google acquired Keyhole in 2004 to develop locative search capabilities, which evolved into Google Earth and Google Maps. Pesce argues that Google Maps does not present an objective view of the world but rather a filtered version designed to direct user behavior toward specific businesses and routes.

# Unknown Knowns 31Oct19.mp3

## Summary

This recording is a keynote address delivered by Mark Pesce, a media and technology commentator and former Apple and Mozilla engineer, to an audience of educators. The talk opens with an acknowledgment of the Gadigal people of the Eora nation and references a preceding Q&A session. Pesce frames his discussion around the concept of "unknown knowns," arguing that while the future is uncertain, we already possess the tools to navigate it. He challenges the prevailing narrative that automation and artificial intelligence will render human skills obsolete, citing the failed predictions of MOOCs replacing teachers and the delayed arrival of self-driving cars as evidence that human connection and complex environmental adaptation remain irreplaceable. Pesce argues that the rise of machine intelligence actually highlights the value of uniquely human traits, specifically the capacity for mentoring. He proposes that education must shift from a model of standardized workforce preparation to one that amplifies these human qualities. This involves integrating peer mentoring and peer assessment into the curriculum from the earliest years, treating these as essential skills alongside literacy and numeracy. He suggests that assessment and mentoring are fundamentally the same act, relying on trust rather than traditional testing. The speaker then addresses the disconnect between university graduates and the job market, attributing it to the rapid pace of automation. He predicts that in the near future, learning will become a continuous part of daily work, similar to the apprenticeship models already used in trades and professions. Pesce urges school leaders and teachers to embrace this "most human age" by mentoring one another, breaking down institutional silos, and modeling the continuous learning culture they expect from their students. He concludes by emphasizing that while the specific shape of the future is unknown, the path forward lies in strengthening human relationships and collaborative learning.

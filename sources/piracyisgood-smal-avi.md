# PiracyIsGood-smal.avi

## Summary

This recording is a talk by Mark Pesce, a media and technology commentator, delivered to an audience on October 18, 2004. The presentation, titled "Piracy is Good," explores the impact of the BitTorrent protocol on television distribution and economics. Pesce begins by discussing the premiere of the new Battlestar Galactica series, noting that while the UK broadcast on Sky One preceded the US airing on Sci-Fi Channel, the resulting file sharing via BitTorrent did not harm ratings. Instead, the ease of access allowed fans to spread word of the show’s quality, leading to record viewership in the US. He explains that BitTorrent operates as a swarm, where popularity increases distribution efficiency, contrasting this with traditional server-based models that crash under high demand. Pesce argues that piracy acts as a marketing tool, citing the leaked pilot of the new Doctor Who series as another example where unauthorized distribution boosted interest and ratings. He highlights a fundamental disconnect between producers and audiences, noting that viewers have been conditioned to expect immediate, on-demand access to content. This expectation conflicts with the traditional economic model where advertisers pay broadcasters, who pay distributors, and finally producers. With piracy breaking this chain, the value of advertising is compromised because the audience is no longer captive to the broadcast schedule. Pesce dismisses the idea of paid downloads, such as the iTunes model, arguing that audiences expect television to be free, particularly in the UK where a license fee covers broadcast costs. The speaker then shifts focus to the Australian television market, describing it as an oligopoly dominated by three commercial networks.

# Rushkoff-MediaSquat-30Jun09.mp3

## Summary

This recording is a live broadcast of The Media Squat on WFMU radio, hosted by Douglas Rushkoff from the Personal Democracy Forum conference at Lincoln Center in New York. The episode features interviews with three guests: Dave Weinberger, Mark Pesce, and Scott Heiferman. Rushkoff opens by introducing the forum as a gathering of individuals working on bottom-up solutions to societal problems, noting the presence of Obama administration officials and the theme of transparency. The first segment features Dave Weinberger, a researcher at the Harvard Berkman Center for Internet and Society. Weinberger discusses the shift in the role of facts from being used as weapons in political arguments to being openly released through platforms like data.gov. He argues that this transparency allows citizens to engage in endless discussion and debate rather than accepting a single government narrative, describing the process as an "un-nailing" of facts. He contrasts the rigid, top-down nature of traditional hierarchies with the messy, human, and fallible nature of hyperlinks and open networks, suggesting that this shift enables a freer type of thought that incorporates differences. The second guest is Mark Pesce, a media commentator and former Apple engineer currently based in Australia. Pesce discusses the collision between open systems, such as Wikipedia, and closed hierarchical organizations, using the recent ban of Scientology editors from Wikipedia as a primary example. He frames this conflict as a broader cultural shift where open networks are wearing down traditional hierarchies, leading to inevitable "crashes" or catastrophic failures as these mismatched systems interact. Pesce also shares that he is working on a book titled Share This Book, which will be written as an open, collaborative project to challenge the traditional concept of single-author ownership. The final guest is Scott Heiferman, founder of Meetup.com. Heiferman discusses the challenge of keeping the platform authentic and community-driven while managing corporate sponsorship interests. The conversation explores the difference between bridging social capital and deep, vulnerable human connection, with Rushkoff and Heiferman agreeing that the current era requires learning how to be in a "truly, openly honest village world" where people can be their authentic selves within a community. The broadcast concludes with credits for the production team and a brief musical outro.

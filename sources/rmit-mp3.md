# RMIT.mp3

## Summary

Keynote to a room full of designers at RMIT: 'you all will be [designers], because this is the future.' He presents a logical argument for why we are 'hurtling into a future of design,' riffing on Buckminster Fuller's call for a 'comprehensive design science revolution' (design every object for maximum utility and efficiency), and on the artifacts that have appeared since Fuller died in 1983. The talk is in three parts — matter, intelligence, and design — which are not separate disciplines but co-evolving, dynamically changing each other, 'becoming a single discipline.' He traces the lineage of thinking machines (Babbage's difference engine, punched-card looms, Hollerith's 1880s census cards still in use in Florida in 2000, then Turing and von Neumann as 'the twin pillars of computer science,' and McCarthy, Papert, and Minsky founding the MIT AI lab). He closes with a design-ethics warning on head-mounted displays: your brain uses SIX cues for depth (parallax is only one); if a helmet gives you only one, your brain trains on that single cue, and when you take it off you're still only using it — which is why pilots aren't allowed to fly a real plane for 24 hours after a simulator. 'Every object you create is a designer in the age of active materials.' Think ethically; design for openness, interaction, and play.

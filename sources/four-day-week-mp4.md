# Four Day Week.mp4

## Summary

This recording is a segment from a conversation or interview featuring Mark Pesce, a media and technology commentator. The discussion centers on the evolution of the Australian work week and the societal resistance to formally adopting a four-day schedule. The speaker, Mark, responds to a question regarding the drivers behind this shift, specifically addressing whether the pandemic was the primary cause. Mark argues that the transition to a shorter work week was already in progress long before the pandemic. He cites Australian Bureau of Statistics data indicating that the average work week was 40 hours in 1984. By 2019, this figure had decreased to 34 hours, and post-pandemic, it has further dropped to 33 hours. He attributes this gradual decline to widespread digitization, which allowed productivity to remain stable even as the number of hours worked decreased. Consequently, Mark contends that Australians have effectively already adopted a four-day work week, even though the change has not been explicitly acknowledged or legislated. Despite the implicit shift, Mark notes a cultural reluctance to consciously embrace the four-day model. He points to numerous studies showing that organizations which formally transition to a four-day week experience increased productivity and higher employee satisfaction. He suggests that society is currently tiptoeing around the opportunity to intentionally define the structure of work. The segment concludes with Mark describing the potential for a conscious shift as a straightforward opportunity that is being overlooked due to a general resistance to grasping the reality of the changes that have already occurred.

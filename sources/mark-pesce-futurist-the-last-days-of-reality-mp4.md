## Summary

This recording is a talk delivered by Mark Pesce at the Golden Age Cinema, likely in 2017 given the reference to events in "May this year." The presentation, titled or themed around the "last days of reality," begins with a guided meditation illustrating how a person in a dark room becomes conditioned to respond to a glowing screen. Pesce uses this metaphor to introduce his central argument: that social media platforms, specifically Facebook, have evolved from simple information distributors into sophisticated engines of behavioral modification. He argues that Facebook’s shift toward curation in 2011 was not merely about content filtering but about using deep data gathering and machine learning to create detailed user profiles. These profiles allow the platform to predict and manipulate emotional states, thereby maximizing user engagement and advertising revenue. Pesce details how this "weaponization of influence" operates through a feedback loop where machine learning observes user reactions to censored content and adjusts future inputs to keep users engaged. He cites a 2015 revelation that Facebook could alter user moods and a 2017 report by The Australian regarding the targeting of emotionally vulnerable teenagers as evidence of this machinery. The speaker identifies four converging technologies that are creating a "post-real" world: the monetization of fake news, machine learning for emotional profiling, neuro-marketing, and augmented reality. He explains that fake news thrives on these platforms because it generates high engagement, creating a trap where Facebook cannot fully demonetize it without hurting its own metrics. Furthermore, he highlights the role of political actors like Cambridge Analytica, which uses these tools to target individual voters with highly personalized propaganda, influencing outcomes such as Brexit and the election of Donald Trump.

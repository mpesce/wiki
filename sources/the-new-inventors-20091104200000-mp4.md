# The New Inventors-20091104200000.mp4

## Summary

This recording is an episode of the Australian Broadcasting Corporation television program The New Inventors, hosted by Mark Pesce. The show features three inventors presenting their creations to a panel of judges, which includes engineer James Bradfield Moody, designer Alison Page, and futurist Mark Pesce. The first invention presented is the Jam Bot, a computer software developed by James Bradfield Moody that listens to live musical audio signals and improvises accompaniment. The system extracts the musical essence of the performance, such as tempo and bar structure, to generate rhythmic responses. While currently focused on percussion, the inventor aims to expand its capabilities to detect tones and notes for a wider range of instruments. The panel discusses its potential applications in interactive music video games and as a standalone instrument, noting its ability to follow moving tempos and adapt to different musical styles. The second invention is the Carrymaster, a collapsible, tripod-based shopping trolley designed by Keith McArthur and Victor Taranto. The device weighs approximately 11.5 kilograms, holds 15 shopping bags, and features all-terrain wheels and an anti-tilt stabilizer. It is designed to be lightweight and easy to maneuver, serving as an alternative to standard supermarket trolleys for those who prefer to walk to stores. The inventors demonstrate its stability and ease of use, highlighting its ergonomic design that allows users to lean on the frame for support. The panel praises its originality and practicality for reducing the physical strain of carrying groceries. The third invention is the Roach Wrapper, a manual stretch-wrapping tool created by Glenn Roach. The device allows workers to wrap pallets of goods efficiently without bending over or moving backwards, which are common causes of workplace back injuries. It features a handle for steering, a brake for tension adjustment, and a guide roller for pivoting. The inventor explains that the tool improves consistency and safety, with one user reporting that he no longer needs medication for back pain after using it. After deliberating on originality, design, and marketability, the judges award the win to Glenn Roach for the Roach Wrapper, citing its practical utility and immediate solution to a widespread workplace safety issue.

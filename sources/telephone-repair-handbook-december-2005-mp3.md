# Telephone Repair Handbook December 2005.mp3

## Summary

Research talk (with graduate student Angus Fraser) presenting a new ubiquitous-computing platform, framed by a prediction he made on the New Inventors panel: 'in five years, you're going to be using your mobile telephone 10 times more than you do today' — met by the audience with 'wearied acceptance' and dread. He interrogates the friction between the joy of communication and 'the porcupine bed of our technologies.' He argues the modern mobile phone is not just a GSM/GPRS terminal but a node on at least FOUR different networks (and five once it gets Wi-Fi) — we just haven't recognized the power of that versatile interface, still treating Bluetooth as a way to 'push discrete blocks of data' rather than as a packet-switched network. Using his Sony Ericsson K750i as an example (1 GB memory, Symbian OS, J2ME, vector-map 3D chips), he shows the device is far more capable than we use it. He ends by drawing on ubiquitous-computing research (citing Stephen Blythe's 'My Social Fabric') and proposing a fifth stage of internet use: 'our data shadows stand alongside our physical selves, working to maintain and improve our effectiveness across the breadth of our social networks.' The infrastructure is already in place; 'this is a software problem, which means it will be solved quickly.'

## Summary

In this video recording, Mark Pesce introduces and explains the concept of Parahelia, a proposed three-day holiday observed around January 2nd or 3rd, when the Earth is at perihelion and closest to the Sun. Pesce frames the holiday as a structured response to the catastrophic Australian bushfires of the previous year, arguing that a dedicated period is needed to discuss climate change and humanity's role in it. The holiday is designed to break out of political and social loops by dedicating each day to a specific purpose: the first day is for a fearless assessment of the past year's successes and failures, the second day is for celebrating leaders who provide guidance on climate decisions, and the third day is for making resolutions for the future. Pesce begins the first day's assessment by noting that 2020 saw a significant drop in carbon dioxide emissions due to pandemic-related lockdowns and economic shutdowns. He argues that this period offered a glimpse into a lifestyle less centered on constant consumption and travel, revealing that things could be different. However, he warns that the pandemic also taught people that pulling back from economic growth causes severe hardship, creating a fear that could lead society to pit economic interests against planetary health. He urges viewers to avoid this trap and instead find ways to create economic growth within the bounds of sustainability. For the second day's celebration of leadership, Pesce nominates Indigenous man Victor Steffensen for a hypothetical Australian Order of Climate Merit. He highlights Steffensen's work with the grassroots organization Firesticks and his book Fire Country, which promotes cultural burning practices.

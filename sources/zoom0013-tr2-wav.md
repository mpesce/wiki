# ZOOM0013_Tr2.WAV

## Summary

This recording is the opening keynote and panel discussion for BlockHack 16, a two-day blockchain hackathon hosted by Westpac in late May 2016. The event, held on the 28th floor of a new building, brings together internal Westpac teams to explore blockchain applications. The host begins by acknowledging the traditional lands of Indigenous Australians and introduces the judges, Nitin Gaur from IBM and Nick Wales from UNSW. He explains that the event originated from an internal survey revealing high interest in blockchain within the bank. The host draws a parallel between the early days of the World Wide Web in 1994 and the current moment for blockchain, predicting that distributed ledger technology will fundamentally change finance. A panel featuring Rob Wilson, Westpac’s CTO, Mike Baldwin, Head of Transaction Solutions, and Ian Hill, Head of Innovation, discusses the strategic importance of blockchain. Wilson describes blockchain as part of a major disruption wave aimed at removing friction from customer lives and enabling mass collaboration without central control. Baldwin notes that while the technology is new, Westpac has already conducted a successful proof of concept using the Ripple network for international payments to Fiji and New Zealand, with a direct implementation planned for the near future. Hill emphasizes the need to solve real-world problems and suggests that blockchain acts as a protocol for exchanging value and trust, similar to how the internet protocol enabled data exchange. The panelists advise the hackathon teams to focus on customer friction, ensure solutions cannot be easily replicated by traditional means, and adopt a "build to think" approach to iterate quickly. The session concludes with audience questions regarding media hype, regulatory challenges, and the role of banks as trusted intermediaries. The panelists argue that while Bitcoin 1.0 faced adoption hurdles, enterprise-focused iterations are more promising. They suggest that regulators, such as APRA, are actively engaging with banks to understand the technology, and that innovation often precedes regulatory adjustment, similar to the rise of Uber. The host provides logistical instructions, including emergency procedures, meal times, and the use of a mobile app for updates. He introduces mentors and event staff, assigns team leaders to guide participants to their rooms on the 27th and 28th floors, and encourages the teams to begin their work.

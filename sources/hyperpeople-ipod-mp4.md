# hyperpeople-iPod.mp4

## Summary

This recording is a talk by Mark Pesce, a media and technology commentator, delivered at a conference in San Francisco. The event appears to be associated with Erowid, as Pesce mentions a donation table for their organization and references a previous talk given in Hungary. While no specific calendar date is stated, the content references the 2004 release of the Battlestar Galactica pilot and the 2005 wedding of Prince Charles and Camilla, placing the talk in early 2005. Pesce argues that a "balkanization of epistemology" is occurring, where technology creates a bewildering social menu that erodes common understanding. He uses the distribution of Battlestar Galactica via BitTorrent to illustrate the end of centralized mass media, explaining how peer-to-peer technology allows information to "swarm" and makes the audience into creators. He contrasts this with the rise of Wikipedia, which he describes as a "knowledge swarm" that aggregates human expertise into a collective factual record, surpassing Encyclopedia Britannica in volume. The latter part of the talk addresses the psychological dangers of this shift. Pesce warns that personalized media consumption, exemplified by devices like the iPod, creates a "tunnel" of self-referential experience that isolates individuals and unravels the social fabric of shared reality. He suggests that the solution lies in "swarming of understanding" through trust-based social networks. He highlights emerging tools like Outfoxed and Isellipedia, which allow users to share ratings and ask questions within trusted friend circles. The talk concludes with a challenge to develop an "ecology of the common mind," including dietary guidelines for media and internal hecklers to counteract the seduction of personalized content. The session includes a Q&A where Pesce discusses the limitations of static social networks, the role of the blogosphere, and the emergent nature of trust.

# ASX New Money LBR.mp4

## Summary

This recording is a talk by Mark Pesce, a media and technology commentator, discussing the evolution of money and the rise of digital currencies. The presentation begins with Pesce’s personal experience adopting a new digital banking app in Australia, which allowed him to transition entirely to mobile payments within a week. He contrasts this ease of use with the global reality where billions of people own smartphones but lack access to traditional banking or credit cards, creating a significant gap in digital commerce. Pesce explains that bridging this gap requires a new form of money: digital currency. He distinguishes digital currency from existing digital payments, noting that currencies are countable, transferable, and non-duplicable, qualities pioneered by Bitcoin. However, Bitcoin’s slow settlement times make it unsuitable for everyday commerce. The talk highlights Facebook’s Libra proposal, announced in June 2019, as a major development. Libra aims to provide a stable, fast digital currency for the unbanked populations in the developing world, leveraging Facebook’s massive user base. Pesce argues that despite regulatory pushback from central banks, Libra’s focus on the developing world makes it difficult to stop. He also discusses the People’s Bank of China’s accelerated development of a digital yuan, driven by both the threat of Libra and the opportunity to enhance its social credit system through transaction data. The speaker predicts that the next five years will see rapid global adoption of digital currencies, potentially faster than the smartphone revolution. This shift will enable microtransactions, transforming business models from subscriptions to pay-per-use services. Pesce concludes by urging businesses to prepare for this transition, emphasizing that digital currencies will provide the infrastructure for new economic growth and innovation, particularly for small and medium enterprises in developing economies.

# Three Day Twenty Hour Caring.mp4

## Summary

This recording is an interview segment featuring Mark Pesce discussing the future of work structures, specifically the transition toward a shorter workweek. The interviewer notes the growing trend in Australia and other countries and asks Pesce about his prediction that most Australians will move to a three-day workweek within twenty years. Pesce responds by clarifying that the shift is toward a twenty-hour workweek, which may manifest as three days for some roles but could be distributed differently depending on the specific position and individual circumstances. Pesce attributes this change to the pandemic-induced transition, which forced a holistic reevaluation of work-life balance by eliminating the physical separation between professional and personal spheres. He argues that this fusion has led to a reworking of societal priorities, with life gaining the upper hand and a new emphasis on a "care culture." He connects this shift to demographic needs, stating that as civilization ages, there is a desire for higher birth rates, which requires reducing the pressure on individuals to allow for more time dedicated to care. Pesce emphasizes that this additional care time must be shared equitably between men and women, noting that it currently falls disproportionately on women. The discussion concludes with Pesce addressing the perception that a twenty-hour workweek represents slacking off. He asserts that the model is highly productive within those twenty hours and that the freed-up time is also used productively for holistic human development rather than mere leisure. He frames the change not as a reduction in output, but as a broader redefinition of what people do and how they contribute to society, balancing professional productivity with personal and communal well-being.

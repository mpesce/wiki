## Summary

This recording is an episode of the Australian Broadcasting Corporation television program New Inventors, hosted by science journalist James Bradfield Moody. The show features three inventors presenting their creations to a panel of judges, which includes Bernie Hobbs and Christine Kanimoth. The episode begins with a brief news segment regarding AFL drug policies and a promotional teaser for the television series Rebus, before transitioning to the main competition. The first invention presented is the Kurali electric fence tester by Joe Kurali from Kinross, north of Perth. The device measures electrical energy from a fence without physical contact, using radio waves to beep when the fence is active. Kurali explains that this method is safer than traditional testers that require touching the fence, particularly in wet conditions or when insulation fails. The judges discuss the simplicity of the audible alert compared to visual displays, which can be hard to see in sunlight, and note the potential for mass production at a lower cost. The second invention is the Pool Boy, a mains-powered skimmer designed by John Bailey. Unlike standard skimmers that rely on the pool pump, this device operates independently to circulate water and collect leaves when the main pump is off. It features a wind indicator that automatically activates the unit during windy weather to prevent debris from settling. The panel praises the energy-saving aspect and the clever design, noting that it complements existing filtration systems rather than replacing them. The final invention is the Believe the Cycle, a seatless bicycle created by Robert Duff from Cairns. Duff, a biomechanics expert, designed the bike to allow riders to stand and use a lever action similar to a gym stepping machine. This design aims to improve visibility in traffic and reduce stress on the knees by utilizing natural body mechanics. The hosts and judges test the bike, noting its comfort and stability, while discussing the challenge of convincing consumers to abandon the traditional bicycle seat. The panel votes to determine the winner of the night. Christine Kanimoth selects the electric fence tester for its originality, while James Bradfield Moody and the other judges favor the Pool Boy for its combination of need, design, and energy efficiency.

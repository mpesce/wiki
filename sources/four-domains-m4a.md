# Four Domains.m4a

## Summary

This recording is an interactive lecture delivered by Mark Pesce to an audience of Catholic educators. The talk, which was previously presented in Daisyville and Melbourne, focuses on the intersection of social media, child development, and modern educational theory. Pesce encourages audience participation and notes that the presentation is designed to be provocative to address necessary topics in education. The speaker begins by discussing constructivism, the idea that children learn through continuous interaction with their environment. He cites research by Sherry Turkle regarding the Furby toy, noting that children created a new ontological category between animate and inanimate objects to accommodate the device. Pesce argues that modern tools like Google Earth, Wikipedia, and mobile phones have fundamentally altered children's conceptions of the world, replacing static objects with dynamic, annotated, and instantly accessible information. He highlights that these tools embed lessons about how the world works, often without the awareness of parents or educators. The second major domain of the talk is sharing. Pesce distinguishes between sharing physical objects and sharing information, noting that the latter has become a pervasive cultural force in the last five years. He describes Wikipedia not merely as a database, but as an agreement to share knowledge, which is now driving cultural change and disrupting creative industries that rely on non-sharing economic models. The speaker raises questions for educators regarding institutional policies on Wikipedia and collaboration, asking how schools can harness this sharing culture for positive influence rather than viewing it solely as a threat to academic integrity. Finally, Pesce addresses the ubiquity of mobile phones among children. He references data from Japan and the United States showing high volumes of text messaging among teenagers, a phenomenon he terms co-presence. He notes that mobile devices are becoming standard even in lower primary school grades, serving as tethers for helicopter parenting and constant social connectivity. The talk concludes with the assertion that the mobile phone is the core social technology of the era, with over four billion devices in use globally, and predicts that every school-aged child will be constantly connected via mobile by around 2015.

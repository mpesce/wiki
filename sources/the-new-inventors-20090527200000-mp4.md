# The New Inventors-20090527200000.mp4

## Summary

This recording is an episode of the Australian television program The New Inventors, hosted by James O'Loughlin. The show features three inventors presenting their creations to a panel of judges, which includes inventor Sally Deminks, engineering professor Veena Sahajwala, and futurist Mark Pesce. The first segment introduces Mike Stratford Smith and his Pipe Boss, a tool designed to cut and bevel pipes simultaneously to prevent workplace injuries. Smith explains that the device was inspired by a fatal accident involving a quick-cut saw and demonstrates how the tool uses a chainsaw power head without the associated kickback risks. The judges discuss the tool's safety benefits and its estimated cost of four and a half thousand dollars. The second segment features Dr. David Schubert from Wollongong, who presents the Gada Heel, a medical device intended to prevent pressure ulcers on patients' heels during long surgical operations. Schubert explains that traditional pillows are unstable and do not fully relieve pressure, whereas his device uses a reusable calf support and a disposable foam component to offload the heel and slightly flex the knee to reduce the risk of deep vein thrombosis. The judges note the device's potential to save lives and its suitability for various body types, though they acknowledge that large-scale clinical trials are still required. The final invention is the Avant-Garde caravan, presented by Wayne Barrett from Surfers Paradise. Barrett describes a luxury caravan that utilizes boat-building techniques, specifically injection molding, to create a monocoque structure made of composite fiberglass and PVC foam. This design eliminates the traditional chassis, reducing the risk of corrosion and stone damage to service lines. The judges praise the aerodynamic design and the material science involved, noting that the caravan weighs 2,500 kilos and costs 159,000 dollars. After deliberation, the judges select the Pipe Boss as the winner of the night, placing it in the running for the annual Inventor of the Year award. The episode concludes with a news broadcast segment covering swine flu measures in New South Wales and other local events.

# 1-01 _The Kids are Alright_ - Kincoppal-Rose Bay School.mp3

## Summary

This recording is a lecture delivered by Mark Pesce, a media and technology commentator, to an audience of Australian educators. Speaking from his position as a former Apple and Mozilla engineer, Pesce discusses the central themes of his book, The Playful World: How Technology is Transforming Our Education. He explains that the book was inspired by observing the world his friend’s child, born in 2000, was entering, which differed significantly from the world of his own 1962 birth. Pesce structures his argument around three converging trends: intelligence, activity, and presence, using toys as non-threatening entry points to discuss sophisticated technological shifts. He begins with the Furby, citing research by MIT psychologist Sherry Turkle that shows children create a new ontological category for interactive toys that are neither fully animate nor inanimate. Pesce then addresses the ubiquity of the World Wide Web, noting that students born after 1994 have never known a world without it. He argues that while children are digital natives with constant access to information, they lack the ability to discriminate truth from falsehood, placing the responsibility on teachers and parents to impart discernment. He also discusses the mobile phone, highlighting its role in creating "co-presence," a phenomenon where children maintain a constant, low-level stream of communication with friends regardless of physical proximity. Regarding media, Pesce observes that digitization has turned content into shareable objects, leading children to value media primarily for its social utility and the ability to share it within their networks. The final section focuses on participation culture and social networks. Pesce describes platforms like MySpace and Friendster as extensions of innate human social structures, noting that girls are particularly active in these digital spaces. He introduces the concept of the "three Fs"—finding, filtering, and forwarding—as the new model of media consumption. To engage students, he recommends requiring them to create Wikipedia entries on topics of their choice, thereby combining literacy, research, and online skills. He concludes by advocating for the use of Google Earth in the classroom, describing it as the ultimate toy that allows students to interact with the world as an active, interdependent system rather than a static map.

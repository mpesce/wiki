# Prime Life Event.mp4

## Summary

This recording is a presentation and panel discussion hosted by Prime Life Partners, featuring speakers Abby Bloom, Annie, and Mark Pesce. The event focuses on the emerging longevity economy in Australia, specifically targeting individuals aged 45 and older. The speakers argue that the traditional view of aging as a period of decline must be reframed to recognize older adults as vibrant consumers, workers, and innovators. They highlight that Australia is approaching a demographic turning point in 2030, when the population over 65 will equal or exceed those under 18. Mark Pesce, a futurist and media commentator, presents a framework for longevity opportunities centered on three key gaps. He discusses the difference between lifespan and healthspan, emphasizing the need to extend the period of independent, healthy living. He introduces the concept of the "thin span," defined as the time one can live comfortably, and warns of a potential crisis where seniors may lack the financial and social infrastructure to bridge the gap between their thin span and their full lifespan. Pesce notes that while medical advancements like semaglutide are improving health outcomes, societal structures, such as superannuation and housing, have not adapted to support longer lives. He predicts that by 2050, life will be structured into learning, earning, and returning phases, requiring new models for work and retirement. Abby Bloom and Annie provide context on the practical challenges of this shift, including the high cost of aged care, housing affordability issues, and the need for better financial planning tools. They discuss the "two-track economy," where older Australians hold significant wealth but face barriers in accessing services and housing. The panel highlights various startups and initiatives, such as the Room Exchange for shared housing and technology solutions for home care, aiming to create an ecosystem that supports aging in place. The discussion concludes with a Q&A session addressing topics like tax reform, the role of corporate Australia, and the feminization of wealth transfer, urging the audience to view longevity as a major economic and social opportunity rather than a burden.

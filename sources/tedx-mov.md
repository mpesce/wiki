# tedx.mov

## Summary

This recording is a talk by Mark Pesce, a media and technology commentator, delivered at a TEDx event in Canberra, Australia. The speech focuses on the psychological and social impact of mobile devices, arguing that constant connectivity is eroding our awareness of the physical world and our immediate human connections. Pesce opens with a personal anecdote about nearly being killed by a taxi in Sydney while distracted by his phone, illustrating how devices can sedate our attention to the point of physical danger. He follows this with a story from a friend in California, whose partner became so absorbed in his BlackBerry that their relationship suffered until a trip to the Amazon, where lack of signal forced a temporary reconnection. The central moment of clarity for Pesce occurs on a bright morning in March in Sydney, when he observes a mother struggling to navigate a high curb with a pram. Despite the toddler nearly falling into the street twice, the mother never removes her iPhone from her ear. Pesce uses this incident to argue that devices are overriding fundamental human instincts, such as maternal care, which are essential for survival. He then shifts to a futurist perspective, noting that five billion mobile subscribers exist on a planet of seven billion, making the device synonymous with being human. He describes a positive feedback loop where capital and power drive devices to become more alluring, creating a situation where abandoning technology is impossible because the rest of the world is adopting it. Pesce concludes by revisiting his friend in California, who recently lost her partner in an accident. While the immediate grief was overwhelming, the community used Facebook to share memories and support her, demonstrating the paradox of digital connection. He argues that while the virtual world is not real, the emotional touch it provides is genuine

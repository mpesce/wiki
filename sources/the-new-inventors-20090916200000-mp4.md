# The New Inventors-20090916200000.mp4

## Summary

This recording is an episode of the Australian television program The New Inventors, hosted by Cameron Holmes with guest judge James O'Loughlin. The special focuses entirely on young inventors, featuring three judges under the age of 18: Rachel Latimer, Arti Yilmaz, and Lisa Bell. The show begins by introducing the young judges, who discuss their previous inventions, including an easy girth for horse riding, an automatic pot stirrer, and a button-doer device created to assist a father with carpal tunnel. The first featured inventor is Hunter Carnegie from the Gold Coast, who presents a waterproof pager called the Surf Vibe. Designed to alert surfers when their parents need their attention, the device uses a vibration mechanism housed in a waterproof casing. Hunter explains that the technology could also be used by surf life savers to signal sharks or other dangers to groups of swimmers. The judges praise the practical application of the device and its potential for mass production in various outdoor activities. Next, the program features Heidi Goodall from Mooloolah, a 16-year-old who invented a whale calf feeder. Inspired by the inability to feed an orphaned humpback whale calf named Colin, Heidi designed a buoyant polystyrene tank coated in fiberglass that holds 30 liters of formula. The device includes a teat attachment modeled after those used in successful rehabilitation efforts at SeaWorld San Diego, allowing the calf to feed naturally while floating alongside a boat. The judges discuss the device's potential for aiding smaller cetaceans and its role in reducing the animal's attachment to human handlers. The final segment introduces Austin Teacall from Wagga Wagga, an 18-year-old motorcyclist who developed a safety vest with integrated LED brake lights and indicators. After experiencing a near-miss accident, Austin created a prototype using flexible, waterproof LEDs that plug into the bike's electrical system. The vest is designed to be highly visible to drivers and can be easily removed in the event of a crash. The judges commend the invention for improving road safety and suggest future wireless developments. The episode concludes with a montage of past young inventors and a news broadcast segment covering local Australian events.

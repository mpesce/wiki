# ABC 14Jul20 Video First.mp4

## Summary

This recording is a presentation delivered by Mark Pesce, a media futurist and former Apple and Mozilla engineer, to an audience at the Australian Broadcasting Corporation (ABC). Speaking from a home studio setup that utilizes OBS software to combine video and slides, Pesce outlines the historical shift from traditional broadcasting to a new era of "hypercasting." He traces this evolution from the launch of YouTube in 2005, which transformed the web from a text-based medium to a video-centric one, to the rise of smartphone culture and 4G connectivity. Key milestones include the introduction of live-streaming apps like Meerkat and Periscope in 2015, and the subsequent dominance of TikTok. Pesce argues that social sharing platforms have become social learning systems, allowing users to collectively improve media production skills faster than traditional broadcasters. This has resulted in a figure-ground reversal where broadcasting is now a subset of the broader hypercasting environment, a trend accelerated by the pandemic’s shift to video conferencing tools like Zoom and Microsoft Teams. The latter half of the talk focuses on strategic advice for the ABC’s five-year plan, specifically addressing the goals of delivering personalized digital experiences and prioritizing quality over quantity. Pesce contends that personalization is fundamentally rooted in search functionality, noting that the ABC’s current search capabilities are insufficient compared to the seamless integration of search and sharing found in hypercasting platforms. He suggests the ABC should prioritize metrics related to how often users successfully find its content. Furthermore, he warns that the mandate to prioritize quality over quantity places the broadcaster in a difficult position. As the audience becomes the primary driver of content creation and curation through hypercasting tools, the traditional role of the broadcaster as a singular, authoritative curator is eroding. Pesce concludes by describing broadcasters as shrinking islands in a vast sea of hypercasting, urging the ABC to recognize that the audience now holds the power to shape their own media consumption and identity through the tools provided to them.

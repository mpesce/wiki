# New Cities : New Economies.wav

## Summary

This recording is a business technology talk delivered by Mark Pesce, a media commentator and former Apple and Mozilla engineer, in Adelaide in 2016. The presentation, which appears to be part of a larger event involving a co-speaker named Tom, focuses on how the proliferation of smartphones and high-speed mobile broadband has fundamentally altered the global economy. Pesce argues that while individuals have adapted to this connectivity, most traditional business processes remain outdated, creating a significant gap between market demand and corporate delivery. He uses the rollout of 3G networks in Adelaide in 2004 and the subsequent rise of the iPhone as the primary catalysts for this shift, noting that the smartphone has become the modern equivalent of a marketplace, accessible to the majority of the global population. The core of the talk centers on the concept of business disaggregation, where companies must break down their monolithic structures into distinct, accessible services. Pesce cites Amazon’s 2006 internal memo by CEO Jeff Bezos, which mandated that all teams expose their functionality through service interfaces, as the origin of Amazon Web Services. This strategic move transformed an internal IT asset into a major revenue stream, generating billions in profit and powering the infrastructure for competitors like Netflix. He contrasts this agile approach with the struggles of Fairfax Media, which he describes as a cautionary tale of a company that failed to disaggregate its services and was consequently outcompeted by smaller, specialized firms. Pesce concludes by urging entrepreneurs to view their businesses as collections of services that can be integrated into broader value chains, similar to how Uber expanded from ride-sharing to logistics with Uber Eats. He emphasizes that success in the 21st century requires designing for a world that is always on and always connected, leveraging cloud services and mobile platforms to create new opportunities. The session ends with a structured audience participation segment, where attendees are asked to formulate brief answers to specific questions regarding these business transformations.

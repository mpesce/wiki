## Summary

This recording is an episode of the Australian Broadcasting Corporation's television program The New Inventors, hosted by James O'Loughlin. The show features three inventors presenting their creations to a panel of judges, including Christine and another unnamed judge, with the goal of selecting an Inventor of the Year. The first presenter is Dr. Barry Rogers from Melbourne, who demonstrates a boat and lifebuoy made from a photoluminescent polymer. Rogers explains that his technology uses specially designed crystals that emit light for up to ten hours after exposure to daylight or heat, requiring no electricity or mechanical parts. He highlights the safety benefits for night fishing and the potential for the material to become an Australian standard for marine safety. The second invention is a handheld paper cutter presented by Bill, who describes it as a safe, mouse-sized device that uses wheels to hold paper down while a cutter disk slices through it. The device includes a measuring scale and a guide for straight cuts, offering a safer alternative to scissors for home and office use. The third presenter is Nick Watt from Woodside, South Australia, who introduces the Plasti Pole, a wheelie bin retaining pole made from recycled plastic. Watt explains that the pole features a slam-lock mechanism to secure bins, is resistant to rust and vandalism, and is designed to be more durable and aesthetically pleasing than traditional steel poles. Following the presentations, the judges deliberate on the merits of each invention based on originality, design, market potential, and need. Dr. Barry Rogers is selected as the winner of the Judges' Choice for his glow-in-the-dark boat, particularly due to the life-saving potential of the glowing lifebuoys. The episode concludes with an announcement for a People's Choice Award, allowing viewers to vote via text or phone. The broadcast includes standard ABC news segments and promotional spots for other programs such as Q&A and Midsummer Murders.

## Summary

This recording is a segment from an Australian radio program, likely ABC Radio National, discussing the current state of the technology sector and whether a new dot-com bubble is forming. The host introduces the topic by citing the Economist and Thomson Reuters data indicating over $5 billion in venture capital flowed into new web companies in the first four months of the year, the busiest period since the 2000 crash. The segment features interviews with three experts: Jody Rich, CEO of San Francisco-based People Browser; Violet Ho, managing director at Kroll Consulting in Beijing; and Mark Pesce, a web entrepreneur and honorary associate at the University of Sydney. Jody Rich argues that the current environment is not a bubble but a natural Darwinian process where some companies fail and others succeed. He notes that social media companies like Facebook and Twitter are achieving revenue positivity much faster than search engines did in the late 1990s. He describes the investment climate as highly accessible for social media startups, contrasting it with the difficulty of securing funding in 2009. Rich explains that his company sells sentiment data derived from social media to help corporations understand public opinion and create situational awareness. Mark Pesce, who recently judged a startup competition in San Francisco, describes the atmosphere as one of "frothy exuberance" but notes that investors are looking for non-derivative ideas. He highlights a successful pitch by a company called Green Goose, which creates cheap movement sensors, resulting in a half-million dollar angel investment. Pesce argues that a true bubble is absent because companies are not rushing to go public; Facebook, for instance, remains private, and there is no rush for exits. Violet Ho provides a perspective from China, noting that while foreign investment in Chinese internet firms has been steady for a decade, the regulatory environment is strict. She explains that media is highly controlled, requiring foreign investors to use complex trust agreements with local employees to maintain control, and that due diligence focuses heavily on the reliability of financial statements and subscriber numbers in a rapidly changing landscape. The segment concludes with a brief note that the legal saga surrounding OneTel, involving Jody Rich, was finally resolved in the New South Wales Supreme Court.

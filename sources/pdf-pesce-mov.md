# Pdf-Pesce.mov

## Summary

This recording is a talk by Mark Pesce, a media and technology commentator, delivered at the PDF conference. The introduction notes that the speaker was discovered through his blog post "Hyper Politics and Hyper People" and traveled from Australia to present. The talk explores the concept of hyper-connectivity and its impact on human culture, politics, and society. Pesce begins by discussing the Sapiens Paradox, arguing that while humans possessed the biological hardware for civilization 60,000 years ago, it took 50,000 years to develop the cultural software necessary for complex societies. He traces the evolution of sharing from oral traditions to the printing press, which sparked the Enlightenment and the liberal era. He posits that the current era is defined by mobile technology, which has connected 75% of humanity and acts as a potent tool for eliminating poverty by unleashing innate sociability. This hyper-connectivity accelerates observational learning, a process he terms hypermimesis, where successful behaviors are instantly copied globally. The speaker then examines the political and social consequences of this new order. He argues that traditional liberal institutions, which rely on scarcity and elite control of knowledge, are being overturned by a culture where sharing increases value. He uses Wikipedia as a primary example of this shift, describing it as a project of "mob rule" that is currently facing internal conflicts between conservative archivists and the chaotic nature of open contribution. Pesce warns that this hyper-empowerment leads to a "war of all against all," where political campaigns and governments cannot control the networks they create. He concludes that representative democracy is being obsolesced by a social order that hyper-empowers the individual, predicting a future of small-scale conflicts and a new form of realpolitik as humanity learns to manage these explosive forces.

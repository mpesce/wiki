# The World and Its Double DECO 2200.mp3

## Summary

Lecture (DECO 2200, in a Victorian-era classroom) on AUGMENTED REALITY as 'the central problem in design computing in the middle years of the 21st century.' Mark notes he has worked in VR for 30 years (as of Dec 2019) — 'for almost all of you, I have been working in this field longer than you have been alive' — and is writing a book on augmented reality, the theme of the lecture. He has 'been trying to explain this problem to people for 30 years; when I started, people simply did not understand.' The lecture is about why AR is the central design problem, and it ends on the SOCIAL dimension of design: when we 'inscribe the world with data, there need to be rules' — how do we get to those rules, are they consensual, where do they come from, are my rules the same as yours? 'Every design decision you make in this arena will have real-world social consequences. You would have wanted Mark Zuckerberg to think about that 15 years ago. It's a little late now.' So 'your task isn't just a design task — it is, and you have to acknowledge this, a SOCIAL ENGINEERING task. And that means you can't do it by yourselves. You have to do it in conjunction with a conversation... with your peers and with the greater culture about the right way to do it.'

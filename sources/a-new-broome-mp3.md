# A New Broome.mp3

## Summary

This recording is a keynote talk titled "The New Broom," delivered by Mark Pesce, a professional futurist and former Apple engineer, to an audience in the Kimberley region of Western Australia. Pesce, who flew in from Perth, frames his presentation as a specific analysis of how emerging technologies will benefit the Kimberley more than other regions due to its geographic isolation. He begins by discussing the limitations of artificial intelligence in dense urban environments, using a personal anecdote about navigating Sydney’s light rail construction to illustrate why autonomous vehicles will first be deployed on long-distance interstate routes. He predicts that autonomous electric trucks will drastically reduce logistics costs between Perth and Broome, recommending that the region actively recruit government and private sector tests for these vehicles to establish best practices. Pesce then shifts to the potential of high-capacity autonomous drones, which can carry 150 kilograms over 300 kilometers on a single charge. He suggests that Broome Airport should develop infrastructure tailored to these drones to facilitate medical evacuations and the transport of high-value produce, effectively collapsing the distance to surrounding rural areas. The third major topic is the transformation of tourism through augmented reality. Citing the success of Pokemon Go and new hardware from companies like Magic Leap, Apple, and Google, Pesce argues that the Kimberley can use AR spectacles to layer historical and cultural narratives onto the landscape, deepening the visitor experience beyond natural beauty. Finally, Pesce addresses the financial revolution, introducing the concept of "neobanks" and "metabanks" that allow for the creation of small, local financial institutions without the need for expensive traditional infrastructure. He proposes that the Kimberley could launch its own bank and a local micro-currency, similar to the Brixton Pound in the UK, to keep spending within the local economy. His final recommendation is to establish a local innovation incubator and accelerator to foster startups that leverage these technologies, noting that the arrival of the NBN coincides with these technological shifts to create a unique opportunity for the region to lead in innovation.

# The New Inventors-20090812200000.mp4

## Summary

This recording is an episode of the Australian television program The New Inventors, hosted by James O'Loughlin. The show features three distinct inventions presented to a panel of judges, including engineer James Bradford Moody, science journalist Bernie Hobbs, and journalist Christine Kninnemux. The first invention is the Hyper Slicer, a robotic arm designed for kebab shops that automatically slices meat using a pressure sensor to measure circumference. The inventor explains that the device improves hygiene, reduces heat exposure for staff, and ensures consistent cuts, with the electronics housed separately to avoid grease and heat. The judges note its potential to save labor costs, though they question the necessity of a visual check for meat doneness, which the inventor plans to address with a laser temperature sensor. The second invention involves multimode entanglement, a quantum communication technology developed by Professor Hans Bakwa and Seiji Armstrong from Canberra. They demonstrate a method to entangle multiple channels of light using a single laser beam and a single detector, simplifying the process compared to previous multi-beam systems. The inventors argue that this breakthrough is essential for the next step in computing and communications, moving from digital to quantum, potentially enabling faster and more secure data transmission. The panel discusses the scalability of the technology and its potential applications in quantum cryptography and future mobile devices, noting that existing fiber optic infrastructure could largely be reused. The third invention is the Foxlight, a portable LED system created by a farmer to protect lambs from foxes. The device uses flashing lights to create the illusion of a human patrolling the paddock, exploiting the fox's natural fear of people. The inventor reports a 100 percent success rate over four years of testing, with the lights costing approximately $60 to $70 each. After deliberation, the judges select the multimode entanglement project as the winner for the episode, citing its forward-looking nature and critical role in future communications infrastructure. The episode concludes with a call for viewers to vote for the people's choice award and a brief news segment covering topics such as a plane crash in Papua New Guinea and a murder charge in Sydney.

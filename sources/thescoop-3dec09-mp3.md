# thescoop-3Dec09.mp3

## Summary

This recording is an episode of the podcast The Scoop, hosted by Mark Jones, featuring a panel discussion on the future of the web. The guests are Mark Pesce, a futurist and inventor; Kate Carruthers, a business and technology strategist; and Ross Dawson, a futurist and chairman of Advanced Human Technologies. The conversation covers topics such as augmented reality, immersive 3D environments, computer interface technologies, and internet security. Mark Pesce begins by discussing augmented reality apps on the iPhone, noting that current usability is poor but predicts a "penny drop" moment where the physical world becomes a database. He argues that the world itself contains implicit data that will be made explicit through mobile devices, creating a massive opportunity for a company to become the repository for this global data. Ross Dawson expands on this by linking the concept to the semantic web and the evolution of human-machine interfaces. He highlights the shift from keyboards and screens to richer interfaces like video glasses, contact lens displays, and gesture controls. Dawson also addresses the role of privacy and the potential for intelligent agents to negotiate on behalf of consumers, while noting that interoperability standards remain a barrier to progress. Kate Carruthers provides an enterprise perspective, suggesting that consumer adoption will drive organizational change rather than the other way around. She identifies the "new generation gap" as a willingness to be connected rather than an age-based divide. The panel then addresses security and privacy, with Pesce arguing that security is an evolutionary process rather than a steady state, citing recent iPhone worms as an example. They discuss the trade-offs between sharing personal information on social networks and the risks of data theft. The final segment focuses on the economics of free content. Pesce explains his strategy of giving away his work to increase his value through demonstrated expertise. Dawson and Carruthers discuss social commerce and the breakdown of traditional advertising models, emphasizing that free content must be used strategically to build brand value. The episode concludes with the guests sharing their excitement for tangible user interfaces, immersive virtual realities, and the dynamic transformation of the book format.

# The New Inventors-20090225200000.mp4

## Summary

This recording is a transcript of an episode of the Australian television program The New Inventors, hosted by James O'Loughlin. The show features a panel of judges, including engineer James Bradfield Moody, science journalist Bernie Hobbs, and woodworker Richard Vaughan, who evaluate inventions submitted by the public. The episode begins with a brief news segment regarding humanitarian visas for two men, followed by promotional clips for other ABC One and ABC Two programs such as Cranford, Q&A, and Maybe Baby. The main segment introduces several inventors and their creations. First, Amy Ramalas presents a tap dancing box, a self-contained studio with various surfaces and stairs that allows for home practice without damaging floors. Next, six-year-old Ari demonstrates the paintbrush mate, a device designed to prevent paint from spilling onto tables. Peter Harper from Sydney then presents a hydrogel carrier for aquatic herbicides, which uses guava gum to bind chemicals directly to weeds in waterways, reducing the overall amount of herbicide needed and protecting native plants. Following this, the show features a segment on Dom Dowling's Quakesafe system, a reinforcement method for mud brick buildings in earthquake-prone areas like India and El Salvador. Finally, Rod and Cisco present an adjustable tongue plate for horse bits, designed to prevent the bit from pinching the horse's tongue, thereby improving control for riders and comfort for the animal. The judges deliberate on the merits of the inventions, discussing criteria such as originality, design, and market need. James Bradfield Moody awards the originality prize to the tap dancing box but ultimately selects the horse bit tongue plate as the winner of the night due to its clear market demand and practical solution to a common problem. Richard Vaughan favors the hydrogel for its wide application. The episode concludes with a quote from Amelia Earhart and a mention of the previous week's people's choice award, which went to a back tape invention. The transcript ends with further promotional material for ABC programs including Wild Caribbean and Catalyst.

# LEAP interview 13Oct22.mp4

## Summary

This recording is an interview conducted for a corporate magazine, featuring Mark Pesce, a media and technology commentator and former Apple engineer. The interviewer, who does not state a name but identifies the publication as focusing on innovation rather than specific corporate implementations, introduces Pesce as the host of the podcast The Next Billions. The discussion centers on the definition, current state, and future implications of the metaverse. Pesce notes that he recently completed a series called A Brief History of the Metaverse, which informed his perspective. He argues that the metaverse is not a single place but a set of qualities: interactivity, sensory richness, and planetary-scale connectivity. He contrasts this with current technologies like Twitter or video calls, which lack full sensory presence. Pesce emphasizes that the metaverse will likely evolve as a hybrid of augmented and mixed reality, blending digital data with the physical world rather than replacing it entirely. He predicts that within ten to fifteen years, digital and physical worlds will be so intertwined that having only one would feel incomplete. The conversation covers the technical and social challenges facing the metaverse, including the difficulty of accurately representing human body language and the need for scalable networking to support billions of users simultaneously. Pesce dismisses the idea that the metaverse is primarily a transactional space, comparing it instead to participatory art events like Burning Man, where value is created through shared creativity and interaction. He highlights that while financial systems and NFTs will exist within the metaverse, the core appeal will be social connection and creative expression. The interview also touches on the impact on business, noting that augmented reality will allow users to access real-time data about physical objects, such as supply chain information or maintenance needs. Pesce concludes by discussing the complexity of governance in digital spaces, asserting that while decentralized autonomous organizations have struggled, the fundamental challenge of managing human behavior and conflicting goals remains a critical and ongoing problem for any new digital society.

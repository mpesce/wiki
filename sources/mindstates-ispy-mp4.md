# mindstates-ispy.mp4

## Summary

This recording is a keynote address delivered by Mark Pesce, a media and technology commentator, at a conference, likely the Mind States event in Australia. Pesce references a previous talk given on Thursday night and identifies himself as a thinker who is paid to share his ideas, specifically focusing on the concept of sharing in the digital age. He argues that sharing is an innate human behavior selected for by natural selection, tracing its roots to tribal resource distribution and noting that selfishness and private property only emerged approximately 10,000 years ago with the advent of surplus goods and power structures. Pesce illustrates the mechanics of digital sharing through the "three Fs": finding content, filtering it for specific social connections, and forwarding it. He uses Wikipedia as a primary example of this behavior, recounting a tour with founder Jimmy Wales in Australia where they discussed the platform as a labor of love rather than a commercial enterprise. A significant portion of the talk addresses the "shadow side" of sharing, specifically personal transparency. Pesce shares an anecdote about an unflattering photo of him being used in a presentation by designer Kelly Gottsch and an embarrassing category added to his Wikipedia biography, which he could not permanently remove. He uses these examples to argue that individuals can no longer control their public image, a shift that applies equally to institutions. The speaker then shifts to the political implications of this transparency, citing the failure of mainstream media during the lead-up to the Iraq War as the inciting incident that eroded public trust. He highlights the rise of political blogs like Talking Points Memo, which successfully uncovered the firing of U.S. Attorneys by crowdsourcing the analysis of government document dumps, a tactic that traditional media could not replicate. Pesce concludes by discussing the empowerment of individuals through mobile technology, citing examples such as fishermen in Kerala using phones to optimize market prices and the viral spread of videos like the George Allen controversy. He posits that the 21st century is an era of accelerated natural selection, where self-organizing networks of individuals are outmaneuvering traditional power structures and mass media institutions.

# V20 Day One.mp4

## Summary

This recording is the opening session of the 2020 Virtual V20 Summit, a virtual conference focused on virtual asset regulation and compliance. The event is organized by the International Digital Asset Exchange Association (IDAXA) in partnership with Global Digital Finance. The session features introductory remarks and a keynote address by David Lewis, Executive Secretary of the Financial Action Task Force (FATF), followed by a panel discussion moderated by Malcolm Wright. The main topic is the implementation of FATF Recommendation 16, known as the Travel Rule, which requires virtual asset service providers to share originator and beneficiary information for transactions. Lawrence Wintermar, Executive Co-Chair of Global Digital Finance, opens by tracing the industry’s journey from the first V20 in Osaka in June 2019 to the creation of the InterVASP Messaging Standard (IVMS 101). Mark Pesce, the session chair, highlights the establishment of trust between regulators and industry players since the previous year, noting that the summit aims to translate that trust into concrete compliance solutions. David Lewis presents the FATF’s perspective on progress, stating that while 25 of the FATF’s 39 members have transposed the standards into domestic law, global implementation remains incomplete. He emphasizes the need for a unified global response to prevent regulatory arbitrage and highlights the FATF’s commitment to updating guidance on stablecoins and peer-to-peer transactions. The panel includes Takahiti Habuchi from Japan’s Financial Services Agency, Ian Lee from Singapore’s Monetary Authority of Singapore, Wylum Kwok from the Abu Dhabi Global Market, and a representative from the Bermuda Monetary Authority. These regulators discuss their specific frameworks, noting challenges such as the lack of technical expertise, the need for international cooperation, and the importance of engaging with the private sector to understand emerging risks. The session concludes with a Q&A addressing issues like the cost of compliance for small firms and the application of the Travel Rule to decentralized finance.

# Techno-Pagans at the End of History 10(Esalen).mp3

## Summary

Part of 'Techno-Pagans at the End of History' (Esalen Institute, 2001) — a multi-day participatory weekend co-hosted by Mark Pesce, Douglas Rushkoff, and Erik Davis. The group moves between long-form conversation and open discussion with the audience, spanning virtual reality, psychedelics, nanotechnology, religion, and the end of history. (Episode 10, wrap-up.) 'This is our last session' — slightly abbreviated because the 'Esalen machine' likes you out of your room by noon. It's a wrap-up: 'Mark has some things he wants to say. This is your last chance to get a crack at us in this context.' Mark opens with 'basic housekeeping notes': the weekend has been 'renamed Letting Go and Moving On,' and he's taking an informal survey on whether the end-of-time event should be called 'The E-Schaton' or 'The E-S-Katon.' He jokes about a rap he did the day before about planning something and then not doing it (he was talking about Sunday morning, not Saturday) — 'this morning I have debugged myself for you. I am not wearing my nicotine patch and I've popped [a vitamin].' The audience discussion turns to the value of the internet/web versus television: the dominant mass medium 'right now is television — a very hypnotic [medium],' but 'the market study showed that what people are deserting in favor of the web, when they reallocate their expendable time, is TV. In other words, they're still having as much sex, still walking their dogs as much, still hiking in the wilderness as much, but they're slashing their television viewing. This is the best news out of Gomorrah in a while!' (Mark: 'Is there a Gomorrah.com that you subscribe to?' — 'I own it!').

# Wave of Automation 2Aug19.mp3

## Summary

Talk to educators (2 Aug 2019) on the wave of automation. He opens with the story that Blockbuster passed on buying Netflix when it cost $30M (now worth $150B) and that he saw the last Tower Records (in Shinjuku, a whole building). He mentions his friend Eleanor Huntington (Dean of Engineering, ANU) is an alum, and recalls speaking to primary-school educators in Melbourne in early 2004 about 'this thing they wanted to look at called Wikipedia' — two years later 'everyone's made their peace with it because we've realized it's a great [resource].' The core argument: the educational systems of the late 19th and early 20th centuries 'were established to create a broad class of workers educated once for an industrial culture that raised millions out of subsistence living into middle-class prosperity. And it totally worked.' But 21st-century education is 'revising and reorganizing around a different sort of social contract — one that promises a net of relationality, of mentoring, that will bind us together in a way that keeps us all moving together.' He closes: 'We are moving toward one another. Using our oldest, our most essential human capacity, to build a uniquely human culture for this century... and it begins here in school with educators. And it never, ever ends.'

# The New Inventors-20090805200000.mp4

## Summary

This recording is a broadcast of the Australian Broadcasting Corporation program The New Inventors, hosted by James O'Loughlin. The episode features three inventors presenting their creations to a panel of judges, which includes science journalist Bernie Hobbs, agricultural scientist Chris Russell, and inventor and journalist Christine Kninnemann. The first segment introduces Richard Fitzpatrick from Townsville, who demonstrates the shark claw, a device resembling a handcuff attached to a long pole. This tool allows researchers to safely snare a shark by the tail, immobilize it with a buoy, and attach a satellite tag to its dorsal fin without entering the water, thereby reducing stress on the animal and improving data collection. The second invention is the aeration manager, a system designed to control temperature and moisture in farm grain silos. The device uses predictive technology and a database of grain characteristics to manage up to eight silos with a single unit, eliminating the need for internal sensors that can break. It circulates air to dry and cool the grain to specific targets, preventing spoilage and weevil growth. The third invention, presented by Chris Trollock, is the Rotabraid, an attachment for a standard angle grinder that holds an abrasive band in place with a transparent guard. This tool is intended for sanding curved surfaces and removing bulk material, offering a safer and more accurate alternative to hand sanding for home users. The judges deliberate on the merits of each invention, considering originality, design, and marketability. Bernie Hobbs selects the shark claw for its originality and beautiful design. Chris Russell and Christine Kninnemann both choose the aeration manager, citing its importance for food production and its user-friendly interface. The program concludes with a call for viewers to vote for their favorite invention for the people's choice award, followed by a quote from American writer Christopher Morley. The transcript also includes brief news segments regarding a terror plot trial and a drug smuggling sentence, as well as promotional spots for other ABC programs.

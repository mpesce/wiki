# The New Inventors-20081015200000.mp4

## Summary

This recording is an episode of the Australian television program The New Inventors, hosted by James O'Loughlin on ABC1. The show features three inventors presenting their creations to a panel of judges: agricultural scientist Chris Russell, engineering professor Venus Sahajwala, and inventor and journalist Christine Kninnemann. The first invention is the Sea Scooper, a device designed by Bob Wright to rescue unconscious individuals from the water. It uses a collapsible carbon fiber pole and a weighted net to scoop a person out of the sea and winch them onto the deck in a log-rolling position, which is medically advisable for spinal injuries. The judges praised its ability to address a critical safety gap in boat rescue operations. The second invention is a building site security device created by Brendan Powney. This motion-sensing unit, which can be concealed in a tool case or attached magnetically, sends an SMS alert and makes phone calls to the owner if theft is detected. It is designed to protect expensive tools and machinery from theft, with the inventor noting that GPS tracking is often unreliable in such environments. The third invention is the Easy Bouquet, a biodegradable plastic guide developed by Tony Amorico. Inspired by a shower drain, the device allows users to replicate professional flower arrangements by inserting stems through specific holes, ensuring consistent spacing and shape. It is marketed as a low-cost tool for both professional florists and consumers. The judges debated the merits of each invention, weighing safety needs against market potential. Chris Russell and Venus Sahajwala voted for the Sea Scooper due to its critical safety application, while Christine Kninnemann favored the Easy Bouquet for its massive commercial viability. Bob Wright and the Sea Scooper were declared the winner of the episode. The broadcast concludes with a brief news segment covering political commentary on the financial crisis, a fatal street racing incident at Warwick Farm, and a missing person case involving a murder suspect.

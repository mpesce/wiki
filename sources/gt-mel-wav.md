# GT  MEL.wav

## Summary

This recording is a talk by Mark Pesce, a futurist and former Apple engineer, delivered to an audience likely associated with Grant Thornton, as he mentions traveling for them. The presentation focuses on the evolution of financial systems and the transformative potential of blockchain technology. Pesce begins by discussing the SME Finance Forum in Antalya, Turkey, highlighting companies like First Access, Copocopo, and Verde International that use data analytics to lower the cost of credit checks and integrate small businesses into banking systems. He then transitions to a historical overview of money, tracing its development from ancient coins in Turkey and China to the invention of paper currency during the Song Dynasty, the rise of bills of exchange in Europe, and the digitalization of banking through the IBM System 360 and the SWIFT network. Pesce explains the mechanics of blockchain using a simple analogy involving taxi receipts and cryptographic hashes to demonstrate how data integrity is maintained through chained blocks. He presents real-world applications of this technology, including Full Profile, a fintech company using blockchain to ensure immediate payment for grain in Australia, and Power Ledger, which facilitates decentralized peer-to-peer energy trading in Western Australia. The talk also addresses security risks in the Internet of Things, noting that blockchain can verify software integrity in devices like autonomous vehicles to prevent hacking. The final section covers smart contracts and the concept of the Distributed Autonomous Organization, or DAO. Pesce details how the DAO, a venture capital fund governed by code, raised $150 million but was hacked for $50 million due to a bug in its code, leading to a controversial rollback of the blockchain. He concludes by warning that blockchain will disrupt the banking industry over the next 20 years similarly to how the web disrupted media, advising banks to acquire fintech startups and suggesting that future professionals should pursue dual degrees in law and computer science to navigate the rise of smart contracts.

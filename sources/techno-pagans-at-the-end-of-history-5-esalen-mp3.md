# Techno-Pagans at the End of History 5(Esalen).mp3

## Summary

Part of 'Techno-Pagans at the End of History' (Esalen Institute, 2001) — a multi-day participatory weekend co-hosted by Mark Pesce, Douglas Rushkoff, and Erik Davis. The group moves between long-form conversation and open discussion with the audience, spanning virtual reality, psychedelics, nanotechnology, religion, and the end of history. (Episode 5.) The discussion continues on psychedelics: Mark reflects that in the mushroom he now finds himself 'very [open],' and that intent, context, and 'how fucked up you are' all matter — 'any psychedelic will act psychologically on an obsessional or a complex personality.' He recounts a top-five heart-to experience on LSD at 19 that 'completely rearranged my life' and 'undid me very dramatically.' They discuss North American DMT analogs (Valerius, Mimosa hostilis, and the Mexican species Sarcococa/stachyri viridis as the best DMT source). The session then pivots to NANOTECHNOLOGY: understanding the machinery of the cell at its finest granularity, and how 'the lines between simulation and nanotechnology converged a lot quicker than anyone had thought.' In 1993 a master's student at the University of North Carolina built the 'nano-manipulator' — a scanning tunneling microscope (an STM, 'a pin with an extremely sharp point and a small electric charge') whose tip and charge 'can be used to move individual atoms around.'

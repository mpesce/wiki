# Afternoon Live.mov

## Summary

This recording is a television interview segment from the ABC program Inventors, featuring Mark Pesce discussing the US federal court order to shut down the file sharing service LimeWire. The host introduces the topic by noting that a judge granted the music industry's request to disable all functions of the software, which had over 50 million users in 2009. Pesce explains that LimeWire was the last major commercial enterprise in this space to be targeted, following the earlier closures of Napster and Grokster. He describes the technical operation of LimeWire, noting that the software scans the internet for other running instances to create a peer-to-peer network where users can search for and download shared files. Pesce also highlights a significant security flaw in the software, stating that users who did not configure it correctly were accidentally sharing their entire hard drives, including personal files, not just music. The conversation then shifts to the broader implications of the shutdown and the resilience of file sharing technology. Pesce argues that the industry's enforcement efforts often accelerate innovation, citing how the shutdown of Napster around 1999 or 2000 led to the rapid development of the BitTorrent protocol, which LimeWire utilized. He suggests that the IT community is consistently ahead of legal enforcement, making it difficult to permanently stop file sharing. When asked about the economic impact on the record industry, Pesce posits that suing consumers is an ineffective strategy for maintaining an audience. Instead, he predicts a shift in business models where artists are supported directly by fans who value their work, rather than through traditional recording sales. He notes that this model benefits smaller artists who can sustain a living with a dedicated base of a few thousand true fans through concerts and merchandise

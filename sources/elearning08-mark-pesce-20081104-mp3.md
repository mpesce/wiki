# elearning08_mark_pesce_20081104.MP3

## Summary

Keynote to the eLearning 2008 conference (NSW, 4 Nov 2008), opening with an acknowledgment of the Cadigal people of the Eora nation and welcoming the community as the flexible-learning framework moves into its 2008-2011 strategy on 'embedding e-learning in enterprise and organisations.' The core thesis: share your courseware as broadly as possible — 'not just putting them on your website or YouTube or iTunes, you need to trumpet the existence of these resources from the highest tower. These resources are your calling card.' 'The more something is shared, the more valuable it becomes. You extend your brand with every resource you share.' If Stanford, MIT, and UNSW can share their full courseware online without diluting the education, no institution can refute that example: 'The value of education isn't in the lecture. The value of education is in the EXPERIENCE of attending school here. You can't download an experience. You can't bottle an experience. Experience has to be lived, and that requires a teacher.' In Q&A he cautions against assuming everything works online — language drilling and maths (the two Kevin Rudd cited for laptops-in-schools) might work well online, but 'these things are going to evolve across that continuum between completely embodied and completely disembodied,' and 'we don't have the answers here yet.'

# Smarter Money 19Oct16.mp3

## Summary

This recording is a talk delivered by Mark Pesce, a media and technology commentator and former engineer, to an audience likely composed of banking professionals. Pesce begins by outlining his background, including his invention of a 3D web interface, his role in founding postgraduate programs at USC and AFTRS, and his work as a futurist for the ABC and Swift. He discusses his participation in the G20 Summit in Antalya, Turkey, where he addressed the Global Partnership for Financial Inclusion. The main focus of this section is the disconnect between small and medium enterprises and banking systems in developing nations. Pesce highlights three fintech solutions presented at the summit: First Access, which uses mobile analytics to assess creditworthiness; CopoCopo, which provides merchant services for mobile money systems like M-Pesa; and Verde International, which uses data analytics to increase loan approval rates. The latter half of the talk shifts to the history of money, tracing the evolution from ancient coins in Turkey to paper currency in Song Dynasty China, bills of exchange in Europe, and the digital revolution initiated by the IBM System 360. Pesce explains the mechanics of blockchain technology using a simple analogy involving taxi receipts and cryptographic hashes to demonstrate how data integrity is maintained. He then presents real-world applications of blockchain, including Full Profile, which uses the technology to ensure immediate payment for grain farmers in Australia, and Power Ledger, which facilitates decentralized energy trading. The talk also covers security risks in the Internet of Things, citing the attack on security researcher Brian Krebs and the potential for hackers to compromise autonomous vehicles. Pesce explains smart contracts and discusses the DAO incident, where a bug in a decentralized venture capital fund led to a $50 million theft and a subsequent blockchain fork. He concludes by warning that blockchain will disrupt the banking industry similarly to how the web disrupted media, advising banks to acquire fintech startups and suggesting that future professionals should pursue dual degrees in law and computer science.

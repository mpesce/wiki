## Summary

This recording is a talk by Mark Pesce, a media and technology commentator, delivered on the first day of 2016. The speech focuses on the evolution of visual media, specifically contrasting traditional perspective-based framing with the emergence of immersive 360-degree video technology. Pesce begins by tracing the history of Western art back approximately 600 years, noting the shift toward using perspective and vanishing points to organize visual space within a frame. He argues that this concept of the frame became a dominant principle in modernity, extending from paintings to photography, film, and television. According to Pesce, the frame purports to show reality but is inherently a lie because it obscures or ignores everything outside its boundaries. Pesce then introduces a new type of immersive camera that captures the environment in full 360 degrees, effectively removing the fixed point of view associated with traditional cameras. He explains that this technology allows the viewer to select what is interesting and look where they choose, shifting the agency of attention from the creator to the audience. While the physical placement of the camera still acts as a frame, the expanded scope of possibilities means that the choice of where to look becomes the new ground for engagement. The speaker predicts that this shift will lead to contention over how to guide the viewer's eye, including the use of sound, light, and other techniques to direct attention. However, he concludes by emphasizing that despite these efforts to influence perception, the ultimate choice of where to look remains with the individual viewer.

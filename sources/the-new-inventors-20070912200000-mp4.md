# The New Inventors-20070912200000.mp4

## Summary

This recording is an episode of the Australian Broadcasting Corporation program The New Inventors, hosted by James A Lachlin. The show features three inventors presenting their creations to a panel of judges comprising engineer James Bradfield Moody, science journalist Bernie Hobbs, and woodworker Richard Vaughan. The first segment introduces Ross Thompson from northwestern New South Wales, who presents an on-site feed mill and enricher system. This invention allows farmers to produce fresh, compacted livestock feed on the farm using raw ingredients like grains and aquatic vegetation, reducing costs and transport emissions. Thompson claims the system can reduce feed costs significantly and offers a payback period of six to twelve months for average farm operations. The second segment features Kerry Freeman from Brisbane, who demonstrates Carry Clear, a transparent plastic laptop bag designed to speed up airport security screening. The clear material allows security personnel to view the computer's contents without requiring the traveler to remove the device from its bag, thereby reducing queue times. Freeman notes that the bag is lightweight, scratch-resistant, and compliant with current transport security regulations, with a target retail price of approximately $70. The final invention presented is the Glue It To It liquid applicator by Gary Clark, designed for plumbers. This device encloses toxic glue and primer within a sealed container, using a pump to dispense only the required amount and draining excess back into the container. This process reduces toxic fume exposure by over 30 percent and minimizes waste and fire hazards. After evaluating the inventions based on originality, need, and design, the judges unanimously select Gary Clark’s applicator as the winner of the night. The episode concludes with a call for viewers to vote for the People’s Choice Award and a brief news segment regarding Prime Minister John Howard’s comments on his potential term length.

# Guestroom - Futurist Mark Pesce (1).mp3

## Summary

This recording is an interview from the ABC Local Online program The Guestroom, hosted by Leon Compton. The guest is Mark Pesce, a futurist, author, and former engineer known for his work in virtual reality and the World Wide Web. The conversation takes place in 2010, as indicated by references to current events and specific years. Pesce discusses the rapid acceleration of human communication technology, noting that mobile phones have become central to daily life, creating a strong psychological attachment where users feel naked without their devices. He highlights the tension between the desire for constant connectivity and the value of unconnected time, arguing that unplugging now carries a social cost in terms of missing out on support networks and professional opportunities. Pesce addresses the evolution of technology drivers, stating that consumer electronics, rather than military applications, now lead innovation, with the military adapting off-the-shelf devices for their needs. He touches on the impact of fast food and pornography on technological uptake, suggesting they drive adoption rather than development. The discussion also covers the future of books, where Pesce predicts a blend of physical and digital formats, criticizing current e-books for lacking the interactive, linked nature of the web. He recalls his early work on VRML and the failure of head-mounted displays due to health concerns for children, noting that virtual reality eventually succeeded through motion-sensing controllers like the Nintendo Wii. The interview concludes with a discussion on cybersecurity and national sovereignty. Pesce explains that networks inherently dissolve boundaries, making it difficult for nations to maintain strict control over information flow, as seen in China and North Korea. He compares modern corporate and state hacking to the cyberpunk fiction of the 1980s, describing it as a new arms race. He warns that the interdependence of digital networks creates vulnerabilities, comparing the potential for a continent-wide internet shutdown to the disruption caused by the 2010 European air traffic control crisis due to volcanic ash.

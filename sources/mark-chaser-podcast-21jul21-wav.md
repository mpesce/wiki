# Mark Chaser podcast 21Jul21.wav

## Summary

This recording is a podcast episode featuring Mark Pesce and two unnamed male co-hosts discussing the spread of the Delta variant of SARS-CoV-2 in Australia. The conversation focuses on the severity of the outbreak in New South Wales and South Australia, with the speakers noting that Australia is effectively serving as a testing ground for understanding the transmissibility of the new strain. They highlight that a significant number of cases were infectious in the community before detection, citing data from the "crafty squire" incident which showed individuals becoming massively infectious 30 hours after infection. A paper mentioned in the discussion indicates that the initial viral load of the Delta strain is 1,000 times higher than the original strain, raising concerns about potential outdoor transmission, a mode previously thought to be unlikely. The hosts address the efficacy of current vaccines, acknowledging that while first-generation mRNA vaccines significantly reduce hospitalizations and ICU admissions, they are not perfect. They discuss upcoming vaccine developments, including a spike protein vaccine expected by the end of the year that is described as having higher effectiveness and fewer side effects than current options. The conversation also touches on the political and logistical challenges of managing the pandemic, specifically criticizing the speed of lockdown responses in Sydney. The speakers argue that the nine-day delay before lockdown was too long and suggest that future protocols should involve immediate, short-term lockdowns upon any single community transmission. They reference the South Australia outbreak as a likely quarantine break from Sydney, emphasizing the need for stricter border controls. The episode concludes with a grim outlook on the timeline for vaccination, estimating 30 more weeks to reach herd immunity at the current rate, and a call for a "ring of steel" approach to containment.

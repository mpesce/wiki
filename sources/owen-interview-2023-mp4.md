# Owen-interview-2023.mp4

## Summary

This recording is an interview from the Video Game Newsroom Time Machine series, featuring guest Owen Rowley, a veteran systems administrator and technology pioneer. The host introduces Rowley as a key figure who emerged from the entertainment origins of the video game industry, specifically through his work at Nolan Bushnell’s Pizza Time Theater. Rowley, who was born in Rhode Island and graduated high school in 1965, begins by recounting his service in the United States Air Force, where he was reassigned from language school to computer operations at Strategic Air Command in 1965. He describes working on high-security messaging networks that served as precursors to the Internet, noting that the system’s failure during mock nuclear war exercises helped inspire the development of packet-switching technology. After leaving the military, Rowley moved to California in 1979 and worked in sales for University Art Center, where he supplied design materials to tech companies including Atari and Pizza Time Theater. In 1983, he was hired by Pizza Time Theater to manage a new computer-assisted animation system based on technology from Computer Graphics Lab, a company co-founded by Ed Catmull. Rowley details his role as the Unix systems administrator, handling the installation of VAX equipment and scanning artwork for the production of an animatic titled The Christmas That Almost Wasn’t. He also discusses the company’s acquisition of one of the first Dragon’s Lair laser disc games and the eventual collapse of Pizza Time Theater, which left him with worthless stock options. Following his departure from Pizza Time, Rowley worked at Hewlett Packard, where he helped put the company on the Internet, and later at Informix and Autodesk. At Autodesk, he was involved in early virtual reality demonstrations and the development of VRML, collaborating with Mark Pesce and Tony Parisi. He shares anecdotes about meeting industry figures such as Ted Nelson, who called him a "kook," and Doug Engelbart, who showed him the prototype for the computer mouse. Rowley reflects on his near-misses with major tech successes, including declining a job offer from a startup that became eBay and working at Williams-Sonoma before Amazon’s rise. The interview concludes with a discussion on digital identity, the evolution of the web, and Rowley’s personal online persona, Owen 93.

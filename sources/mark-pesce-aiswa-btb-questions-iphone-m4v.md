## Summary

This recording captures the question and answer portion of a talk or panel discussion featuring Mark Pesce, a media and technology commentator. The session addresses the integration of social media and digital networks into educational environments, specifically within the context of the Australian national curriculum. Pesce discusses the challenges of using first-generation social tools like Facebook in professional and pedagogical settings, noting that these platforms lack the sophistication to segregate personal and professional interactions. He argues that students feel uncomfortable using these tools for learning because the lines between social and educational contexts are blurred, suggesting that dedicated educational networks would alleviate this issue. Pesce emphasizes the importance of teaching digital literacy through real-world examples of online behavior, such as the negative consequences of creating open groups that attract abusive comments. He advocates for a straightforward approach to explaining the nature of the internet to children, comparing it to a sewer that must be understood to be navigated safely. He outlines a developmental model where younger students in grades one through six require strictly regulated environments to learn the rules of the road, while older students gradually gain autonomy. This process aims to make censorship less necessary by helping students understand the reasons for content moderation. The discussion also covers the potential of the national curriculum to function as a living network rather than a static document, allowing for peer review and collective resource sharing. Pesce compares this to the accuracy model of Wikipedia, where community participation helps correct errors over time. He addresses the role of parents and educators in fostering genuine relationships, arguing that digital networks can only amplify existing connections. Finally, he proposes a shift from rigid year-by-year progression to a constructivist continuum where mentors guide students based on their interests and capabilities, creating a deeper, continuous educational relationship.

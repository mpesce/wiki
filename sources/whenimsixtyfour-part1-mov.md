# WhenImSixtyFour-part1.mov

## Summary

This recording is a keynote address delivered by Mark Pesce, a veteran of the early web and hypertext communities, to a mixed audience of human services and health professionals. Speaking in 2010, Pesce frames his talk around the concept of "When I Am 64," a reference to the Beatles song that marks his age at the time and serves as a metaphor for the difficulty of predicting the future. He begins by reflecting on his personal history with the web, noting that he started using it in October 1993, shortly after working on hypertext systems in the 1980s. He argues that the web’s evolution has exceeded his expectations and that predicting its state in 2027 is as difficult as predicting 2010 was from 1993 due to the sensitive dependence on initial conditions. Pesce illustrates this unpredictability by discussing the invention of the web button by his friend Kevin Hughes in Hawaii, an unstandardized innovation that fundamentally shaped user interaction. He also details the history of the wiki, invented at the University of Hawaii in 1995 but dormant until Wikipedia popularized it in the early 2000s. A significant portion of the talk is dedicated to Virtual Reality Modeling Language (VRML), which Pesce co-created with Tony Parisi in 1994. He explains that while VRML was a 3D companion to HTML and received media attention, it failed to gain widespread adoption because early computers lacked the processing power and broadband speeds required for 3D graphics, and users were not yet familiar with 3D animation.

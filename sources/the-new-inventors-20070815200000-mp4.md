# The New Inventors-20070815200000.mp4

## Summary

This recording is a transcript from an episode of the Australian television program The New Inventors, hosted by James O'Loughlin. The judges for the episode include engineer James Bradford Moody, inventor and journalist Christine Canin, and futurist and author Mark Pesce. The show features three inventors presenting their creations to the panel and audience. The first invention presented is the Whale Shark Photo Identification Library by Brad Norman. This system allows tourists to upload photos of whale sharks they encounter while diving. The software analyzes the unique spot patterns behind the gills, which act like fingerprints, to identify individual sharks. This data helps scientists track migration patterns and conserve the threatened species. Norman explains that the algorithm has a 92 percent success rate and that the system is free to use. He notes that the technology, developed with help from a programmer and a NASA employee, could potentially be adapted for other spotted or patterned animals like manta rays or lions. The second invention is the Sling Ski, created by Angelo Spadaccini. It is a backpack-style carrier that allows skiers to transport their skis and poles hands-free. The device uses adjustable ties and a ratchet mechanism to secure the gear, collapsing into a small size for storage in a jacket pocket. Spadaccini demonstrates the device on set, explaining that it was designed to make moving gear from a car to the slopes easier. The judges praise its simple, effective design and potential for mass adoption by ski resorts. The third invention is the Every Drop Water Harvester by Philip Palmer. This DIY device uses an inflatable plug that fits into a stormwater drain. When it rains, the plug blocks the drain, causing water to back up and activate a float switch that turns on a pump. The pump transfers the water to a storage tank for irrigation or toilet flushing. Palmer notes the system can be installed in about 20 minutes and includes an overflow valve for heavy rain. The judges discuss the potential for scaling the technology for urban water management. At the end of the episode, the judges vote for the winner based on originality, design, and need.

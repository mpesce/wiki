# damer-pesce-interview-florence-may1996 (1).mp3

## Summary

This recording is an interview with Mark Pesce, a media and technology commentator, discussing his upcoming book on virtual worlds and the future of digital space. The conversation covers the book’s structure, which includes a starter kit with a CD, social etiquette guides, and a companion website. Pesce outlines his theory of structural coupling, arguing that informational relationships are inherently multi-way and that digital media will invert traditional concepts of physical proximity. He suggests that the current era resembles the Cambrian explosion of the information age, where diverse forms of interaction are rapidly emerging. The discussion shifts to the social and psychological impacts of virtual environments. Pesce reflects on a virtual wedding experience, noting the mental exhaustion of maintaining avatars and the intense social tension involved. He predicts that virtual worlds will subsume traditional media like television and radio, leading to a convergence of devices that will eventually fragment again. He addresses the potential for synthetic organisms and independent AI agents, referencing works like Ghost in the Shell and HAL 9000 to illustrate the dangers of entities that become too autonomous. Pesce also critiques the concept of nation states in cyberspace, favoring the emergence of diverse communities and arguing that zoning in virtual space is analogous to zoning in the physical world. Pesce identifies himself as a radical ecologist, viewing cyberspace as a preeminent environment for planetary management and environmental monitoring. He acknowledges the dual nature of this surveillance, comparing it to Jeremy Bentham’s panopticon, and emphasizes the need to balance technological momentum with social direction. He advises new users to manage technical expectations, recognize that culture is communication, and not fear unfamiliar spaces. The interview concludes with Pesce’s prediction that future generations will have different ego boundaries due to these technologies

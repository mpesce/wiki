# ispy_ipod.m4v

## Summary

This recording is a keynote talk by Mark Pesce, a media and technology commentator, who opens by defining his profession as thinking and sharing those thoughts. He argues that sharing is an innate human behavior, deeply rooted in evolutionary biology and social survival, rather than a modern invention. Pesce illustrates this through the example of Wikipedia, describing it as a simple technology that harnesses the human desire to contribute to a community. He recounts a tour with Wikipedia founder Jimmy Wales, emphasizing that the project is driven by love and positive emotion, which are strongly selected for in an information-rich economy. The talk also addresses the "shadow side" of sharing, noting that while individuals can control what they share, they cannot control what others share about them, a reality Pesce experienced when an embarrassing photo was used in a conference presentation and a controversial category was added to his Wikipedia biography. Pesce transitions to a discussion on the decline of trust in mainstream media, citing the failure of the press to accurately report the lead-up to the Iraq War as the inciting incident. This loss of credibility created an ecological niche for political blogs, such as Talking Points Memo, which function as peer-to-peer networks capable of uncovering truths that traditional outlets miss. He details how TPM’s community collectively analyzed a Department of Justice document dump to expose the firing of US Attorneys, demonstrating how self-organizing groups can outmaneuver large institutions. The speaker contrasts these agile, decentralized networks with the slow, centralized structures of government and corporate media, predicting that the latter will lose their commanding influence as the former become the primary drivers of cultural discourse. The final segment focuses on the democratization of media through mobile technology. Pesce highlights how mobile phones are transforming economies, such as helping fishermen in Kerala optimize market prices, and how they are reshaping journalism and political accountability. He cites the George Allen campaign incident, where a video shot on a mobile phone by a citizen led to the candidate’s downfall, as proof that everyone is now armed with potent tools for scrutiny. Concluding that the 21st century is an era of intense natural selection, Pesce asserts that individuals are empowered to outmaneuver larger forces through their connected networks, urging the audience to consider where this rapid acceleration of sharing and transparency is leading humanity.

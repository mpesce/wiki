# Mark Pesce WTD1 28Jun21.wav

## Summary

This recording is an interview conducted by Mark Pesci, host of the podcast This Week in Startups Australia, with a guest who is a professional futurist and former startup executive. The conversation begins with technical setup for recording on a Rodecaster and PC, after which the guest introduces himself and outlines his career history. He notes that he left MIT in 1982 to work at Contact Systems, where he helped develop the prototype for the Secure ID card, a technology now widely used in industry. He emphasizes that his career has been defined by working at the edge of innovation, primarily within startups, and that he later moved to Australia to start a program at the Australian Film, Television and Radio School before joining the ABC as a panelist on New Inventors. The discussion then shifts to the evolution of the Australian startup ecosystem. The guest recalls arriving in 2003, shortly after the tech market crash, when formal venture capital infrastructure and angel investment were virtually non-existent. He describes the early, informal nature of the community, citing figures like Ian Gardner of VioCorp and events such as Web Directions. He highlights the organic growth of the ecosystem from 2009 to 2011, driven by the post-Great Financial Crisis period and the emergence of global models like Y Combinator. He notes that while Australia has developed strong mentoring capabilities and a collaborative culture distinct from the more individualistic American approach, the capitalization structure has shifted. The ecosystem is now strong in Series A and B funding but has become weaker in seed and pre-seed stages, creating a gap for early-stage startups. The interview concludes with reflections on the impact of the pandemic, which has put the ecosystem on a "low simmer," and the importance of diversity. The guest praises the progress made in increasing the number of women in startup land, crediting individuals like Annie Parker and Julie for their leadership. He mentions his own efforts to balance his podcast lineup, including a period where he exclusively interviewed women entrepreneurs to push for greater representation. The recording ends with the host arranging to share the audio file via Google Drive.

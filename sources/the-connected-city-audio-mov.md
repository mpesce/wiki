# The Connected City - audio.mov

## Summary

This recording is a presentation or lecture delivered by a consultant who worked with the City of Sydney in the second half of 2009 on IT planning and urban management. The speaker discusses the implications of hyperconnectivity on urban life, referencing the Sustainable Sydney 2030 plan and the concept of the connected city. He begins by recounting a personal anecdote from a 1997 visit to Sydney, where the widespread adoption of mobile phones in Australia contrasted with the lower ownership rates in Los Angeles, illustrating how instant connectivity fundamentally alters social behaviors and expectations. The main body of the talk explores how mobile devices transform the city into a pocket-sized, personal network, allowing residents to bypass traditional organizational hierarchies and access resources instantly. The speaker argues that connectivity facilitates a natural shift toward sharing, where residents disseminate information and expertise through their personal networks. This creates a distributed knowledge system where the city is no longer the sole authoritative source of information. He emphasizes that for the city to remain relevant, it must make its resources easy to share and actively engage with these resident-driven networks, recognizing that individuals often trust their peer networks more than statutory authorities. The presentation concludes with a warning that this radical empowerment of residents carries risks. As individuals gain access to the collective experience of the entire city, they may become overly aggressive in navigating municipal systems, potentially leading to social friction. To counter this, the speaker advises that the city must develop its own internal knowledge networks and ensure its employees are equally empowered and connected. He frames the connected city as a human network where residents learn from one another, ultimately leading to a society where every individual possesses the capabilities of the most expert among them. The recording ends with the speaker confirming the session was successfully captured, noting that the content aligns with the thinking behind a book he is writing.

# ppm-2019-10-21-tik-tok.mp3

## Summary

This recording is a news broadcast segment reporting on the rapid growth of TikTok and the associated geopolitical concerns regarding its Chinese ownership. The report, delivered by journalist Oliver Gordon, features interviews with musician Adam Friedman, University of Sydney digital cultures program academic Mark Pesci, and Australian Strategic Policy Institute analyst Fergus Ryan. The segment discusses the platform's impact on the music industry, its potential to disrupt the current tech landscape, and allegations of politically motivated censorship. The report begins by highlighting TikTok's popularity among young Australians and its status as the first Chinese social media app to successfully crack the Western market. Adam Friedman, a member of the band Cookie Cutter, explains his strategy of writing catchy 15-second clips to gain exposure. Although he has over a million videos of people dancing to his music, he has earned zero dollars directly from TikTok, viewing the platform primarily as an advertisement to drive listeners to his other music. Mark Pesci notes that TikTok has over 550 million active users and generated eight billion dollars in revenue in half a year, suggesting it is a permanent fixture in the tech industry. He argues that ByteDance, TikTok’s owner, has the potential to shift the market away from a Facebook-centered world toward a more competitive environment. The latter portion of the broadcast addresses security and censorship issues. Fergus Ryan states that politically motivated censorship is occurring on the platform, citing leaked content moderation guidelines published by The Guardian. These guidelines reportedly instructed moderators to censor videos mentioning Tiananmen Square, Tibetan independence, and Falun Gong. Ryan provides an example of a UK user having a video of a pride march taken down. A TikTok spokesperson responded by stating that these specific guidelines were replaced in May with localized approaches involving local moderators and policies.

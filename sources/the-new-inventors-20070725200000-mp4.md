# The New Inventors-20070725200000.mp4

## Summary

This recording is an episode of the Australian television program The New Inventors, hosted by James A. Lachlan. The show features a panel of experts, including engineer James Bradfield Moody, interior designer Alison Page, and woodworker Richard Vaughan, who evaluate three new inventions. The first invention presented is the Sub Lounge, designed by Jane Tuck from Melbourne. This piece of furniture combines a stereo system with a couch, using an amplifier and subwoofer located under the seat to transmit bass vibrations through the aluminum body. Tuck demonstrates the device by lying on it, describing the sensation as a full-body massage, and notes that the design maximizes contact between the user and the vibrating surface. The panel discusses its comfort, cost, and potential applications for people with hearing impairments. The second invention is Pure Wine, created by an inventor who explains a method to eliminate sulfites, a preservative that causes allergic reactions in some people. The system involves a rubber stopper under the bottle cap containing a calibrated amount of hydrogen peroxide. When the bottle is opened, the peroxide is released into the wine, neutralizing the sulfites without affecting the flavor. A taste test conducted on the show reveals that panel members cannot distinguish the treated wine from the control bottle. The inventor notes that the process adds approximately one dollar to the retail price and that winemakers are familiar with the chemistry, though the timing of the release is the innovation. The final invention is the Tube Terminator, a mobile disposal and processing system for fluorescent lamps developed by Carl Lipinskas. The machine safely removes toxic mercury vapor from broken tubes, pulverizes the glass to reduce volume, and captures aerosols using HEPA and activated carbon filters. It is designed for use by councils and large organizations to handle the significant volume of discarded fluorescent lights. The panel debates the merits of the three inventions across categories such as design, originality, marketability, and safety. Ultimately, the judges select the Tube Terminator as the winner of the night, citing its environmental importance and practical application. The episode concludes with a prompt for viewers to vote for the People's Choice Award.

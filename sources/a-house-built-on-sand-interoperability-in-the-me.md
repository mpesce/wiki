# A House Built on Sand - Interoperability in the Metaverse.mp4

## Summary

In this talk, Mark Pesce, a media and technology commentator and former engineer, discusses the current state of the metaverse, noting that despite three decades of development, the concept remains largely empty and underdeveloped. Speaking from a virtual environment, Pesce references his work with Tony Parisi twenty-eight years prior on the Virtual Reality Modeling Language (VRML), which he describes as an early attempt at the metaverse. He argues that while modern technology has advanced significantly, progress on the metaverse has been surprisingly slow. Pesce highlights that current commercial activities, such as the trading of virtual real estate deeds, are superficial and lack foundational stability. He uses the film This Is Spinal Tap as an analogy to illustrate how trivial technical discrepancies, such as unit mismatches in scale, can cause significant problems in a digital environment. Pesce contends that the metaverse is fundamentally a set of human agreements rather than a purely technological achievement. He points out that numerous platforms, including Decentraland, The Sandbox, Cryptovoxels, and Meta’s Horizon Worlds, claim to be the metaverse, but none are interoperable with one another. According to Tony Parisi’s definition, there should be only one metaverse, yet the current landscape is fragmented into walled gardens. Pesce questions the value and enforceability of property deeds within these isolated ecosystems, arguing that they are currently redeemable only within their specific platforms. He emphasizes that establishing a common origin and relative relations between these platforms requires complex human agreements that are currently missing. The speaker concludes by stating that the metaverse is currently just a powerful idea that requires substantial human work to become reality.

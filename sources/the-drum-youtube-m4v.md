# The Drum-YouTube.m4v

## Summary

This recording is an interview segment from the Australian Broadcasting Corporation program The Drum, featuring Mark Pesce, an author, futurist, and virtual reality expert. The discussion takes place in the context of the early 2011 WikiLeaks controversy, referencing the recent release of diplomatic cables and the subsequent global reaction. The host and Pesce explore the rapid evolution of social media, noting that Twitter has grown from a small startup to a platform with over 175 million users in just four years. A central theme of the conversation is the erosion of trust in online information. Pesce argues that as individuals rely more heavily on social networks for verification, the bonds of trust become critical. He suggests that without these reinforced connections, the digital landscape can become unstable, leading to a state where users must constantly verify sources through personal networks rather than traditional institutions. The dialogue shifts to the implications of information warfare and government security. Pesce predicts that governments will become more restrictive and paranoid rather than open, citing the U.S. military’s ban on USB sticks in facilities as an example of increasing sclerosis. He discusses the concept of disinformation campaigns, comparing current efforts to muddy the waters around WikiLeaks to the record industry’s past use of spoof files to damage the market for digital music. The speakers acknowledge that truth is often the first casualty in such conflicts, with state actors potentially testing their capabilities through groups like Anonymous or Operation Payback. The role of media organizations is also examined, with the consensus that they must use social media to rebuild and maintain trust with their audiences, as seen with The Guardian and the ABC. In the final portion of the interview, Pesce discusses the emerging technology of the Microsoft Kinect, a three-dimensional camera device for the Xbox

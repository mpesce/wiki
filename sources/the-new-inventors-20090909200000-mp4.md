# The New Inventors-20090909200000.mp4

## Summary

This recording is an episode of the Australian television program The New Inventors, hosted by James O'Loughlin. The show features three inventors presenting their creations to a panel of judges, which includes engineer James Bradfield Moody, science journalist Bernie Hobbs, and futurist Mark Pesce. The episode begins with an introduction to the first inventor, Tony Barlow, who presents a rotating portable easel designed to prevent artist injuries and allow for 360-degree access to the canvas. The judges discuss the ergonomic benefits and the practicality of the clamping mechanism, noting its potential for both painting and drawing. The second segment features Matthew, a builder who introduces a storm seal product. This is a polyethylene film that shrinks under heat to create a tight, weatherproof seal over damaged roofs, replacing traditional tarpaulins. The inventor demonstrates the application process using a heat gun, explaining that the material withstands hail and wind, and can be installed by a single person. The judges praise the product's durability and its potential to reduce construction delays caused by rain. The final invention is presented by Stuart Kearns from Hobart, who demonstrates a smart defrosting system for commercial freezers. The device uses sensors to measure air movement and thermal properties to detect ice formation, triggering defrost cycles only when necessary to save energy and maintain food quality. The judges debate the merits of the three inventions, with Moody and Hobbs favoring the defroster for its global market impact and energy savings, while Pesce votes for the storm seal due to its design and biodegradable properties. The defroster is declared the winner of the judges' choice, and viewers are invited to vote for the People's Choice Award. The broadcast concludes with a brief news segment covering political inquiries, port security, and a weather report.

# hypereconomics 2.flv

## Summary

This recording captures a public lecture by Mark Pesce, a media and technology commentator and former Apple engineer, delivered to an audience of students, lecturers, and industry professionals associated with the ITMP or MBITM program. The event takes place on the same day as the death of Steve Jobs, a fact noted by the chair in the opening remarks. Pesce, who is introduced as an inventor of VRML, writer, and educator, presents a talk that serves as a preview for the book following his 2011 publication, The Next Billion Seconds. The main topic of the lecture is the emergence of a "hyper-economy," a state where pervasive connectivity eliminates traditional market frictions. Pesce illustrates this concept using examples from the developing world, such as fishermen in Kerala using mobile phones to find the best markets for their catch and farmers in Kenya using SMS services to check crop prices. He argues that these technologies allow individuals to bypass informational asymmetries, resulting in higher earnings and more efficient markets. The talk then shifts to Western examples, analyzing how companies like Kogan, Uber, Airbnb, and Zarly are disrupting traditional industries by aggregating supply and demand through software. Pesce posits that these firms are intermediate arbitrageurs that will eventually be disintermediated as businesses and individuals adopt API-based interfaces, allowing for the direct assembly of production value chains. Pesce further predicts that this shift will render traditional employment structures obsolete, replacing them with a "gig" economy where individuals assemble their own value chains. He discusses the implications for finance, suggesting that fiat currencies and central banks will lose power as frictionless virtual currencies, such as Bitcoin, become viable mediums of exchange for global commerce. In the subsequent Q&A session, Pesce addresses concerns regarding government taxation, corporate hierarchy, and the potential for a "war of all against all." He concludes that while the transition is inevitable, it will favor those who are well-networked and adaptable, leading to a future that is more tribal than organizational. The event concludes with the chair thanking Pesce and directing the audience to a nearby pub for networking.

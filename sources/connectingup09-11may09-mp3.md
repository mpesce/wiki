# ConnectingUp09-11May09.mp3

## Summary

This recording is a conference presentation by Mark Pesce, identified as an inventor, writer, and futurist known for creating VRML. Speaking at a session that follows a talk by Cheryl, Pesce discusses how social media and hyperconnectivity are transforming organizational structures. He begins by citing the Birds and Bees text line in North Carolina, a service allowing teenagers to text questions about sex to adults, as an example of unmediated connectivity that challenges traditional conservative oversight. Pesce argues that this connectivity creates new human capabilities, specifically hyper intelligence through knowledge pooling and hypermimesis, which allows behaviors to be rapidly copied and replicated. Pesce contrasts two opposing methods of organization: the hierarchy, or tower, and the cloud. He explains that hierarchies suffer from the burden of omniscience, requiring top-level knowledge of all activities, while clouds carry the burden of connection, requiring constant maintenance of social and informational relationships. Using the Obama campaign as a case study, he illustrates how centralized systems like Project Houdini crashed under the weight of grassroots data, demonstrating that hierarchical structures often cannot cope with the speed and volume of cloud-based activity. He notes that while clouds can become formidable storms capable of challenging towers, they lack a central head to target, making them resilient to suppression. Attacks on cloud networks, such as those against Wikipedia or file-sharing sites, often only intensify the network's resilience and dedication. The speaker concludes by defining leadership in the cloud not as positional power, but as authority derived from dedication and love for the cause. He advises organizations to stop fearing the cloud, to structure themselves to resemble clouds where possible by allowing multiple authentic voices, and to recognize that they are in a delicate growth process. He warns that fighting the tide of connectivity will lead to failure, while adapting to it offers the opportunity to harness new forms of power. The session ends with the moderator opening the floor for a question and answer period, where an audience member named Ian Stevenson begins a question regarding government grant relationships.

# Pandemic Time Pesce 3Ai 15May20 final.mp4

## Summary

This recording is a lecture delivered by Mark Pesce, a futurist and former Apple engineer, to an audience at the Australian National University. Speaking via Zoom on his first day of teaching, Pesce acknowledges the Gadigal people of the Eora nation before addressing the concept of "dense time" in the context of the global pandemic. He explains that his role as a futurist is to help people understand the timescales they operate in, noting that the pandemic has forced humanity into a new temporal reality where the future is no longer predictable based on past events. Pesce draws parallels between the pandemic and the concept of the technological singularity, defining it not as a machine takeover but as a period of exponential change where the forward planning horizon contracts drastically. He cites a conversation with a friend in Massachusetts to illustrate the psychological impact of this uncertainty, where the inability to predict the next moment causes significant distress. Using the metaphor of a rising horizon becoming a wall, Pesce describes how the rapid spread of the virus in March 2020 compressed the reliable planning window for many to approximately 30 minutes. This period of "dense time" was characterized by panic buying, lockdowns, and a complete disruption of normal temporal rhythms. As the acute phase of the pandemic subsided in Australia, Pesce notes that the forward planning horizon has slowly expanded, allowing for predictions over days and eventually weeks. However, he warns that dense time is not over globally, as other nations like the United States remain in the acute phase. He argues that societies are now operating on multiple, conflicting timescales, creating a need for new skills in planning and empathy. Pesce concludes by suggesting that humanity must learn to navigate these periods of high temporal density, proposing the idea of a "Short Now Foundation" to complement the Long Now Foundation, and emphasizing that understanding this new temporal landscape is crucial for future resilience.

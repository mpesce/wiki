## Summary

This recording is a speech delivered by Mark Pesce to an audience in Jamaica, addressing a group of individuals interested in mind-altering substances. The speaker frames the talk as a counter-offensive against the cultural and linguistic constructs that have defined the War on Drugs. He argues that the prevailing view that drugs are bad is not a scientific fact but a form of dogma created by cultural arbiters who use language to control reality and social policy. Drawing on Robert Anton Wilson and George Orwell, Pesce explains that those who control language effectively define what society considers real, a tactic historically employed by figures like Mussolini and Goebbels. The main topic of the speech is the need for the audience, whom he identifies as modern shamans, to reprogram their own internalized cultural narratives. Pesce contends that the War on Drugs is not merely a conflict between governments and traffickers, but an internal struggle where individuals judge their own actions based on socially constructed concepts of good, bad, legal, and illegal. He asserts that human socialization is a biological drive that leads people to internalize these linguistic assignments, creating what he calls mind-forged manacles. However, he emphasizes that individuals are not responsible for their own imprisonment if they understand the mechanism of this cultural conditioning. The speaker concludes by calling on the audience to act as culture workers who reframe the polarizing language surrounding drug use. He urges them to stop hiding in silence and feeling shame, instead claiming their experiences as authentic and reasonable.

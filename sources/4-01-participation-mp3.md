# 4-01 Participation.mp3

## Summary

AFTRS keynote. Mark reports a 'penny dropped' epiphany: while in LA he saw Al Gore's An Inconvenient Truth playing (in only two US theaters) and realized everything the science community had been warning about — global warming, our responsibility — had 'become a trend,' moved from the sidelines to center stage. He then makes the case for PARTICIPATION: blogs, instant messaging, Skype/VoIP, SMS, podcasts — 'none of these existed a decade ago,' yet they are now a great part of our lives, because 'inside these we are each finding our own opportunities to participate,' and that opportunity 'is turning out to be completely irresistible' — 'the place where the bond raisings are happening in our modern world.' He uses WIKIPEDIA as the key example: an edit-yourself encyclopedia, a huge act of collective participation. He surveys the spectrum of participation — from 'easy' participation (thumbs-up news filters like Digg, where readers collectively vote the front page) to the heavy lifting of editors (like Boing Boing, where an editor reads constantly to turn out 'nugget after nugget in predigested form') — and concludes we as individuals and communities are going to develop our own participatory tools.

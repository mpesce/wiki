# WhenImSixtyFour-part3.mov

## Summary

This recording is a lecture or talk by Mark Pesce, a media and technology commentator, focusing on the intersection of digital social networks and personal health. The speaker begins by referencing a recent report titled Health, Lies, and Wealth, which found that strong social networks are a significant cofactor in life expectancy, alongside education and homeownership. Pesce argues that digital tools should be applied to maintain these essential connections for wellness. He illustrates this with examples of emerging technologies, such as a prototype device called the Sanamba that allows family members to monitor an elderly parent's medication adherence, and the anticipated use of video conferencing on the next generation of Apple iPads to keep distant relatives connected with aging family members. He describes this trend as a form of benevolent surveillance, distinct from adversarial state monitoring, where individuals voluntarily share data to support one another. The discussion then shifts to the concept of Wiki Medicine, where patients form online communities to share insights and support that go beyond what specialists provide. Pesce notes that these groups often suffer from misinformation because participants lack a way to verify each other's credibility. He proposes that integrating social graphs into these platforms could help establish trust and identify reliable sources of information, similar to rating systems used by eBay. He envisions a future medical network centered on the patient, connecting their family, medical team, and community of co-afflicted individuals. In this model, nurse practitioners would act as filters between the doctor and the broader community, preventing physician burnout while facilitating knowledge transfer. Although medical professionals, including his friend Andrew Pesci of the AMA, may resist this shift, Pesce believes the boundaries between physicians and communities will become progressively fuzzier. Pesce concludes by addressing the policy implications of this connected future

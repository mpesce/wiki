## Summary

This recording is a short talk or lecture delivered by Mark Pesce, a media and technology commentator. The speech begins with a historical analysis of Western art, noting that approximately 600 years ago, the concept of perspective became a dominant organizing principle of modernity. Pesce explains that artists began using vanishing points to construct works within a specific frame, an approach that carried over into photography, filmmaking, and television. He argues that the frame purports to show reality but is inherently a form of limitation, as what is excluded from the frame is just as significant as what is included. He asserts that every traditional frame is a lie because it chooses to obscure or ignore parts of the world. Pesce then transitions to the present moment, explicitly stating that it is the first day of 2016. He introduces a new type of immersive camera that captures the environment in full 360 degrees, effectively removing the traditional single point of view. This technology allows the viewer to select what is interesting and look in any direction, shifting the focus from a fixed composition to an expanded scope of possibilities. While Pesce acknowledges that the physical placement of the camera still constitutes a form of framing, he emphasizes that the new ground for creative and political contestation lies in guiding the viewer's attention within that immersive space. He predicts that creators will continue to use sound, light, and other techniques to direct the gaze, but ultimately, the choice of where to look remains with the individual viewer. The talk concludes with the assertion that while the medium has changed, the fundamental human element of choosing what to pay attention to persists.

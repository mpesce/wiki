# Skype interview March 2019.mp4

## Summary

This recording is an interview conducted for a documentary on the history of virtual reality, featuring Mark Pesce, a media and technology commentator and co-inventor of VRML. The conversation takes place in March 2019, as indicated by Pesce’s reference to the 25th anniversary of his early work. The interview begins with a discussion regarding consent forms and the commercial use of the footage by Anthem Sports and Entertainment, with Pesce agreeing to participate for the documentary but requiring case-by-case clearance for any promotional use. Pesce recounts his early career at a startup called Sheba, where he was introduced to virtual reality through an interview with Jaron Lanier in the magazine Mondo 2000. This led him to co-found Onosendai Corporation, named after a term in William Gibson’s Neuromancer, to develop consumer-grade VR systems. He details the development of a low-cost orientation sensor that was licensed to Sega for their Virtua VR system. Pesce holds up a Sega Genesis cartridge containing test code for the device, which he describes as the only known physical evidence of the project. He explains that the Sega VR was eventually canceled, likely due to concerns that the headset caused visual processing issues in children, leading to potential product liability lawsuits. The latter portion of the interview shifts to Pesce’s current work on augmented reality and the "Mixed Reality System" (MRS). He argues that social VR is an anthropological problem rather than a technological one and advocates for open standards to manage permissions in shared virtual and augmented spaces. He cites the launch of Pokemon Go in Australia as an example of how AR can inadvertently invade real-world social spaces without proper regulatory frameworks. Pesce also discusses the potential for augmented reality to be used for manipulation, referencing his 2017 essay on how Facebook’s algorithms could be integrated with AR to create immersive, polarizing experiences. The interview concludes with logistical arrangements for the release form and a brief exchange about Pesce’s previous television work in Australia.

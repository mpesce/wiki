# ABJ-JJJ-5Oct06-Interview.mp3

## Summary

This recording is a segment from the Australian radio program Hack, broadcast on Triple J, focusing on the culture and economics of digital downloading. The show features a historical overview by reporter Michael Atkin, detailing the rise and fall of peer-to-peer services like Napster and Kazaa, and the subsequent shift toward BitTorrent. The segment includes interviews with several listeners, including Eamon, Matt, and Ben, who discuss their personal habits and ethical views on piracy. A key theme is the disparity in Australian television broadcasting schedules, which drives high rates of TV downloading compared to other countries. The main guest is Mark Pesce, founder of the media and technology consultancy Future Street, who joins the host to discuss the legal and technological landscape. Pesce argues that the music industry is actually making record profits despite piracy, noting that artists often receive a small fraction of digital sales. He addresses the controversy surrounding YouTube, suggesting that legal threats from Universal Studios are likely a negotiating tactic rather than a genuine attempt to shut the platform down, especially given Warner Music’s partnership with the site. Pesce predicts that if centralized services are targeted, decentralized alternatives like the open-source application Democracy will gain traction. The discussion also touches on the future of television, with Pesce highlighting Apple’s upcoming iTV initiative to connect computers and televisions. The segment concludes with listener Warren explaining his motivation for downloading, citing frustration with network scheduling and the convenience of on-demand access.

# Techno-Pagans at the End of History 3(Esalen).mp3

## Summary

Part of 'Techno-Pagans at the End of History' (Esalen Institute, 2001) — a multi-day participatory weekend co-hosted by Mark Pesce, Douglas Rushkoff, and Erik Davis. The group moves between long-form conversation and open discussion with the audience, spanning virtual reality, psychedelics, nanotechnology, religion, and the end of history. (Episode 3, 'body and boundary.') Mark recounts a story he calls 'a scientific story, maybe,' with scientific verisimilitude that can be put to the test. In 1992 he turned his San Francisco basement into a lab, building head-mounted displays 'with ocular components so poor I can focus on almost any source,' sticking his head into virtual worlds. Simultaneously, a 'rising sense of ontological uncertainty about what the real is' emerged — his psychedelic experience starting to conflate with his non-psychedelic experience of reality. 'Fortunately, serendipitously... in walks a bona fide man scientist. His name is Dr. David Warner' (then at Loma Linda Medical Research Center, Riverside). The discussion turns to how personalities can conflate — taking 'inhuman points of view' — and to TVision, a supercomputer that creates a live view of the planet (talking to weather satellites and geographical data), which he calls 'the finest, the most seminal work of early virtual reality.'

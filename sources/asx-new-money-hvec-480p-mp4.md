## Summary

This recording is a lecture or talk delivered by Mark Pesce, a media and technology commentator, focusing on the evolution of money and the imminent shift toward digital currencies. The speaker begins by recounting his personal experience adopting a new digital bank in Australia, noting how quickly he transitioned from cash to contactless payments using a digital debit card issued via Apple Pay. He contrasts this ease of use in developed nations with the significant gap in the developing world, where billions of people own smartphones but lack access to traditional banking infrastructure or credit cards. Pesce argues that this gap necessitates a new form of money: digital currency, which differs from standard digital payments by offering zero-cost, instantaneous settlement suitable for microtransactions. He highlights existing solutions like Kenya’s M-Pesa and China’s Alipay, which have successfully bridged parts of this divide by enabling peer-to-peer transfers and merchant payments without traditional bank accounts. The talk then pivots to Facebook’s Libra proposal, announced in June 2019, describing it as a stablecoin backed by a basket of securities designed to facilitate global commerce for the unbanked. Pesce explains that while regulators in developed nations have pushed back against Libra due to concerns over monetary control and data privacy, the project is likely to succeed in the developing world where Facebook serves as the primary internet access point. The speaker further discusses the geopolitical implications of digital currencies, noting that the People’s Bank of China accelerated its own digital yuan initiative in response to Libra, aiming to integrate it into its social credit system and expand influence in client states. He predicts that the next five years will see rapid global adoption of these technologies, driven by the smartphone ecosystem. For businesses, Pesce emphasizes that digital currencies will enable new economic models, such as pay-per-use services and microtransactions, which are currently hindered by the friction and costs of existing payment systems. He concludes by urging businesses to rethink their models to leverage these new financial instruments for growth and innovation.

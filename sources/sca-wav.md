# SCA.wav

## Summary

This recording is a keynote talk delivered by Mark Pesce, a futurist and former Apple and Mozilla engineer, at an event hosted by Podcast One. The audience is addressed directly, and the speaker introduces himself as a professional futurist who analyzes trends and data to help organizations anticipate future changes, comparing his work to that of a climate scientist. He references a William Gibson quote about the future being unevenly distributed and notes that the current moment resembles the early growth of the World Wide Web in 1994, which Pesce claims to have invented the first 3D interface for. The talk focuses on the rise of artificial intelligence, specifically how these systems learn from mistakes rather than through magic. Pesce uses the example of AlphaGo defeating the world champion in the game of Go to illustrate how AI excels at specific tasks by learning from errors, similar to how children learn with building blocks. He contrasts this with the human advantage of versatility across many different tasks. The speaker discusses the dual nature of AI tools, citing Facebook’s controversial use of data to target vulnerable teenagers as a negative example, and Maxwell Plus, a Brisbane startup using AI to improve prostate cancer MRI detection, as a positive one. He argues that while AI is powerful, it lacks context and can make dangerous or stupid decisions, such as suggesting suicidal navigation routes in Los Angeles. Therefore, humans must remain in the loop as "co-pilots" to keep these systems on track. The latter part of the talk explores the future of work and personal life. Pesce predicts that businesses will use AI to optimize productivity and connect employees with complementary skills, leading to a productivity leap comparable to the introduction of personal computers. He envisions the emergence of personal AI tools that act as career coaches and wellness advisors, integrating data from health trackers and professional networks to guide individual decisions. He warns that these tools will create complex interactions between competing business AIs and personal AIs, requiring constant human oversight to prevent conflict and ethical lapses. The talk concludes with the idea that while AI will reveal human weaknesses, it will also highlight what is truly human, making people stronger when working in partnership with machines.

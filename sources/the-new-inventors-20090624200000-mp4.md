# The New Inventors-20090624200000.mp4

## Summary

This recording is an episode of the Australian television program The New Inventors, hosted by James O'Loughlin. The show features three inventors presenting their creations to a panel of judges, which includes engineer James Bradfield Moody, surgeon Professor Fiona Wood, and interior designer Alison Page. The first invention presented is a pair of wheel skates by Michael Jenkins from Adelaide. These devices combine the mechanics of a bicycle with the feel of a roller skate, featuring carbon fiber wheels and pneumatic tires that allow for high speed and maneuverability on various surfaces. The host tests the skates, noting their low center of gravity and ease of use, while the inventors discuss the high cost due to the carbon fiber construction. The second invention is a haptic gripper developed by Dr. Saeed Nahavandi and Simon. This device attaches to existing single-point haptic interfaces to provide multi-point contact, allowing users to feel texture and grip virtual objects in three-dimensional space. The inventors demonstrate the technology using a virtual tooth model, highlighting its potential applications in surgical training, teleoperation, and rehabilitation for post-stroke patients. The judges discuss the scalability of the technology and its ability to provide repeatable force feedback for medical use. The final invention is a downpipe attachment system called the Clipjack, presented by Bill Lindemann from Brisbane. Designed for building sites, this system allows for the quick installation and removal of temporary downpipes to manage rainwater runoff, improving site safety and reducing sediment pollution. The device uses a bayonet fitting that clips onto gutter droppers, offering a cheap and durable solution that can be easily removed for maintenance. Following the presentations, the judges debate the marketability, originality, and need for each invention. They ultimately select the haptic gripper as the winner of the episode, citing its future implications for telehealth and industrial applications. The episode concludes with a call for viewers to vote for the people's choice award and a brief news segment covering topics such as the OSCAR affair, swine flu deaths, and US-Iran relations.

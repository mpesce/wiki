# Walking with the Ancestors.mp3

## Summary

Talk for a memorials/celebrant industry audience. Mark starts by debunking the 'futurist = psychic' stereotype: 'a futurist is much more like a climatologist' — they look at the data and trends and tell you how to lean into the coming world. He invokes William Gibson's rule of thumb (who coined 'cyberspace'): 'The future's already here. It's just not evenly distributed.' His first example: a city in southern Russia (Ufa) where a gravestone was recently engraved to look just like a giant iPhone, down to a screensaver image of the deceased. He asks why a futurist is talking about Facebook and memorials: 'everything.' We collate all our memories on Facebook, and 'Facebook works out the emotional flavor associated with every one of those memories' — and emotional flavor 'is the essential ingredient you need to curate.' Curation has always been a human task, but Facebook's AI profiling is good enough to 'do a passable job' of curating an individual's social media into a memorial. The pieces are already there. He lays out a roadmap: in 5 years Facebook-style memorials; in 10 years memorials that curate and present from profiled emotions; in 15 years CONVERSATIONAL versions (conversational engagement with the dead); in 30 years the ability to 'walk into another world of memory and walk with the ancestors' — via AR spectacles, the way a cemetery today helps ease the transition into memory. 'Our future has a lot more memory in it' — 'a roadmap into the next billion seconds' and a business opportunity.

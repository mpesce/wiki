# Smartsheet - Reimagining the Future of Work HEVC.mp4

## Summary

This recording is a talk delivered by a speaker, likely Mark Pesce, discussing the future of work and the transition from output-oriented to outcome-oriented labor following the pandemic. The speaker references a recent conversation with a client and cites his own column in IEEE Spectrum to frame the discussion. He begins by analyzing a chart of United States productivity from 1947 to 2019, noting that despite four trillion dollars in IT investment, productivity plateaued. He argues that the March 2020 lockdown revealed the true utility of this infrastructure, allowing office work to continue without disruption. Using a metaphor of switching from stone knives to chainsaws, he explains that the tools did not change, but the understanding of their capability did, fundamentally altering the workforce structure. The speaker categorizes the post-pandemic workforce into three distinct cohorts. The first group, comprising about 25 percent of workers, are extroverts who thrive in the office and will return, though they now face a reduced peer group and require managerial support to maintain connections. The second group, the remote workers, has doubled in size and is projected to reach 25 percent of the workforce permanently. These individuals often prefer remote work for lifestyle reasons, such as cooking or family time, and are driving a "no normal" trend where employees quit or strike rather than return to mandatory office attendance. The speaker highlights examples from Apple and The Washingtonian to illustrate this power shift. The third and largest cohort, approximately 40 percent of the workforce, consists of hybrid workers. The speaker emphasizes that hybrid work requires continuous negotiation between employers and employees, as rigid policies are ineffective. He notes that 40 percent of American workers intend to change jobs within 12 months, favoring organizations that offer flexibility. Consequently, the office is being redesigned as a space for connection, serendipity, and team building rather than individual task completion. The talk concludes by urging organizations to support managers, who now face increased workloads and must develop new skills in negotiation, wellness monitoring, and digital tool utilization to manage these diverse worker types effectively.

# Surveillance capitalism

## TL;DR

Pesce's corpus names surveillance capitalism directly: platforms profile users through data harvesting and machine learning, then curate perception itself for engagement and profit.

## Overview

The concept clusters around a single, repeatedly delivered lecture — "Technology as a Drug," given at the EGA conference from December 2017 and reworked through 2018 — and its core evidence is Facebook. Pesce cites The Australian's report that Facebook pitched advertisers on targeting teenagers as young as fourteen at moments of emotional vulnerability, and connects it to the 2014 PNAS study in which Facebook manipulated the news feeds of 689,000 users to demonstrate emotional contagion without consent. His diagnosis: machine learning systems built to maximise engagement for advertising revenue profile each user so thoroughly that the feed becomes a "global cognitive bias amplifier," curating personalised realities that erode the public sphere and accelerate polarisation — the definition of surveillance capitalism as a system where pervasive data collection and AI "deprive users of free will" [[sources/ega-wav]] [[sources/the-last-days-of-reality-ega-8dec17-mkv]] [[sources/ega-mp3]].

The lecture's extensons map the model's outward spread. Cambridge Analytica's role in the Brexit and US elections shows the same profiling weaponised for political influence, and China's social credit system shows it adopted by the state [[sources/slv-wav]]. Casinos, political campaigns, and Google's sensor-laden sidewalk installations in New York mark the migration from feeds into physical space. The anticipated next stage is augmented reality: AR glasses as "a new layer of surveillance" that would let a platform curate a user's entire visual reality based on their behavioural profile — constant capture blended seamlessly with perception itself [[sources/ega-mkv]].

Two further sources round out the treatment. At a State Library of Victoria lecture, Pesce gave the theme its philosophical frame: the Enlightenment's conviction that knowledge liberates has a shadow side in which information is harvested to manipulate, and designers bear ethical responsibility for what their systems amplify [[sources/slv-wav]]. And at the Sydney Writers' Festival, the term entered general political discussion — Scott Ludlam contrasting the internet's open, collaborative origins with its "current state of privatization and surveillance capitalism," and the panel weighing GDPR and Australia's Access and Assistance Act against the extractive data economy [[sources/sydney-writers-festival-3may19-mp3]]. Pesce's practical counsel stays consistent across every version: "know your substance" (healthy paranoia toward free digital services), "know your source" (verify provenance rather than accept second-hand truths), "know yourself" (mindfulness about one's own emotional relationship to the feed). The corpus thus treats surveillance capitalism not as an abstraction but as an operating condition — named, evidenced, projected forward into AR, and answered with a personal discipline.

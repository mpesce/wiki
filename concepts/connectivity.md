# Connectivity

## TL;DR

Connectivity is Pesce's epochal claim and his recurring caution: hyperconnectivity — every human connected to every other, first by voice, then by smartphone — dissolves market frictions and rewires institutions, while eroding solitude, childhood independence and trust, demanding a deliberate practice of 'connection hygiene.'

## Overview

Connectivity begins in the corpus as an observed fact before it becomes a theory. On his first Australian trip in 1997 he noticed Sydney's mobile phone penetration far exceeded Los Angeles's, and the observation structures his next two decades: connectivity changes behaviour before it changes technology ([[sources/the-connected-city-audio-mov]]). The half-population milestone — 3.4 billion mobile connections in March 2007 — becomes his evidence that a "human network" had been built in a single decade, carrying both utopian and dystopian possibility: Twitter users mapped the Sichuan earthquake faster than the BBC, and the same tooling publicly shamed a bully within hours ([[sources/nextwave-mp4]]). At the Singularity Summit he grounds the claim in evolution: human sociality is our species' core adaptation, and the mobile internet is a phase transition into collective, "zero-dimensional" social learning ([[sources/singularity-summit-mp4]]).

The economic argument is the concept's centre of gravity. In [[sources/hypereconomics-flv|Hypereconomics]] and the Planet of the Apps keynote, hyperconnectivity eliminates market frictions: Kerala fishermen find the best prices for their catch, Kenyan farmers read SMS market data, M-Pesa bypasses banking entirely, and app-based middlemen like Uber aggregate supply and demand into "frictionless markets" — with Pesce predicting that platform capitalism would itself be disrupted by decentralised, API-assembled value chains ([[sources/hypereconomics-flv]]; [[sources/planet-of-the-apps-auckland-21may15-mp4]]). Australian examples — Kogan's pricing, the NBN's promise of unmetered connectivity "ending the era of rationed connectivity" — localise the argument for home audiences ([[sources/nexus-m4v]]).

But the corpus gives equal weight to the costs, particularly in the dense 2011 cluster. With Australian smartphone penetration passing fifty percent — the first major country to reach it — he argues that constant connection has eroded solitude and internal reflection, and proposes "connection hygiene" as a discipline: boundaries, technology Sabbaths, negotiated device-free time ([[sources/abc702-disconnect-13sep11-mp3]]). The education talks document the cost to childhood: average age of first phone ownership falling from eleven to eight or nine, the phone becoming "a tether for parental surveillance," helicopter parenting enhanced by GPS tracking and spy watches, with Sherry Turkle's warning that electronic tethers inhibit the development of independence ([[sources/hyperconnectededucation-vitta-30aug11-wav]]; [[sources/thenextbillionsecondsineducation-1sep11-wav]]; [[sources/newkit-3aug11-mp3]]). Even the trusted framing of the era — "unprecedented sharing and learning" — is presented as a paradox in which the same connectivity amplifies ignorance and misinformation, making trust "a fundamental, bidirectional resource that is increasingly scarce" ([[sources/truth-trust-and-story-26jul19-mp3]]).

The late corpus completes the arc. In his 2020 ABC talk, connectivity is the substrate of hypercasting — the personalisation and search that broadcasters must now learn from platforms ([[sources/abc-14jul20-video-first-mp4]]); in the 2022 LEAP interview, "planetary-scale connectivity" becomes one of the defining qualities of the metaverse, alongside interactivity and sensory richness ([[sources/leap-interview-13oct22-mp4]]). And the Better-Than-Crack keynote supplies the concept's emotional vocabulary: connectivity as "secret lover," as compulsion, as the mobile phone reshaping social behaviour the way earlier seductions reshaped theirs ([[sources/better-than-crack-mp3]]). Across 57 sources the pattern holds: connectivity is treated as genuinely civilisational in scope — closer in significance to language than to any gadget — yet never as an unalloyed good; every promised capability arrives bundled with a cost that must be managed, individually and institutionally.

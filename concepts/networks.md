# Networks

## TL;DR

Networks are the corpus's connective tissue: both the physical infrastructure Pesce engineer-verifies — packet networks, mobile handsets, the NBN, smart-city plumbing — and the organising metaphor for an age in which swarms and clouds displace hierarchies, for better and for much worse.

## Overview

Pesce comes to networks as an infrastructure man before a metaphor man: he wrote firmware for X.25 packet assembler/disassemblers in 1984, worked on CSU/DSUs and dial-up at Shiva Corporation, and helped carry VRML through the ISO standards process — credentials he deploys precisely when he wants an audience to trust the engineering underneath the poetry ([[sources/broadcast-and-autonomy-acma-30oct18-mp3]]). The 2005 research talk [[sources/telephone-repair-handbook-december-2005-mp3|Telephone Repair Handbook]] is the purest version: a mobile phone is not a voice terminal but "a node on at least four different networks" (five with Wi-Fi), and the ubiquitous-computing future is "a software problem, which means it will be solved quickly." The same engineer's-eye view drives his UNSW talk on externalisation — the calculator externalised mathematics, the mobile phone externalises the social brain — and his study of Japanese teenagers' continuous co-presence by SMS, where the phone becomes "a device for us to explore the connections that make us part of a community" ([[sources/uponwhatmeat-unsw-15mar06-mp3]]; [[sources/3-02-socio-ability-reinventing-the-mobile-phone]]).

By the late 2000s the metaphor has overtaken the hardware. Networks of people become swarms and clouds set against towers and hierarchies: BitTorrent's swarming ends centralised media control ([[sources/hyperpeople-mp4]]), and the Obama campaign's Project Houdini — crashed by its own grassroots data — shows hierarchies unable to survive contact with clouds, which "carry the burden of connection" but have no head to target and grow stronger when attacked ([[sources/connectingup09-11may09-mp3]]). The urban applications follow: in the connected-city talks a city is no longer the sole authoritative source of information, residents trust peer networks over statutory authorities, and governance must adapt to a population with "situational awareness previously reserved for intelligence agencies" ([[sources/the-connected-city-audio-mov]]; [[sources/connectedcity-2aug11-h-264-for-video-podcasting]]; [[sources/new-cities-new-economies-wav]]).

The corpus is equally clear about the costs. At Linux.conf.au 2011 he argues that mimesis — the spread of behaviours through social networks — has been weaponised by platforms that capture the social graph in closed systems, and that the WikiLeaks deplatforming proves the internet is not inherently free: infrastructure owners can and do strangle networks ([[sources/lca2011-keynote-mov]]). The same year he offers the networks themselves to educators as megatrend number one — the NBN "ending the era of rationed connectivity" ([[sources/nexus-m4v]]) — while the plumbing keynote of 2019 extends the network metaphor to physical infrastructure, where smart faucets connect to cloud intelligence and hackable water systems need antivirus ([[sources/a-series-of-tubes-wpc-11sep19-mp3]]). His podcast's framing of networks as amplifiers of both intelligence and anxiety — blogs as "complex ecologies with a low threshold for anxiety," outrage moving from blog to email to front page within hours — captures the double edge that runs from the 2005 hardware talks to the COVID-era security segments: every network that empowers the swarm also exposes it ([[sources/the-new-new-mp3]]; [[sources/ntn-20200402-1107-tech-during-covid-brings-out-t]]).

That tension is the concept's through-line. The Human Use of Human Networks proposes dynamic social networks as knowledge amplifiers against "decision paralysis" from infinite content ([[sources/the-human-use-of-human-networks-mp3]]); Better-Than-Crack documents networks as seduction, tracing mobile adoption in India and Kenya where phones became currency and GDP-correlated wealth creation ([[sources/better-than-crack-mp3]]); and the libraries talk insists networked abundance still requires the institutions — libraries, archives, public broadcasters — that curate it ([[sources/1-02-libraries-in-the-age-of-infinite-media-mp3]]). Across 79 sources, networks are never neutral pipes: they are the era's political architecture, and whoever holds the topology holds the power.
